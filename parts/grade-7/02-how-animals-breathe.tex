\chapter{How Animals Breathe}\label{ch:g7:how-animals-breathe}

The trout under the bridge, the earthworm under the lawn,
the dragonfly above it and you on it are all doing the same
thing this minute: taking \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} from your surroundings
(\cref{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}). But your
surroundings differ --- air, \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil}, water --- and so does the
equipment. This chapter is a tour of the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} kingdom's
\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} machines.

\section{One problem, four machines}

\begin{proposition}[The common specification]\label{prop:g7:how-animals-breathe:spec}
Every \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} organ, whatever its owner, is built to one
specification, met before in
\cref{rem:g4:breathing:sponge}: a \emph{large}, \emph{thin},
\emph{moist} surface where the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}'s \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} (or body
fluid) comes close enough to its surroundings for \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} to
cross in and \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{carbon dioxide} out. Large for enough crossing;
thin for easy crossing; moist because the gases cross
dissolved. The machines differ in how they hold that
surface to the surroundings.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{definition}[The four machines]\label{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}
\omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{Animals} hold their exchange surfaces in four main ways:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{lungs}\index{lung}: air-filled pockets folded
        \emph{inside} the body, ventilated by \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing}
        movements --- \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{mammals}, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds}, \omterm{prop:g4:classifying-animals:classes}{reptiles}, adult
        \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{amphibians};
  \item \emph{gills}\index{gill}: feathery surfaces held
        \emph{outside or at the body's edge}, washed by a
        current of water --- \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish}
        (\cref{exo:g4:breathing:11}), \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}{tadpoles}, many water
        larvae, mussels;
  \item \emph{tracheae}\index{trachea (insect)}: a network
        of fine air tubes piped from openings along the
        body straight to every organ --- \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects}; air is
        delivered door-to-door, largely without the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}'s
        help;
  \item \emph{skin breathing}\index{skin breathing}: the
        whole moist \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} as exchange surface --- the
        earthworm entirely; \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{amphibians} as a large
        supplement to their simple lungs.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Watching the machines run]\label{ex:g7:how-animals-breathe:watch}
Each machine shows its rhythm. The trout's mouth and \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gill}
covers pump in steady alternation --- water in at the
mouth, over the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills}, out under the covers. The resting
grasshopper's abdomen \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulses} --- pumping its air tubes. The
frog's throat flutters --- pushing air into its \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs}
(no \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{rib} cage to do it). Only the earthworm shows nothing:
\omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{skin breathing} has no movements at all, which is exactly
why \cref{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}
distinguished the swap from the pumping.
\end{example}

\section{The machine fits the medium --- and the life}

\begin{proposition}[Why each machine where]\label{prop:g7:how-animals-breathe:fits}
The machines map onto media and lives
(\cref{def:g4:adapted-to-their-world:adaptation} at work on
\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills} suit water: their feathery surfaces float
        apart in it, washed constantly --- and collapse
        uselessly in air (\cref{exo:g4:breathing:11}'s
        suffocating trout);
  \item \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} suit air: folded inside, they stay moist
        where open water would be lost --- but they cannot
        harvest water's sparse dissolved \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen};
  \item \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{tracheae} suit small bodies: superb over millimetre
        distances, they deliver too slowly over long ones
        --- one reason \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects} stay small;
  \item \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{skin breathing} suits small, moist-skinned lives in
        damp places: the worm's whole surface barely
        serves its thin body, and dry air kills it ---
        remember the earthworms stranded after rain.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Two lives, two machines in one animal]\label{ex:g7:how-animals-breathe:frog}
The frog runs the whole chapter in one life: \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills} as a
tadpole (a water-dweller's machine,
\cref{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}), \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} plus moist \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}
as an adult --- the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} simple, the \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} doing much of
the work, especially through the cold pond-bottom winter
(\cref{exo:g6:life-through-seasons:11}: that dormant frog
breathes through its \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} alone). \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:metamorphosis}{Metamorphosis} rebuilds
even the \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} machinery.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Breathing air while living in water]\label{ex:g7:how-animals-breathe:airinwater}
Water-dwellers with \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} must commute. The dolphin and
whale surface to blow and inhale
(\cref{ex:g6:classification-kinship:whale}: mammal \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs},
inherited from land ancestors); the pond snail climbs to
the surface to refill; the diving beetle carries a silvery
air bubble under its wing covers --- a diver's tank,
renewed at the surface. \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{Habitat} is negotiable; the
machine's medium is not.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Reading an animal's breathing]\label{met:g7:how-animals-breathe:read}
Given an unfamiliar \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item where does it live --- and can it leave? (water,
        air, \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil}; commuter or resident);
  \item look for the machine's signs: \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gill} covers or
        feathery tufts; nostrils and a chest that moves;
        rows of tiny openings along an insect body; or
        nothing visible but a moist \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin};
  \item watch the rhythm: pumping covers, pulsing abdomen,
        rising chest, surfacing visits;
  \item name the machine --- and check it against the
        medium with
        \cref{prop:g7:how-animals-breathe:fits}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{remark}[The specification never changes]\label{rem:g7:how-animals-breathe:same}
Under the variety, examine any of the four machines close
up and \cref{prop:g7:how-animals-breathe:spec} reappears:
the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gill}'s \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}, the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lung}'s pockets, the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{tracheae}'s
finest tips and the worm's \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} are all large-thin-moist
meeting places of surroundings and body. Four machines,
one specification --- \omterm{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:principle}{unity of life}
(\cref{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:principle}), written in
\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} equipment.
\end{remark}

\section{Problem: The Pond Census of Breathers}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- one pond, every
machine at work}]\label{pb:g7:how-animals-breathe:1}
An afternoon at the pond with net, viewer box and
notebook. The census: trout fry and sticklebacks; \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}{tadpoles}
(some with back \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}); adult frogs; pond snails cruising
the surface film; a diving beetle flashing silver; mayfly
larvae with feathery tufts under stones; and, in the bank's
damp lawn, earthworms.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Naming the machines.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Assign each census member its \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} machine.
  \item The mayfly larva's feathery tufts wave constantly
        even in still water. What are they, and what is
        the waving for?
  \item Which census members must visit the surface, and
        why exactly those?
  \item The legless \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}{tadpoles} and the legged ones may
        differ inside as much as outside. What rebuild is
        under way, by
        \cref{ex:g7:how-animals-breathe:frog}?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The machines under
strain.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item A hot, windless week lowers the pond's dissolved
        \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} (\cref{ch:g7:oxygen-in-the-water} will
        explain why). Rank the census: who suffers first
        --- the gill-users, or the surface commuters?
        Why?
  \item That week, the sticklebacks hang just under the
        surface, mouths working at the film. Interpret
        the behavior with the specification: what is
        special about the water's top millimetre?
  \item A careless wader stirs clouds of fine mud. Which
        machine clogs, and what would you expect the
        affected \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} to do?
  \item After a night's rain, the lawn's earthworms lie
        out on the path. Explain the classic sight:
        which machine, and which condition, drove them
        up?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The larger map.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Draw up the census as a table: \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}, machine,
        medium, commuter or resident. What single
        specification heads every machine column, by
        \cref{rem:g7:how-animals-breathe:same}?
  \item The pond freezes over in January, sealing the
        surface. Predict the winter fates: the frogs
        (\cref{exo:g6:life-through-seasons:11}), the
        snails, the beetle, the \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} --- who is troubled,
        who provided for?
  \item A newcomer arrives: a water spider that weaves a
        silk bell and stocks it with surface air. Place
        its trick beside the beetle's, and name the
        machine it keeps serving.
  \item Conclude: why does one small pond need four
        different \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} machines to be fully
        inhabited? One sentence, using \emph{medium} and
        \emph{\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen}}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
