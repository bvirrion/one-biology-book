\chapter{Organs at Work}\label{ch:g7:organs-at-work}

Sprint up three flights of stairs and take stock: \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}
burning, \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} hammering, breath racing --- and, oddly,
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} flushing and warm. Nobody ordered these changes; they
ordered themselves, and in perfect proportion. This chapter
measures what a working organ actually consumes, and how
the body reorganizes its deliveries the moment work begins.

\section{What a working muscle consumes}

\begin{proposition}[The muscle's bill]\label{prop:g7:organs-at-work:bill}
A working \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} spends, and its spending can be measured.
Compared with the same \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} at rest, a hard-working
\omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item takes far more \emph{\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen}} from the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}
        crossing it --- several times its resting draw;
  \item takes far more \emph{glucose}\index{glucose} --- the
        \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}'s ready \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion}{sugar-nutrient}, its everyday fuel;
  \item releases correspondingly more \emph{\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{carbon dioxide}}
        --- and \emph{heat}.
\end{itemize}
This is \cref{prop:g7:what-is-respiration:power}'s cellular
burning, read from an organ's meters: fuel and \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} in,
work, waste gas and warmth out.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Measuring across a muscle]\label{ex:g7:organs-at-work:measure}
Physiologists compare \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} \emph{entering} a \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} with
\omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} \emph{leaving} it. At rest, the leaving \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} has
given up a modest share of its \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} and \omterm{prop:g7:organs-at-work:bill}{glucose}. In hard
work the same comparison shows the leaving \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} stripped
--- most of its deliverable \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} taken --- while \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{carbon
dioxide} has jumped. Same organ, same meters: only the work
changed, and the bill followed it.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[The warmth is the receipt]\label{rem:g7:organs-at-work:warmth}
The working body's heat is not an accident: burning fuel
always yields warmth, and \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} is the body's furnace
room. Shivering is the trick run deliberately --- \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}
set to work at nothing but warmth
(\cref{ex:g3:balanced-diet:rest}'s standing charge, briefly
doubled). The sprint's flushed \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} is the cooling system
carrying furnace heat away --- next section's business.
\end{remark}

\section{Deliveries on demand}

\begin{proposition}[The body reorganizes its supply]\label{prop:g7:organs-at-work:supply}
The moment work begins, the supply lines reorganize to pay
the bill (\cref{ex:g4:heart-and-blood:effort} announced it;
here is the full dispatch):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing}} deepens and quickens ---
        reloading the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} faster at the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs}
        (\cref{ex:g4:breathing:running});
  \item the \emph{\omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart}} beats faster and harder --- more
        rounds per minute
        (\cref{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse});
  \item the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} is \emph{redirected}: the \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:vessels}{vessels}
        serving the working \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} (and the cooling
        \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}) widen, while those of the resting \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:tract}{digestive
        tract} narrow --- the same \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}, rerouted to the
        floor that is spending.
\end{enumerate}
All three follow the work automatically, steered by the
\omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}'s unasked services
(\cref{rem:g5:brain-in-command:more}).
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The dispatch, felt from inside]\label{ex:g7:organs-at-work:felt}
The stair sprint's stock-taking, explained line by line:
racing breath --- reloading; hammering \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} --- faster
rounds; burning \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} --- the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}' bill being paid at
the limit; flushed warm \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} --- furnace heat carried out
to the cooling surface. Even the classic stitch and the
sluggishness after a big meal fit: \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion}{digestion} and sprint
compete for the rerouted \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}, which is why
\cref{met:g4:journey-of-food:habits} advised against
racing on a full \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:tract}{stomach}.
\end{example}

\begin{definition}[Recovery]\label{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}
When the effort stops, \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse} and \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} do not: they
fall back only gradually --- the
\emph{recovery}\index{recovery} phase. The body is settling
debts: reloading its \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} stores, clearing the effort's
wastes, shedding the accumulated heat. The fitter the
body, the faster its recovery --- which is why recovery
time is itself a fitness measurement.
\end{definition}

\section{Training, and the limits of the machine}

\begin{proposition}[Training raises the ceiling]\label{prop:g7:organs-at-work:training}
Exercised regularly, the whole supply chain strengthens
(\cref{rem:g4:heart-and-blood:rest},
\cref{met:g4:breathing:care}): the \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} moves more \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}
per beat, the \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} deepen, the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}
themselves grow and store more fuel, and their fine
\omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:vessels}{vessels} multiply. Result: the same effort costs a trained
body a lower \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse}, and its ceiling --- the hardest effort
it can pay for --- rises. The machine is rebuilt by its
own use.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Two runners, one hill]\label{ex:g7:organs-at-work:runners}
Untrained and trained run the same hill. Untrained: \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse}
at $180$ halfway, gasping, forced to walk --- the supply
lines cannot pay the bill, and the debt calls a halt.
Trained: \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse} $150$ at the top, speech possible, \omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery}
in three minutes. Same hill, same bill --- different
ceilings, built by months of exactly such hills taken
gently.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Reading your own machine]\label{met:g7:organs-at-work:read}
A self-experiment with a watch
(\cref{met:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse} supplies the \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse}
technique):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item record the resting \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse}, seated and calm;
  \item do three minutes of steady effort --- step-ups,
        skipping;
  \item record the \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse} at once, then each minute until
        it returns to rest: the \omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery} time;
  \item repeat weekly through a term of regular exercise:
        watch resting \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse}, effort \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse} and \omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery}
        time all drift down --- the rebuild of
        \cref{prop:g7:organs-at-work:training}, in your
        own data.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\section{Problem: The School Fitness Study}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- a term of pulse data,
read like a physiologist}]\label{pb:g7:organs-at-work:1}
The class runs \cref{met:g7:organs-at-work:read} every
Monday for a term: resting \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse}, \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse} after three
minutes of step-ups, and \omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery} time. Two anonymized
records: student A --- week 1: rest $84$, effort $168$,
\omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery} $9$ minutes; week 12: rest $72$, effort $150$,
\omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery} $5$ minutes. Student B --- week 1: rest $70$,
effort $150$, \omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery} $4$ minutes; week 12: rest $69$,
effort $148$, \omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery} $4$ minutes.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Inside one session.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item During the step-ups, list what is happening to a
        thigh \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle}'s \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} and \omterm{prop:g7:organs-at-work:bill}{glucose} draw, and to
        its \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{carbon dioxide} and heat output.
  \item Explain why \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} and \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse} climb
        \emph{together} during the minutes of effort.
  \item A student feels warm and flushed by minute three.
        Give the furnace-and-cooling account.
  \item Why does the \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse} not drop to resting the moment
        the step-ups stop?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Reading the term.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Describe A's changes across the term, and name
        the rebuilds of
        \cref{prop:g7:organs-at-work:training} behind
        each number.
  \item B's numbers barely moved. Give the two honest
        readings --- and which extra fact about B's life
        outside class would decide between them.
  \item Week 5, a student measures right after a hurried
        lunch and gets odd numbers. Which rule of
        \cref{prop:g7:organs-at-work:supply} interfered,
        and what should the protocol say about measuring
        times?
  \item Why must each student always measure the same
        way --- same effort, same timing? (An old friend
        among methods; name it.)
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The physiologist's
letter.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Write the two-sentence summary of A's term for
        the class notice board, using \emph{ceiling} and
        \emph{\omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery}}.
  \item A parent asks whether a low resting \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse} is
        worrying. Answer with
        \cref{exo:g4:heart-and-blood:11}'s cyclist.
  \item The class wants one number to track next term.
        Argue for \omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery} time over effort \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse}, using
        \cref{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}.
  \item Close with the study's moral in one sentence:
        what rebuilt A's machine?
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
