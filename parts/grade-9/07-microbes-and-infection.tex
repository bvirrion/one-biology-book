\chapter{Microbes and Infection}\label{ch:g9:microbes-and-infection}

They have shadowed this book since grade one's invisible
\omterm{def:g1:healthy-habits:germs}{germs} (\cref{def:g1:healthy-habits:germs}): risen to
workforce in the bakery
(\cref{ch:g6:producing-our-food}), to \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposer} crews in
the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil}, to evolving adversary in the hospital ward
(\cref{ex:g9:how-species-change:resistance}). This chapter
finally gives the \omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:fermentation}{microbes} their full account: what they
are, how the harmful minority attacks, and how the body's
outer defenses --- and ours --- hold the doors.

\section{The microbial world}

\begin{definition}[Microbes, sorted]\label{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}
\emph{Microorganisms}\index{microorganism} --- \omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:fermentation}{microbes} ---
are \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living things} too small to see unaided. The main
kinds:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{bacteria}\index{bacteria}: single \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}
        without a true \omterm{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:parts}{nucleus}, far smaller than your
        \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}, dividing fast
        (\cref{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:unicellular}'s
        world) --- everywhere, in numbers beyond count;
  \item \emph{single-celled \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{fungi} and others}: \omterm{ex:g6:producing-our-food:bread}{yeasts}
        (\cref{ex:g6:producing-our-food:bread}), molds'
        \omterm{def:g4:plants-without-seeds:spore}{spores}, the pond drop's swimmers;
  \item \emph{viruses}\index{virus}: smaller still, and
        different in kind --- a packaged scrap of genetic
        text (\cref{def:g9:genes-and-dna:dna}'s letters)
        in a coat, with \emph{no \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} and no life of its
        own}: a virus does nothing until it enters a
        living \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} and turns that \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}'s reading
        machinery to copying the intruder's text. On the
        signs of \cref{prop:g1:living-or-not:signs},
        viruses sit at life's very edge.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Mostly harmless, often essential]\label{prop:g9:microbes-and-infection:mostly}
The harmful reputation is a minority report. Most \omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:fermentation}{microbes}
ignore us; many serve us: the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposer} crews
(\cref{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}), the food
workforces, the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil}'s chemists --- and your own
\emph{resident flora}\index{resident flora}: the trillions
of harmless \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria} carpeting \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} and gut, aiding
\omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion}{digestion} and, by sheer occupation, crowding intruders off
the ground. Only a small minority --- the
\emph{pathogens}\index{pathogen} --- cause disease.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[One body, three relations]\label{ex:g9:microbes-and-infection:relations}
On your \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} this minute: residents by the billion,
holding the surface (relation one: alliance). In your
yogurt at breakfast: workers, souring on command
(relation two: employment,
\cref{ex:g6:producing-our-food:yogurt}). On the rusty
nail in the shed: \omterm{def:g4:plants-without-seeds:spore}{spores} of the lockjaw bacterium,
waiting for a deep cut (relation three: the pathogen
minority, and the reason for
\cref{ch:g9:immune-defenses} and the tetanus booster).
\end{example}

\section{Infection: the attack, staged}

\begin{definition}[Infection's stages]\label{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:stages}
\emph{Infection}\index{infection} runs in stages, each
with its name:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{transmission}: the pathogen travels ---
        touched surfaces and hands, coughed droplets,
        contaminated food and water, \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}, and the
        sexual route \cref{def:g8:contraception:condom}
        flagged;
  \item \emph{entry}: through a breach --- cut \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}, or
        the body's open doors: airways, gut, and the
        routes above;
  \item \emph{multiplication}: warm, wet and fed, the
        arrivals divide --- \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria} by
        \cref{prop:g6:decomposers-and-soil:speed}'s
        conditions-rule, hours doubling their numbers;
        \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{viruses} by turning \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} into copy-shops;
  \item \emph{disease}: symptoms --- from the damage
        itself, from bacterial poisons, and partly (next
        chapter) from the defense's own battle.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Two invaders, contrasted]\label{ex:g9:microbes-and-infection:contrast}
The cut's bacterium against the winter's cold \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus}.
The bacterium: enters the breach, multiplies \emph{between}
\omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} in the warm wet tissue, its wastes poisoning the
neighborhood --- the throb and pus of a septic finger.
The \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus}: rides a droplet to the airway lining, enters
the lining \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} themselves, and each hijacked \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}
releases a crowd of copies before failing --- the raw
throat and streaming nose of its trail. Different
mechanics; the antibiotic that starves the first cannot
touch the second, for the second borrows \emph{your}
machinery, and drugs against it are hard to aim.
\end{example}

\section{Holding the doors}

\begin{proposition}[The first defenses]\label{prop:g9:microbes-and-infection:doors}
Before any battle, the body holds its borders:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}}: dry, layered, self-renewing --- the
        wall (\cref{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}'s organ,
        on garrison duty);
  \item \emph{linings and their brooms}: the airways'
        cleaning surfaces
        (\cref{prop:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:smoke}
        showed them overloaded), tears' and \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:tract}{saliva}'s
        rinsing chemistry, the \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:tract}{stomach}'s acid bath
        (\cref{pb:g7:digestion-and-nutrients:1}
        sterilizes as it digests);
  \item \emph{the residents}: the flora's occupied
        ground, little room for landings;
\end{itemize}
and \omterm{def:g1:healthy-habits:hygiene}{hygiene} extends them: the soap of
\cref{met:g1:healthy-habits:hands} (now with its full
mechanism --- transmission interrupted), safe water,
cooked and cold-kept food
(\cref{rem:g6:producing-our-food:goodbad}'s
conditions-refusal), the sterilized needle, the \omterm{def:g8:contraception:condom}{condom}'s
barrier. Every \omterm{def:g1:healthy-habits:hygiene}{hygiene} rule of this book is a
transmission or entry stage, blocked.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The two doctors who moved the numbers]\label{ex:g9:microbes-and-infection:history}
Two names anchor the history. Semmelweis, 1840s Vienna:
childbed fever deaths collapsed on his maternity ward
when doctors washed hands between autopsy room and
delivery --- transmission interrupted before anyone had
seen the traveler. Pasteur, a generation later, showed
the travelers themselves: \omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:fermentation}{microbes}, not ``bad air'',
souring the wine, killing the silkworms, infecting the
wounds --- and heat (his \emph{pasteurization}) or
sterilization stopping them. Germ theory turned
hospitals from dangers into defenses within a lifetime.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Auditing an infection risk]\label{met:g9:microbes-and-infection:audit}
For any situation --- kitchen, wound, ward, crowd:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item name the plausible pathogen and its kind
        (bacterium, \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus}, \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{fungus});
  \item trace its transmission route to you;
  \item find its entry door;
  \item ask what multiplies it --- warmth, moisture,
        food, time;
  \item block the cheapest stage: wash, cook, cover,
        chill, sterilize, vaccinate
        (\cref{ch:g9:immune-defenses} explains the
        last).
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\section{Problem: The Outbreak Notebook}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- a school outbreak,
traced stage by stage}]\label{pb:g9:microbes-and-infection:1}
A (fictional) school outbreak: within one week, half of
class 9B reports fever and cough; cases then dot the
other classes. The school nurse keeps an outbreak
notebook and asks the \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{biology} class to help read it.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Reading the spread.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The first case --- the index --- sat in 9B; the
        next eight share her row and her table-tennis
        partners. Name the likely transmission route,
        and the entry door.
  \item Cases double every two days at first. Which
        stage's arithmetic is showing, and whose
        machinery is doing the copying if the agent is a
        \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus}?
  \item The nurse's checklist: fever's onset, cough,
        raw throat. Which stage do symptoms mark, and
        from what two sources do they arise?
  \item Why did the outbreak leap classes at exactly the
        week's two whole-school assemblies?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The counter-attack.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The school's measures: sick students home;
        windows opened
        (\cref{exo:g4:breathing:10}); hand-washing
        drilled; shared equipment wiped. Assign each
        measure its blocked stage.
  \item A parent demands antibiotics for all. Draft the
        doctor's answer for a viral outbreak --- and the
        selection warning
        (\cref{exo:g9:how-species-change:10}) if
        antibiotics were scattered anyway.
  \item The canteen simultaneously reports two \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:tract}{stomach}
        cases traced to an unrefrigerated dessert.
        Audit that side-outbreak by
        \cref{met:g9:microbes-and-infection:audit} ---
        different agent kind, different stages.
  \item Why does the nurse map \emph{who sat where}
        rather than treat cases as random? What is an
        outbreak map, in transmission terms?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The debrief.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Write the outbreak's biography in the four
        stages, index case to fade-out.
  \item The outbreak faded before reaching every
        student. Offer two mechanisms --- the measures'
        blocks, and the next chapter's hint: those
        already immune from last year's cousin strain.
  \item Add the history paragraph: which two
        nineteenth-century lessons
        (\cref{ex:g9:microbes-and-infection:history})
        did the school's whole response descend from?
  \item Close the notebook with the audit method as a
        motto: one sentence on where outbreaks are
        cheapest to stop.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
