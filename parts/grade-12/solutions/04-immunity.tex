\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g12:immunity} --- Immunity}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:1}
Any four barrier defences: intact skin, mucus, cilia, stomach acid, lysozyme
in tears or saliva, normal microbiota, flushing by urine or tears, and similar
physical or \omterm{def:g12:nerves-hormones:synapse}{chemical} barriers that act before phagocytes engage a \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{pathogen}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:2}
An \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{antigen} is a molecule (often foreign) that can be specifically recognised
by adaptive immune receptors. An \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{antibody} is an immunoglobulin \omterm{def:g10:molecules-of-life:proteins}{protein} that
binds a specific \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{antigen} or epitope. \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{Antibodies} are the soluble effectors that
match \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{antigens} selected during \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{B-cell} responses.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:3}
\omterm{def:g12:immunity:innate}{Innate immunity} is fast, broadly targeted, and does not form classical
antigen-specific long-term memory. \omterm{def:g12:immunity:adaptive}{Adaptive immunity} is slower on first
encounter, highly antigen-specific, and forms memory so re-exposure is faster
and stronger.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:4}
A \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{plasma cell} is an activated \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{B-cell} effector specialised to secrete large
amounts of \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{antibody} of one \omterm{def:g10:membranes-enzymes:ES}{specificity} into blood and secretions.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:5}
Any three: neutralisation (blocking \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{pathogen} or toxin), opsonisation (tagging
for \omterm{def:g12:immunity:phagocytosis}{phagocytosis}), agglutination (clumping), and activation of complement.
Together these link specific recognition to clearance.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:6}
\omterm{def:g12:immunity:tcell}{Helper T cells} ($T_H$) coordinate responses: they help activate \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{B cells} and
\omterm{def:g12:immunity:phagocytosis}{macrophages}. \omterm{def:g12:immunity:tcell}{Cytotoxic T cells} ($T_C$) kill virus-infected or abnormal \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{cells}
that display \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{antigen} on MHC \omterm{def:g10:biodiversity-classification:hierarchy}{class} I. Coordination versus direct killing is the
core distinction.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:7}
Each lymphocyte clone bears receptors of one \omterm{def:g10:membranes-enzymes:ES}{specificity}. \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{Antigen} selectively
activates only matching clones, which proliferate and differentiate into
effector and memory \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{cells} --- \omterm{prop:g12:immunity:clonal}{clonal selection}. That explains both \omterm{def:g10:membranes-enzymes:ES}{specificity}
and the expansion of useful \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{cells} during infection.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:8}
Memory B and \omterm{def:g12:immunity:tcell}{T cells} generated by the first infection mount a rapid \omterm{prop:g12:immunity:kinetics}{secondary
response} on re-exposure. \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{Antibody} and cellular effectors often clear the virus
before severe disease develops, so symptoms are milder or absent in an
immunocompetent host.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:9}
\omterm{def:g12:immunity:vaccine}{Vaccination} induces the host's own adaptive response and memory (\omterm{def:g12:immunity:active-passive}{active
immunity}), which can last months to years. Passive injection of \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{antibodies}
protects only while those \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{antibodies} persist; it does not by itself generate
host memory from that transfer.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:10}
\omterm{def:g12:immunity:tcell}{Helper T cells} are required to fully activate many \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{B-cell} responses and to
help cytotoxic \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{T-cell} responses. Preferential infection and loss of $T_H$
\omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{cells} therefore collapses coordination of both antibody-mediated and
cell-mediated arms, causing progressive immunodeficiency.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:11}
Use an inactivated toxin (toxoid) or a toxin subunit as \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{antigen} so the vaccine
is safe but still raises neutralising \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{antibodies}. The desired memory is mainly
memory \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{B cells} (with $T_H$ help) that rapidly produce toxin-neutralising
\omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{antibody} on later exposure.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:12}
Benefit: \omterm{def:g12:immunity:inflammation}{inflammation} recruits immune \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{cells}, increases delivery of defences,
and helps isolate microbes. Risk: tissue damage, pain, or chronic inflammatory
disease. Anti-inflammatory treatment is used in principle during autoimmune
flares or severe excess \omterm{def:g12:immunity:inflammation}{inflammation} where the response itself harms the host.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:13}
After first \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{antigen}: a lag of days while rare clones expand, then a modest
primary peak, then decline as effectors die and \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{antigen} is cleared. After
second \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{antigen}: little lag, a steeper rise to a higher \omterm{def:g10:ecosystems:trophic-roles}{secondary} peak because
memory \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{cells} are more numerous and respond faster --- the kinetic signature of
\omterm{def:g12:immunity:adaptive}{immunological memory}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:immunity:14}
\omterm{def:g12:immunity:allergy-auto}{Allergy} recognises a normally harmless external \omterm{def:g12:immunity:allergy-auto}{allergen}; \omterm{def:g12:immunity:allergy-auto}{autoimmunity}
recognises \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{self-antigens}. Neither is ``weak immunity'': both are active,
specific responses aimed at the wrong target or with excessive force. They are
failures of regulation and tolerance, not of having too few lymphocytes in
general.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g12:immunity:1}
\textbf{Part I.} (1)~Mucus, cilia, epithelium, antimicrobial peptides.
(2)~Phagocytes, interferons, complement, \omterm{def:g12:immunity:inflammation}{inflammation}. (3)~Fever may inhibit
some \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{pathogens} but stresses the host if extreme.

\textbf{Part II.} (4)~\omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{Antibodies}/\omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{B cells} limit free virus; $T_C$ kill infected
\omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{cells}. (5)--(6)~Clonal expansion of specific \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{B cells}; vaccinated hosts show a
faster, higher \omterm{def:g10:ecosystems:trophic-roles}{secondary} \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{antibody} curve on re-exposure. (7)~Antigenic drift
changes epitopes so prior \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{antibodies} bind less well.

\textbf{Part III.} (8)~\omterm{def:g12:immunity:active-passive}{Active immunity}: host generates effectors and memory.
(9)~\omterm{prop:g12:immunity:vaccine}{Herd immunity} reduces transmission when enough hosts are immune; more
contagious \omterm{def:g12:immunity:pathogen}{pathogens} need higher coverage. (10)~Live-attenuated vaccines often
strong but riskier in immunocompromised hosts; killed/subunit safer;
mRNA/\omterm{def:g10:molecules-of-life:proteins}{protein} can focus the response. (11)~Passive \omterm{def:g12:immunity:bcell}{antibodies} give immediate
protection but are short-lived and do not alone create durable memory.
(12)~Integrated narrative linking barriers, innate speed, adaptive memory and
\omterm{def:g12:immunity:vaccine}{vaccination} as in the chapter.
\end{solution}
