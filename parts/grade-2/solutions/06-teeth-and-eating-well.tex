\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g2:teeth-and-eating-well} --- Teeth and Eating Well}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:1}
\omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{Incisors} cut; \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{canines} tear; \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{molars} grind.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:2}
The \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{incisors} are at the front (with the \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{canines} at the corners
beside them); the \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{molars} are at the back.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:3}
The \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{incisors} slice off the bite; the \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{canines} help grip and pull
it free; the tongue carries it back; the \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{molars} grind it to a
paste; then it is swallowed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:4}
There are $20$ \omterm{prop:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:sets}{baby teeth}. From about age six they loosen and
fall out one by one, pushed from below by the growing \omterm{prop:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:sets}{adult
teeth}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:5}
Because they are the last set: a lost baby tooth is replaced by
an adult one, but a lost adult tooth is never replaced. The adult
set must last the whole of life.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:6}
Fruit and vegetables; bread and cereals; dairy (\omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk}, yogurt,
cheese); meat, fish or \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}; with treats only small and
occasional. The one drink the body truly needs is water.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:7}
Vitamins from the apple, lasting energy from the bread, and water
--- real nourishment, and honest chewing work for the teeth.
Snack B gives mostly sugar, which feeds the tooth-attacking \omterm{def:g1:healthy-habits:germs}{germs}
instead.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:8}
Open answer --- in the mirror: flat sharp-edged \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{incisors} at the
front, a pointed \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{canine} at each corner, broad bumpy \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{molars} at the
back, matching the figure.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:9}
Flat cutting \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{incisors} and grinding \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{molars} are herbivore-style
tools; pointed \omterm{def:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:kinds}{canines} are carnivore-style. Having both means a
menu of both \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} and meat: an \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{omnivore}'s mouth, like the
blackbird's and the wild boar's menus in that chapter.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:teeth-and-eating-well:10}
Missing: fruit and vegetables (none all day!) and water as the
everyday drink; meat, fish or \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} are absent too. Too often:
sugary things --- sweets, soda, biscuits. One fix: fruit at
breakfast and snack, vegetables and some fish or \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg} at lunch or
supper, water instead of soda, treats kept to one.
\end{solution}
