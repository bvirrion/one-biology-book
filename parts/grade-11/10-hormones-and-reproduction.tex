\chapter{Hormonal Control of Reproduction}\label{ch:g11:hormones-and-reproduction}

A woman keeps a chart: her temperature every morning, a small rise
around the middle of each month; a bleeding every twenty-eight days or
so. A laboratory measuring four \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} in her blood every day would
draw four curves that rise and fall in a fixed order, month after
month, each peak causing the next. A man's laboratory chart would show
the same \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} at nearly constant levels, day after day. The organs
are different; the control system --- a centre in the brain, a gland
beneath it, the gonad, and messages carried by the blood in both
directions --- is the same. This chapter reads the charts, and shows
how understanding them made it possible to prevent a pregnancy, or to
obtain one.

\section{Hormones and their loops}

\begin{definition}[Hormone, feedback]\label{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}
A \emph{hormone}\index{hormone} is a molecule secreted into the blood
by a gland and acting, at a distance, on the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} that carry a
\emph{receptor} for it. A hormonal system is regulated by
\emph{feedback}\index{feedback}: the hormone produced at the end of a
chain acts back on the glands at its start. \emph{Negative feedback}
--- the product reduces the command --- holds a level constant;
\emph{positive feedback} --- the product increases the command ---
produces a surge.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The common chain]\label{prop:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:chain}
In both sexes the same three-tier chain controls the gonads. A centre
of the brain, the \emph{hypothalamus}, secretes in pulses a releasing
\omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormone}, GnRH, into the small vessels that lead to the
\emph{pituitary gland} just beneath it; the pituitary responds by
secreting two \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} into the general blood, \emph{LH} and
\emph{FSH}; these act on the gonads, which produce both gametes and
sex \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} --- testosterone in the testis, oestrogens and
progesterone in the ovary. The sex \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} act on the body, and act
back on the hypothalamus and the pituitary.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\section{The male: a constant level}

\begin{proposition}[Control of the testis]\label{prop:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:male}
The testis has two compartments. The \emph{seminiferous tubules},
stimulated by FSH and by testosterone, produce sperm continuously
from \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:puberty}{puberty}, some hundred million a day. The \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} between the
tubules, stimulated by LH, secrete \emph{testosterone}, which
maintains the reproductive tract, the secondary characteristics and
sperm production. \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:hormones}{Testosterone} inhibits the hypothalamus and the
pituitary: when it rises, GnRH, LH and FSH fall and the testis
secretes less; when it falls, they rise. This negative \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} holds
the level nearly constant, within small daily fluctuations, from
\omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:puberty}{puberty} to old age.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Evidence]
Castration of an animal raises its LH severalfold within days; an
injection of testosterone brings LH back down. A lesion of the
hypothalamus abolishes LH, FSH and testosterone together; injecting
GnRH in pulses restores all three, while a continuous infusion of GnRH
does not --- the pulsing matters. Men treated with testosterone for
other reasons see their sperm production fall: the external \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormone}
switches off their own pituitary.
\end{proof}

\section{The female: cycles}

\begin{proposition}[The ovarian cycle]\label{prop:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:ovary}
From \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:puberty}{puberty} to menopause the ovary runs a cycle of about 28 days.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Follicular phase} (days 1--13): under FSH, a follicle
        --- an egg \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} surrounded by its nurse \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} --- grows and
        secretes rising amounts of \emph{oestrogens}.
  \item \emph{Ovulation} (day 14): a surge of LH ruptures the mature
        follicle, which releases its egg into the oviduct.
  \item \emph{Luteal phase} (days 15--28): the emptied follicle
        becomes the \emph{corpus luteum}, which secretes
        \emph{progesterone} and oestrogens; if no pregnancy begins it
        degenerates after about twelve days, the two \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} fall,
        and a new cycle starts.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{proposition}[The uterine cycle and the feedbacks]\label{prop:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:uterus}
The lining of the uterus follows the ovary's \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones}: oestrogens
make it thicken during the follicular phase; progesterone makes it
secretory and ready to receive an embryo during the luteal phase;
the fall of both at the cycle's end makes it break down and bleed
--- \emph{menstruation}, days 1 to 5 of the next cycle. The \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedbacks}
change sign during the cycle: at low and moderate levels, oestrogens
and progesterone inhibit the hypothalamus and pituitary (negative
\omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}); but the high oestrogen of the mature follicle, sustained
for two days, \emph{stimulates} them (positive \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}), which
produces the LH surge that triggers ovulation. After ovulation, the
progesterone of the corpus luteum restores the negative \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}, and
no second surge occurs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Evidence]
Daily blood measurements give the curves of the next figure: the LH
surge follows the oestrogen peak by a day and precedes ovulation by a
day; progesterone appears only after ovulation. Removing the ovaries
raises LH and FSH; small doses of oestrogens lower them; a large,
sustained dose given at mid-cycle provokes an LH surge --- the sign of
the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} reverses with the dose. A woman whose pituitary cannot
respond to oestrogen's rise has no surge and does not ovulate, though
her follicles grow.
\end{proof}

\begin{method}[Reading a cycle chart]\label{met:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:read}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Find the LH peak: ovulation is the following day; the
        follicular phase is everything before, the luteal phase
        everything after.
  \item Check the order: oestrogen peak, then LH surge, then
        ovulation, then progesterone. A curve out of order is a sign
        of a disorder.
  \item Count: the luteal phase is close to 14 days in every woman;
        cycle length varies through the follicular phase. Ovulation
        is therefore 14 days before the next period, not 14 days after
        the last.
  \item Interpret a drug or a lesion by asking which \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormone} it adds
        or removes, and what the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} then does to the others.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\section{Choosing: contraception}

\begin{proposition}[Hormonal contraception]\label{prop:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:contraception}
The combined pill supplies, every day, small doses of an oestrogen
and a progesterone-like molecule. At those levels the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} is
negative throughout: FSH stays low, no follicle matures, no oestrogen
peak occurs, no LH surge, no ovulation. The lining of the uterus,
kept thin, and the mucus of the cervix, kept thick, add two barriers.
Stopping the pills for a week lets the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} fall and the lining
bleed. Other methods act elsewhere: a copper device in the uterus
prevents fertilisation and implantation; the emergency pill, taken
within days of intercourse, delays ovulation; condoms stop the
gametes from meeting --- and are the only method that also stops
sexually transmitted infections.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Reading the pill's effect]\label{ex:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:pill}
On the chart of a woman taking the pill, the four curves are flat:
oestrogens and progesterone at the low, steady level the pills
supply, LH and FSH below their cycle minimum, no peak anywhere.
Miss the pills for a few days early in the pack and FSH escapes: a
follicle may start, and the rest can follow. The pill works because
the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} works, and a forgotten pill is a hole in the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}.
\end{example}

\section{Obtaining: assisted reproduction}

\begin{proposition}[When a pregnancy does not come]\label{prop:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:art}
About one couple in seven consults after a year of trying. The causes
are shared: absent or irregular ovulation, blocked oviducts, too few
or immobile sperm, and in a third of cases no identified cause. The
treatments follow the biology:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{ovulation induction}: FSH injections, or a drug that
        blocks oestrogen's negative \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}, make follicles mature;
        an LH-like injection then triggers ovulation at a chosen hour;
  \item \emph{insemination}: prepared sperm placed in the uterus at
        ovulation;
  \item \emph{in vitro fertilisation}: after stimulation, eggs are
        collected from the ovary with a needle, fertilised in a dish
        --- if necessary by injecting one sperm into each egg --- and
        one or two embryos are placed in the uterus three to five
        days later. About one attempt in three or four results in a
        birth; the rest can be repeated with frozen embryos.
\end{itemize}
Each step of the natural chain that fails is replaced by the
corresponding intervention.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The numbers of one attempt]\label{ex:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:ivf}
Stimulation yields 10 eggs; 8 are mature; 5 fertilise; 3 reach the
five-day stage; 1 is transferred and 2 frozen. The transfer succeeds
in about a third of cases; with the two frozen embryos, the chance of
a birth from the one collection rises to about 60\%. The technique
has produced several million children since 1978; it has also raised
questions --- about embryos not used, about the age of parents, about
what may be chosen --- that the biology does not answer.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Two systems that meet]\label{rem:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:brain}
The \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} of this chapter control the gonads and the body; they do
not, in humans, control sexual behaviour the way they do in many
animals, in which mating is tied to the ovarian cycle. Human sexual
activity is largely independent of the cycle and depends on the
brain's systems of desire, pleasure and attachment --- circuits of
reward shared with other motivations --- and on the person's history,
culture and choices. Reproduction and sexuality overlap; they are not
the same thing, and the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} govern only the first.
\end{remark}

\section{Problem: The Chart}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- one woman's cycle charted hormone
by hormone, read for its feedbacks, disturbed by a pill, and assisted
when it fails}]
\label{pb:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:1}
Blood samples are taken daily through one cycle. Rounded values, as
percentages of each \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormone}'s maximum:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccccccc}
day & 2 & 6 & 10 & 12 & 13 & 14 & 18 & 22 & 27 \\ \hline
oestrogens & 10 & 20 & 55 & 100 & 80 & 45 & 45 & 60 & 15 \\
LH & 15 & 15 & 20 & 35 & 100 & 60 & 15 & 15 & 12 \\
FSH & 45 & 35 & 30 & 35 & 70 & 45 & 22 & 25 & 25 \\
progesterone & 3 & 3 & 3 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 55 & 100 & 15 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Reading the chart.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item On which day does ovulation take place? Justify from two
        \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones}.
  \item Give the follicular and luteal phases, with their lengths.
  \item Which structure of the ovary secretes the oestrogens of day
        10? Of day 22? Which secretes the progesterone of day 22?
  \item Describe the state of the uterine lining on days 6, 22 and 28.
  \item Why is FSH highest at the start of the cycle and lowest in
        the luteal phase?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedbacks}.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Between days 2 and 10, oestrogens rise while FSH falls.
        Which \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} is at work? Explain.
  \item Between days 12 and 13, oestrogens are at their peak and LH
        rises sevenfold. Which \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}? What condition on the
        oestrogen level makes it possible?
  \item Between days 18 and 27, oestrogens are moderate, progesterone
        high, and LH low. Which \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}, and why is there no second
        surge?
  \item The corpus luteum degenerates on day 26. Predict the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones}
        of day 28 and what follows.
  \item Explain why the cycle is self-restarting: what rise on day 1
        launches the next follicle?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The pill.}
The same woman takes the combined pill for a month, which supplies
oestrogens at 25\% and a progesterone-like \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormone} at 40\% of their
maxima, every day.
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Predict the levels of FSH and LH throughout the month, and
        the fate of the follicles.
  \item Will there be an oestrogen peak? An LH surge? Ovulation?
  \item During the seven pill-free days, what happens to the lining
        of the uterus, and why is this not a true menstruation?
  \item She forgets the pills on days 5 to 8. Explain the risk, step
        by step.
  \item Explain why a progesterone-only pill, at a level that keeps
        the negative \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}, can also prevent ovulation.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part IV --- When the chain fails.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Another woman's chart shows FSH and LH always below 10\% and
        oestrogens below 10\%. Where is the defect? What treatment
        would restore ovulation, and by what route?
  \item A third woman's chart is normal but her oviducts are blocked.
        Which steps of \cref{prop:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:art}
        replace the blocked step?
  \item In IVF, a single injection of an LH-like \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormone} is given 36
        hours before the eggs are collected. Explain what it imitates
        and why the timing is exact.
  \item After stimulation, 10 follicles mature instead of 1. Explain,
        with FSH, why the natural cycle usually ripens only one.
  \item State the result: the day of ovulation on the chart, the two
        signs of the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} that produced it, and the mechanism by
        which the pill suppressed it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
