\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b1:cell-unit-of-life} --- The Cell: Unit of Life}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:1}
All \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:organism}{organisms} are made of \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells} (every \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:tissue}{tissue} examined under the
microscope, plant and animal, shows them); every \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} comes from a \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell}
(division observed in embryos and algae; Pasteur's flasks exclude
spontaneous generation); the hereditary material passes at division
(chromosomes followed through mitosis).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:2}
\omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{Nucleus} (absent / present), membrane-bounded \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{organelles} (absent /
present), chromosome (one circular / several linear), size
(\qtyrange{0.5}{5}{\micro m} / \qtyrange{10}{100}{\micro m}), \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} wall
of peptidoglycan (bacteria) / of cellulose, chitin or none,
cytoskeleton (rudimentary / elaborate).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:3}
$\qty{1}{mm}/\qty{1}{\micro m} = 1000$ bacteria; $1000/20 = 50$ animal
\omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:4}
Resolution depends on wavelength and numerical aperture, not on
\omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:resolution}{magnification}; beyond about $1000\times$ the image enlarges the blur
of the diffraction limit (\qty{0.2}{\micro m}) without separating
anything new.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:5}
$0.61\times 550/0.65 = \qty{516}{nm}$; in blue, \qty{422}{nm}. Two
bacteria \qty{500}{nm} apart: barely, in blue light; ribosomes
\qty{30}{nm} apart: no, by a factor of fifteen.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:6}
Purple: thick peptidoglycan wall retaining the crystal violet--iodine
complex, no outer membrane (Gram-positive). Pink: thin peptidoglycan
under an outer membrane, dye washed out, counterstained (Gram-negative).
\emph{E. coli} is a Gram-negative rod: the pink rods.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:7}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:8}
Succinate dehydrogenase is mitochondrial: \qty{80}{\%} of the
mitochondria are in pellet 2; the \qty{15}{\%} in pellet 1 are
mitochondria trapped in unbroken \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells} and debris or dragged down with
the nuclei; the \qty{5}{\%} in the supernatant come from mitochondria
broken during homogenisation. Lactate dehydrogenase is cytosolic and
soluble: it stays in the final supernatant.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:9}
(a) Phase-contrast light microscopy on living \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells} (the process
takes minutes and needs live \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells}). (b) Light microscopy of a
stained section, or TEM for certainty (mitochondria are at the light
limit). (c) TEM (pores are \qty{100}{nm}). (d) SEM (surface relief).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:10}
The clear flask excludes that air itself, or boiled broth itself,
generates life: air enters freely through the open neck. The cloudy
flask, after contact with the dust, shows that what the neck had
trapped --- particles from the air --- is the source of the \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:organism}{organisms}.
Together they prove that microbes come from microbes carried by dust.
A sealed boiled flask left open the objection that sealing had excluded
the air a "vital force" needed; the swan neck admits air and excludes
only dust.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:11}
$10^5 = 2^{n}$ gives $n = 16.6$ doublings, i.e.\ \qty{415}{min}, about
\qty{7}{h}. $10^8\times 10^3\,\mathrm{mL}\times\qty{1}{pg} = \qty{0.1}{g}$
per litre. Growth stops because oxygen (which the medium can supply at
only a few milligrams per litre), a mineral, or the accumulation of
acids and wastes becomes limiting, not the glucose.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:cell-unit-of-life:12}
A \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} ten times larger in each dimension has a tenth of the membrane
surface per unit volume; since membranes carry the energy-transducing
and transport machinery, the large \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} would starve. The eukaryote
multiplies membrane by folding it inside itself (endoplasmic reticulum,
inner mitochondrial membrane, thylakoids) and thereby also creates
closed compartments in which incompatible processes --- acid digestion,
oxidative chemistry, DNA storage --- run separately at their own
conditions. The image is apt as far as membranes go, and incomplete:
the eukaryote also gained a cytoskeleton and a \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{nucleus}, and its
mitochondria are descendants of engulfed bacteria, not folds.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b1:cell-unit-of-life:1}
\textbf{1.} Pellet 1: nuclei (and unbroken \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells}, debris). Pellet 2:
mitochondria (with lysosomes and peroxisomes). Pellet 3: microsomes
(fragments of endoplasmic reticulum) and ribosomes.
\textbf{2.} Cold slows the enzymes (including proteases and
phospholipases released by breakage) and preserves the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{organelles};
isotonic so that \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{organelles} neither swell and burst nor shrink.
\textbf{3.} Mitochondria (inner membrane); cytosol.
\textbf{4.} $500 + 200 + 300 + 950 = \qty{1950}{mg}$, \qty{97.5}{\%}:
losses on tube walls and in transfers.
\textbf{5.} SDH: $15 + 60 + 5 + 4 = 84$, \qty{84}{\%} (some activity
lost by damage to mitochondria). LDH: $20 + 12 + 8 + 350 = 390$,
\qty{97.5}{\%}.
\textbf{6.} $2000/10 = \qty{200}{mg/g}$: \qty{20}{\%} of the fresh mass (most of the rest is water).
\textbf{7.} $v = \frac{2}{9}\times(5\times 10^{-7})^2\times 100\times
\num{9.81e4}/10^{-3} = \qty{5.4e-4}{m/s}$; \qty{5}{cm} in \qty{92}{s}:
\qty{20}{min} is ample.
\textbf{8.} At $1000\,g$, $v = \qty{5.4e-5}{m/s}$; in \qty{600}{s},
\qty{3.3}{cm}. Uniformly distributed over \qty{5}{cm}, the mitochondria
within \qty{3.3}{cm} of the bottom reach it: \qty{65}{\%}, if nothing
else intervened.
\textbf{9.} Only \qty{15}{\%} actually pellets: a real pellet does not
trap everything that reaches it (the packed nuclei are resuspended
gently and the loose upper layer stays in the supernatant), and the
formula overestimates the speed of non-spherical, hydrated \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{organelles}.
Mitochondria inside unbroken \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells} and adhering to nuclei also enter
pellet 1.
\textbf{10.} $v = \frac{2}{9}\times(4\times 10^{-6})^2\times 100\times
9810/10^{-3} = \qty{3.5e-3}{m/s}$; \qty{14}{s}.
\textbf{11.} $v = \frac{2}{9}\times(1.2\times 10^{-8})^2\times 600\times
\num{9.81e5}/10^{-3} = \qty{1.9e-5}{m/s}$; \qty{5}{cm} in \qty{2650}{s},
\qty{44}{min}. At $\num{10000}\,g$ the speed is \qty{1.9e-6}{m/s}: in
\qty{20}{min}, \qty{2.3}{mm}, so ribosomes stay in supernatant 2.
\textbf{12.} Speed goes as $r^2$: at low force only the largest particles
move appreciably, so each step pellets one class and \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves} the smaller
ones. Too short, the class is incompletely pelleted; too long, the next
class begins to contaminate the pellet.
\textbf{13.} \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:fractionation}{Homogenate} $100/2000 = \qty{0.05}{U/mg}$; pellet 1
$15/500 = 0.03$; pellet 2 $60/200 = 0.30$; pellet 3 $5/300 = 0.017$;
supernatant $4/950 = 0.004$.
\textbf{14.} Purification $0.30/0.05 = 6$; yield $60/100 =
\qty{60}{\%}$.
\textbf{15.} $0.017/0.05 = 0.33$: pellet 3 is depleted in mitochondria; the little activity it holds is from fragments of mitochondrial membrane produced by homogenisation, which sediment with the microsomes.
\textbf{16.} LDH: \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:fractionation}{homogenate} $400/2000 = \qty{0.2}{U/mg}$; supernatant
$350/950 = \qty{0.37}{U/mg}$; purification $1.8$; yield \qty{87.5}{\%}.
\textbf{17.} The cytosol is half the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell}'s protein, so even a pure
cytosol can only double the specific activity; the mitochondria are a
sixth or less, so isolating them concentrates their enzymes sixfold or
more. The yield is high for the soluble enzyme because it is not lost
with damaged \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{organelles}.
\textbf{18.} No: pellet 2 holds \qty{200}{mg} of protein and traps
supernatant between its particles; \qty{12}{U} is \qty{3}{\%} of the
total, consistent with trapped cytosol. Test: wash the pellet
(resuspend and re-spin): a trapped enzyme \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves}, a bound one stays.
\textbf{19.} If pellet 2 is \qty{60}{\%} mitochondria, pure mitochondria
have $0.30/0.6 = \qty{0.5}{U/mg}$; the liver's \qty{100}{U} then
correspond to \qty{200}{mg} of mitochondrial protein, \qty{10}{\%} of
the \qty{2000}{mg}.
\textbf{20.} TEM of fixed, embedded, thinly sectioned, heavy-metal
stained pellet: bean-shaped profiles with a double membrane and cristae,
with some lysosomes (dense bodies) and peroxisomes among them.
\textbf{21.} Look at a drop of the resuspended pellet under the phase-contrast microscope and count intact \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells} among the free nuclei; if many, homogenise more thoroughly (more strokes, tighter pestle) and re-spin, since trapped \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{organelles} lower every yield.
\textbf{22.} \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:fractionation}{Differential centrifugation} separates by size (speed
$\propto r^2$) and the three \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{organelles} have similar sizes; in a
density gradient a particle stops at its own density regardless of
size, and the three densities differ.
\textbf{23.} Intact lysosomes keep the enzyme inside a membrane through
which the substrate does not pass (latency); the detergent breaks the
membrane and exposes it. The lysosomes in the pellet are therefore
mostly intact.
\textbf{24.} Water is hypotonic: \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{organelles} swell and burst.
Mitochondria and lysosomes rupture, so pellet 2 shrinks; succinate
dehydrogenase (membrane-bound) partly goes to pellet 3 as membrane
fragments; acid phosphatase (soluble inside the lysosome) is released
into the final supernatant.
\textbf{25.} Purification factor $6$, yield \qty{60}{\%}. The factor
is limited by contamination (lysosomes, peroxisomes, trapped cytosol:
pellet 2 is \qty{60}{\%} mitochondria); the yield by mitochondria lost
to pellet 1 (trapped) and broken during homogenisation.
\end{solution}
