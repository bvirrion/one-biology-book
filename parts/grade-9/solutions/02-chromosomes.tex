\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g9:chromosomes} --- Chromosomes}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:1}
A threadlike body of the \omterm{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:parts}{nucleus} --- its contents coiled
for transport. Visible, well stained, when the \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}
divides; loose and invisible between divisions.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:2}
46 in every body \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}; sorted as 23 pairs; 23 --- one
partner per pair --- in the \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:3}
$23 + 23 = 46$: \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization} restores the pairs, one
partner of each from the mother, one from the father ---
both parents in every body \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} of the child.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:4}
Unhalved \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes} would double the deck each generation:
46-gametes make 92-children, then 184-grandchildren. The
halving keeps the \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} steady at 46.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:5}
XX (a girl's \omterm{def:g9:chromosomes:chromosomes}{karyotype}) and XY (a boy's). The father's:
\omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} all carry X, sperm carry X or Y half and half --- the
arriving sperm decides.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:6}
The recognizable trait-set called Down syndrome. It proves
the threads carry trait-instructions: one thread's count
changed, many \omterm{def:g9:heredity-and-traits:traits}{traits} shift.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:7}
In the halving division: which partner of each pair a
\omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gamete} receives is settled independently, pair by pair ---
$2^{23}$ possible half-decks per parent, before
\omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization} multiplies two such spaces together.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:8}
Determinants, not \omterm{def:g9:heredity-and-traits:traits}{traits}: threads carry instructions ---
compact, cell-borne. Carried unshown: both partners ride
in every \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} whatever is displayed. Selections differing
child to child: the halving deals one partner per pair,
independently each time.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:9}
Each \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization} is a fresh flip: the sperm \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population}
is half X, half Y at every attempt, and no past \omterm{def:g2:born-grow-die:cycle}{birth}
reaches into the race. Three girls are three fair flips
--- the fourth is as fair.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:10}
(a) A body \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} of a girl or woman: full deck, XX. (b) A
\omterm{def:g8:reproductive-systems:male}{sperm cell} carrying the Y: a \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gamete}, half-deck --- its
\omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization} would make a boy. (c) A body \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} with the
pair-21 extra: 47 threads --- the \omterm{def:g9:chromosomes:chromosomes}{karyotype} of Down
syndrome, XX: a girl's.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:11}
Every \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg} the queens supplied carried an X --- their
contribution to pair 23 admits no alternative. The X-or-Y
variety rides in the kings' \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes} alone: if the record
must assign the flip's coin, it sat in the royal sperm ---
and in any case the flip is chance, blamable on no one.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:chromosomes:12}
The puzzle: one deck, many \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} kinds --- if every \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}
holds the same 46 threads of instructions, why is a \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{neuron}
not a \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}? Direction of resolution: \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} must
differ in which instructions they \emph{read}, not which
they hold --- the deck is a library, and specialization is
a borrowing policy. What the readable units on a
\omterm{def:g9:chromosomes:chromosomes}{chromosome} are is the next chapter's question.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g9:chromosomes:1}
\textbf{1.} A: a body \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} of a boy or man --- 46, XY. B:
a body \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} of a girl or woman --- 46, XX. C: a \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gamete} ---
23, one partner per pair; its X makes it either an \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg
cell} or an X-sperm.

\textbf{2.} A \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gamete}. Its 23 records the special halving
division: from a 46-cell, one partner of each pair taken,
no pair complete.

\textbf{3.} 47 threads, the extra at pair 21, 23rd pair
XX: the \omterm{def:g9:chromosomes:chromosomes}{karyotype} of a girl with Down syndrome --- one
miscounted thread, a shifted development.

\textbf{4.} The father's --- his sperm's X-or-Y. Odds at
the meeting: one in two, each way.

\textbf{5.} Because the body grew from one \omterm{prop:g8:sexual-reproduction:core}{zygote} by
copying divisions: each division duplicates the deck and
deals a full copy to each daughter \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} --- 46 faithful
copies from \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} to \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{neuron}.

\textbf{6.} The \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes}' halving division, with its rule:
one partner from each of the 23 pairs --- a complete
half-deck, no pair doubled, none dropped.

\textbf{7.} That the child receives an X from it: whatever
partner arrives from the other side, C's contribution to
pair 23 is fixed --- an \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{X-gamete} writes X into the
child's 23rd pair unconditionally.

\textbf{8.} Each of the 23 pairs contributes either
partner, independently: $2 \times 2 \times \dots$, 23
times --- $2^{23}$ half-decks, several million, from one
parent. \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{Fertilization} pairs one such deal from each
parent: the two spaces multiply, and the shuffle's
arithmetic explains why siblings never repeat.

\textbf{9.} Nothing was done wrong: the extra 21 arises
in a halving accident --- a pair's partners failing to
separate --- a mischance of the mechanism, not of the
parents' acts or worth. The 47th thread shifts the
child's development; it changes nothing about the child
being wholly their child --- the counseling truth and
the cytogenetic truth agree.

\textbf{10.} One in two, exactly as at every \omterm{def:g2:born-grow-die:cycle}{birth}: the
sperm \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population} is half X, half Y at each \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization},
and past flips leave no trace in it. The run feels
meaningful; the mechanism keeps no memory.

\textbf{11.} The decision is made at \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}, by
which sperm arrives --- inside the father's contribution,
in the race's last millimetres. A diet reaches neither
the sperm \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population}'s half-and-half manufacture nor the
race's outcome: the claim has no link to block or bias
--- \cref{met:g8:contraception:evaluate}'s question 1,
failed again.

\textbf{12.} \omterm{def:g9:heredity-and-traits:heredity}{Heredity} rides on countable threads in the
\omterm{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:parts}{nucleus}: halved into \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes}, restored at \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization},
trait-shifting when miscounted, sex-deciding at pair 23
--- four photographs, one vehicle.
\end{solution}
