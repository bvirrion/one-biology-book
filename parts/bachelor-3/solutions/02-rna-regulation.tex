\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:rna-regulation} --- Non-coding RNAs and Post-transcriptional Regulation}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:1}
miRNA: encoded in the genome as a hairpin, \qty{22}{nt}, cut by \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:mirna}{Drosha}
then \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:rnai}{Dicer}, pairs imperfectly through its seed and represses
translation and stability of many messengers. \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{siRNA}: from long
double-stranded RNA (viral, transgene, experimental), \qty{21}{nt},
Dicer-dependent, pairs perfectly and directs \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:rnai}{Argonaute} to cleave the
target. \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{piRNA}: from single-stranded transcripts of \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:pirna}{piRNA clusters},
\qtyrange{26}{31}{nt}, Dicer-independent, loaded on PIWI proteins in
the germ line, cleaves transposon transcripts (ping-pong) and directs
\omterm{def:b3:chromatin-epigenetics:nucleosome}{chromatin} silencing of transposon DNA.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:2}
Pol II transcribes the pri-miRNA; \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:mirna}{Drosha} (with DGCR8) cuts the hairpin
out in the nucleus; exportin-5 exports the pre-miRNA; \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:rnai}{Dicer} cuts off the
loop, leaving a \qty{22}{nt} duplex; one strand is loaded into
\omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:rnai}{Argonaute}; the seed (nucleotides 2--8) pairs with a 3$'$ UTR site;
\omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:rnai}{Argonaute} recruits the deadenylase and decapping machinery and blocks
initiation --- the messenger is translated less and decays faster.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:3}
RNA made in vitro by a phage polymerase contained double-stranded
contaminants (the polymerase also copies the template's other strand
and runs off ends), so ``sense'' preparations carried the real
trigger. Fire and Mello purified each strand, showed that either alone
did little, then annealed them deliberately: the double-stranded RNA
was at least a hundred times more potent, a few molecules per cell
sufficing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:4}
Iron-starved: IRP binds the IREs. Ferritin: translation initiation is
blocked, no storage protein is made (control of translation).
Transferrin receptor: the messenger is protected from its nuclease and
accumulates, so more receptor is made and more iron imported (control
of messenger stability).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:5}
$\gamma = \ln 2/\qty{120}{min} = \qty{0.0058}{min^{-1}}$; $m^{*} =
5/0.0058 \approx 870$ molecules. Decay doubled: $m^{*} \approx 430$,
half-time $\qty{120}{min}/2 = \qty{60}{min}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:6}
Total 3$'$ UTR sequence $2\times 10^{7}$ nt; chance matches $2\times
10^{7}/4^{7} = 2\times 10^{7}/\num{16384} \approx \num{1200}$ sites.
A real target is distinguished by conservation of the site across
species, favourable context (an AU-rich neighbourhood, a site away from
the stop codon, extra pairing at the 3$'$ end of the miRNA), the
co-expression of miRNA and target in the same cell, and experiment: the
messenger falls when the miRNA is present and no longer does when the
site is mutated.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:7}
(a) XX, \emph{tra} null: SXL is made but TRA is not, \emph{dsx} takes
the default male splice --- a somatic male (sterile). (b) XY with
constitutive TRA: TRA-2 is present in both sexes, so DSX is made in the
female form --- female somatic development (a sterile
``pseudo-female'', the germ line following other cues). (c) No
\emph{dsx}: neither DSX form, so neither programme of terminal
differentiation is imposed --- an intersex with mixed or incomplete
characters.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:8}
At steady state the messenger, and hence the protein, is proportional
to the messenger half-life: $\qty{180}{min}/\qty{15}{min} = 12$-fold
more protein. The protein is a normal growth signal that is meant to
be transient, a pulse after each stimulus; made twelve-fold and
continuously, it drives proliferation without a stimulus. Dose and
timing, not sequence, make it oncogenic.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:9}
(a) Laboratory mother, wild father: the eggs carry no \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:pirna}{piRNAs} against P
elements (the mother's lineage never met them), the paternal P elements
transpose freely in the germ line of the offspring --- sterile
(dysgenic). (b) Wild mother: her eggs contain P-element \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:pirna}{piRNAs}, which
silence the elements in the offspring --- fertile. The asymmetry is
maternal deposition of \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:pirna}{piRNAs}, an inheritance of RNA, not of genes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:10}
The protein integrates its messenger over its own lifetime $\tau_{p}$.
Messengers live $\tau_{m} = 1/(\gamma + \gamma' R)$; during $\tau_{p}$
the protein therefore sees about $\tau_{p}/\tau_{m}$ independent
messenger lifetimes, each a random birth--death event. The relative
fluctuation of an average over $N$ independent events falls as
$1/\sqrt{N}$, so the shorter $\tau_{m}$ under \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{microRNA} control --- at the
same mean messenger, which requires a proportionally higher $k$ ---
gives a smoother protein. The cell pays for the \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{microRNA} and the extra
transcription to buy precision and speed: a level set by a balance of
fast synthesis and fast decay is both less noisy and quicker to
readjust than the same level set by slow synthesis and slow decay.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:11}
Worm: the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase makes new \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{siRNAs} from the target
messenger itself, so the signal is renewed as long as the target is
transcribed, survives dilution through divisions and, with a
double-stranded-RNA channel between cells, spreads to other tissues and
into the germ line for a few generations. Mammal: no amplification, so
the \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{siRNAs} are consumed and diluted at each division, act only in the
cells that received them, and fade within days in dividing cells.
Consequence: an \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{siRNA} drug must be delivered into every target cell,
works best in cells that do not divide (hepatocytes, hence the liver
drugs), and needs repeated dosing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:rna-regulation:12}
(a) Functional RNA: destroying the transcript after it is made (\omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{siRNA},
antisense oligonucleotide) gives a phenotype, and expressing the RNA
from a transgene elsewhere in the genome rescues it. (b) Functional
transcription: inserting an early polyadenylation signal that stops
transcription (or deleting the promoter) gives the phenotype, while
destroying the RNA post-transcriptionally does not, and an ectopic
transgene does not rescue --- the act of transcription (or the DNA
element) matters, as for many enhancer-associated RNAs. (c) Noise:
neither manipulation changes anything, the RNA is at less than a copy
per cell, its sequence and expression are not conserved.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:rna-regulation:1}
\textbf{1.} $\gamma = \ln 2/40 = \qty{0.0173}{min^{-1}}$; $m^{*} =
3/0.0173 \approx 170$ messengers.
\textbf{2.} $\delta_{p} = \ln 2/180 = \qty{0.00385}{min^{-1}}$; $P^{*} =
\beta m^{*}/\delta_{p} = 2\times 173/0.00385 \approx 9.0\times 10^{4}$
proteins.
\textbf{3.} Decay $4\gamma = \qty{0.069}{min^{-1}}$; $m^{*} = 3/0.069
\approx 43$; time constant $1/0.069 = \qty{14}{min}$.
\textbf{4.} $\beta = 1$: $P^{*} = 43/0.00385 \approx 1.1\times 10^{4}$;
repression $8$-fold ($4$ from decay, $2$ from translation).
\textbf{5.} Messenger half-time $\ln 2/(4\gamma) = \qty{10}{min}$. The
protein then decays with its own half-life, \qty{3}{h}: the protein's
degradation limits the switch.
\textbf{6.} Yes: the messenger falls only to $1/4$ (still easily
detected) while the protein falls to $1/8$ and keeps falling as the
old protein turns over; the early observation that the protein
vanished ``while the mRNA remained'' reflected mostly the translational
half of the repression.
\textbf{7.} $10^{6}/250 = 4000$ molecules per egg.
\textbf{8.} $4000/550 \approx 7$ per cell at hatching. $4000/2^{n} < 1$
for $n \ge 12$ divisions from the zygote --- about three divisions
after hatching.
\textbf{9.} The polymerase copies the target messenger into new
double-stranded RNA, cut into secondary \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{siRNAs}: the trigger is
regenerated from the target itself, so it survives dilution and spreads
(a channel passes double-stranded RNA between cells and into the germ
line). It fades because amplification needs both the target and a
primary trigger; once the injected RNA is gone the secondary pool is
diluted generation by generation and the germline resets.
\textbf{10.} $5000/2^{n} < 100$: $2^{n} > 50$, $n = 6$ days ($78$
copies left).
\textbf{11.} Non-dividing cells do not dilute the \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:rnai}{RISC}; the limit is the
chemical lifetime of the \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{siRNA} (nuclease degradation, slowed by
chemical modification of the strands) and the slow leakage of the
endosomal depot that feeds the cytoplasm.
\textbf{12.} $2\times 10^{7}/\num{16384} \approx \num{1200}$ chance
matches. Genetics decided: \emph{lin-14} loss gives the opposite
phenotype to \emph{lin-4} loss, \emph{lin-14} gain-of-function alleles
were deletions of exactly the 3$'$ UTR sites, and the seven sites were
conserved.
\textbf{13.} Level $\propto$ half-life: $50/10 = 5$-fold rise.
\textbf{14.} $1/0.05 = 20$-fold fall in ferritin synthesis.
\textbf{15.} Import/storage rises $5\times 20 = 100$-fold from iron-rich
to iron-poor.
\textbf{16.} The cluster assembles only when cytosolic iron is available,
so the protein's conformation is a direct readout of the iron
concentration, without receptor, hormone or transcription --- like a
riboswitch, a metabolite sensed by the molecule it controls.
\textbf{17.} Ferritin is translated regardless of iron: serum ferritin
very high, serum iron and stores normal (no overload). In the lens,
which has no turnover, ferritin accumulates for years and crystallises
--- an early cataract.
\textbf{18.} The hairpin is only a landing site; the effect is whatever
the bound protein physically obstructs --- scanning ribosomes at the
5$'$ end, a nuclease at the 3$'$ end.
\textbf{19.} Two chemistries cover two conditions: the iron--sulfur
cluster of IRP1 is also destroyed by oxidants and low oxygen, so IRP1
responds to the redox state, while the iron-dependent degradation of
IRP2 responds to iron itself across the physiological oxygen range;
redundancy makes the regulon robust, and the two report different
things.
\textbf{20.} $12\times 48\times 33\times 2 = \num{38016}$. $1 - (1 -
50/\num{38016})^{50} = 1 - 0.99868^{50} \approx 1 - e^{-0.066} =
0.064$: about \qty{6}{\%}.
\textbf{21.} Identical isoforms bind homophilically and repel: the
branches of one neuron avoid each other and spread out, while branches
of different neurons, sharing almost no isoform, may cross --- a private
identity per cell.
\textbf{22.} $2^{n}$; $\num{1024}$ for $n = 10$. Fewer in reality:
inclusion is decided by cell-type-specific factors and coordinated
across exons, many combinations shift the reading frame and are
destroyed by nonsense-mediated decay, and the exons are not
independent.
\textbf{23.} \emph{Sxl} also controls \omterm{def:b3:chromatin-epigenetics:x-inactivation}{dosage compensation}: in females
SXL blocks the translation of \emph{msl-2}, so the two X chromosomes
are not hypertranscribed; without SXL an XX embryo assembles the MSL
complex on both X chromosomes and doubles their output --- a lethal
overdose of every X-linked gene, before sex is even at issue.
\textbf{24.} The early promoter fires only while the X:A signal is
present; the SXL it makes forces productive splicing of the transcript
from the maintenance promoter, which is active in both sexes, so once
SXL exists it keeps making itself and the signal is no longer needed
--- the same reader-recruits-writer, self-sustaining logic as the
\omterm{def:b3:chromatin-epigenetics:nucleosome}{chromatin} marks, here with a protein and a splice.
\textbf{25.} LIN-14 protein repressed $8$-fold; messenger switch
half-time \qty{10}{min}; an unamplified RNA falls below one molecule
per cell after $12$ divisions.
\end{solution}
