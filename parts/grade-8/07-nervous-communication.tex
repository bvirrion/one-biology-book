\chapter{Nervous Communication}\label{ch:g8:nervous-communication}

\Cref{ch:g5:brain-in-command} drew the loop --- report,
decide, order --- and measured its fifth of a second. This
year we open the cables. What actually travels along a
\omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{nerve}? What are \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nerves} made of? And what happens at the
gaps where one wire ends and the next begins --- the gaps
where, it will turn out, both memory and many poisons do
their work?

\section{The wiring and its cells}

\begin{definition}[Neurons and nerve messages]\label{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}
The \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nervous system}'s working \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} are the
\emph{neurons}\index{neuron}
(\cref{rem:g6:cell-unit-of-life:twoways} listed them):
each has a \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} body and a long thin fiber --- some fibers
reach from \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{spine} to toe, the body's longest \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}. A
\emph{nerve}\index{nerve} is a cable bundling thousands of
such fibers. Along each fiber travel \emph{nerve
messages}\index{nerve message}: brief electrical signals,
racing at up to \qty{100}{m/s}
(\cref{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}'s figure, now with
its carrier).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Coding by frequency]\label{prop:g8:nervous-communication:code}
The signals themselves are all alike --- what varies is
their \emph{rate}. A gentle touch sends a slow patter up
the sensory fiber; a strong one, a rapid drumroll. The
\omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nervous system}'s language has one word repeated at
different speeds: intensity is coded as frequency, on
every line, in and out.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Three lines, one language]\label{ex:g8:nervous-communication:lines}
The loop's cast (\cref{prop:g5:brain-in-command:loop}),
rewired: \emph{sensory \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{neurons}} carry the reports ---
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} to \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{spinal cord}, \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eye} to \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}; \emph{motor \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{neurons}}
carry the orders --- cord to \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle}, ending where the
fiber meets the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} and its drumroll sets the
contraction's strength
(\cref{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}); and between
them, in \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} and cord, uncounted \emph{connecting
\omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{neurons}} do the deciding. Reflex or symphony, the traffic
is the same signals at different rates on different
lines.
\end{example}

\section{The gaps: synapses}

\begin{definition}[The synapse]\label{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}
\omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{Neurons} do not touch. Where one fiber's end meets the
next \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} lies a microscopic gap --- the
\emph{synapse}\index{synapse}. The electrical signal
cannot jump it; instead the arriving fiber releases a
\emph{chemical messenger} into the gap, which crosses,
docks on the far side, and --- if enough arrives ---
starts the signal afresh in the next \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{neuron}. Electrical
along the wire, chemical at every junction: the whole
system alternates the two.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Why gaps, not solder]\label{prop:g8:nervous-communication:whygaps}
The gaps look like a flaw and are the system's genius:
\begin{itemize}
  \item they make junctions \emph{adjustable} --- a
        \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapse} crossed often grows stronger, one unused
        fades: learning
        (\cref{rem:g5:brain-in-command:training}'s
        ``better paths'', found --- they are strengthened
        \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapses});
  \item they make signals \emph{one-way} --- messengers
        release on one side only, so traffic cannot run
        backward;
  \item they let one \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{neuron} \emph{weigh} thousands of
        inputs --- excitatory and damping voices summed
        at each \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} before it fires: the deciding of the
        loop, performed at junctions.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The juggler's synapses]\label{ex:g8:nervous-communication:juggler}
\Cref{rem:g5:brain-in-command:training}'s juggler,
explained at last: a month of practice conducted the same
signals across the same junctions until those \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapses}
strengthened --- the routing that was laboriously weighed
now runs pre-weighted. Skill is chemistry consolidated at
the gaps; ``practice makes permanent'' is a statement
about \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapses}.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[The gaps are the vulnerable points]\label{rem:g8:nervous-communication:vulnerable}
Whatever acts on the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nervous system} from outside acts at
the \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapses} --- the one place the signal becomes
chemistry, dockable and dupable. Anesthetics quiet them;
venoms jam them (some paralyze by blocking the
muscle-junction's messenger, some by flooding it); and
\cref{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}'s alcohol, and
every drug that changes mood or perception, works by
imitating, blocking or exhausting the gaps' messengers.
\Cref{ch:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system} builds on
exactly this point.
\end{remark}

\section{Fast wires, slow mail: two systems compared}

\begin{proposition}[Nerves against hormones]\label{prop:g8:nervous-communication:compare}
The body now shows two communication systems, built for
opposite jobs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{nervous}: signals in milliseconds, along
        dedicated lines, to precise addresses --- one
        \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle}, one gland --- and gone when the signal
        stops: the system of catches, speech and
        reflexes;
  \item \emph{hormonal}
        (\cref{def:g8:puberty:hormones}): messengers in
        minutes to months, broadcast by \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} to every
        organ built to hear, ordering slow, body-wide
        programs --- growth, cycles, \omterm{def:g8:puberty:puberty}{puberty}'s rebuild.
\end{itemize}
Telegraph and postal service: the body runs both, and
\cref{ch:g8:hormonal-communication} gives the postal
service its own chapter.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{method}[Diagnosing a communication, system by system]\label{met:g8:nervous-communication:diagnose}
Given any bodily coordination, assign its system:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item time it: milliseconds --- nervous; minutes to
        years --- hormonal (or both, in relay);
  \item map its reach: one precise address --- nervous;
        body-wide --- hormonal;
  \item find the route: nameable wire path --- nervous;
        bloodstream broadcast --- hormonal;
  \item check persistence: gone with the signal ---
        nervous; running while the messenger circulates
        --- hormonal.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{example}[The method on three cases]\label{ex:g8:nervous-communication:cases}
The blink at a puff of air: milliseconds, one \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle}
ring, wired path, gone at once --- nervous. The growth
burst: years, whole-body, bloodstream, running for
seasons --- hormonal. The racing \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} of the sprint
(\cref{prop:g7:organs-at-work:supply}): both in relay ---
\omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{nerve} signals set the pace up within a beat, and a
bloodstream messenger (the famous surge you feel as a
fright's hot wave) holds the whole body's alert for
minutes. Telegraph first, post to follow.
\end{example}

\section{Problem: The Reflex Bench}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- the knee-jerk,
instrumented}]\label{pb:g8:nervous-communication:1}
The classic bench demonstration: the seated volunteer,
the little hammer below the kneecap, the foot's involuntary
kick --- now instrumented with timers and this chapter.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The circuit.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The hammer stretches a tendon; the foot kicks
        about a fiftieth of a second later. Lay out the
        circuit: which \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{neuron} kinds, through where ---
        and why the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} is not on this loop.
  \item A fiftieth of a second is faster than the
        ball-catch's fifth. Account for the difference
        with the circuit's length and junction count.
  \item The volunteer cannot suppress the kick, though
        warned. What does this say about where the reflex
        is decided --- and about one-way traffic at the
        \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapses}?
  \item Immediately after the kick, the volunteer
        \emph{feels} the tap. Explain the delay's
        second, slower journey.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Bench variations.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Tap harder: the kick strengthens. Which coding
        rule of \cref{prop:g8:nervous-communication:code}
        showed itself?
  \item The class doctor explains that this reflex is
        tested at every check-up. What does a present,
        symmetrical knee-jerk certify about cord, \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nerves}
        and junctions?
  \item A volunteer clenching their jaw kicks harder ---
        a known amplification. What does this reveal
        about connecting \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{neurons}' voices reaching even
        ``brainless'' loops?
  \item Local anesthetic in the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{leg} abolishes both kick
        and feeling. Name the point of attack, by
        \cref{rem:g8:nervous-communication:vulnerable}.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The write-up.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Draw the reflex as
        \cref{prop:g5:brain-in-command:loop}'s three-step
        loop, marking where this one shortcuts.
  \item Compare reflex and catch in a two-column table:
        path, junctions, time, suppressibility, role of
        practice.
  \item The write-up's last section asks: ``why do fast
        protective loops bypass the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}?'' Answer with
        the hot-pan logic
        (\cref{exo:g5:brain-in-command:5}).
  \item One sentence to close: what the bench showed
        about the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nervous system}'s language, wiring and
        junctions at once.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
