\chapter{Protecting the Nervous System}\label{ch:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system}

Two chapters have shown what the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nervous system} is: wiring
whose junctions learn, decide --- and can be fooled
(\cref{rem:g8:nervous-communication:vulnerable}). This
year's close turns that anatomy into protection. What do
\omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep} loss, noise, screens and the psychoactive substances
actually do, junction by junction, to the machine that is
you --- and what does protecting it look like in practice,
at fourteen?

\section{Maintenance: sleep, and the senses' limits}

\begin{proposition}[Sleep is synapse maintenance]\label{prop:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:sleep}
The filing-and-repairs of
\cref{prop:g5:brain-in-command:protect} has a junction-level
reading: in \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep}, the day's strengthened \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapses} are
consolidated --- practice made permanent
(\cref{ex:g8:nervous-communication:juggler}) happens
largely overnight --- and the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}'s housekeeping clears
the day's chemical wear. Short nights leave yesterday's
learning half-filed and tomorrow's weighing sluggish: the
\omterm{def:g3:stages-of-life:stages}{adolescent} \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}, mid-rewiring
(\cref{rem:g8:puberty:moods}) and clock-drifted late,
needs its hours most exactly when school starts earliest.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The revision paradox]\label{ex:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:revision}
Two students before a test: one revises to midnight and
on to two; the other revises to ten and \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleeps}. The
second usually wins --- the material crossed their
junctions \emph{and was consolidated}; the first's extra
hours cost the very filing that makes revision stick.
``\omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{Sleep} on it'' is \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapse} engineering.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Loud is a dose]\label{prop:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:noise}
The \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{ear}'s sensory \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} --- the hair-fine receivers that
turn sound into \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:neuron}{nerve messages} --- do not regrow
(\cref{prop:g1:the-five-senses:fragile} said it gently;
here is the mechanism). Loud sound kills them by dose:
volume times exposure. Concerts and earbuds at full
volume spend, hour by hour, a capital of receivers that
no \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep} restores --- the whistling after a loud night
(\cref{ex:g1:the-five-senses:loud}) is the overdose
warning. The rule is a budget: lower, shorter, with
pauses --- and earplugs where the amplifiers rule.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\section{The junction-fooling substances}

\begin{proposition}[Psychoactive substances act at synapses]\label{prop:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:substances}
Every substance that changes mood, perception or alertness
works at \cref{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}'s
gaps --- imitating a messenger, blocking one, or forcing
its release:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{alcohol} damps the whole system's junctions:
        the loop slows end to end --- judgment, \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:reaction}{reaction
        time} (\cref{ex:g5:brain-in-command:driver}'s
        metres lengthen), balance, memory; the \omterm{def:g3:stages-of-life:stages}{adolescent}
        \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}'s still-building junctions take the damping
        deepest;
  \item \emph{tobacco's nicotine} imitates a natural
        messenger and, repeated, rewires the reward
        junctions toward \emph{\omterm{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}{addiction}}
        (\cref{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}'s trap,
        now with its mechanism: the \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapses} adjust to
        expect the dose --- and protest without it);
  \item \emph{cannabis} imitates another messenger family,
        muddying exactly the junctions of memory,
        motivation and timing --- costliest, again, in a
        \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} under construction;
  \item the harder the substance, the cruder the
        junction-forcing --- and the faster the reward
        rewiring that is \omterm{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}{addiction}'s engine.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{remark}[Addiction, mechanically]\label{rem:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:addiction}
\Cref{rem:g5:healthy-choices:never}'s trap has a wiring
diagram now: the reward junctions, dosed repeatedly,
\emph{adapt} --- receptors trimmed, thresholds raised ---
until the substance is needed to feel normal, and its
absence is a system protest. That \omterm{def:g4:adapted-to-their-world:adaptation}{adaptation} is the
easy-entrance-hard-exit asymmetry made of \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapses}:
starting is one decision; stopping is against rewired
junctions. The still-adjustable \omterm{def:g3:stages-of-life:stages}{adolescent} \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} rewires
fastest --- which is why every statistic finds early
starters trapped deepest, and why start-never remains the
cheap move.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[The screen loop]\label{ex:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:screens}
No molecule, same junctions: feeds and games are tuned
--- deliberately --- to tickle the reward wiring with
unpredictable small prizes, the schedule that trains
junctions hardest. The result is a mild cousin of the
trap: checking that resists stopping, evenings that
vanish, \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep} pushed late
(\cref{prop:g5:healthy-choices:screens}'s hours, plus
\cref{prop:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:sleep}'s
costs). Same defense as ever: decide the hours in
advance, and keep the last one screen-free.
\end{example}

\section{Practice: running your own protection}

\begin{method}[The nervous system's owner's manual]\label{met:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:manual}
The care list of \cref{met:g5:healthy-choices:run},
re-issued with mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{\omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep} the hours} --- consolidation and
        housekeeping run at night; guard the last hour
        from screens;
  \item \emph{budget loudness} --- receiver capital does
        not regrow: volume down, pauses in, earplugs
        where it roars;
  \item \emph{helmets} where falls are fast
        (\cref{ex:g5:brain-in-command:helmet}) --- wiring
        does not mend like \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone};
  \item \emph{no junction-foolers} for a \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} under
        construction --- and the ``no'' decided calmly in
        advance
        (\cref{ex:g5:healthy-choices:answers}'s pocket
        sentence, now with its full justification);
  \item \emph{use the machine} --- learning, sport,
        music: junctions strengthen with traffic, and a
        trained loop is the asset every other habit
        protects.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Help exists, and works]\label{ex:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:help}
For anyone already tangled --- their own habit or a
friend's, substances or screens: the addresses of
\cref{ex:g8:contraception:help} serve here too. School
nurses and doctors treat \omterm{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}{addiction} as what this chapter
shows it is --- a wiring condition, not a character
verdict --- and rewired junctions can re-adapt: harder
than never starting, far from hopeless. The worst plan
is the playground's: silence.
\end{example}

\section{Problem: The Prevention Evening}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- the school's
parents-and-students evening, scripted with
mechanisms}]\label{pb:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:1}
The class prepares the school's prevention evening: four
stations --- \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep}, sound, substances, screens --- each
with a demonstration and a mechanism card.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The stations'
cards.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Write the \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep} card: the demonstration is two
        volunteers' ruler-test scores
        (\cref{met:g5:brain-in-command:ruler}), rested
        against short-nighted. Predict the scores and
        give the junction mechanism.
  \item Write the sound card: a decibel meter, a pair of
        earplugs, and the sentence ``you cannot grow
        them back''. Fill in the mechanism.
  \item Write the substances card: one diagram of a
        \omterm{def:g8:nervous-communication:synapse}{synapse}, three arrows labeled imitate, block,
        force. Assign alcohol, nicotine and a venom from
        \cref{exo:g8:nervous-communication:11} to their
        arrows.
  \item Write the screens card: no molecule --- so what
        does the station's phone-with-notifications
        demonstration share with the substances
        station?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The questions from
the floor.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item A parent: ``we drank at their age and turned
        out fine.'' Answer with the construction-site
        asymmetry --- what differs at fourteen?
  \item A student: ``one cigarette can't rewire
        anything.'' Answer with
        \cref{rem:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:addiction}
        and \cref{rem:g5:healthy-choices:never} --- what
        is the first dose's real price?
  \item A parent: ``aren't screens just the new
        television?'' Distinguish the schedules: what
        makes unpredictable small prizes the harder
        trainer?
  \item A student, quietly: ``and if someone already
        can't stop?'' Give
        \cref{ex:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:help}'s
        answer, addresses included.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The closing
talk.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The talk opens with the juggler and closes with
        \omterm{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}{addiction} --- ``the same adjustability, read
        twice''. Draft that argument in four sentences.
  \item It then hands every student the pocket sentence
        of \cref{ex:g5:healthy-choices:answers}. Explain,
        mechanism included, why the sentence must be
        composed \emph{now}.
  \item The evening's poster shows the owner's manual's
        five items. Order them for a fourteen-year-old's
        week, most protective first, and defend your
        first choice.
  \item Close the evening in one sentence: what is being
        protected, and why is fourteen the decisive
        decade's middle?
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
