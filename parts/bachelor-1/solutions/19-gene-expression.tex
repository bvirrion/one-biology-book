\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:2}
A $5'$ cap (modified guanine), a $3'$ \omterm{prop:b1:gene-expression:processing}{poly-A tail}, and the removal of

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:3}
AUG CCG AAA GUU UGA: Met-Pro-Lys-Val, then stop.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:4}
A: the incoming charged tRNA is admitted and checked. P: the tRNA
carrying the growing chain; the bond forms between its chain and the
A-site \omterm{def:b1:proteins:aminoacid}{amino acid}. E: the emptied tRNA on its way out.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:5}
Coding $2400 - 600 = 1800$ \omterm{def:b1:nucleic-acids:nucleotide}{nucleotides}: 599 residues (600 \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:code}{codons}, one
a stop). Time $599/4 = \qty{150}{s}$. \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:ribosome}{Ribosomes} $1800/90 = 20$ at once.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:6}
The message is read in threes from a fixed start; one or two extra
bases shift every \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:code}{codon} downstream and the rest of the \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} is
gibberish, usually ending at a premature stop. Three extra bases add
one \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:code}{codon} and restore the frame: the \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} has one extra residue
and a few wrong ones between the insertions, and often still works.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:7}
CAG (Gln) to UAG (stop): the chain ends at residue 150, a truncated,
almost certainly inactive \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} (nonsense mutation). CAG to CAA:
still Gln, silent. CAG to CGG: Arg for Gln, a substitution
(missense) whose effect depends on the position.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:8}
The \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:ribosome}{ribosome} checks only the codon--anticodon pairing; it cannot see
the \omterm{def:b1:proteins:aminoacid}{amino acid}. If cysteine on its tRNA is converted chemically to
alanine, the \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:ribosome}{ribosome} inserts alanine wherever the message says
cysteine: the tRNA, not the \omterm{def:b1:proteins:aminoacid}{amino acid}, is read. Hence the synthetase,
which pairs each \omterm{def:b1:proteins:aminoacid}{amino acid} with the right tRNA, is the true
translator.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:9}
$400\times 4 = 1600$ bonds; the \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:ribosome}{ribosome}'s two GTP per residue are
half of it, the synthetases' two the other half.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:10}
In eukaryotes the transcript is made in the nucleus and the
\omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:ribosome}{ribosomes} are in the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:organelle}{cytosol}, separated by the envelope; and the
transcript is not a messenger until it has been spliced and capped.
The separation makes possible the processing itself --- alternative
splicing, the control of export, and a check that the message is
complete before it is read.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:11}
Four messengers: with both \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{exons} (190 \omterm{def:b1:nucleic-acids:nucleotide}{nucleotides} added), with 3 only
(90), with 4 only (100), with neither (0). A frame is kept if the
added length is a multiple of 3: 0 and 90 keep it; 100 and 190 shift
it, so the messengers with \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{exon} 4 alone or both \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{exons} read \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{exon} 5
out of frame and truncate the \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein}. Alternative splicing must
respect the frame, and \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{exons} are often multiples of three for that
reason.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:gene-expression:12}
Arbitrary: nothing in chemistry says that UUU must mean Phe; the
assignments are conventions held by the synthetases. Optimised: the
third position is the most degenerate, so the errors and mutations
that fall there are often silent, and \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:code}{codons} that differ by one base
tend to encode similar \omterm{def:b1:proteins:aminoacid}{amino acids}, so a substitution is often mild
--- the code minimises the damage of error. Frozen: a synthetase
that changed its specificity would alter every \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} of the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} at
once, thousands of them, in the same instant; almost no \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} survives
that, so the code cannot drift and has been fixed since the common
ancestor of all living things.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b1:gene-expression:1}
\textbf{1.} $30\,000/30 = \qty{1000}{s}$, about \qty{17}{min}.
\textbf{2.} $28\,000/30\,000 = \qty{93}{\%}$.
\textbf{3.} $30\,000/2000 = 15$.
\textbf{4.} $2\times 30\,000 = \num{60000}$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}.
\textbf{5.} $1000/10 = 100$ polymerases on the \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{gene}; 360 messengers
per hour.
\textbf{6.} Seven \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{introns}, mean $28\,000/7 = \qty{4000}{}$ \omterm{def:b1:nucleic-acids:nucleotide}{nucleotides}.
\textbf{7.} Transcription (\qty{17}{min}); the \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{introns} are spliced as
they are made, and the last one adds only a minute.
\textbf{8.} \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{Gene} $30\,000\times 0.34\,\mathrm{nm} = \qty{10}{\micro m}$; messenger \qty{0.68}{\micro m}.
\textbf{9.} $500/4 = \qty{125}{s}$.
\textbf{10.} $2000/80 = 25$.
\textbf{11.} One chain finishes every $80/4 = \qty{20}{s}$: 180 per
hour.
\textbf{12.} $2/\ln 2 = \qty{2.9}{h}$ of full production: about 520
\omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{proteins}.
\textbf{13.} $4\times 500 = 2000$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}.
\textbf{14.} $60\,000/520 = 115$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} per \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein}.
\textbf{15.} About 2100 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}, \qty{95}{\%} of it in translation.
\textbf{16.} $1 - (1 - 10^{-5})^{1503} \approx 1503\times 10^{-5} =
\qty{1.5}{\%}$.
\textbf{17.} $1 - (1 - 10^{-4})^{500} \approx \qty{5}{\%}$.
\textbf{18.} Transcription: $1.5\%\times 0.75\times 0.67 \approx
0.75\%$ of messengers defective, hence of \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{proteins}; translation:
$5\%\times 0.67 = 3.3\%$; about \qty{4}{\%} of the molecules.
\textbf{19.} A \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} error is confined to one molecule, which is
soon degraded; a messenger error is copied into hundreds of \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{proteins};
a replication error is inherited by every descendant for ever. The
cost of an error, and hence the accuracy worth paying for, rises at
each step back.
\textbf{20.} $3000/180 = 17$ messengers present.
\textbf{21.} $17\times 25 = 420$ \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:ribosome}{ribosomes}.
\textbf{22.} $\num{3e9}\times 4 = \num{1.2e10}$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} per day
$= \qty{2e-14}{mol}$, \qty{1e-9}{J} per day, i.e.\ \qty{1.2e-14}{W}
--- \qty{6}{fW} per cubic micrometre.
\textbf{23.} $10^9$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} per second is $\num{8.6e13}$ per day: \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein}
synthesis is a hundredth of it in this slowly renewing \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} (in a
dividing bacterium it is half).
\textbf{24.} No mature messenger: the primary transcripts accumulate
in the nucleus and the \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} disappears as its messengers decay,
within hours. The bacterial \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein}, whose \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{gene} has no \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{introns}, is
unaffected.
\textbf{25.} About 2100 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} per molecule --- 2000 for translation, a
hundred for its share of the transcript; first \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} after
\qty{1000}{s} of transcription, a minute of processing and export,
and \qty{125}{s} of translation: about twenty minutes.
\end{solution}
