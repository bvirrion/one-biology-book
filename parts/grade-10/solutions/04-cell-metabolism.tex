\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g10:cell-metabolism} --- Cell Metabolism}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:1}
\omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{Metabolism} is the set of all chemical reactions in a \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell}, breaking
molecules down for energy and building the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell}'s own. An \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzyme} is a
\omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{protein} that speeds up one specific reaction, binding its substrate
and releasing the product, without being consumed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:2}
Respiration: $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} + 6\,\mathrm{O_2} \to
6\,\mathrm{CO_2} + 6\,\mathrm{H_2O}$ + energy. \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:autotrophy}{Photosynthesis}:
$6\,\mathrm{CO_2} + 6\,\mathrm{H_2O}$ + light $\to \mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}
+ 6\,\mathrm{O_2}$. The overall balance of matter is reversed; but the
reactions, \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzymes}, places (\omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{mitochondrion} versus \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{chloroplast}) and
energy sources (chemical bonds versus light) are entirely different.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:3}
Heterotrophs: mushroom, carrot root \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell}, yoghurt bacterium, human
muscle \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell}. Autotrophs: moss, chlorella.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:4}
Carbon dioxide inflates the balloon; ethanol accumulates in the
liquid. The flask lacks oxygen: with air bubbled through, the yeast
would respire, make no ethanol and use far less sugar.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:5}
Catalase is a \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{protein}; boiling destroys its shape and hence its
activity, and hydrogen peroxide does not decompose fast on its own.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:6}
$(7.9 - 0.9)/7 = \qty{1.0}{mg/L}$ per minute. It flattens because
the oxygen is exhausted; the yeast then switches to \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation},
which consumes no oxygen.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:7}
$0.8 + 0.2 = \qty{1.0}{mg/L}$ per minute: the light slope is
production minus the respiration that continues in the light.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:8}
\qty{18}{g} is \qty{0.1}{mol} of glucose; it needs \qty{0.6}{mol} of
oxygen, $0.6 \times 32 = \qty{19.2}{g}$, and releases \qty{0.6}{mol}
of carbon dioxide, $0.6 \times 44 = \qty{26.4}{g}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:9}
Germinating peas respire their reserves and release heat; boiled peas
are dead, their \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzymes} destroyed, so no reaction and no heat. Sealed
airtight, the oxygen would run out and respiration stop.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:10}
The exposed half stains blue-black (starch made by \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:autotrophy}{photosynthesis} in
the light); the covered half does not. The 48 hours of darkness let
the leaf use up its previous starch, so that any starch found is due
to the day's light.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:11}
\omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{Fermentation} yields about fifteen times less ATP per glucose than
respiration; to obtain the ATP it needs, a fermenting \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} must break
down fifteen times more sugar per minute.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:12}
During the race the muscle's need for ATP outran the oxygen the blood
could deliver; the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} switched to lactic \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}, whose product,
lactic acid, accumulated. In a slow jog the oxygen supply keeps pace,
respiration alone provides the ATP, and no acid is made.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:13}
The environment changed (no light, organic food); the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes} did not.
The descendants rebuild \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{chloroplasts}, so the instructions were never
lost: the loss was a change of equipment in use, not of the
possibilities the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes} allow.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:14}
The plant photosynthesises in the light, supplying oxygen and \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:mineral-organic}{organic
matter} (leaves the snail eats); the snail respires, supplying carbon
dioxide to the plant. In the dark the plant only respires: both
consume oxygen, nobody produces it, and both die within days.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:cell-metabolism:15}
$30/2 = 15$ times more glucose per minute. Brewers want ethanol, which
only \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation} makes; a respiring yeast would turn the sugar into
carbon dioxide, water and more yeast, and no alcohol.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g10:cell-metabolism:1}
\textbf{1.} Alcoholic \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}, because the sealed vat holds no
oxygen: $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} \to 2\,\mathrm{C_2H_5OH} +
2\,\mathrm{CO_2}$.

\textbf{2.} $200/180 \approx \qty{1.11}{mol}$.

\textbf{3.} Ethanol $2 \times 1.11 = \qty{2.22}{mol}$, i.e. $2.22
\times 46 \approx \qty{102}{g}$; volume $102/0.79 \approx
\qty{129}{mL}$; about 13\% by volume.

\textbf{4.} $2.22 \times 44 \approx \qty{98}{g}$ of carbon dioxide;
$2.22 \times 24 \approx \qty{53}{L}$ of gas.

\textbf{5.} About \qty{53000}{L} $= \qty{53}{m^3}$. The cellar holds
$4 \times 6 \times 2.5 = \qty{60}{m^3}$: the gas released would nearly
fill it.

\textbf{6.} $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} + 6\,\mathrm{O_2} \to
6\,\mathrm{CO_2} + 6\,\mathrm{H_2O}$.

\textbf{7.} Oxygen $6 \times 1.11 = \qty{6.67}{mol}$, $6.67 \times 32
\approx \qty{213}{g}$; carbon dioxide $6.67 \times 44 \approx
\qty{293}{g}$.

\textbf{8.} Three times more: respiration turns all six carbons of
glucose into carbon dioxide, \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation} only two, the other four
staying in the ethanol.

\textbf{9.} With respiration, fifteen times longer: each glucose gives
fifteen times more ATP.

\textbf{10.} Ethanol is made only by \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}; an aerated vat would
give carbon dioxide, water and yeast, and no wine.

\textbf{11.} $1.11 \times 70 \approx \qty{78}{kJ}$ per litre;
\qty{78000}{kJ}, i.e. \qty{78}{MJ}, for the vat.

\textbf{12.} $78/4.2 \approx \qty{18.5}{\celsius}$.

\textbf{13.} $20 + 18.5 \approx \qty{38.5}{\celsius}$: at the edge of
the yeast's limit, and a warm cellar would push it over. A large vat
loses heat slowly because its surface is small compared with its
volume, so it must be cooled deliberately.

\textbf{14.} \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{Enzymes} are \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{proteins}; heat unfolds them and they stop
working. Without its \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzymes} the yeast cannot transform the sugar,
however much remains.

\textbf{15.} In the chemical bonds of the ethanol: about
\qty{2800}{kJ} per mole of glucose, which is why ethanol burns.

\textbf{16.} On the floor, in a layer that thickens as \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}
proceeds. A candle carried low goes out where the carbon dioxide has
displaced the oxygen --- a warning before a person is affected.

\textbf{17.} Lactic \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}. It yields fifteen times less ATP;
brain and heart cannot meet their needs that way, and the lactic acid
accumulates. Loss of consciousness follows within a minute or two.

\textbf{18.} Ethanol at high concentration damages the yeast's
membranes and \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzymes}: the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}' own product poisons their
\omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{metabolism}.

\textbf{19.} The bubbles are carbon dioxide from \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}. The
ethanol evaporates in the oven (it boils at \qty{78}{\celsius}).

\textbf{20.} About 13\% alcohol by volume and \qty{53}{L} of gas per
litre of must; both follow from the yeast fermenting, without oxygen,
each glucose into two ethanol and two carbon dioxide.
\end{solution}
