\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b1:mammal-organization} --- Functional Organization of a Mammal}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:mammal-organization:1}
Vertebrate: bilateral symmetry, \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:bodyplan}{cephalisation}, dorsal vertebral axis
enclosing the spinal cord, ventral \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:bodyplan}{coelom} with the viscera, four limbs.
Mammalian: the \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:bodyplan}{diaphragm} dividing the \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:bodyplan}{coelom} into thorax and abdomen,
hair, milk, regulated body temperature.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:mammal-organization:2}
Nutrition: digestive (stomach), respiratory (lung), circulatory
(heart), excretory (kidney). Relation: nervous (brain), sense \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:organ}{organs}
(eye), endocrine (thyroid), skeleton and muscles (femur, biceps), skin.
Reproduction: genital (ovary, testis), mammary glands.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:mammal-organization:6}
The capillary wall lets water and small ions through freely, so they
equilibrate, but holds back proteins. The plasma proteins draw water
into the vessels osmotically; without them water would leak into the
interstitial space, which would swell (oedema), and the plasma volume
would fall.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:mammal-organization:7}
In parallel each \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:organ}{organ} receives blood at full arterial oxygen and
pressure, and its flow can be regulated independently. In series the
brain would receive blood already depleted by the muscles, at lower
pressure, and its supply would fall precisely during exercise.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:mammal-organization:8}
$hS\Delta T = 80 - 20 = \qty{60}{W}$; $h = 60/(1.8\times 13) =
\qty{2.6}{W.m^{-2}.K^{-1}}$. At \qty{33}{\celsius} air the
non-evaporative loss vanishes; the skin must warm above the air (vessel
dilation raises it toward \qty{36}{\celsius}) and sweating must carry the
rest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:mammal-organization:9}
Heat to remove $800 - 200 - 150 = \qty{450}{W}$, i.e.\ \qty{1620}{kJ/h},
\qty{675}{g} of sweat per hour. Without sweating, $450/(70\times 3500) =
\qty{1.8e-3}{K/s}$, about \qty{0.11}{K/min}: \qty{3}{K} in half an hour.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:mammal-organization:10}
Small bodies lose heat fastest per gram (large $S/V$) and need a
continuous, high, reliable production: brown fat produces heat
chemically at a steady rate without movement, whereas shivering
requires developed muscles (which newborns lack), interferes with
movement and feeding, and is intermittent. Brown fat also sits near the
great vessels and warms the blood directly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:mammal-organization:11}
The warmed hypothalamus reads ``too hot'': the dog pants and dilates its
skin vessels despite the cold, and its core temperature falls, by a
degree or two within the hour. Without a hypothalamus there is no \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:homeostasis}{set
point}: the dog neither shivers in the cold nor pants in the heat, and
its temperature drifts toward the room's in both cases.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:mammal-organization:12}
The \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:compartments}{extracellular fluid} (\omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:compartments}{interstitial fluid} and plasma), of nearly
constant sodium-rich composition, temperature and volume, bathes every
cell as sea water bathed the first animals. Its constancy is maintained
by negative-feedback loops (kidney for volume and ions, lung for gases,
liver and pancreas for glucose, hypothalamus for temperature). Its
``shores'' are the \omterm{prop:b1:organism-environment:surfaces}{exchange surfaces} --- gut, lung, kidney, skin ---
where it meets the outside, and the circulation is the current that
stirs it.
\end{solution}
