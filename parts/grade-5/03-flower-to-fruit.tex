\chapter{From Flower to Fruit}\label{ch:g5:flower-to-fruit}

\Cref{ch:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds} watched the cherry blossom
become a cherry and left one question open: \emph{what
actually happens} when a grain of \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen} lands on the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistil}?
This year, armed with \cref{ch:g4:animal-reproduction}'s idea
of \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization} and \cref{ch:g5:first-look-at-cells}'s \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells},
we can open the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flower}'s machinery properly.

\section{Flowers have male and female parts}

\begin{proposition}[The flower's two roles in one]\label{prop:g5:flower-to-fruit:roles}
Reproduction in flowering \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} is \emph{sexual}
reproduction, exactly in
\cref{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}'s sense --- and most
\omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flowers} carry both roles at once:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{\omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{stamens}} are the \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{male} part: each grain of
        \emph{\omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen}} carries the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant}'s \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{male} reproductive
        \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells};
  \item the \emph{\omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistil}} is the \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{female} part: deep inside it
        lie the \emph{ovules}\index{ovule}, each holding an
        \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg cell}.
\end{itemize}
The ``future \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds}'' of
\cref{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts} now have their real
name: \omterm{prop:g5:flower-to-fruit:roles}{ovules}, waiting to be fertilized.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{remark}[Same plan as the animals]\label{rem:g5:flower-to-fruit:sameplan}
\omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{Male} \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}, \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg cells}, \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}: the vocabulary of
\cref{ch:g4:animal-reproduction} translates word for word.
The \omterm{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:principle}{unity of life} again
(\cref{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:principle}) --- daisies
and dolphins reproduce on one underlying plan, however
different the machinery looks.
\end{remark}

\section{Pollination, then fertilization}

\begin{definition}[The two steps]\label{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}
Making a \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed} takes two distinct steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{pollination}\index{pollination}: a \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen} grain
        travels from a \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{stamen} to the top of a \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistil} ---
        carried by an insect
        (\cref{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}) or by
        the wind;
  \item \emph{fertilization}\index{fertilization (plants)}:
        the landed grain grows a fine tube \emph{down through
        the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistil}}; through it, the \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{male} \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} reaches an
        ovule and joins its \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg cell}.
\end{enumerate}
Pollination is the journey; fertilization is the meeting.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Why unvisited flowers fall]\label{ex:g5:flower-to-fruit:unvisited}
\Cref{rem:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:bees}'s orchard rule now
has its mechanism: no visit, no \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen} on the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistil}; no
\omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen}, no tube, no \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}; and an unfertilized
\omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flower} withers whole --- no swelling \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistil}, no cherry. The
missing step was invisible, a \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}'s journey down a tube ---
but the empty \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:tree}{branch} in July is visible enough.
\end{example}

\section{After the meeting: seed and fruit}

\begin{proposition}[What each part becomes]\label{prop:g5:flower-to-fruit:becomes}
After \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}, the transformation of
\cref{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform} runs with new
precision:
\begin{itemize}
  \item each \emph{fertilized ovule} becomes a
        \emph{\omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed}} --- the tiny \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} inside it
        (\cref{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}) grows from
        the fertilized \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg cell}, exactly as an \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} grows
        from its own
        (\cref{ex:g5:first-look-at-cells:one});
  \item the \emph{\omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistil}} around them swells into the
        \emph{\omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruit}};
  \item \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{petals} and \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{stamens}, their work done, wither and
        fall.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Counting pips]\label{ex:g5:flower-to-fruit:pips}
Cut an apple across: five little chambers, a few pips in
each. Every pip is one ovule that was reached by one \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen}
tube. A lopsided apple with empty chambers on one side tells
of a poorly pollinated \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flower} --- too few grains landed to
fertilize all the \omterm{prop:g5:flower-to-fruit:roles}{ovules}, and the \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruit} swelled unevenly.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Wind-borne pollen]\label{ex:g5:flower-to-fruit:wind}
Grasses, wheat and hazel trees have no \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{petals}, no scent, no
nectar --- nothing to offer visitors. Their \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen} rides the
\emph{wind}: made in enormous quantities, dust-fine, thrown
into the air to land where luck takes it. The strategy trades
precision for quantity --- the spore-cloud logic of
\cref{rem:g4:plants-without-seeds:sporeseed}, played with
\omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen}. (It is that airborne \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen}, in passing, that tickles
hay-fever noses in June.)
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Diagnosing a fruitless plant]\label{met:g5:flower-to-fruit:diagnose}
A \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} flowered well but bears no \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruit}. Ask in order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Did pollinators reach it? (Indoors, netted, no
        \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects} about, cold rainy flowering weeks?)
  \item If not, pollinate by hand: touch a soft brush to the
        \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{stamens} of one \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flower}, then to the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistil} of
        another;
  \item did frost or damage kill the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flowers} before the
        \omterm{prop:g5:flower-to-fruit:roles}{ovules} could be fertilized
        (\cref{exo:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:9})?
  \item If \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruit} now sets, the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant}'s machinery was sound
        --- only the journey of step 1 had failed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:1}
Which part of the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flower} is \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{male}, and what does it make?
Which is \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{female}, and what lies inside it?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:2}
Define \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{pollination} and \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}, and say which comes
first.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:3}
Tell the journey of one \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen} grain, from \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{stamen} to \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg
cell}, in four steps.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:4}
After \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}: what does each fertilized ovule become?
What does the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistil} become? What happens to the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{petals}?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:5}
What is each apple pip, in this chapter's vocabulary? What
does a lopsided apple with empty chambers tell?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:6}
How do grasses and hazels pollinate without \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{petals}, scent or
nectar? What is the cost of their method?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:7}
Why does a \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flower} that no insect visited usually make no
\omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruit}? Name the missing step.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:8}
Try it (with a flowering balcony or windowsill \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant}):
pollinate two \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flowers} by hand with a soft brush, as in
\cref{met:g5:flower-to-fruit:diagnose}, and mark them with
thread. Do the marked \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flowers} set \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruit} more often than the
others?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:9}
List the parallels between \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} and \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} reproduction:
\omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{male} \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}, \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg cells}, \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}, the new individual's
first \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}. Where does each item live, in the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flower}'s
case?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:10}
A cherry grower's spring was cold and rainy: bees barely
flew for two weeks, though no frost came and the trees
blossomed magnificently. Predict July's harvest and explain
it with \cref{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:flower-to-fruit:11}
A greenhouse tomato grower installs a hive of bumblebees
indoors, though no \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flower} outside can be reached from the
sealed greenhouse. Using this chapter, explain exactly what
service the bumblebees sell --- and why fans blowing air
across the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} can partly replace them for tomatoes
(whose \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen} shakes loose), while for cherry trees they
could not.
\end{exercise}
