\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b2:microbial-metabolism} --- Microbial Metabolism and Biofilms}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:1}
Cyanobacterium: photo-litho-autotroph (light, water, $\mathrm{CO_2}$).
\emph{Nitrosomonas}: chemo-litho-autotroph (ammonium oxidation,
$\mathrm{CO_2}$). \emph{E.~coli} on glucose: chemo-organo-heterotroph.
Purple non-sulfur bacterium on succinate in light:
photo-organo-heterotroph. \emph{Thiobacillus} on sulfide in the dark:
chemo-litho-autotroph.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:2}
Oxygen (surface millimetres), nitrate (just below, where oxygen is
gone), manganese and iron oxides (the brown-to-grey transition),
sulfate (the black sulfidic layer, thick in marine sediments),
$\mathrm{CO_2}$ for \omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:anaerobic}{methanogenesis} (deepest, or just below the
surface in sulfate-poor fresh water).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:3}
$5\,\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} + 24\,\mathrm{NO_3^-} + 24\,\mathrm{H^+}
\to 30\,\mathrm{CO_2} + 12\,\mathrm{N_2} + 42\,\mathrm{H_2O}$: 4.8
nitrate ions per glucose (each nitrate accepts 5 electrons, each
glucose gives 24).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:4}
A surface-attached microbial community embedded in a self-made
matrix of polysaccharides, proteins and DNA. Stages: attachment,
microcolony and matrix secretion, maturation with towers and
channels, dispersal. Dental plaque and caries; infections of
catheters and implants (and the lungs in cystic fibrosis); fouling
and corrosion of pipes and hulls --- or, usefully, the trickling
filters of water treatment.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:5}
Eight electrons fall from \qty{-0.42}{V} to \qty{-0.24}{V}:
$\Delta G^{\circ\prime} = -8\times 96.5\times 0.18 =
\qty{-139}{kJ/mol}$ of methane (tabulated \qty{-131}{kJ/mol}), i.e.\
\qty{35}{kJ} per $\mathrm{H_2}$: at most 0.7 ATP per hydrogen, in
practice about half of one. The energy per substrate molecule is so
small that methanogens grow slowly (\omterm{def:b2:unicellular-diversity:division}{generation times} of hours to
days) and convert almost all their substrate into gas rather than
cells.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:6}
$\mu = 1.2\,S/(5 + S)$: \qty{0.20}{h^{-1}} ($T = \ln 2/\mu =
\qty{3.5}{h}$), \qty{0.60}{h^{-1}} (\qty{1.2}{h}), \qty{1.09}{h^{-1}}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:7}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:8}
Captured: $0.25\times 275 = \qty{69}{kJ}$, i.e.\ 1.4 ATP per
ammonium; $12/1.4 = 8.7$, about nine ammonium ions per carbon --- and
several times more once the cost of making NADH by driving electrons
uphill from nitrite is counted. A heterotroph gets its carbon already
reduced and organised, and thirty ATP per glucose; a nitrifier must
buy both its energy and its reducing power from a poor donor and
build every carbon from $\mathrm{CO_2}$, so it converts a tiny
fraction of the substrate it processes into cells.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:9}
$L_p = \sqrt{2\times 1.2\times 10^{-9}\times 0.2/0.5} =
\sqrt{9.6\times 10^{-10}} = \qty{31}{\micro m}$. Doubling $c_0$
multiplies $L_p$ by $\sqrt 2$: \qty{44}{\micro m}; quadrupling $q$
halves it: \qty{15}{\micro m}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:10}
Top to bottom: water with algae and cyanobacteria (oxygenic
photosynthesis); a rusty band of iron oxidisers where $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$
meets oxygen; a white veil of colourless sulfur oxidisers
(\omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:trophic}{chemolithotrophs}) where sulfide meets oxygen; a purple band of
purple sulfur bacteria (\omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:anoxygenic}{anoxygenic photosynthesis} on sulfide); a
green band of green sulfur bacteria (the same, tolerating more
sulfide, less light); black mud of fermenters and sulfate reducers.
(a) Purple sulfur bacteria need both light from above and sulfide
from below, so they sit at the interface where the two gradients
cross. (b) Sulfate reducers make $\mathrm{H_2S}$, which precipitates
iron as black FeS. (c) Closed for matter: sulfur, carbon and nitrogen
cycle between oxidised and reduced forms without leaving; open for
energy: light enters, heat leaves, and without light every cycle
stops.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:11}
Production $pn$, loss $kA$: $A^* = pn/k$. $A_c = 10^{-8}\times
6.02\times 10^{23} = 6.0\times 10^{15}$ molecules per litre; $n_c =
kA_c/p = 5\times 10^{-4}\times 6.0\times 10^{15}/5 = 6\times 10^{11}$
per litre, $6\times 10^{8}$ per millilitre. The dense culture
($10^{9}$) is above threshold, seawater ($10^{5}$) four orders of
magnitude below it. In a \omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:biofilm}{biofilm} the cells sit at $10^{11}$ per
millilitre of matrix, and the matrix slows the signal's escape
(effectively lowering $k$), so a microcolony of a few thousand cells
in a nanolitre reaches quorum while the same cells dispersed in a
litre never would.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:microbial-metabolism:12}
Nitrogen fixation (only \omterm{def:b2:unicellular-diversity:prokaryote}{prokaryotes} have nitrogenase): stopped, the
combined nitrogen of the biosphere would drain away by
\omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:anaerobic}{denitrification} and production would collapse within decades.
Nitrification and \omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:anaerobic}{denitrification} (\omterm{def:b2:unicellular-diversity:prokaryote}{prokaryotes} only): stopped,
ammonium would accumulate and nitrate vanish, and nitrogen would
never return to the air. \omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:anaerobic}{Methanogenesis} and \omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:anaerobic}{anaerobic respiration}
(\omterm{def:b2:unicellular-diversity:prokaryote}{prokaryotes} only): stopped, organic matter in anoxic sediments,
rumens and marshes would accumulate unmineralised and carbon would
leave the cycle; equally, oxygenic photosynthesis was a bacterial
invention, and without it there would be no oxygen to respire. The
eukaryotes are a late and chemically narrow addition to a
prokaryotic planet.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:microbial-metabolism:1}
\textbf{1.} $-24\times 96.5\times (0.82 + 0.43) = \qty{-2900}{kJ/mol}$.
\textbf{2.} Nitrate: $-24\times 96.5\times 1.17 = \qty{-2700}{kJ/mol}$;
sulfate: $-24\times 96.5\times 0.21 = \qty{-490}{kJ/mol}$.
\textbf{3.} Oxygen yields more, so aerobes out-compete denitrifiers
for the organic matter while oxygen lasts, and oxygen represses the
nitrate reductases; \omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:anaerobic}{sulfate reduction} needs anoxia \emph{and} the
absence of nitrate, so its smell means the tank is under-aerated and
the nitrate exhausted --- and $\mathrm{H_2S}$ is toxic and corrodes
the concrete.
\textbf{4.} $0.4\times 2900/50 = 23$ ATP; $0.4\times 2700/50 = 22$;
$0.4\times 490/50 = 4$.
\textbf{5.} About six times more ($23/4$).
\textbf{6.} Two ATP per glucose means a yield of about \num{0.1}, so
\qty{90}{\%} of the substrate's energy stays in the products (acids,
alcohols, $\mathrm{H_2}$), which the methanogens convert to methane
with a similarly small gain; little energy, little biomass, and the
carbon leaves as gas.
\textbf{7.} $10^4\times 0.3 = \qty{3000}{kg}$ of COD per day.
\textbf{8.} Biomass $0.4\times 3000 = \qty{1200}{kg}$ of COD; the
remaining \qty{1800}{kg} of COD are oxidised and need \qty{1800}{kg}
of $\mathrm{O_2}$.
\textbf{9.} $1800/2 = \qty{900}{kWh}$ per day.
\textbf{10.} $10^4\times 0.04 = \qty{400}{kg}$ of nitrogen;
$4.57\times 400 = \qty{1830}{kg}$ of $\mathrm{O_2}$.
\textbf{11.} At steady state $\mu = 1/\theta \le \mu_{\max}$: washout
below $\theta = 1/\mu_{\max} = \qty{2}{d}$.
\textbf{12.} $D = \qty{0.1}{d^{-1}}$: $S^* = 1\times 0.1/(0.5 - 0.1)
= \qty{0.25}{g/m^3}$.
\textbf{13.} $S^* = 0.1/(0.25 - 0.1) = \qty{0.67}{g/m^3}$, and the
washout limit rises to \qty{4}{d}; the operator must lengthen the
sludge age (waste less sludge): at $\theta = \qty{20}{d}$, $S^* =
0.05/0.2 = \qty{0.25}{g/m^3}$ again.
\textbf{14.} $2.86\times 400 = \qty{1144}{kg}$ of COD per day.
\textbf{15.} Effluent ammonium $0.25\times 10^4 = \qty{2.5}{kg/d}$,
\qty{0.6}{\%} of the load; the rest, \qty{397}{kg} of nitrogen,
leaves as $\mathrm{N_2}$: \qty{99}{\%}.
\textbf{16.} Saved: \qty{1144}{kg} of $\mathrm{O_2}$. Total demand:
$(1800 - 1144) + 1830 = \qty{2490}{kg}$ of $\mathrm{O_2}$ per day.
\textbf{17.} $1200\times 0.6\times 0.35 = \qty{252}{m^3}$ of methane
per day.
\textbf{18.} $252\times 36 = \qty{9070}{MJ} = \qty{2520}{kWh}$;
electricity $0.35\times 2520 = \qty{880}{kWh}$ per day.
\textbf{19.} Aeration $2490/2 = \qty{1245}{kWh}$ per day: the methane
covers $880/1245 = \qty{71}{\%}$ of it.
\textbf{20.} $D_e\,c'' = q$, so $c = c_0 + ax + qx^2/2D_e$; with
$c(L_p) = 0$ and $c'(L_p) = 0$: $a = -qL_p/D_e$ and $c =
(q/2D_e)(L_p - x)^2$, with $L_p^2 = 2D_ec_0/q$.
\textbf{21.} $L_p = \sqrt{2\times 1.5\times 10^{-9}\times 0.2/0.3} =
\sqrt{2\times 10^{-9}} = \qty{45}{\micro m}$.
\textbf{22.} $45/300 = \qty{15}{\%}$ aerobic. Below, denitrifiers
reduce the nitrate diffusing down from the nitrifying surface layer
to $\mathrm{N_2}$, using the organic matter that diffuses in.
\textbf{23.} Within one film the oxygen gradient creates an aerobic
layer (nitrification) over an anoxic one (\omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:anaerobic}{denitrification}), a few
tens of micrometres apart; the aerated tank is oxic throughout, so
\omterm{def:b2:microbial-metabolism:anaerobic}{denitrification} needs a separate anoxic tank.
\textbf{24.} Chlorine reacts with and is consumed by the matrix
polymers before it reaches the deep cells; the deep cells are
starved, slow-growing or dormant and much less sensitive; only the
surface layer dies and the film regrows from below.
\textbf{25.} Oxygen demand \qty{2490}{kg} per day; methane
\qty{252}{m^3} per day; the methane supplies \qty{71}{\%} of the
aeration electricity (\qty{880}{kWh} of \qty{1245}{kWh}).
\end{solution}
