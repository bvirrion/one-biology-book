\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g6:decomposers-and-soil} --- Decomposers and the Soil}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:1}
Mineral grains (ground rock), organic remains in every stage
of decay, water, and air --- plus its living population. The
dark layer is the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{humus}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:2}
On dead \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} --- fallen \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}, dead wood,
droppings, corpses. Visible: earthworms, woodlice,
millipedes, springtails (or dung beetles, fly larvae). The
felt-of-threads feeder: a \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{fungus}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:3}
Back into \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{mineral matter} --- \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{humus}, then dissolved \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral
salts}, and gas to the air. It reverses the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}' trade,
which built organic from mineral.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:4}
November: fallen whole. Winter: softened and mined by \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{fungi},
laced to a \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{skeleton} by springtails and woodlice. Spring:
dragged down and eaten by an earthworm, cast out as crumbs.
Summer: black \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{humus}; its minerals in the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil} water, drunk
by \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{roots} --- perhaps the oak's own.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:5}
Because the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers} eat it as fast as the years drop it:
each carpet is dismantled to \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{humus} and minerals before the
next has fully fallen.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:6}
Warm, damp and aired. The work nearly stops in winter ---
cold --- which is why the carpet lies thick until the warm
damp spring clears it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:7}
\omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{Producers} $\rightarrow$ \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumers} (\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} eaten); \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}
and \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumers} $\rightarrow$ dead remains, droppings, fallen
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} $\rightarrow$ \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers} (the dead eaten);
\omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers} $\rightarrow$ \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral salts} in the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil} water
$\rightarrow$ drunk by the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}: a closed wheel.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:8}
Workers: worms, woodlice, springtails, \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{fungi} and the unseen
crews. Raw material: the kitchen's organic waste. Product:
dark \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{humus} whose minerals feed the beds. It runs warm
because the workers, like all \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumers}, burn part of what
they eat --- the heap is heated by its staff's own living.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:9}
\omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{Decomposer} --- dragging \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} underground and eating
decayed matter --- and plougher: its burrows air the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil},
its casts knead mineral and organic together. Whole fields'
\omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil} passes through worm guts, which is why the old
naturalists crowned it farmland's maker.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:10}
Mulched bed: dark crumbly \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{humus}; a rich census --- worms,
woodlice, springtails; surface litter visibly vanishing
season by season; verdict, a turning cycle worth feeding.
Trampled path: pale compacted dust; a near-empty tray;
whatever falls lies unrotted; verdict, a starved, airless
\omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil} whose crew has gone.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:11}
Air. Sealed and sodden, the airless bag stops the aired
workers and lets only sour, airless rotting run. The fix:
out of the bag, into a loose, moist, turned heap ---
moisture kept, air admitted.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:decomposers-and-soil:12}
What piles up: the dead --- every autumn's carpets, every
generation's remains, mountains of undismantled \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic
matter}. What runs out: the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil}'s \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral salts}, never
restocked once the return arrow is cut; the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers} drink
the last dissolved minerals and then can build no more,
whatever the \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}. Both halves are the same cut arrow ---
decomposition --- seen from its two ends.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g6:decomposers-and-soil:1}
\textbf{1.} So the bags differ in one thing only --- the
mesh --- and any weight difference can be pinned on it: the
fair test, buried.

\textbf{2.} Whether the unseen \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil} life alone can dismantle
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} --- the coarse bag mixes visible and unseen workers;
the fine bag isolates the unseen.

\textbf{3.} Whether \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} rule the work: same \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves},
same mesh, but dry gravel against damp bank --- it tests
\cref{prop:g6:decomposers-and-soil:speed}'s damp condition
in the field.

\textbf{4.} Coarse loses most, fine less, sealed almost
nothing --- predicted straight from who and what each bag
admits.

\textbf{5.} Coarse: the full crew worked --- fastest
dismantling. Fine: only the unseen worked --- real but
slower loss. Sealed: no workers and no air --- almost no
true decomposition, only sour airless spoilage.

\textbf{6.} The \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{fungi}'s threads mine and soften the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf};
springtails and woodlice nibble it to lace; the worm drags
fragments down, eats them and casts out worked crumbs; the
unseen crews finish every fragment to \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{humus} and minerals.

\textbf{7.} That it is a true decomposing workforce in its
own right: weight left the bag with every visible \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}
barred, so the dismantling continued at the scale below
sight.

\textbf{8.} Dampness above all --- gravel drains and dries
--- and with it the working population a moist bank holds;
dry \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} idle whatever crew arrives.

\textbf{9.} Into the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil} and the air: part became \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{humus}
and dissolved \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral salts} in the bank's \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil} water, and
part was burned by the working \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers} and left as gas
--- the lost grams are all accounted for, none vanished.

\textbf{10.} No coincidence: the buried \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}' minerals
were released exactly there, and nettles are famous
gluttons for rich ground. The green stripe is the cycle's
last arrow made visible --- decomposition feeding
\omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}.

\textbf{11.} The landfill wrap is the sealed bag at city
scale: airless and crewless, it denies decomposition its
workers and \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}, so the waste persists as the
bag's sludge did --- while the same peelings in an aired,
damp, worker-filled heap return to \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil} in months.

\textbf{12.} The winter proved the carpet is cleared by
the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil}'s \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers} --- visible crew and unseen crews
together, fastest where warm, damp and aired. Their
clearing feeds the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil}'s mineral stores, and through
them the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}: the wheel of matter, turning in a
hedge bank.
\end{solution}
