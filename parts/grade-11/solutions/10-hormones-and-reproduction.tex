\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g11:hormones-and-reproduction} --- Hormonal Control of Reproduction}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:1}
A \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormone} is a molecule secreted into the blood by a gland and acting
on distant \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} carrying its receptor. \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{Feedback} is the action of the
end product on the glands at the start of the chain: negative when it
reduces the command (holding a level constant), positive when it
increases it (producing a surge).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:2}
Hypothalamus (GnRH), pituitary (LH and FSH), gonad (testosterone in
the testis; oestrogens and progesterone in the ovary).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:3}
The seminiferous tubules produce sperm; the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} between them
secrete testosterone.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:4}
Follicular phase (days 1--13): growing follicle, oestrogens.
Ovulation (day 14): LH surge. Luteal phase (days 15--28): corpus
luteum, progesterone and oestrogens.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:5}
The degeneration of the corpus luteum at the end of the cycle: the
fall of progesterone and oestrogens makes the thickened lining of the
uterus break down and bleed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:6}
Oestrogen peak day 12, LH peak day 13, ovulation day 14, progesterone
peak day 21.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:7}
Castration removes testosterone, hence its inhibition of the
hypothalamus and pituitary: LH rises. Injected testosterone restores
the inhibition and LH falls --- negative \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} in both directions.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:8}
On day 12 the oestrogen level is high and sustained, which reverses
the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} to positive and triggers the surge. On day 20 oestrogens
are moderate and progesterone is high, and the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} is negative:
LH is held down.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:9}
Day 21: the luteal phase lasts about 14 days whatever the cycle
length, so ovulation is 14 days before the next period; the extra
week is in the follicular phase.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:10}
The pill's steady, moderate oestrogen and progesterone-like \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormone}
keep the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} negative all month: FSH stays too low for a
follicle to mature, so no oestrogen peak occurs, no positive \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback},
no LH surge, no ovulation.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:11}
The external testosterone inhibits his hypothalamus and pituitary;
LH and FSH fall; his testes, no longer stimulated, stop secreting and
stop making sperm, and shrink.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:12}
Without oestrogen's inhibition, GnRH and FSH rise; follicles mature;
their oestrogen peak (acting on the pituitary, still able to respond)
triggers an LH surge and ovulation. It is used when ovulation fails
because the follicles receive too little FSH.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:13}
The follicle grows and secretes (ovary and FSH work), but the
oestrogen peak does not produce a surge: the positive \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} fails
in the hypothalamus or the pituitary. An injection of an LH-like
\omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormone} at the time of the peak can trigger ovulation.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:14}
Kept alive, the corpus luteum keeps secreting progesterone: the lining
is maintained (no menstruation) and the negative \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} holds LH
and FSH down (no new follicle or ovulation). The \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormone} appears in
the blood and urine within days of implantation, which is what the
tests detect.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:15}
$12 \times 0.6 \times 0.4 \approx 2.9$: about 3 embryos. Probability
of no birth in three transfers: $0.65^3 \approx 0.27$; at least one
birth: about 73\%.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:1}
\textbf{1.} Day 14: the LH surge of day 13 precedes ovulation by a
day, and progesterone, which appears only after ovulation, rises from
day 14.

\textbf{2.} Follicular phase days 1--13 (13 days); luteal phase days
14--27 or 28 (about 14 days).

\textbf{3.} The growing follicle on day 10; the corpus luteum on day
22, for both oestrogens and progesterone.

\textbf{4.} Day 6: thin, beginning to thicken under oestrogens. Day
22: thick and secretory under progesterone, ready for an embryo. Day
28: breaking down, bleeding, as both \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} have fallen.

\textbf{5.} At the start the previous cycle's \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} have fallen and
their inhibition with them, so FSH rises; in the luteal phase
progesterone and oestrogens inhibit it strongly.

\textbf{6.} Negative \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}: the rising oestrogens of the follicle
inhibit the pituitary, and FSH falls.

\textbf{7.} Positive \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}: oestrogens at their maximum for about
two days stimulate the hypothalamus and pituitary instead of
inhibiting them, and LH surges.

\textbf{8.} Negative: progesterone with moderate oestrogens inhibits
the pituitary; without a sustained oestrogen peak, no positive
\omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} and no second surge.

\textbf{9.} Progesterone and oestrogens collapse; the lining breaks
down and bleeds on day 1 of the next cycle; FSH, released from
inhibition, rises.

\textbf{10.} The fall of the luteal \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} removes the negative
\omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback}, FSH rises, and a new follicle begins to grow: the end of
one cycle is the signal for the next.

\textbf{11.} FSH and LH low all month (negative \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} maintained
by the pill's \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones}); no follicle matures.

\textbf{12.} No peak (no maturing follicle), no surge, no ovulation.

\textbf{13.} The pill's \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} fall and the lining, thin, breaks
down and bleeds; it is a withdrawal bleed, not the end of a luteal
phase --- there was no corpus luteum.

\textbf{14.} Without the pill's \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{hormones} the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} lifts, FSH rises,
a follicle may start to grow and secrete; if it reaches its oestrogen
peak, an LH surge and ovulation can follow, and the thin lining and
thick mucus are no longer maintained.

\textbf{15.} Progesterone alone inhibits the hypothalamus and pituitary
enough to prevent the LH surge, and thickens the cervical mucus.

\textbf{16.} The hypothalamus or pituitary: the ovary is not
stimulated. Pulses of GnRH (if the pituitary works) or injections of
FSH and LH restore follicle growth and ovulation, bypassing the
missing tier.

\textbf{17.} In vitro fertilisation: eggs collected from the ovary,
fertilised in a dish, embryo placed in the uterus --- the meeting of
the gametes and the transport that the oviduct would have done.

\textbf{18.} The natural LH surge; ovulation follows the surge by
about 36 hours, so the eggs are collected just before they would be
released and lost.

\textbf{19.} Naturally, the rising oestrogens of the leading follicle
lower FSH, and the smaller follicles, starved of FSH, degenerate;
only the largest survives. Injected FSH overrides this selection and
keeps all of them growing.

\textbf{20.} Ovulation on day 14; the negative \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} that lowered
FSH as the follicle grew, then the positive \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} that turned the
oestrogen peak into an LH surge; the pill held the \omterm{def:g11:hormones-and-reproduction:hormone}{feedback} negative
all month, so no follicle matured and no surge occurred.
\end{solution}
