\chapter{From Gene to Protein}\label{ch:g11:gene-expression}

In 1961 a biochemist fed a cell-free extract of bacteria with an
artificial \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:transcription}{RNA} made of nothing but the letter U, repeated. The extract
made a \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} made of nothing but one \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acid}, phenylalanine,
repeated. With that single experiment the first word of the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{genetic
code} was read: UUU means phenylalanine. Within five years all
sixty-four words were known, and they turned out to be the same words in
a bacterium, a wheat plant and a human. This chapter follows the path
from a \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}'s sequence of \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotides} to a \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein}'s sequence of \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino
acids} --- the path every trait of \cref{ch:g11:mutations} runs along.

\section{Genes make proteins}

\begin{proposition}[One gene, one protein]\label{prop:g11:gene-expression:onegene}
A \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} is expressed when the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} uses its sequence to make the
\omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} it encodes. Most traits depend on \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{proteins} --- \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzymes},
structural fibres, carriers, receptors --- and a \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation} changes a
trait by changing the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} its \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} produces.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Evidence]
Beadle and Tatum (1941) irradiated spores of a bread mould and
collected mutants that could no longer grow on minimal medium unless
one specific substance, such as the \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acid} arginine, was added.
Each mutant lacked one \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzyme} of the chain of reactions that makes
that substance; each defect was inherited as a single \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}; and
mutants blocked at different steps of the same chain carried
\omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} in different \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes}. One \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}, one \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzyme} --- and, as later
work generalised, one \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}, one polypeptide chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Protein, amino acid sequence]\label{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}
A \emph{protein}\index{protein} is a chain of \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids}, from a few
dozen to several thousand long, drawn from a set of twenty kinds. The
\emph{sequence} --- the order of the \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids} --- is what the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}
specifies; once made, the chain folds into a definite
three-dimensional shape determined by that sequence, and the shape
determines the function. Change one \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acid} and the fold, and the
function, may change.
\end{definition}

\section{Transcription: the gene copied into RNA}

\begin{definition}[Messenger RNA and transcription]\label{def:g11:gene-expression:transcription}
\emph{RNA}\index{RNA} (ribonucleic acid) is a single-stranded chain of
\omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotides} like those of \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:information}{DNA}, except that its sugar is ribose and the
base uracil (U) replaces thymine (T). \emph{Transcription}\index{transcription}
is the copying of a \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}'s sequence into a molecule of
\emph{messenger RNA}\index{messenger RNA} (mRNA): the \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzyme}
\emph{RNA polymerase} opens the \omterm{prop:g10:universal-dna:helix}{double helix} along the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}, reads one
strand (the \emph{template strand}) and assembles the complementary
RNA, U facing A, A facing T, G facing C and C facing G. The mRNA has
the sequence of the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}'s other strand, with U for T; it leaves the
\omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{nucleus} for the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cytoplasm}.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[A transcript]\label{ex:g11:gene-expression:transcript}
Template strand \texttt{3'-TAC GGA CTT ATC-5'} gives the mRNA
\texttt{5'-AUG CCU GAA UAG-3'}. The \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}'s other strand reads
\texttt{5'-ATG CCT GAA TAG-3'}: the mRNA is that strand's sequence
with U for T, which is why \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} sequences are conventionally written
as that strand, the \emph{coding strand}.
\end{example}

\section{The genetic code}

\begin{definition}[Codon and the genetic code]\label{def:g11:gene-expression:code}
The mRNA is read in consecutive groups of three \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotides}, the
\emph{codons}\index{codon}, from a defined starting point. The
\emph{genetic code}\index{genetic code} is the correspondence between
the 64 possible codons and the 20 \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids}: 61 codons specify an
\omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acid}, three (UAA, UAG, UGA) are \emph{stop} signals ending the
chain, and AUG, which specifies methionine, also serves as the
\emph{start} codon. The code is \emph{degenerate} --- most \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids}
have several codons --- and \emph{universal}: the same table serves
every known organism, with rare minor variants.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[How the code was read]\label{prop:g11:gene-expression:readcode}
Every \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon}'s meaning was established experimentally.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Evidence]
Nirenberg and Matthaei (1961) added synthetic \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:transcription}{RNA} of a single repeated
\omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotide} to a cell-free extract containing \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosomes}, \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids}
and the \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzymes} of \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} synthesis: poly-U yielded a chain of
phenylalanines, poly-C a chain of prolines, poly-A of lysines.
Synthetic \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:transcription}{RNAs} of repeating pairs and triplets, and later the binding
of single triplets to \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosomes}, assigned the remaining \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codons} by
1966. The three-letter length had been inferred from the arithmetic
(two letters give only 16 combinations, too few for 20 \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids};
three give 64) and confirmed by \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations}: inserting one or two
\omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotides} into a \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} destroys its \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein}, inserting three
restores a nearly normal one.
\end{proof}

\begin{method}[Translating a sequence]\label{met:g11:gene-expression:translate}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Write the coding strand (or transcribe the template strand):
        replace T by U to get the mRNA.
  \item Find the first AUG: it is the start, and the reading frame is
        fixed from it.
  \item Cut the mRNA into consecutive triplets from the AUG and read
        each in the table.
  \item Stop at the first stop \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon}; the \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids} listed, in
        order, are the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein}'s sequence.
  \item For a mutant, repeat and compare: same \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} (silent), one
        \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acid} changed (missense), premature stop (nonsense),
        everything changed after the \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation} (frameshift).
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Four mutations, four outcomes]\label{ex:g11:gene-expression:outcomes}
Coding strand \texttt{ATG CCT GAA TTC TAG}: mRNA
\texttt{AUG CCU GAA UUC UAG}, \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} Met--Pro--Glu--Phe.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{ATG CC\textbf{C} GAA TTC TAG}: CCC is still Pro ---
        \emph{silent}.
  \item \texttt{ATG CCT G\textbf{C}A TTC TAG}: GCA is Ala ---
        Met--Pro--Ala--Phe, \emph{missense}.
  \item \texttt{ATG CCT \textbf{T}AA TTC TAG}: UAA is stop ---
        Met--Pro, \emph{nonsense}, a truncated \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein}.
  \item \texttt{ATG CC\textbf{A}T GAA TTC TAG}: an inserted A shifts
        the frame: \texttt{AUG CCA UGA}\dots{} Met--Pro--stop ---
        \emph{frameshift}.
\end{itemize}
\end{example}

\section{Translation: the message read}

\begin{proposition}[Translation]\label{prop:g11:gene-expression:translation}
\emph{Translation}\index{translation} takes place on the
\emph{\omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosomes}}, in the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cytoplasm}. A \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosome} binds the mRNA at its
start \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon} and moves along it \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon} by \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon}. Each \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon} is matched
by a \emph{transfer \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:transcription}{RNA}} (tRNA), a small \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:transcription}{RNA} carrying, at one end,
the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{three-nucleotide} \emph{anticodon} complementary to the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon} and,
at the other, the corresponding \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acid}. The \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosome} joins each
\omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acid} brought in to the growing chain and releases the empty tRNA;
at a stop \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon} it releases the finished chain, which folds. Several
\omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosomes} read one mRNA in succession, and one mRNA yields hundreds
of copies of the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} before it is degraded.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Speed and yield]\label{ex:g11:gene-expression:speed}
A \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosome} adds 5 to 20 \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids} per second: a \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} of 300 \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino
acids} takes under a minute. \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{Ribosomes} follow each other on an mRNA
about 80 \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotides} apart, so a message of 900 \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotides} carries a
dozen at once; over its life of a few hours a single mRNA produces a
thousand \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} molecules. A \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} of \emph{E.~coli} makes some
$\num{20000}$ \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} molecules per second; a red blood \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell}'s
precursor, some $\num{5e8}$ haemoglobin molecules in its lifetime.
\end{example}

\section{One gene, several messages}

\begin{proposition}[Maturation of the message in eukaryotes]\label{prop:g11:gene-expression:splicing}
In \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:prokaryote}{eukaryotic cells} the sequence of a \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} is interrupted by
\emph{introns}\index{intron}, segments that are transcribed but then
cut out of the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:transcription}{RNA} before it leaves the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{nucleus}; the segments kept and
joined end to end, the \emph{exons}, form the mature mRNA. Many \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes}
can be cut in more than one way, keeping different sets of exons
(\emph{alternative splicing}), so that one \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} yields several
distinct mRNAs and several related \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{proteins}: the $\num{20000}$ human
\omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes} encode well over $\num{100000}$ \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{proteins}.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{remark}[The flow of information]\label{rem:g11:gene-expression:flow}
\omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:information}{DNA} is transcribed into \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:transcription}{RNA}, \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:transcription}{RNA} is translated into \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein}, and
\omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} makes the trait: the information flows one way. A changed
\omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} never rewrites the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}, which is why a lifetime of training
or sunburn is not inherited, and why only \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} of the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:information}{DNA}
(\cref{ch:g11:mutations}) change what the next generation receives.
The same three steps run in every \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} of every organism, in the same
code: the universality of \cref{ch:g10:universal-dna}, now down to the
last word.
\end{remark}

\section{Problem: A Gene Read Four Ways}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- one short gene transcribed,
translated, mutated three times and timed: from the DNA to the amino
acids, and the sixty seconds a protein takes}]
\label{pb:g11:gene-expression:1}
The coding strand of a short \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} reads:
\begin{center}
\texttt{ATG GCT TGG AAA CCC GAA TAC GGT CAT TTC TGA}
\end{center}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Reading the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Write the template strand.
  \item Write the mRNA transcribed from the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}.
  \item Translate it, \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon} by \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon}, and give the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein}'s sequence
        of \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids}.
  \item How many \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids} does the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} contain, and how many
        \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotides} of the mRNA are used to specify them, stop
        included?
  \item Which \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon} started the reading, and what fixes the reading
        frame for all the others?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Three mutants.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Mutant 1: \texttt{ATG GCT TGG AAA CCG GAA TAC GGT CAT TTC TGA}.
        Give the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} and classify the \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation}.
  \item Mutant 2: \texttt{ATG GCT TGG AAA CCC GAA TAC GGT CAT TTC TGA}
        with the ninth \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon} \texttt{CAT} replaced by \texttt{CGT}.
        Give the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} and classify.
  \item Mutant 3: \texttt{ATG GCT TGA AAA CCC GAA TAC GGT CAT TTC TGA}.
        Give the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} and classify. Which single substitution
        produced it?
  \item Mutant 4: a T is inserted after the sixth \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotide}:
        \texttt{ATG GCT TTG GAA ACC CGA ATA CGG TCA TTT CTG A}. Give
        the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} and classify.
  \item Rank the four mutants from least to most disruptive for the
        \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} and justify.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part III --- Why the code is what it is.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item How many \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codons} specify leucine? Serine? Tryptophan? Which
        \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acid} is most likely to be changed by a random
        substitution in its \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon}, and which least?
  \item Compute the fraction of all substitutions at the third
        position of a leucine \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon} \texttt{CUx} that are silent.
  \item Explain why a code of two letters could not work, and why a
        code of four is not needed.
  \item Poly-U gives poly-phenylalanine and poly-A poly-lysine. Which
        entries of the table do these two experiments establish?
  \item The code is the same in the bacterium and in a human. State
        the consequence for \omterm{prop:g10:universal-dna:universal}{transgenesis} and the consequence for
        kinship.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part IV --- Timing the factory.}
A \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosome} adds 10 \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids} per second and \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosomes} follow each
other 80 \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotides} apart along an mRNA; each mRNA lives 2 hours.
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item How long does one \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosome} take to translate this \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}'s
        \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein}? And a \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} of 600 \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids}?
  \item How many \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosomes} can read an mRNA of 1800 \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotides} at
        once?
  \item If a new \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosome} starts every 8 seconds, how many copies of
        the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} does one mRNA yield in its lifetime?
  \item A bacterium needs \num{2000} copies of an \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:metabolism}{enzyme} in 10
        minutes. How many mRNAs of the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}, transcribed at once, are
        needed?
  \item State the result: the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} encoded by the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}, the
        \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation} among the four that abolished it, and the time one
        \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{ribosome} needs to build it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
