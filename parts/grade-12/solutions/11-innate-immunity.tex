\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g12:innate-immunity} --- Innate Immunity}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:1}
Innate: present from birth, acts within hours, recognises classes of
intruders by shared patterns, responds identically every time. Adaptive:
takes days, recognises each intruder individually, remembers it (and
exists only in \omterm{def:g10:common-ancestry:bodyplan}{vertebrates}).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:2}
Redness and heat: widened vessels bringing more blood. Swelling: leaky
vessel walls letting plasma into the tissue. Pain: nerve endings
sensitised by the \omterm{prop:g12:innate-immunity:inflammation}{mediators}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:3}
Molecular patterns shared by classes of microbes (wall fragments,
viral \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{nucleic acids}) and molecules released by damaged \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}. They
release \omterm{prop:g12:innate-immunity:inflammation}{mediators}: histamine, prostaglandins, cytokines.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:4}
Contact and binding by receptors; engulfing by the membrane; digestion
in a vesicle with \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzymes} and reactive chemicals; display of \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein}
fragments on the surface.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:5}
They block the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} that makes prostaglandins, reducing swelling,
pain and fever. They do not remove the cause or kill any microbe.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:6}
Innate at about half a day; adaptive at about day 9; the curves cross
around day 5--6.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:7}
The trigger is a molecular pattern recognised by the sentinel \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells},
not a living intruder. Sterile saline carries no pattern and no
damage: at most a slight response to the needle.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:8}
Dead neutrophils, dead and living bacteria, digested debris and
plasma. Neutrophils arrive over hours and die over a day; pus is what
accumulates once they have fought.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:9}
Hay fever is histamine released by mast \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} against pollen: blocking
histamine removes the response. In an infection histamine drives only
the first minutes; the later swelling, pain and recruitment run on
prostaglandins and cytokines, which the antihistamine does not touch.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:10}
At 12 hours 100\% against 55\%; at 36 hours 50\% against 30\%. The
drug lowers the swelling at every time but the curve still falls to
zero at about 72 hours: it does not shorten the response.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:11}
Torn \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligament} fibres and crushed \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} release the molecules of
damaged \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}, which the sentinel \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}' receptors recognise as they
would a microbe: damage alone is a trigger.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:12}
Without neutrophils the first days are lost and ordinary bacteria
overwhelm the body: the innate response is what holds the line at
once. Without \omterm{def:g12:innate-immunity:immunity}{adaptive immunity} the innate response clears the first
infections, but persistent or repeated ones, which need specific
antibodies and memory, eventually win: the adaptive response finishes
what the innate one contains.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:13}
An abscess is an inflammation fighting living bacteria: damping it
slows their clearance and risks spreading. Arthritis is an
inflammation without any microbe, directed at the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint} itself: there
the response is the disease, and reducing it is the treatment.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:14}
Innate receptors are fixed by the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes} and recognise patterns shared
by whole classes; nothing about a second meeting changes them. The
adaptive system builds receptors specific to each intruder and keeps
the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} that made them: it recognises the individual, and keeps the
recognition.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:innate-immunity:15}
Inflammation is the cure when it is directed at a real intruder or
injury, proportionate, and resolving in days: it clears the site and
starts repair. It is the disease when it is directed at something
harmless (pollen, the body's own \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joints}), excessive, or unending:
then its \omterm{prop:g12:innate-immunity:inflammation}{mediators} and \omterm{def:g12:innate-immunity:phagocytosis}{phagocytes} damage the tissue they were meant to
protect. What decides is the target and the duration, not the
mechanism, which is the same in both.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g12:innate-immunity:1}
\textbf{1.} Three doublings in 2 hours: $\num{10000} \times 8 =
\num{80000}$; six in 4 hours: $\num{640000}$.

\textbf{2.} $200 \times 5 = 1000$ per hour --- far below the
$\num{10000}$ new bacteria the first hour's doublings add: growth
continues.

\textbf{3.} $\num{50000} \times 20 = 10^6$ bacteria.

\textbf{4.} At hour 2 there are fewer than $\num{80000}$ bacteria; the
first hour's neutrophils alone can remove a million. The \omterm{def:g12:innate-immunity:phagocytosis}{phagocytes}
are winning from hour 2.

\textbf{5.} Within a few hours the bacteria are gone; what remains is
the dead neutrophils, debris and fluid that will be the pus, and
macrophages clearing it.

\textbf{6.} Redness and heat: histamine and prostaglandins widen the
vessels. Swelling: histamine makes the walls leaky and plasma seeps
out. Pain: prostaglandins sensitise the nerve endings.

\textbf{7.} The leaky vessels are how the neutrophils and the plasma's
defensive \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{proteins} reach the tissue; the fluid pressing on the
sensitised nerves is the pain.

\textbf{8.} Each heartbeat pushes more blood into the widened vessels;
the swollen tissue cannot expand, so each pulse raises the pressure on
the sensitised nerves: a throb in time with the heart.

\textbf{9.} Dead neutrophils, bacteria dead and alive, digested debris,
plasma.

\textbf{10.} The patterns disappeared with the bacteria and the debris
was cleared: no pattern, no recognition by the sentinels, no
\omterm{prop:g12:innate-immunity:inflammation}{mediators}; the vessels return to normal.

\textbf{11.} It reduces the early redness, heat and leak due to
histamine; not the later swelling and pain due to prostaglandins, nor
the recruitment of neutrophils.

\textbf{12.} It blocks the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} that makes prostaglandins: pain,
part of the swelling, and the fever fall.

\textbf{13.} The cream switches off most \omterm{prop:g12:innate-immunity:inflammation}{mediators}, so fewer neutrophils
are called; with fewer \omterm{def:g12:innate-immunity:phagocytosis}{phagocytes} the bacteria may not be cleared and
the infection may spread.

\textbf{14.} All three act on the signs and none kills bacteria; the
corticosteroid, by cutting the recruitment of \omterm{def:g12:innate-immunity:phagocytosis}{phagocytes}, can let the
infection last longer.

\textbf{15.} Cytokines reaching the brain, raising the temperature set
point; the extra heat speeds the \omterm{def:g12:innate-immunity:phagocytosis}{phagocytes} and slows the bacteria.

\textbf{16.} The cytokines they took up at the site, which tell the
lymphocytes what kind of intruder it was; they report to the
lymphocytes of the lymph node.

\textbf{17.} About a week to ten days. For a splinter cleared by the
innate response in a few hours, they will not be needed --- but they
will be made, and remembered.

\textbf{18.} The adaptive response is faster and stronger the second
time, thanks to memory; the innate response is exactly the same.

\textbf{19.} \omterm{def:g11:antibiotic-resistance:antibiotic}{Antibiotics} kill bacteria, taking the place of the
missing \omterm{def:g12:innate-immunity:phagocytosis}{phagocytes} against bacterial infections; they cannot replace
the \omterm{def:g12:innate-immunity:phagocytosis}{phagocytes}' clearing of debris, their action on fungi, or the
report to the lymph node.

\textbf{20.} Around hour 2 the \omterm{def:g12:innate-immunity:phagocytosis}{phagocytes} overtake the bacteria;
histamine and prostaglandins behind redness, heat and swelling,
prostaglandins behind pain; the \omterm{prop:g12:innate-immunity:handover}{dendritic cell} carries the report.
\end{solution}
