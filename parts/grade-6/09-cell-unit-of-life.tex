\chapter{The Cell, Unit of Life}\label{ch:g6:cell-unit-of-life}

\Cref{ch:g5:first-look-at-cells} ended with a promise: the
little rooms are not empty. This year the school \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:microscope}{microscope}
comes back out --- with better slides and sharper questions.
What exactly is in a \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}? Do \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} and \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} differ?
And is a \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} a \emph{part} of life, or can one \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} be a
whole \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living thing}? The smallest unit of \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{biology} deserves
one thorough chapter.

\section{Inside the cell}

\begin{definition}[The cell's parts]\label{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:parts}
Seen properly, every \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} shows the same basic equipment:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the \emph{membrane}\index{membrane}, the \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}'s
        thin outer envelope, which holds it together and
        controls what enters and \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves};
  \item the \emph{cytoplasm}\index{cytoplasm}, the jelly
        filling where the \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}'s work goes on;
  \item the \emph{nucleus}\index{nucleus}
        (\cref{rem:g5:first-look-at-cells:nucleus}'s dark
        spot), the command center that directs the \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}'s
        life and its division.
\end{enumerate}
Membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus: the three-part kit of nearly
every \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} in every \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant}, \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} and \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{fungus}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Plant cells carry extras]\label{prop:g6:cell-unit-of-life:plantextras}
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{Plant} \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} add equipment of their own:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a stiff \emph{cell wall}\index{cell wall} outside
        the \omterm{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:parts}{membrane} --- the walls that make onion \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}'s
        brickwork (\cref{ex:g5:first-look-at-cells:classics})
        and give \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} their standing stiffness without
        \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones};
  \item green grains of the light-catching substance in the
        \omterm{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:parts}{cytoplasm} --- the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}' workshops
        (\cref{prop:g6:plants-are-producers:produce}),
        absent from every \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell};
  \item often a large water-filled pocket whose pressure
        keeps the \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} plump --- wilting
        (\cref{ex:g1:plants-around-us:wilt}) is these
        pockets going slack.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\section{Whole lives in one cell}

\begin{definition}[Unicellular living things]\label{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:unicellular}
A \emph{unicellular}\index{unicellular} \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living thing} is a
complete organism of exactly one \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}
(\cref{rem:g5:first-look-at-cells:onecelled}). In one
\omterm{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:parts}{membrane} it feeds, moves, senses and reproduces --- by
dividing in two
(\cref{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:growth}'s division, as a
whole life story). The \omterm{ex:g6:producing-our-food:bread}{yeast} of
\cref{ex:g6:producing-our-food:bread} is one; so are the
swimmers of any pond drop, and most \omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:fermentation}{microbes} of \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:soil}{soil},
yogurt and hands.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[A drop of pond water, toured]\label{ex:g6:cell-unit-of-life:drop}
One drop from the aquarium's green wall, under the lens: a
slipper-shaped \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} rowing past with beating hairs, faster
than anything else in view; green single \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} --- swimming
\omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}, catching \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light} like any \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf}; a blob that flows
around its food and swallows it whole. Each is feeding,
moving, avoiding --- a complete animal-or-plant's life,
budgeted into one \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[One cell or many: two ways to be alive]\label{rem:g6:cell-unit-of-life:twoways}
A \omterm{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:unicellular}{unicellular} organism does everything with one \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}; your
body does everything with tens of thousands of billions,
\emph{specialized} --- cheek \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} for lining, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}
that contract (\cref{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}),
\omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nerve} \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} that carry
\cref{prop:g5:brain-in-command:loop}'s messages. Many \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}
allow division of labor; one \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} means every trade in one
room. Both plans work: the \omterm{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:unicellular}{unicellulars} are the most
numerous \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living things} on Earth.
\end{remark}

\section{The cell theory}

\begin{proposition}[The cell theory]\label{prop:g6:cell-unit-of-life:theory}
Two sentences, centuries in the making, sum up this
chapter and \cref{ch:g5:first-look-at-cells}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item every \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living thing} is made of one or more \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells},
        the smallest units of life;
  \item every \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} comes from the division of an existing
        \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}.
\end{enumerate}
No exception has ever been found --- not in the deepest
sea, not in the oldest rock pool. The second sentence has a
sweeping consequence: every \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} alive today continues an
unbroken chain of divisions reaching back to the earliest
life --- \omterm{prop:g6:classification-kinship:kinship}{kinship} (\cref{prop:g6:classification-kinship:kinship})
written at the \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}'s scale.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{method}[Reading any organism at cell scale]\label{met:g6:cell-unit-of-life:read}
Faced with any \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living thing}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item ask first: one \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}, or many?
  \item if many: expect specialized kinds --- lining,
        carrying, contracting, commanding;
  \item \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} or \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}? check for walls and green grains
        at the \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:microscope}{microscope};
  \item every growth, repair or reproduction you observe:
        translate it into divisions of \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} --- sentence 2
        guarantees the translation exists.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{example}[The theory at work in this book]\label{ex:g6:cell-unit-of-life:atwork}
Sentence 1 underwrote the \omterm{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:principle}{unity of life}
(\cref{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:principle}); sentence 2
underwrote growth-by-division
(\cref{ex:g5:first-look-at-cells:one}), the \omterm{ex:g6:producing-our-food:bread}{yeast} doubling
in the dough, \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers} multiplying in the warm heap,
and the microbe workforces of
\cref{ch:g6:producing-our-food}. One small theory, carrying
a large share of this year's \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{biology}.
\end{example}

\section{Problem: The Microscopy Afternoon}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- four slides, one
theory}]\label{pb:g6:cell-unit-of-life:1}
The class's microscopy afternoon: four stations, four
slides --- onion \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} (A), cheek scrape (B), a drop of
pond water (C), a smear of yogurt (D). Each team tours all
four and keeps drawings.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Stations A and B.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item At station A the team draws straight-walled
        ``bricks'', each with a dark spot squeezed to one
        side. Name the wall, the spot, and the crowding
        culprit.
  \item At station B: rounded \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}, no walls, spots
        central. List the equipment A's \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} have that
        B's lack --- and the kit both share.
  \item A teammate asks why the onion \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}, from a \omterm{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}{bulb}
        grown underground, show no green grains. Answer
        with the grains' job and the \omterm{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}{bulb}'s address.
  \item Whose \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} are on slide B? State what that
        implies by \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} theory sentence 2: where did each
        of those \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} come from?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Stations C and D.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Station C: the slipper-shaped rower, green
        swimmers, the flowing blob. For each, name the
        life activities visible in one minute's watching
        --- and conclude what each one is.
  \item The green swimmers are single \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} yet
        \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}. Which piece of equipment must the
        drawing show to prove it?
  \item Station D's yogurt smear shows tiny paired and
        chained \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} in vast numbers. Connect them to
        \cref{ex:g6:producing-our-food:yogurt}: what did
        these \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} do to the \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk}, and what are they, in
        this chapter's vocabulary?
  \item One team claims slide D shows ``no \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living
        things}, just specks''. What single observation
        --- given patience or a warm slide --- would
        settle whether the specks live, by \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} theory
        sentence 2?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- Writing up.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The write-up must sort all four slides by: one
        \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} or many? \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant}, \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} or neither? Do it.
  \item Every drawing of the afternoon shows the same
        three-part kit. State the kit, and what its
        universality is evidence of
        (\cref{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:principle}).
  \item The teacher closes: ``everything you drew today
        was once one \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}.'' Defend the sentence for the
        onion, the cheek \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}' owner, and the yogurt
        chains.
  \item Formulate the afternoon's finding as the \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}
        theory --- two sentences --- and mark, for each
        slide, which sentence it illustrated best.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
