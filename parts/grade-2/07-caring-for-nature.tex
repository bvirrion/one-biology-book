\chapter{Caring for Nature}\label{ch:g2:caring-for-nature}

After the picnic, one family \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} the meadow exactly as it found
it; another \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} bags, bottle caps and a trampled anthill. The
meadow's rabbits, ants and daisies cannot ask us to be careful ---
but they are \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} sharing our places, and how we behave
in \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{nature} matters to them. Caring for \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{nature} is something anyone
can learn, at any age.

\section{Nature is where living things live}

\begin{definition}[Nature and the environment]\label{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}
\emph{Nature}\index{nature} is the world of \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} and
their surroundings: meadows, forests, ponds, seas --- and the air,
water and soil they depend on. The surroundings a \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living thing}
depends on are called its \emph{environment}\index{environment}.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Everything is somebody's home]\label{ex:g2:caring-for-nature:home}
A rotting log in the forest looks like rubbish --- it is a palace:
beetles under the bark, woodlice beneath, moss on top, a hedgehog
wintering in the hollow. Kick it apart for fun and four households
lose their home. In \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{nature}, almost everything is somebody's home,
food, or shelter.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[We are part of it]\label{rem:g2:caring-for-nature:part}
People are \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} too: the air we breathe, the water we
drink and the food we eat all come from \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{nature}. Caring for it is
not only kindness to rabbits --- it is looking after the world we
ourselves live from, as \cref{ch:g2:where-animals-live} said of
every \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}.
\end{remark}

\section{What harms, what helps}

\begin{proposition}[Small actions, real harm]\label{prop:g2:caring-for-nature:harm}
In \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{nature}, harm rarely comes from wanting to be bad --- it comes
from not thinking:
\begin{itemize}
  \item litter stays for years and can trap or poison \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals};
  \item picking every \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{flower} \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} none to make \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seeds} --- and
        none for the bees;
  \item loud shouting and chasing frightens \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} away from
        their food and their young;
  \item trampled banks, overturned stones and broken \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:tree}{branches}
        each undo a small home.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The bottle cap]\label{ex:g2:caring-for-nature:cap}
A dropped plastic cap is nothing to us and stays in the meadow for
many, many years --- long enough for grown-ups to have children
who find the same cap. \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{Nature} has no dustbin lorry: whatever we
leave, stays.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[The visitor's rules]\label{met:g2:caring-for-nature:rules}
Out in \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{nature}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item carry all your litter home --- all of it;
  \item look at \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{flowers}, insects and nests instead of collecting
        them; pick at most a few common \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{flowers}, never all of a
        clump;
  \item put back every stone and log you turn over --- someone
        lives under it;
  \item stay on paths near fragile spots like stream banks;
  \item watch \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} from a distance, quietly, and leave their
        young alone.
\end{enumerate}
Leave the place so that nobody can tell you were there.
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Helping on purpose]\label{ex:g2:caring-for-nature:help}
Caring can go beyond not harming. A saucer of water in a heat
wave, a small wild corner of the garden left unmowed, \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seeds} for
the birds in the cold, a school pond like the one of
\cref{ex:g2:where-animals-live:schoolpond}: each one gives some
\omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} food, water or a home they had lost.
\end{example}

\section{Sorting and saving}

\begin{proposition}[Waste can be given back]\label{prop:g2:caring-for-nature:recycle}
Much of what we throw away can be used again.
\emph{Sorting}\index{sorting waste} waste --- glass with glass,
paper with paper, peelings to the compost --- lets bottles become
new bottles and peelings become soil, instead of everything piling
up. And what is \emph{saved} --- water, paper, electricity ---
never becomes waste at all.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The compost bin]\label{ex:g2:caring-for-nature:compost}
Apple cores and carrot peelings, in a compost bin, turn slowly
into dark crumbly soil --- with the help of worms, woodlice and
\omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} too small to see. Next spring, that soil feeds the
tomatoes. Nothing was wasted; the peelings went back into the
cycle of life.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Small hands count]\label{rem:g2:caring-for-nature:count}
Turning off the tap while brushing teeth, both sides of the paper,
the compost bin, the litter carried home: each is small, and
millions of people do them --- or don't. \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{Nature} is cared for, or
harmed, mostly in small everyday choices like yours.
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:caring-for-nature:1}
What is a \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living thing}'s \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{environment}? Name three things in yours
that come from \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{nature}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:caring-for-nature:2}
Who can lose their home when a rotting log is kicked apart? Name
three inhabitants from \cref{ex:g2:caring-for-nature:home}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:caring-for-nature:3}
Give four visitor's rules of \cref{met:g2:caring-for-nature:rules}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:caring-for-nature:4}
Why must litter go home with us? Answer with the bottle cap of
\cref{ex:g2:caring-for-nature:cap}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:caring-for-nature:5}
Why should we not pick all the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{flowers} of a clump? Give the two
reasons of \cref{prop:g2:caring-for-nature:harm}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:caring-for-nature:6}
What happens to apple cores in a compost bin? Who does the work,
and what do we get at the end?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:caring-for-nature:7}
Give two ways of helping \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{nature} on purpose, from
\cref{ex:g2:caring-for-nature:help}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:caring-for-nature:8}
Try it: on your next walk, count the pieces of litter you see
(without touching dangerous ones). Which kind is most common?
Where should each kind have gone?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g2:caring-for-nature:9}
At the pond, Emma wants to take three \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}{tadpoles} home in a jar ``to
help them grow safely''. Using this chapter and
\cref{exo:g2:where-animals-live:10}, what would you advise
instead?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g2:caring-for-nature:10}
``One bottle cap is nothing.'' Use
\cref{rem:g2:caring-for-nature:count} to answer: what happens
when a million visitors each think the same sentence --- in the
bad direction, and in the good one?
\end{exercise}
