\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g12:respiration-fermentation} --- Cellular Respiration and Fermentation}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:1}
A \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:nucleotide}{nucleotide} with three phosphates in a row, the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell}'s energy
currency. Using it removes the last phosphate, releasing about
\qty{30}{kJ} per mole to drive a process, and leaves ADP to be
recharged.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:2}
\omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{Glycolysis} in the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cytoplasm}: 2 \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} and 2 reduced NAD, two pyruvates.
\omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:mitochondrion}{Krebs cycle} in the mitochondrial matrix: carbon dioxide, loaded
carriers, 2 \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP}. Respiratory chain in the inner membrane: carriers
unloaded onto oxygen, water, about 26 \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:3}
Two membranes, the inner one folded into cristae, enclosing the
matrix. Matrix: the \omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:mitochondrion}{Krebs cycle}. Inner membrane: the respiratory chain
and \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} synthesis.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:4}
It unloads the reduced NAD of \omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{glycolysis} onto pyruvate, so that
\omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{glycolysis} can continue without oxygen. Yield: the 2 \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} of \omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{glycolysis}
only, plus lactate or ethanol and $\mathrm{CO_2}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:5}
About 30 \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} against 2. Respiration leaves carbon dioxide and water;
\omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation} leaves lactate, or ethanol and carbon dioxide, still rich
in energy.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:6}
Glucose: no effect --- \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{mitochondria} cannot use it. Pyruvate: oxygen
consumption starts --- the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{mitochondrion}'s \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{substrate}. ADP: consumption
speeds up --- oxygen use is coupled to \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} synthesis. Cyanide:
consumption stops --- the respiratory chain is the site of oxygen use.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:7}
\omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{Glycolysis}, which converts glucose to pyruvate, takes place in the
\omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cytoplasm}; the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{mitochondrion} lacks its \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzymes} and takes in pyruvate,
not glucose.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:8}
Each glucose loads two NAD; the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} has only a small stock of NAD,
and once all of it is loaded \omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{glycolysis} has no carrier to hand its
hydrogen to and halts. \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{Fermentation} unloads the NAD onto pyruvate and
keeps the pathway running.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:9}
Respiration: $60/30 = 2$ glucose per second. \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{Fermentation}: $60/2 = 30$
glucose per second, fifteen times more.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:10}
Cyanide blocks the respiratory chain, so no electrons reach oxygen and
about 26 of the 30 \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} per glucose are lost at once; \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}
cannot cover the shortfall. The brain and heart use \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} fastest and
have the smallest reserves, so they fail within minutes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:11}
The carbon dioxide is released in the mitochondrial matrix, by the
\omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:mitochondrion}{Krebs cycle} (and the step that admits pyruvate to it): none in
\omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{glycolysis}. The water is made at the end of the respiratory chain,
when electrons and protons join oxygen.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:12}
The muscle got 2 \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} per glucose fermented; rebuilding that glucose
from lactate costs the liver about 6 \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP}, paid by respiring other
fuel. The body has spent 6 to get 2: \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation} borrows energy that
must be repaid with interest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:13}
In air respiration gives 30 \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} per glucose, so little glucose is
needed and none is diverted to ethanol; sealed, \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation} gives 2,
so fifteen times more glucose is consumed and ethanol is made. Dough
rises on carbon dioxide, which both routes produce (respiration six
per glucose, \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation} two); in the dense dough oxygen runs out
quickly anyway.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:14}
Heat: the energy of the chain, no longer captured as \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP}, is released
directly. A newborn loses heat fast and cannot shiver well; brown fat
is a built-in heater.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g12:respiration-fermentation:15}
Electrons: \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:autotrophy}{photosynthesis} takes them from water and puts them into
sugar; respiration takes them from sugar and gives them to oxygen.
Gases: \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:autotrophy}{photosynthesis} consumes $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and releases
$\mathrm{O_2}$; respiration the reverse. Energy: \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:autotrophy}{photosynthesis} takes
light in and stores it in sugar; respiration takes it out of sugar as
\omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} and heat. \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{Organelles}: \omterm{def:g12:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{chloroplast} and \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{mitochondrion}. Each consumes
what the other produces: \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:autotrophy}{photosynthesis} would exhaust the
$\mathrm{CO_2}$ and respiration the $\mathrm{O_2}$ and the food.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g12:respiration-fermentation:1}
\textbf{1.} A third of \qty{1000}{W} is \qty{300}{W}: \qty{18}{kJ} per
minute, i.e. \qty{0.6}{mol} of \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} per minute.

\textbf{2.} About \qty{300}{g} per minute --- against a few grams held:
the stock is recycled continuously.

\textbf{3.} \qty{50}{g} is \qty{0.1}{mol}: each molecule is recharged
about 6 times a minute.

\textbf{4.} $0.6/30 = \qty{0.02}{mol}$ of glucose per minute (about
\qty{3.6}{g}).

\textbf{5.} $6 \times 0.02 = \qty{0.12}{mol}$ of $\mathrm{O_2}$, about
\qty{2.9}{L} per minute --- below her \qty{4}{L/min} maximum, and
consistent with the \qty{20}{kJ} per litre of oxygen of
\cref{ch:g10:exercise-and-energy}: the \qty{1000}{W} can be sustained
by respiration.

\textbf{6.} 2 in the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cytoplasm}, 28 in the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{mitochondrion}: about 93\%
of the \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP}.

\textbf{7.} $6 \times 0.02 = \qty{0.12}{mol}$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ per
minute, all from the \omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:mitochondrion}{Krebs cycle} and pyruvate entry in the matrix.

\textbf{8.} $30 \times 30 = \qty{900}{kJ}$ of the \qty{2870}{kJ}, about
a third; the rest is heat, which she sheds by sweating and by the
blood flow to her skin.

\textbf{9.} The chain's \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{proteins} and the ATP-making \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzymes} sit in the
membrane; the rate of \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} synthesis is proportional to the area that
holds them.

\textbf{10.} It raises the rate at which respiration can make \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP}
(and the share of fat she can burn), hence the power she can sustain
without \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}; it does not change the yield per glucose or the
oxygen per glucose.

\textbf{11.} $800 \times 30 = \qty{24}{kJ}$: \qty{0.8}{mol} of \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP}.

\textbf{12.} Respiration: $400 \times 30 = \qty{12}{kJ}$, i.e.
\qty{0.4}{mol}; \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation} must supply the other \qty{0.4}{mol}.

\textbf{13.} $0.4/2 = \qty{0.2}{mol}$ of glucose, leaving \qty{0.4}{mol}
of lactate.

\textbf{14.} Respiration: $1/30$ glucose per \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP}; \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}: $1/2$
--- fifteen times more glucose. The muscle accepts it because \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} is
needed now and \omterm{def:g10:exercise-and-energy:glycogen}{glycogen} is there; the debt is repaid later.

\textbf{15.} The lactate must be oxidised or rebuilt into glucose,
which costs \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} made by respiration: oxygen consumption stays above
rest until the debt is cleared.

\textbf{16.} \qty{500}{W} for an hour is \qty{1800}{kJ}: $1800/38
\approx \qty{47}{g}$ of fat.

\textbf{17.} Fat is more reduced --- richer in hydrogen --- so each
gram needs more oxygen to be fully oxidised; per litre of oxygen it
yields slightly less energy than glucose.

\textbf{18.} \omterm{prop:g12:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{Glycolysis} and its \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}: fat cannot be fermented,
so a sprint, which runs on \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}, cannot use fat at all --- only
glucose and \omterm{def:g10:exercise-and-energy:glycogen}{glycogen}.

\textbf{19.} The liver releases glucose from its \omterm{def:g10:exercise-and-energy:glycogen}{glycogen} and rebuilds
some from lactate, keeping the blood glucose near \qty{1}{g/L}
(\cref{ch:g12:glucose-and-diabetes}); the brain's supply is
protected.

\textbf{20.} About \qty{0.6}{mol} of \omterm{def:g12:respiration-fermentation:atp}{ATP} per minute; about
\qty{3}{L} of oxygen per minute pays for them; the respiratory chain
of the mitochondrial inner membrane makes some nine tenths of them.
\end{solution}
