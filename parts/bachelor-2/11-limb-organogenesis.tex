\chapter{Organogenesis: Building the Tetrapod Limb}\label{ch:b2:limb-organogenesis}

Three days after a hen's egg is laid, a bump the size of a pinhead
appears on each flank of the embryo. Four days later it is a wing,
with a humerus, a radius and ulna, a wrist and three digits, in the
right order, of the right lengths, with muscles attached to the
right bones. Cut off the bump's rim of thickened skin and the wing
stops growing; graft a sliver of its rear margin onto its front and
it grows six digits in mirror image. The \omterm{def:b2:limb-organogenesis:bud}{limb bud} is the classic
case of \emph{organogenesis} --- the building of an organ from a
field of cells by signals that say how far out, how far forward and
how far up each cell is --- and the experiments that decoded it are
among the clearest in biology. This chapter follows the bud from
its outgrowth to its skeleton.

\section{The bud and its three axes}

\begin{definition}[The limb bud]\label{def:b2:limb-organogenesis:bud}
\index{limb bud}\index{apical ectodermal ridge}\index{zone of polarising activity}
The \emph{limb bud} is a swelling of \emph{lateral plate mesoderm}
covered by ectoderm, arising at a fixed position along the flank
(the \emph{limb field}) where the Hox code of the body axis permits
it; its mesoderm will make bone, cartilage, tendon and dermis, and
cells that migrate into it from the somites will make the muscles.
The bud has three axes, each governed by a signalling centre.
\emph{Proximo-distal} (shoulder to fingertip): the \emph{apical
ectodermal ridge} (AER), a thickened strip of ectoderm along the
tip, secretes \omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:aer}{fibroblast growth factors} (FGFs) that keep the
mesoderm beneath it dividing and undifferentiated.
\emph{Antero-posterior} (thumb to little finger): the \emph{zone of
polarising activity} (ZPA), a block of mesoderm at the posterior
margin, secretes the morphogen \emph{\omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:zpa}{Sonic hedgehog}} (Shh).
\emph{Dorso-ventral} (back of the hand to palm): the dorsal
ectoderm secretes Wnt7a. Forelimb and hindlimb buds look alike and
use the same signals; they differ in a transcription factor
expressed before the bud appears (Tbx5 in the forelimb, Tbx4 in the
hindlimb), which is why a wing is a wing.
\end{definition}

\section{Outgrowth: the proximo-distal axis}

\begin{proposition}[The apical ectodermal ridge]\label{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:aer}
\index{fibroblast growth factor}\index{progress zone}
The AER is required for outgrowth and its FGFs are sufficient: the
mesoderm under it, the \emph{\omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:aer}{progress zone}}, divides every twelve
hours or so, and cells that leave the zone as the tip advances stop
dividing, condense and differentiate. The skeleton forms in
proximo-distal order --- \emph{stylopod} (humerus or femur),
\emph{zeugopod} (radius and ulna, tibia and fibula), \emph{autopod}
(wrist and digits) --- each segment specified in turn as the bud
grows, and the segments correspond to nested domains of Hox gene
expression (Hoxa/d 9--10 in the stylopod, 11 in the zeugopod, 13 in
the autopod). A \omterm{def:b2:limb-organogenesis:bud}{limb bud} grows some tenfold in length in four days
while the reach of the ridge's signal stays the same few hundred
micrometres, so that the ridge patterns the limb not all at once but
as a moving front, the way a printer lays down a page line by line.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Evidence]
Saunders (1948) removed the AER from chick wing buds at successive
stages: removed early, the bud yielded only a stump of humerus;
removed a day later, humerus and forearm but no hand; removed later
still, a nearly complete wing --- the later the operation, the more
distal the level of truncation, so the ridge is needed for each
segment in turn. Grafting a second ridge produced a second
outgrowth; a bead soaked in FGF, placed on a bud whose ridge had
been removed, restored a complete limb (Niswander, 1993), and a bead
placed on the flank between the limb fields induced an extra limb.
The mice lacking the ridge FGFs have no limbs.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Growth by division]\label{thm:b2:limb-organogenesis:growth}
A bud whose cells divide every $T$ hours multiplies its cell number
by $2^{t/T}$ in a time $t$; four days of twelve-hour cycles give
$2^{8} = 256$. If the bud's volume grows a thousandfold in those
four days, division supplies a factor of 256 and the remaining
factor of four must come from cell enlargement and the matrix
that cartilage cells secrete around themselves. Conversely, a
segment that takes one cell cycle to specify --- the roughly twelve
hours by which the successive truncation levels of Saunders'
experiment are separated --- corresponds to one doubling of the
\omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:aer}{progress zone}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$N(t) = N_0 2^{t/T}$; with $t = \qty{96}{h}$ and $T = \qty{12}{h}$,
$t/T = 8$. The ratio of the required factor to the division factor,
$1000/256 \approx 4$, is what growth other than division must
provide.
\end{proof}

\section{Digits: the antero-posterior axis}

\begin{proposition}[The zone of polarising activity]\label{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:zpa}
\index{Sonic hedgehog}\index{morphogen!Shh}
The ZPA specifies which digit forms where. Its cells secrete \omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:zpa}{Sonic
hedgehog}, which spreads across the bud from the posterior margin,
and mesoderm cells read the concentration --- and the time they have
been exposed to it --- as a position: the highest dose gives the
most posterior digit, lower doses more anterior ones, and below a
threshold no digit at all. The gradient has the exponential form of
\cref{ch:b2:vertebrate-development}, with a decay length of a few
tens of micrometres, and its thresholds cut the bud into the
primordia of the digits: in the chick wing, from posterior to
anterior, digits 4, 3 and 2. A second source of the signal at the
anterior margin produces a second, mirror-image set; a weaker
source, or a shorter exposure, produces only the anterior digits of
the extra set. The same gene, in the same role, patterns the digits
of every tetrapod, and a \omterm{def:b2:genome-diversification:mutation}{mutation} that adds an anterior source of
Shh gives polydactyly in cats, chickens, mice and people.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Evidence]
Saunders and Gasseling (1968) grafted a block of posterior-margin
mesoderm from one chick wing bud to the anterior margin of another:
the host grew a wing with six digits in the order 4-3-2-2-3-4, a
mirror image about the middle. Tickle (1975) showed that the number
of extra digits depended on the number of grafted cells --- a dose.
Riddle and Tabin (1993) found that the grafted tissue expressed the
gene \emph{\omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:zpa}{Sonic hedgehog}}, that the gene was expressed nowhere else
in the bud, and that cells engineered to express it, grafted
anteriorly, reproduced the mirror duplication; a bead of the protein
did the same. Chick mutants with an anterior ectopic patch of Shh
have extra anterior digits.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Digit boundaries from the gradient]\label{thm:b2:limb-organogenesis:thresholds}
With Shh at concentration $c_0$ at the posterior margin and a decay
length $\lambda$, the boundary of the territory of a digit whose
threshold is $T_i$ lies at $x_i = \lambda\ln(c_0/T_i)$ from the
margin. With $\lambda = \qty{50}{\micro m}$ and thresholds of $0.6$,
$0.25$ and $0.08$ of $c_0$ for digits 4, 3 and 2, the territories
are $0$--$26$, $26$--$69$ and $69$--$126$ micrometres, and the
tissue beyond \qty{126}{\micro m} forms no digit. Doubling the source
moves every boundary outward by $\lambda\ln 2 = \qty{35}{\micro m}$
--- enough, in a bud of a few hundred micrometres, to add a digit at
the anterior edge; a second source at the anterior margin lays down
the same territories in reverse.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From $c(x) = c_0 e^{-x/\lambda}$, $c = T_i$ at $x_i = \lambda
\ln(c_0/T_i)$: $50\ln(1/0.6) = 25.5$, $50\ln 4 = 69$, $50\ln 12.5 =
126$. Replacing $c_0$ by $2c_0$ adds $\lambda\ln 2$ to each $x_i$.
\end{proof}

\section{Coordination and sculpting}

\begin{proposition}[The axes talk to each other]\label{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:loop}
\index{feedback loop!developmental}
The centres maintain one another: FGF from the ridge keeps the ZPA
expressing Shh, and Shh, through a relay in the mesoderm, keeps the
ridge expressing FGF; remove either and the other fades within a
day. Wnt7a from the dorsal ectoderm is needed for full Shh
expression as well, and it drives the dorsal fate of the mesoderm
(a mouse without it has two palms). This mutual dependence is a
\emph{positive feedback loop} that locks the three axes together:
the limb grows only where it is being patterned, and is patterned
only where it is growing, so that a bud never makes a hand without
an arm or digits of the wrong sort. When outgrowth is complete the
loop is broken --- the mesoderm stops relaying, Shh stops, the ridge
regresses --- and the limb stops growing at its proper size.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[From pattern to bone]\label{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:skeleton}
\index{chondrogenesis}\index{apoptosis!interdigital}
Once specified, mesoderm cells leaving the \omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:aer}{progress zone}
\emph{condense} into rods and plates the shape of the future bones,
differentiate into cartilage cells that secrete a matrix rich in
collagen and proteoglycans, and lay down a cartilage model of each
skeletal element; joints form where a band of cells is told not to
become cartilage; blood vessels invade the models and bone replaces
cartilage from the centre outward, leaving growth plates at the
ends that keep the bone lengthening until adulthood
(\emph{endochondral ossification}). Muscle precursors from the
somites migrate in, divide, fuse into fibres
(\cref{ch:b2:cell-differentiation}) and attach through tendons made
by the limb's own mesoderm. Between the digit rays, the tissue is
removed by programmed cell death: the cells of the interdigital
webs, told by a bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) signal, activate
their own destruction and are cleared in a day, which separates the
fingers. A duck keeps its webs because a BMP inhibitor is expressed
in its interdigital tissue; a chick given the same inhibitor on a
bead grows webbed feet.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Evidence]
Interdigital mesoderm cut from a chick leg bud and grafted
elsewhere dies on schedule --- the death is intrinsic, decided
before the tissue is removed; the same tissue from a duck survives.
Beads of BMP placed between the toes of a duck cause the web to die;
beads of the inhibitor gremlin placed between the toes of a chick
save it. A mouse lacking a BMP receptor in the limb keeps its webs.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The same limb, differently grown]\label{ex:b2:limb-organogenesis:evolution}
The bat's wing is a hand whose digits 2 to 5 grew several times
longer than a mouse's --- an enhancer of a limb gene, transplanted
from bat to mouse, lengthens the mouse's forelimb bones --- and
whose interdigital tissue survived, because the web expresses the
BMP inhibitor the duck uses. The whale's flipper is a hand with
extra phalanges and no interdigital death. The horse's leg is a
single digit with the others reduced to splints. The snake has no
limbs because its flank never expresses the signals that start a
bud. And the fins of fish, which lack an autopod, are built by the
same AER and ZPA but stop their Hox programme before the late phase
that makes digits; the fossil \emph{Tiktaalik} (375 million years
old) shows the wrist appearing in a fin. Limb evolution is mostly
the modulation of a conserved programme --- how long each phase
runs, how far each signal reaches, which cells die.
\end{example}

\section{Exercises}

\end{exercise}

\end{exercise}

List, in order, the steps from a patch of lateral plate mesoderm

\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:b2:limb-organogenesis:4}
What does the limb look like if the AER is removed (a) as soon as

at all but replaced by a bead of FGF?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:limb-organogenesis:5}
With $\lambda = \qty{40}{\micro m}$ and thresholds of $0.5$, $0.2$

\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:limb-organogenesis:6}
Predict the digit pattern of a chick wing after (a) a full ZPA
grafted to the anterior margin, (b) a graft of a quarter as many

\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:limb-organogenesis:7}
A chick \omterm{def:b2:limb-organogenesis:bud}{limb bud} has \num{5000} cells when the ridge forms and its
cells divide every \qty{12}{h}. How many cells after four days? If
the limb at that stage has $4\times 10^{6}$ cells, what must be
true of the cell cycle or of cell death?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:limb-organogenesis:8}
Explain why a chick has free toes and a duck webbed feet, and what a
bead of BMP inhibitor between the toes of a chick embryo does. What
does the intrinsic timing of interdigital death (it happens on
schedule in a graft) tell you about how the cells were instructed?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:limb-organogenesis:9}
A mouse lacks both Hoxa13 and Hoxd13; another lacks Hoxa11 and
Hoxd11. Predict the skeleton of each forelimb and say what the
result shows about the relation between Hox domains and segments.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:limb-organogenesis:10}
Explain the positive feedback between the AER and the ZPA, why it
makes the limb robust, and what would happen to a bud in which Shh
could not maintain FGF. Contrast it with the positive feedback of
labour in \cref{ch:b2:mammal-reproduction}: what ends each loop?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:limb-organogenesis:11}
A bat's third digit is eight times longer than a mouse's relative
to body size. If digit cartilage lengthens exponentially at rate
$r$ per day during a growth window, and the bat's window is four
days longer than the mouse's, what $r$ is needed? Give two
developmental changes (one in growth, one in cell death) that make
a wing out of a hand.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:b2:limb-organogenesis:12}
\omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:zpa}{Sonic hedgehog} patterns the \omterm{prop:b2:vertebrate-development:neurulation}{neural tube} from the floor plate
(\cref{ch:b2:vertebrate-development}) and the digits from the ZPA.
Discuss why evolution reuses a few signals for many jobs, how the

what this implies for the effect of a \omterm{def:b2:genome-diversification:mutation}{mutation} in such a gene.
\end{exercise}

\section{Problem: A Wing Built in a Week}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- the chick wing followed from bud
to skeleton: its growth by division, Saunders' ridge removals timed,
the Shh gradient computed and its duplications predicted, the webs
sculpted and the wing compared with a bat's, ending on the number of
doublings, the digit boundaries and the width a bud needs for a
mirror duplication}]
\label{pb:b2:limb-organogenesis:1}
A chick wing bud appears at 3 days of incubation with \num{2000}
mesoderm cells and a length of \qty{0.4}{mm}; by 7 days the wing is
\qty{4}{mm} long and its volume is \num{1000} times the bud's. \omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:aer}{Progress
zone} cells divide every \qty{12}{h}. Ridge removals: at 3.0 days the
limb is a stump; at 3.5 days a humerus only; at 4.0 days humerus,
radius and ulna; at 4.5 days a wing lacking only the digit tips.
Shh: $D = \qty{1e-12}{m^2/s}$, half-life \qty{29}{min}; thresholds
$0.6$, $0.25$, $0.08$ of $c_0$ for digits 4, 3, 2. Bud width (posterior
to anterior) \qty{600}{\micro m}. Interdigital webs: three regions
of \qty{300}{\micro m}$\times$\qty{300}{\micro m}$\times$\qty{200}{\micro m}, cells
of \qty{1000}{\micro m^3}, cleared in \qty{24}{h}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Growth.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item How many doublings occur in four days? How many cells does
        that give from \num{2000}?
  \item Compare with the thousandfold volume increase: what factor
        must cell enlargement and matrix provide?
  \item By what factor did the length grow? If the bud grew as a
        cylinder of constant radius, what volume factor would that
        be, and what does the difference say about the shape?
  \item The ridge's FGF reaches \qty{300}{\micro m} into the mesoderm.
        What fraction of the bud is under its influence at 3 days
        and at 7 days?
  \item Explain why a signal of fixed reach in a growing organ
        produces a proximo-distal sequence.
  \item A cell leaves the \omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:aer}{progress zone} at 4 days. Which segment does
        it join?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Timing the segments.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item From the removal series, give the time by which each segment
        (stylopod, zeugopod, autopod) has been specified.
  \item How many cell cycles separate the specification of
        successive segments?
  \item Explain the result in terms of the \omterm{prop:b2:limb-organogenesis:aer}{progress zone} and the
        Hox domains.
  \item An FGF bead is placed on a bud whose ridge was removed at 3.5
        days. Predict the limb.
  \item A second ridge is grafted onto the dorsal surface of an
        intact bud at 3.5 days. Predict.
  \item Why does the bud stop growing at 7 days rather than
        continuing indefinitely?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The gradient.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Compute $k$ and $\lambda$ for Shh.
  \item Compute the three digit boundaries and territory widths.
  \item How wide is the anterior region that forms no digit in a
        \qty{600}{\micro m} bud?
  \item A \omterm{def:b2:genome-diversification:mutation}{mutation} doubles Shh output. Recompute the boundaries and
        say whether an extra digit forms and which.
  \item A ZPA is grafted to the anterior margin. What minimum bud
        width allows a complete mirror set 4-3-2-2-3-4 without
        overlap of the two digit-2 territories? Is the chick bud wide
        enough?
  \item Predict the pattern if the bud were only \qty{200}{\micro m}
        wide.
  \item Why does a graft of a quarter of the ZPA give only 2-2-3-4?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part IV --- Sculpting and evolution.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Compute the number of cells in the three webs and the rate
        of cell death during clearance, in cells per second.
  \item A duck's interdigital cells express a BMP inhibitor. Predict
        the effect of a bead of BMP in a duck web and of the
        inhibitor in a chick web.
  \item A bat's digit cartilage grows for four extra days at an
        exponential rate of \qty{0.5}{d^{-1}}. By what factor is it
        longer than the chick's? Name a second change that makes the
        wing membrane.
  \item A fish fin bud has an AER and a ZPA but no digits. What is
        missing, and what does \emph{Tiktaalik} show?
  \item If the web cells had instead been produced by division from
        \num{1000} founders at \qty{12}{h} per cycle, how long would
        making them have taken? Compare with the day it takes to
        remove them.
  \item State the result: the doublings in four days, the three digit
        boundaries, and the minimum width for a mirror duplication.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
