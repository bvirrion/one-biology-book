\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:sensory-systems} --- Sensory Systems}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:1}
By the wire, not the message: each sensory nerve is a \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:principles}{labelled line}
ending in its own region of the brain, and whatever arrives along the
optic nerve is read as light --- which is why pressure on the eyeball
produces a flash and a blow to the ear a ring.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:2}
In the dark, cyclic GMP holds cation channels open and a steady inward
``dark current'' keeps the cell depolarised and releasing glutamate.
Light activates \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{rhodopsin}, transducin and phosphodiesterase, which
destroys cGMP; the channels close, the inward current stops, and the
cell hyperpolarises and releases less --- light is signalled by a
decrease.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:3}
$I = I_{0}10^{L/10}$: \qty{20}{dB}, \qty{1e-10}{W/m^2}; \qty{60}{dB},
\qty{1e-6}{W/m^2}; \qty{110}{dB}, \qty{0.1}{W/m^2}. Loudest to softest:
$10^{9}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:4}
Sweet, umami and bitter through G-protein-coupled receptors (one
sweet, one umami, about twenty-five bitter); salt through the sodium
channel ENaC; sour through a proton channel. Most of ``flavour'' is
the smell of volatile molecules reaching the nose from the mouth; with
the nose blocked only the five \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:chemical}{tastes} remain and food seems bland.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:5}
Fechner: $S = (1/0.02)\ln(10\,000/100) = 50\ln 100 \approx 230$ steps.
Counting: $1.02^{n} = 100$ gives $n = \ln 100/\ln 1.02 = 233$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:6}
$P = 1 - e^{-a}\sum_{k=0}^{5}a^{k}/k!$: $a = 2$: $0.017$; $a = 6$:
$0.55$; $a = 12$: $0.98$. Half the flashes are seen at $a \approx 5.7$;
with \qty{10}{\%} absorption the flash must deliver about $57$
photons at the cornea.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:7}
$55/3\times 1.3 \approx 24$; in \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:cochlea}{decibels} of pressure, $20\log_{10}24
\approx \qty{28}{dB}$. Fused bones cannot transmit the vibration, so
the sound reaches the \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:cochlea}{cochlea} only by conduction through the skull,
without the middle ear's gain: a conductive loss of some
\qtyrange{30}{50}{dB}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:8}
The \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:cochlea}{hair cell}'s channel is opened directly by the \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:cochlea}{tip link} pulling on
it --- no messenger, no enzyme --- so it responds in microseconds; the
photoreceptor amplifies one photon through two enzymatic stages, which
take tens of milliseconds. Hearing gains the timing precision needed to
locate sounds by interaural delays and to follow phase; vision gains
the sensitivity to count single photons, and pays in speed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:9}
$H(0.05) = -0.05\ln 0.05 - 0.95\ln 0.95 = 0.150 + 0.049 = 0.199$.
Human: $\ln\binom{400}{20} \approx 400\times 0.199 = 80$, about
$10^{34}$ patterns. Mouse: $\ln\binom{1100}{55} \approx 1100\times 0.199
= 219$, about $10^{95}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:10}
Mean $500\times 0.1/40 = 1.25$ spontaneous events per window. $P(k
\le 5) = e^{-1.25}(1 + 1.25 + 0.78 + 0.33 + 0.10 + 0.03) = 0.998$, so
$P(k \ge 6) \approx 2\times 10^{-3}$. With a threshold of one, a
spontaneous event would occur in most windows ($1 - e^{-1.25} = 0.71$)
and the dark would be full of flashes; at six, false alarms come once
in five hundred windows. The threshold is set where the noise of the
\omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{rods}, not their sensitivity, dictates.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:11}
Largest delay $0.2/340 = \qty{590}{\micro s}$ (sound from one side).
Near the midline $\Delta t \approx (d/c)\sin\theta$, so $\qty{10}{\micro
s}$ corresponds to $\sin\theta = 0.017$, about \qty{1}{\degree}. Above
\qty{4}{kHz} the period (\qty{250}{\micro s}) is shorter than the
neurons can lock to, and a delay of several hundred microseconds spans
more than one cycle, making the phase ambiguous; the brain then uses
the level difference between the ears and the place code.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:sensory-systems:12}
Far from the edge, $r = I(1 - 2w)$: $0.6$ on the dark side, $1.2$ on the
bright. At the last dark receptor ($I = 1$, neighbours $1$ and $2$): $r
= 1 - 0.2\times 3 = 0.4$; at the first bright one: $r = 2 - 0.6 = 1.4$.
The step is exaggerated into an undershoot and an overshoot --- Mach
bands. The \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{retina} gains contrast and edges, and a smaller dynamic range
to transmit; it loses fidelity to absolute levels, and produces
illusions of brightness.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:sensory-systems:1}
\textbf{1.} $0.01/3.6\times 10^{-19} = 2.8\times 10^{16}$ photons a
second.
\textbf{2.} Pupil area $\pi(3.5\times 10^{-3})^{2} = 3.8\times 10^{-5}$
m$^{2}$. At \qty{1}{km}: fraction $3.8\times 10^{-5}/1.26\times 10^{7} =
3\times 10^{-12}$, $8.5\times 10^{4}$ photons a second. At \qty{10}{km}:
$850$ a second.
\textbf{3.} In \qty{0.1}{s} with \qty{10}{\%} absorbed: $850$ at
\qty{1}{km}, $8.5$ at \qty{10}{km} --- both above six; the candle is
seen at ten kilometres (most of the time).
\textbf{4.} Six absorbed per window needs $600$ photons a second into
the pupil: $4\pi d^{2} = 3.8\times 10^{-5}\times 2.8\times 10^{16}/600
= 1.8\times 10^{9}$ m$^{2}$, $d \approx \qty{12}{km}$. There, $P(k \ge
6 \mid a = 6) = 0.55$: the candle is seen in about half the windows.
\textbf{5.} $e^{-6} = 0.0025$. At threshold the count fluctuates from
window to window --- $6 \pm 2.4$ --- so the source seems to come and go:
the scintillation of a faint star (to which the atmosphere adds its
own).
\textbf{6.} The background's fluctuation, $\sqrt{10^{4}} = 100$ photons
per window, swamps the candle's $8.5$: detection requires the signal to
exceed the noise of the background, not the threshold of the dark eye.
\textbf{7.} $\qty{1}{pA}/200 = \qty{5}{fA}$ per channel; $5\times
10^{-15}/1.6\times 10^{-19} = 3\times 10^{4}$ ions a second.
\textbf{8.} One thirtieth; about thirty simultaneous photons close every
channel and saturate the \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{rod}.
\textbf{9.} $10^{-12}\times 0.2 = 2\times 10^{-13}$ C, $1.3\times 10^{6}$
cations: one photon controls over a million charges.
\textbf{10.} The \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{rod} saturates within milliseconds, every channel shut,
and its \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{rhodopsin} is bleached faster than it is regenerated; \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{cones},
with lower gain and faster shut-off, keep responding and shift their
range across six decades of light.
\textbf{11.} High gain needs long integration (each stage takes time and
the response is prolonged to accumulate), so the \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{rod} is slow and
sensitive; the \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{cone}'s smaller amplification and quicker shut-off give a
response in \qty{20}{ms}, fast enough for movement, at the cost of
needing a hundred photons.
\textbf{12.} $10^{8}/40 = 2.5\times 10^{6}$ spontaneous isomerisations a
second across the \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{retina} --- but only $1.25$ per window in any patch of
$500$ \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:retina}{rods}, far below the threshold of six, so the noise is rejected
patch by patch; the threshold and the pooling exist precisely for this.
\textbf{13.} \qty{0.1}{W/m^2}; on \qty{5.5e-5}{m^2}, \qty{5.5e-6}{W}.
\textbf{14.} $5.5\times 10^{-6}\times 7200 = \qty{0.04}{J}$ --- about four
hundred raindrops' worth over two hours.
\textbf{15.} $10^{-12}\times 5.5\times 10^{-5} = 5.5\times 10^{-17}$ W;
in \qty{10}{ms}, $5.5\times 10^{-19}$ J --- about the energy of one or
two visible photons.
\textbf{16.} $0.3/5000 = 6\times 10^{-5}$ rad, $0.003^{\circ}$; the
deflection is about three hydrogen atoms.
\textbf{17.} $10^{(110 - 85)/10} = 316$: two hours at \qty{110}{dB}
deliver the energy of $630$ hours at \qty{85}{dB} --- nearly a month of
working days.
\textbf{18.} About $120$ just-noticeable steps, one per \omterm{def:b3:sensory-systems:cochlea}{decibel}.
\textbf{19.} $2^{400} = 10^{400\log_{10}2} = 10^{120}$.
\textbf{20.} $\ln\binom{400}{20} \approx 400\,H(0.05) \approx 80$, less a
Stirling correction of about $2$: some $10^{34}$ patterns.
\textbf{21.} Count the receptors in one pattern but not the other: $5 +
5 = 10$ of $40$ (a Hamming distance); patterns that overlap by three
quarters drive nearly the same glomeruli and are read as nearly the
same smell.
\textbf{22.} $10^{12}/10^{34} = 10^{-22}$ of the patterns. The code is not
the limit: receptors are noisy, many are tuned alike so their
activities are correlated, real molecules produce only a small subset of
patterns, and the brain's discrimination and memory of patterns are
finite.
\textbf{23.} $\ln\binom{1100}{20} \approx 1100\,H(0.018) = 1100\times
0.091 \approx 100$, about $10^{43}$ --- some $10^{9}$ times the human
number of twenty-receptor patterns (and far more if the mouse uses
more receptors per odour).
\textbf{24.} General smell hardly changes --- the code is redundant and
other receptors carry every odorant --- but an odorant that depends on
that receptor at low concentration becomes undetectable or changes its
character: a specific anosmia, as with the many people who cannot
smell androstenone.
\textbf{25.} The candle is seen to about \qty{12}{km}; threshold sound
delivers $5.5\times 10^{-19}$ J to the eardrum in \qty{10}{ms}, a photon
or two; a human nose can form some $10^{34}$ twenty-receptor patterns.
\end{solution}
