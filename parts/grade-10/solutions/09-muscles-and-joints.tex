\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g10:muscles-and-joints} --- Muscles and Joints}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:1}
\omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{Cartilage} on the bone ends (smooth sliding, load spreading); capsule
(seals the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint}) with its synovial fluid (lubrication); \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligaments}
(hold the bones together, limit the movements); in the knee, menisci
(spread the load).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:2}
A \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:muscle}{tendon} joins a muscle to a bone and transmits its pull; a \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligament}
joins bone to bone across a \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint} and limits its movement. Neither
contracts.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:3}
A muscle can only pull by shortening; it cannot push. To move a \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint}
both ways, a flexor and an extensor are needed, each undoing what the
other did.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:4}
Sprain: a \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligament} stretched or torn. Muscle tear: \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:muscle}{muscle fibres}
ruptured. Dislocation: a bone end forced out of its \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint}, with the
capsule and \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligaments} stretched or torn.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:5}
Slow fibres: slow, weak, tireless, rich in \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{mitochondria} and
capillaries, red, run on respiration. Fast fibres: fast, powerful,
tire in a minute, few \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{mitochondria}, pale, run on the phosphate store
and \omterm{def:g10:cell-metabolism:fermentation}{fermentation}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:6}
Load $2 \times 9.8 = \qty{19.6}{N}$; biceps force $19.6 \times 32/4
\approx \qty{157}{N}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:7}
A push may come in either direction, and each muscle can resist only
one. Both contracting stiffen the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint} in both directions at once;
whichever way the push comes, one of them is already pulling against
it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:8}
The fibre shortens by \qty{0.6}{cm}; the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:muscle}{tendon} moves the bone by
\qty{0.6}{cm} at \qty{5}{cm} from the elbow, so the hand moves
$0.6 \times 35/5 = \qty{4.2}{cm}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:9}
Mobility requires a shallow socket and loose \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligaments}; the same
looseness lets the head of the humerus leave the socket when a force
pushes it beyond the range the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligaments} can hold.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:10}
Dehydration and salt loss through sweating, and fatigue of the fibres
at the end of the race. Remedy: stretch the muscle gently and drink
water with salt.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:11}
The sprinter's thigh is 70\% fast fibres and the proportion is largely
inherited; training enlarges fibres and improves their equipment but
does not convert their number. The untrained person starts from half
and half and has more slow fibres to develop.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:12}
$4 \times 70 \times 9.8 \approx \qty{2.7}{kN}$. Stress $2744/100
\approx \qty{27}{N/mm^2}$: about a quarter of the failure stress, a
safety factor of four.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:13}
Muscle is richly supplied with blood and contains reserve \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} that
rebuild fibres: weeks. \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{Ligaments} have few vessels, so nutrients and
repair \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} arrive slowly: months. \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{Cartilage} has no blood vessels at
all and almost no dividing \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}; it is fed by the synovial fluid only
and barely repairs.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:14}
Bodybuilding thickens the fibres (more myofibrils in each) and
stretching lengthens the muscle--tendon unit and the capsule's
tolerance. Fibre number is fixed early in life; adult muscle grows or
shrinks by changing the size of existing fibres.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:muscles-and-joints:15}
On stairs the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint} carries several times body weight; thinner
\omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{cartilage} transmits that pressure to the bone beneath, which has
nerves and hurts. With less \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{cartilage} the menisci are the last thing
spreading the load. Strong muscles around the knee absorb part of the
shock and steady the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint}, reducing the peak pressure on what
\omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{cartilage} remains.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g10:muscles-and-joints:1}
\textbf{1.} $70 \times 9.8 = \qty{686}{N}$.

\textbf{2.} $686 \times 10/5 \approx \qty{1.4}{kN}$.

\textbf{3.} $686 \times 20/5 \approx \qty{2.7}{kN}$.

\textbf{4.} Two and four times body weight: on every stair the
quadriceps pulls with the weight of four people, which is why thighs
burn on a long climb.

\textbf{5.} The hamstrings, at the back of the thigh; they relax and
are stretched while the quadriceps straightens the knee, contracting
lightly to steady the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint}.

\textbf{6.} Ground force $3 \times 686 \approx \qty{2.06}{kN}$; \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:muscle}{tendon}
force $2058 \times 20/5 \approx \qty{8.2}{kN}$, twelve times body
weight.

\textbf{7.} $8232/120 \approx \qty{69}{N/mm^2}$: about two thirds of
the failure stress. Each landing uses most of the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:muscle}{tendon}'s margin.

\textbf{8.} Load $8.2 + 2.1 \approx \qty{10.3}{kN}$. With the menisci:
$10300/600 \approx \qty{17}{N/mm^2}$; without: $10300/100 \approx
\qty{103}{N/mm^2}$, six times more.

\textbf{9.} Six times the pressure on the same \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{cartilage} at every
landing: the surface is crushed faster than it can be maintained, and
it wears.

\textbf{10.} A film of fluid between the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{cartilages}, so that they
slide during the bend without dry friction, and a slight cushioning of
the impact.

\textbf{11.} A twist of the tibia under the femur, with a sideways
bend: rotations the knee is not built for, which the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligaments} are
there to resist.

\textbf{12.} The quadriceps pulls only along the leg, straightening the
knee; it has no action against a twist. And the injury happens in a
few hundredths of a second, faster than any muscular response.

\textbf{13.} Blood from the torn \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligament}'s and capsule's vessels,
plus extra synovial fluid: the capsule was breached or inflamed and
the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint} filled.

\textbf{14.} The \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligament}, poorly supplied with blood, needs months
and often surgery; the torn meniscus, \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{cartilage} almost without
vessels, usually does not heal and the torn part is trimmed or
sutured.

\textbf{15.} The hamstrings are the quadriceps' antagonists and pull
the tibia backwards; a strong pair contracting together stiffens the
knee against the forward slide the torn \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligament} no longer prevents,
protecting the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint} before and after surgery.

\textbf{16.} About 4000 times a day at \qty{1.4}{kN} or more.

\textbf{17.} About $\num{1.5e6}$ loadings a year. Tendinitis is damage
accumulating faster than the slow repair of \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:muscle}{tendon} tissue; only rest
lets repair catch up, since strength does not reduce the load of each
step.

\textbf{18.} Landings $40 \times 8.2 \approx \qty{330}{kN}$ in all;
steps $4000 \times 1.4 \approx \qty{5600}{kN}$ --- the steps carry
seventeen times more total load, but the landings carry the peaks that
threaten rupture.

\textbf{19.} Doubling the ground force doubles the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:muscle}{tendon} and \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{joint}
forces at every stride; cycling and swimming work the muscles while
loading the knee far below body weight.

\textbf{20.} The quadriceps \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:muscle}{tendon} carries four times body weight on a
stair and about twelve on a landing; the \omterm{def:g10:muscles-and-joints:joint}{ligaments}, which those loads
never test, were the tissue the twist found.
\end{solution}
