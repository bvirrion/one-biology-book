\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g1:living-nonliving} --- Living and Nonliving Things}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-nonliving:1}
Answers vary. Living examples: a plant, a classmate, a pet, an
insect. Nonliving examples: a book, a chair, a window, a stone.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-nonliving:2}
Nonliving. It does not grow; it does not need food or water; it
cannot have young. (It only moves when a person rides it.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-nonliving:3}
Living: dog, rose, ant. Nonliving: pencil, rain, glass.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-nonliving:4}
False. The photograph is paper and ink (or a screen). It cannot
grow, feed or fly. The real butterfly was living; the picture is
not.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-nonliving:5}
Yes --- a healthy seed is living. It can grow into a plant when it
has water, warmth and air. It came from a living plant and can make
a new plant. It is just resting.
\end{solution}
