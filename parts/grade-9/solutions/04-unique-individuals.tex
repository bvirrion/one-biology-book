\begin{solution}{exo:g9:unique-individuals:12}
For example: one shuffle deals every human a never-repeated
selection from the parental decks, and \omterm{prop:g9:genes-and-dna:explains}{mutation} salts each
generation with spellings never seen at all --- hence all
different. Yet every deck is written in the same four
letters, read by the same code, and agrees with any other
human deck in over $99$ letters of each hundred --- hence
all kin. The mechanisms are not rivals but one system:
common text, endlessly re-dealt --- \omterm{prop:g6:classification-kinship:kinship}{kinship} supplying the
deck, the shuffle guaranteeing no hand is ever repeated.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g9:unique-individuals:1}
\textbf{1.} Because \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:dna}{DNA} lives in the \omterm{def:g6:cell-unit-of-life:parts}{nucleus}, and the
hair's shaft is dead keratin --- the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{root}'s \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} carry the
nuclei a full reading needs.

\textbf{2.} Variable positions are where individuals
differ --- the identifying scatter. The shared positions
would establish only that the hair is human: kinship-level
truth, useless for naming one person.

\textbf{3.} That the hair's \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} and the suspect's share
one deal --- one individual, out of all humans ever, with
the single exception of an identical twin, who shares the
deal by origin.

\textbf{4.} Rule the twin in or out by other evidence ---
whereabouts, witnesses, the case's ``how it got there'':
the profile genuinely cannot separate the pair, so the
court must.

\textbf{5.} A relative shares, by descent, a large
fraction of the variable spellings --- more than a
stranger, fewer than a match: partial overlap graded by
\omterm{prop:g6:classification-kinship:kinship}{kinship} is the dealing run backward up the tree.

\textbf{6.} Level: a profile is deck-level data --- the
variable positions read are chosen precisely for saying
nothing about \omterm{def:g9:heredity-and-traits:traits}{traits}. Weave: temperament and conduct are
ranges lived in lives. Variety: ``criminal \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{genes}'' grades
people against a phantom normal. Twins: shared decks,
different lives. The profile names \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}, not character.

\textbf{7.} The number of independent variable positions:
each added position divides the matching \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population}
further --- enough positions, and ``once in the city''
becomes once in humanity. The lab reads as many as the
standard demands.

\textbf{8.} The \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{biology} asserts whose \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} the hair
grew from. The case must still establish placement ---
when, how, innocently or not, the hair arrived: matching
is not narrative, and courts convict on narratives
proven.

\textbf{9.} The halving-and-fertilization deal makes each
deck unrepeatable, and \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:dna}{DNA}'s spelling differences make
the deal readable --- \omterm{def:g9:chromosomes:chromosomes}{chromosomes} shuffle it, the
molecule records it.

\textbf{10.} On \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:dna}{DNA}'s chemical stability: the text
neither fades nor rewrites in a drawer --- the same
double strand, intact after thirty years, reads as it was
dealt.

\textbf{11.} Serving: identification that clears the
innocent and resolves parentage; transplant matching that
finds the nearest deal. The warned misuse: reading the
\omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species}' variety as ranks --- difference pressed into
hierarchy, the bad \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{biology} behind old injustices.

\textbf{12.} No two \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilizations} have ever dealt the
same deck: the parental half-deck spaces multiplied by
the \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gamete} lottery exceed all humans ever conceived ---
so a full-profile match names one individual, and the
court may treat the hair as signed.
\end{solution}
