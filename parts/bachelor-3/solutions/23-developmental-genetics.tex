\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:developmental-genetics} --- Developmental Genetics and Evolution of Form}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:1}
Maternal-effect: \emph{bicoid}, embryos from mutant mothers lack head
and thorax. Gap: \emph{Kr{\"u}ppel}, a contiguous block of segments
missing. Pair-rule: \emph{fushi tarazu} (or \emph{even-skipped}), every
other segment missing. Segment polarity: \emph{hedgehog} (or
\emph{engrailed}), part of each segment replaced by a mirror image of
the rest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:2}
A gene whose product is deposited in the egg by the mother, so that the
embryo's phenotype follows her genotype. Her embryos lack head and
thorax and carry posterior structures at both ends; they die. The
paternal wild-type allele is in the zygote, but \emph{bicoid} mRNA is
made during oogenesis by the mother's nurse cells and anchored at the
pole before fertilisation; the zygote's own copy would be transcribed
hours later, after the gradient's time has passed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:3}
\omterm{def:b3:developmental-genetics:hox}{Colinearity}: the order of \omterm{def:b3:developmental-genetics:hox}{Hox genes} along the chromosome matches the
order of their expression domains along the body. Posterior prevalence:
where several are expressed, the most posterior one sets the identity.
A mouse lacking \emph{Hoxc8} shows an anterior transformation --- its
first lumbar vertebra takes the identity of a thoracic one and carries
a rib. \emph{Antennapedia} in the head converts antennae to legs, the
identity of the second thoracic segment.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:4}
A grafted dorsal lip induced a second complete axis made mostly of host
cells: the graft instructed its neighbours --- an \omterm{def:b3:developmental-genetics:organiser}{organiser}. Its
molecules are secreted BMP antagonists (Chordin, Noggin, Follistatin).
Ectoderm becomes neural when BMP signalling is absent and epidermis
when BMP is present, so neural is what ectoderm does when told nothing:
the \omterm{def:b3:developmental-genetics:organiser}{organiser} works by silencing an instruction.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:5}
$k = \ln 2/(35\times 60) = \qty{3.3e-4}{s^{-1}}$; $\lambda = \sqrt{4/
3.3\times 10^{-4}} = \qty{110}{\micro m}$; ratio $\mathrm{e}^{250/110}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:6}
Four copies double $c_{0}$: the boundary moves back by $\lambda\ln 2 =
\qty{69}{\micro m}$ to \qty{309}{\micro m}; one copy halves it: forward
by \qty{69}{\micro m} to \qty{171}{\micro m}. Each shift is
\qty{14}{\%} of egg length --- larger than observed, which is itself
evidence for mechanisms that damp the dose dependence.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:7}
$\delta x = \lambda\,\delta c/c$: for \qty{8}{\micro m}, $\delta c/c =
8/100 = \qty{8}{\%}$. A Poisson count $N$ has relative error
$1/\sqrt{N}$, so $N = 1/0.08^{2} \approx 160$ molecules must be counted
--- a few per cent of the molecules present in a nucleus, or a single
count integrated over time.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:8}
$k = \ln 2/35 = \qty{0.0198}{min^{-1}}$: $1 - \mathrm{e}^{-kt}$ gives
$0.70$ at \qty{60}{min}, $0.83$ at \qty{90}{min}, $0.91$ at
\qty{120}{min}, $0.95$ at \qty{150}{min}. The gradient is still rising
by several per cent while it is read, which would shift a boundary by
$\lambda\ln(1/0.9) \approx \qty{10}{\micro m}$, about one nucleus, over
the reading window: the embryo must either read at a fixed time or use
a read-out insensitive to the overall level.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:9}
A second ZPA gives a mirror-image duplication, 4-3-2-2-3-4: the
anterior cells now see high Shh and become posterior digits. A bead
releasing little Shh gives a partial duplication --- an extra anterior
digit 2, say 2-2-3-4 --- because the anterior cells see only the low
concentration that specifies digit 2.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:10}
Particular solution $s/k$; homogeneous solutions $\cosh(x/\lambda)$,
$\sinh(x/\lambda)$; no flux at $0$ kills the $\sinh$; $c(L) = 0$ fixes
the amplitude: $c(x) = (s/k)\bigl[1 - \cosh(x/\lambda)/\cosh(L/\lambda)\bigr]$.
The profile is a plateau near $x = 0$ that falls to zero in a boundary
layer of width $\lambda$ at the sink --- not an exponential from a
point, but a level field with an edge; the highest concentration is
farthest from the sink. For $L \ll \lambda$, $\cosh u \approx 1 +
u^{2}/2$ and $c \approx s(L^{2} - x^{2})/(2D)$: a parabola independent
of $k$, since molecules reach the sink before they can decay.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:11}
$x_{1} = \lambda\ln(1/0.3) = 1.20\lambda$ and $x_{2} = \lambda\ln(1/0.08)
= 2.53\lambda$: the first region is $1.20\lambda$ wide, the second
$1.32\lambda$, both independent of $c_{0}$, which shifts them together.
The third region, $L - 2.53\lambda$, absorbs all the variation in egg
length: in a \qty{450}{\micro m} egg with $\lambda = \qty{100}{\micro m}$
the abdomen is \qty{197}{\micro m}, in a \qty{550}{\micro m} egg
\qty{297}{\micro m} --- the pattern is not scaled. Mechanisms: a second
gradient from the posterior pole (Nanos, Caudal, or the terminal
system) read together with Bicoid, so that positions are fixed by the
ratio; or an ``expander'' molecule made where the morphogen is low that
lengthens $\lambda$ until the gradient fills the egg.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:developmental-genetics:12}
A coding change alters the protein wherever it acts: \emph{Pitx1} also
builds the \omterm{def:b3:endocrinology:axes}{pituitary} and jaw, \emph{\omterm{def:b3:developmental-genetics:organiser}{sonic hedgehog}} also patterns the
neural tube, gut and teeth, so a broken protein is lethal or
pleiotropically crippling. An enhancer drives expression in one tissue
at one time; deleting it removes the structure that tissue would have
built and leaves every other use of the protein intact. Enhancers are
modular and often partly redundant, so intermediate steps are viable,
and the same route --- a switch lost --- has been taken independently in
many stickleback lakes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:developmental-genetics:1}
\textbf{1.} $k = \ln 2/(35\times 60) = \qty{3.3e-4}{s^{-1}}$; $1/k =
\qty{3030}{s} = \qty{50}{min}$.
\textbf{2.} $\lambda = \sqrt{4/3.3\times 10^{-4}} = \qty{110}{\micro m}$;
the egg is $4.5\lambda$.
\textbf{3.} $\mathrm{e}^{4.5} \approx 90$ from pole to pole;
$\mathrm{e}^{8.5/110} = 1.08$, an \qty{8}{\%} change per nucleus.
\textbf{4.} $J = 50\times\sqrt{4\times 3.3\times 10^{-4}} = 50\times
0.036 = \qty{1.8}{nM\,\micro m\,s^{-1}}$. One nM is $0.6$ molecules
per \unit{\micro m^3}, so about $1.1$ molecules per \unit{\micro m^2}
per second.
\textbf{5.} Nuclear volume $\tfrac{4}{3}\pi\times 27 = \qty{113}{\micro
m^3}$. At \qty{50}{nM}, $30$ molecules per \unit{\micro m^3}:
\num{3400} molecules. At mid-egg $c = 50\,\mathrm{e}^{-2.27} =
\qty{5.2}{nM}$: about \num{350} molecules.
\textbf{6.} $kt = 1.19$ at \qty{60}{min}: $0.70$; $kt = 2.38$ at
\qty{120}{min}: $0.91$. The gradient is within \qty{10}{\%} of steady
when read --- still rising by an amount that would move a boundary by
$\lambda\ln(1/0.91) \approx \qty{10}{\micro m}$, a nucleus.
\textbf{7.} $x_{1} = 110\ln(1/0.3) = \qty{132}{\micro m}$
(\qty{26}{\%}); $x_{2} = 110\ln(1/0.08) = \qty{278}{\micro m}$
(\qty{56}{\%}).
\textbf{8.} Four copies: $c_{0} = \qty{100}{nM}$; both boundaries move
back by $\lambda\ln 2 = \qty{76}{\micro m}$, to $208$ and
\qty{354}{\micro m}. Six copies: $\lambda\ln 3 = \qty{121}{\micro m}$,
to $253$ and \qty{399}{\micro m}.
\textbf{9.} One copy: forward by \qty{76}{\micro m}, to $56$ and
\qty{202}{\micro m}. The head region shrank from $132$ to
\qty{56}{\micro m}; the thorax kept its width (\qty{146}{\micro m}) and
moved forward; the abdomen grew from $222$ to \qty{298}{\micro m}.
\textbf{10.} $\delta x = 110\times 0.1 = \qty{11}{\micro m}$, $1.3$
nuclear spacings.
\textbf{11.} One nucleus: $\delta c/c = 8.5/110 = \qty{7.7}{\%}$. From
\qty{10}{\%}, $n = (10/7.7)^{2} \approx 1.7$: two independent readings
(or the average of two neighbouring nuclei) suffice.
\textbf{12.} $1/\sqrt{350} = \qty{5.3}{\%}$: a single instantaneous
count is already more precise than the \qty{10}{\%} assumed, so the
observed noise is not counting noise alone but comes from binding
kinetics and the read-out machinery --- and averaging over time
improves on it.
\textbf{13.} $278/450 = \qty{62}{\%}$ and $278/550 = \qty{51}{\%}$ of
egg length: an \qty{11}{\%} discrepancy, six nuclei. The pattern does
not scale with a fixed $\lambda$.
\textbf{14.} If $D \propto L^{2}$ with $k$ fixed, $\lambda \propto L$ and
$x_{T}/L = (\lambda/L)\ln(c_{0}/T)$ is the same in every egg: perfect
scaling. But $D$ is a property of the molecule and the cytoplasm, not of
the egg's size, so this is implausible as stated; scaling in real
embryos is partial and comes from other read-outs.
\textbf{15.} $c^{5}/(c^{5} + T^{5}) = 0.1$ at $c/T = 9^{-1/5} = 0.64$ and
$0.9$ at $c/T = 9^{1/5} = 1.55$: a factor $2.4$ in concentration,
$\Delta x = 110\ln 2.4 = \qty{97}{\micro m}$ --- eleven nuclei, far
wider than the observed boundary of two or three; the gradient's
read-out alone is not sharp enough.
\textbf{16.} Steepness maps a given concentration to on or off more
decisively, so the transition region is narrower; but a \qty{10}{\%}
error in the concentration still moves the point where the threshold
is crossed by $\lambda\,\delta c/c$: the response can be a perfect step
and the step still lands in the wrong place.
\textbf{17.} If both were on, each would be repressing the other, so
at least one would be driven off; with cooperative repression the only
stable states are one-on-one-off, and a boundary between them is a
switch, not a slope.
\textbf{18.} $\mathrm{d}\ln\lambda = \tfrac{1}{2}(0.02 - 0.06) = -0.02$
per degree: $\lambda$ falls \qty{2}{\%} per degree, \qty{10}{\%} for
\qty{5}{\celsius}, to \qty{99}{\micro m}; the \emph{hunchback}
boundary moves from $278$ to \qty{250}{\micro m}, three nuclei forward.
Flies develop between \qty{18}{} and \qty{29}{\celsius} with normal
proportions, so something compensates --- a source or read-out that
shifts with temperature the other way.
\textbf{19.} $2^{8} = 256$ codes; fourteen used. With posterior
prevalence only the most posterior gene on matters, so at most nine
distinct identities (eight genes, or none): the code is a ranking, not
a binary word.
\textbf{20.} The third thoracic segment becomes a second: \emph{Ubx}
repressed the wing programme that a thoracic segment runs by default,
and removing the repressor releases the ancestral state --- four wings,
as in the fly's four-winged ancestors.
\textbf{21.} The goose's cervical region runs to vertebra 22: a long
neck of many vertebrae, the mouse's seven. The gene is the same; the
anterior limit of its expression moved posteriorly --- a regulatory
change in where a Hox gene is switched on.
\textbf{22.} Each margin is now a source; Shh is highest at both edges
(digit 4), falls toward the centre (3, then 2), and never reaches the
low level that specifies digit 1, so the middle holds two digits 2 back
to back: 4-3-2-2-3-4.
\textbf{23.} \emph{Pax6} is a conserved master regulator that can
trigger whatever eye programme the host carries: the fly built a fly
eye, so the mouse gene carries no mouse-eye information, only the
command ``build an eye here.'' It shows \omterm{def:b3:developmental-genetics:evodevo}{deep homology} of the gene
network, not homology of the organs: the compound eye and the camera
eye evolved separately from an ancestor that had a \emph{Pax6}
photoreceptor patch.
\textbf{24.} Five digits across \qty{2}{mm} is a wavelength of
\qty{0.4}{mm}; six needs \qty{0.33}{mm}, a shortening of a sixth.
Since $\lambda \propto \sqrt{D_{\text{inh}}/k_{\text{inh}}}$, raise the
inhibitor's decay by \qty{44}{\%} or lower its diffusion by
\qty{31}{\%}; in the hand plate, reducing the dose of the posterior \omterm{def:b3:developmental-genetics:hox}{Hox
genes} does it, and the mutant mice have six, seven or more thin
digits.
\textbf{25.} $\lambda \approx \qty{110}{\micro m}$; a doubling of the
source shifts every boundary by \qty{76}{\micro m}; \qty{10}{\%} noise
in the concentration is \qty{11}{\micro m}, about one nucleus, of
positional error.
\end{solution}
