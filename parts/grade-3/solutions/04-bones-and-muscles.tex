\begin{solution}{exo:g3:bones-and-muscles:1}
The \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{skull} protects the brain; the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{spine} holds the trunk upright
(a bendable column); the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{ribs} form a protective cage around
heart and lungs; the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{limb} \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones} carry \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arms} and \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:bones-and-muscles:3}
No --- a bent \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone} is a broken \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone}. All bending happens at the

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:bones-and-muscles:4}
It is a column of more than $20$ small \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones}, each able to turn
a few degrees on its neighbors; the many small bends add up to
one big supple curl.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:bones-and-muscles:5}
It shortens and thickens, and so pulls the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone} it is attached
to. No \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} can push.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:bones-and-muscles:6}
Because a \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} only pulls, one \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} can move the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{joint} only
one way. Bending: the front \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{contracts} and pulls the
forearm up while the back one relaxes. Straightening: the back
\omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{contracts} while the front one relaxes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:bones-and-muscles:7}
The living \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone} slowly rebuilds the break itself; the cast only
holds the pieces still while it works. It shows \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone} is living
material, fed by blood --- not dead like chalk.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:bones-and-muscles:8}
Bending: the front of the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arm} swells and hardens (that \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} is
contracting). Straightening: the front softens while the back of
the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arm} tightens --- the pair swapping roles.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:bones-and-muscles:9}
\omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{Knees} are \omterm{prop:g3:bones-and-muscles:joints}{joints} built for big folds, moved by the strongest
\omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} of the body; the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{spine} is built for gentle bends spread
over many small \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones}. Lifting with bent \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{knees} and a straight
back gives the heavy work to the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} and spares the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{spine}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:bones-and-muscles:10}
Each string, like each \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle}, can only pull. The puppet-maker's
answer is the biologist's: two strings per movement --- one to
pull the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arm} up, another to pull it back down --- working in
turns, exactly like the body's \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} pairs.
\end{solution}
