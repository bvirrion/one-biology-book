\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g1:my-body} --- My Body}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:my-body:1}
Head, trunk, \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{limbs} (two \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arms} and two \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}). The neck joins the
head to the trunk.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:my-body:2}
An \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arm} ends in a hand; a \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{leg} ends in a foot.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:my-body:3}
(a) The \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{elbow}. (b) The \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{knee}. (c) The \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{wrist}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:my-body:4}
The \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{shoulder} turned the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arm} across, the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{elbow} bent it, and the
\omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{wrist} tilted the hand to the ear.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:my-body:5}
Alike: the same overall plan --- a head, a body (trunk), four
\omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{limbs}. Different: you stand upright on two \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} with two free
\omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arms}, while the cat walks on all four; and the cat has a tail,
which you do not.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:my-body:6}
Just looking, you guess the top of the head at a slant and the mark
lands too high or too low. The flat book, level and touching the
wall, brings the very top of the head straight to the wall: the
same fair measurement every time.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:my-body:7}
They show that your body grew taller between the two dates. No ---
growing is too slow to feel; only the marks make it visible.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:my-body:8}
Open answer: walking well, sitting down, picking things up,
clapping, writing --- nearly every movement turns out to use
\omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{knees}, hips, \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{elbows}, \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{shoulders} or \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{wrists}. Without \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{joints}, the body
would move like one stiff stick.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:my-body:9}
That every body is normal at its own size and its own speed: some
children grow sooner, some later, and all reach their own grown-up
size. Height marks are for watching yourself grow, not for racing
anyone.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:my-body:10}
Cut at the hips (so the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} can bend forward to sit), at the
\omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{knees} (so the lower \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} can hang down), and it sits better still
with a cut at the neck and \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{shoulders} to lean and balance. The cuts
copy the body's \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{joints}: hip, \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{knee}, neck, \omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{shoulder}.
\end{solution}
