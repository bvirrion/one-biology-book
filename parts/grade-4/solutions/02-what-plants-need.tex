\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g4:what-plants-need} --- What Plants Need}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:1}
Water, light, air, warmth, and \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral salts} (drunk dissolved in
the soil's water).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:2}
Exactly one --- the thing being tested; everything else must be
identical. The untouched pot is the control.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:3}
Pale yellow instead of green, strangely tall and stretched, weak
--- then it gives up. Light was missing, so the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} could not
make its green substance, nor its food.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:4}
Tiny amounts of nourishing substances present in the soil. The
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{roots} drink them dissolved in the soil's water.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:5}
Because harvests carry the soil's \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral salts} away year after
year; compost restocks them. It feeds the \emph{soil}, and the
soil's water then carries the salts into the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{roots}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:6}
An \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} grows out of the \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} it eats; a \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} eats
no one --- it makes its own matter from water, \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral salts},
air and the \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} of light caught by its green \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:7}
Not from the soil: from water, from the air, with light
providing the \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} --- built in the green \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}. (The soil
contributed only water and small amounts of \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral salts}.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:8}
Open answer. Expected: the watered pot green and upright, the
dry twin wilting within days --- and since only water differed,
water gets the credit.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:9}
There is no control: one pot, and everything at once ---
perhaps the pot simply had good light and diligent watering
(singers visit their \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} often!). The fair version: two
identical pots, same \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds}, soil, window and watering; sing to
one only; compare after a month. Only then would a difference
point to the singing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:10}
Light: it grows submerged but in lit water, its green \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}
still catching the light through it. The water around it also
supplies what soil water supplies --- dissolved \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral salts},
drunk over its whole surface instead of through \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{roots}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:what-plants-need:11}
\omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{Mineral salts}: water, light, air and warmth were equal, and
pure sand with rainwater provides almost no salts. It grew at
first on the reserves packed in its own tissues (as the buried
\omterm{prop:g2:from-seed-to-plant:cycle}{seedling} grew on the \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed}'s store) --- reserves spent, the
shortage showed.
\end{solution}
