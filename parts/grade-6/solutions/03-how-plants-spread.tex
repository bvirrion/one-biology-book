\begin{solution}{exo:g6:how-plants-spread:1}

\omterm{def:g6:how-plants-spread:dispersal}{Dispersal} is the carrying of \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds}, \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruits} or \omterm{def:g4:plants-without-seeds:spore}{spores} away

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:how-plants-spread:9}
The arching, tip-rooting \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{stems} do the metre --- dense, sure
occupation of the ground next door. The bird-eaten berries do
the mile --- rare long-range landings that found new patches
far away.
\end{solution}

\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} decide who may stay: the inhabitants match the

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:how-plants-spread:11}
Only the long-range services: wind (plumed and dust-fine
\omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds}, \omterm{def:g4:plants-without-seeds:spore}{spores}), sea water (floating \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruits} like the coconut),
and \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds} (gut-carried \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} of fleshy \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruits}). Predict
plumed, dust-fine, floating or berry-borne equipment among
the pioneers --- no heavy, carrier-less \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g6:how-plants-spread:12}
The sugar buys transport: a bird paid in flesh carries the
\omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} far from the parent's shade and deposits them with
fertilizer --- distance, the first gift. The tough coats are
the other half because the courier is a \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:tract}{digestive tract}: the
payment must be digestible and the passengers must not be.
Sweetness recruits the carrier; the coats survive it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g6:how-plants-spread:1}
\textbf{1.} Poppies: waked from \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} already in the
ploughed soil --- the annual's waiting stock. Dandelions and
thistles: flown in on the wind, by their plumed \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds}.

\textbf{2.} \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{Birds} eating blackberries elsewhere perch on the
fence posts and deposit the \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} below them, fertilizer
included --- so the \omterm{prop:g2:from-seed-to-plant:cycle}{seedlings} map the perches, not the wind.

\textbf{3.} Its dryness: an open, sunlit field offers none
of the dampness a fern's delicate spore-grown stages need.
Arrival happened; establishment was refused.

\textbf{4.} Vegetative advance --- creeping underground
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{stems}. It moves only ground-to-adjacent-ground from
established \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants}, so it must walk in from the edge; it has
no airborne stage to land in the middle.

\textbf{5.} In the soil, as \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds}: \omterm{prop:g6:life-through-seasons:herbs}{annuals} beaten in the
standing competition persist as the \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed} bank, waiting for
the next bare ground.

\textbf{6.} The \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{inside-animal} service (berries eaten,
\omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} deposited from perches --- thickest under fence line
and hedges) and, for hawthorn equally, the same
bird-courier; the perch-shaped pattern is the evidence.

\textbf{7.} Oak acorns and chestnuts are heavy, carrier-less
without help --- they fall at the parent's foot. Their
courier is the jay (and the squirrel): burying acorns as
winter stores and forgetting some, they \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} oaks far from
the parent.

\textbf{8.} Each year's vegetation shades the ground below
it, cooling and darkening the surface: the sun-loving
pioneers' own success removes their \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}, and the
changed \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} favor shade-bearers next --- colonists
alter the site, and the altered site chooses new colonists.

\textbf{9.} Wave 1, the soil's waiting \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} (instant ---
they were already there). Wave 2, the wind-riders (weeks:
the air delivers constantly). Wave 3, animal-carried shrubs
(years: perch by perch). Wave 4, heavy-seeded trees (last:
they wait for jays' and squirrels' buried forgettings).

\textbf{10.} The cracks offer no room: a shrub's \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{roots} need
soil volume a pavement seam lacks; and the succession's
later waves need their couriers and \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} --- perching
\omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds}' deposits mostly miss the cracks, and the baked dry
seam refuses what does arrive. Year one's wind-riders are
the only service the site can accept.

\textbf{11.} The trend runs bare earth, herbs, shrubs,
pioneer trees --- each wave taller and shadier: extended,
it ends in woodland, as the willows and birches already
show. The exception: a mowed or grazed meadow, where human
touch removes the shrub wave every year --- proof that only
\emph{left alone} does the field walk to forest.

\textbf{12.} For example: ``\omterm{def:g6:how-plants-spread:dispersal}{Dispersal} delivered colonists to
the bare field --- from the soil, the wind, the \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds} ---
and at each stage the field's \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} decided which
arrivals could stay. \omterm{prop:g6:how-plants-spread:travel}{Colonization} thus ran in waves, each
wave changing the \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} and choosing the next.''
\end{solution}
