\chapter{Plants Are Producers}\label{ch:g6:plants-are-producers}

Weigh a watered pot, \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} an acorn in it, and come back
after years to weigh the young oak: kilograms of wood, bark,
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf} and \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{root} --- while the pot's soil has scarcely lost a
gram (\cref{ex:g4:what-plants-need:tree} first sprang this
surprise). Kilograms of tree, made from what? This chapter
pins the answer down --- and names the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants}' unique trade.

\section{Organic and mineral matter}

\begin{definition}[Mineral and organic matter]\label{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}
Matter, for a biologist, comes in two kinds:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{mineral matter}\index{mineral matter}: matter
        of the non-living world --- water, air's gases,
        rock, the dissolved \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral salts} of
        \cref{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs};
  \item \emph{organic matter}\index{organic matter}: matter
        made by \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living things} and composing their bodies ---
        wood, \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf}, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle}, fat, sugar, wool. Burnable,
        rottable, and always traceable to a living maker.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Sorting a pocketful]\label{ex:g6:plants-are-producers:sorting}
From a coat pocket after a walk: a pebble --- mineral; a
hazelnut --- organic (a hazel made it); a plastic button ---
organic by origin, oddly enough (plastics are made from oil,
the transformed remains of ancient \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living things}); a drop of
rain --- mineral; a woolly glove --- organic (a sheep made
the wool).
\end{example}

\section{The producers' trade}

\begin{proposition}[Plants produce organic matter from mineral matter]\label{prop:g6:plants-are-producers:produce}
A green \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant}, given only \emph{mineral} ingredients ---
water and dissolved salts from the soil, a gas taken from
the \emph{air} --- and the \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} of \emph{\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}}, makes
\emph{organic} matter: its own sugar, and from it wood,
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf} and \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{root}. This making is the trade
\cref{prop:g4:what-plants-need:make} promised to explain:
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} are \emph{producers}\index{producer} --- the only
makers of new \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} from purely mineral supplies.
The green substance of the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} is the light-catching
machinery; the manufacture happens in the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf}, by day.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The evidence, assembled]\label{ex:g6:plants-are-producers:evidence}
Three earlier observations now click together. The weighed
pot: the oak's kilograms did not come from the soil --- they
came mostly from air and water. The pale prisoner of
\cref{ex:g4:what-plants-need:pale}: no \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}, no
manufacture --- the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} starves in the dark with a full
larder of minerals. The mineral-starved mint of
\cref{exo:g4:what-plants-need:11}: salts are needed --- but
as small supplies, not as the bulk. Bulk from air and
water, \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} from \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}, seasoning from the soil.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[The gas swap, both ways]\label{rem:g6:plants-are-producers:gas}
The air gas the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf} takes in is \emph{carbon dioxide} ---
the very gas every breather exhales
(\cref{prop:g4:breathing:exchange}); and manufacturing
releases \emph{oxygen} to the air --- the very gas every
breather needs. \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{Plants} also breathe, day and night, like
all \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living things}; but by day a working \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf}'s manufacture
far outruns its \omterm{def:g4:breathing:movements}{breathing}. The world's breathable air is
the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}' by-product --- one more debt every \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}
owes the green.
\end{remark}

\section{Producers feed everyone --- and store for themselves}

\begin{proposition}[The first link, explained]\label{prop:g6:plants-are-producers:firstlink}
\Cref{prop:g3:food-chains:start} decreed every \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{food chain}
starts with a \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant}; now the decree has its reason. Only
\omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers} make \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} from \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{mineral matter}; every
\omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumer} (\cref{def:g5:food-webs:roles}) can only
\emph{transform} \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} it eats. So all the \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic
matter} in every living body --- rabbit, fox, buzzard, you
--- was first manufactured in a \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf}, and the food pyramid
of \cref{prop:g5:food-webs:pyramid} stands on the green
floor because that is where matter enters the living world.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The plant's own pantry]\label{ex:g6:plants-are-producers:pantry}
What a \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} makes beyond the day's needs, it stores as
organic reserves: the potato \omterm{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}{tuber} and tulip \omterm{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}{bulb}
(\cref{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}), the bean's
thick halves (\cref{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}), the
carrot's fat \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{root} (\cref{exo:g1:plants-around-us:10}), the
sweetness of the beet. Every one is summer \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}, banked as
matter --- and every one is exactly what farmers, and
eaters, harvest.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Tracing any food to its leaf]\label{met:g6:plants-are-producers:trace}
Take any food on the table:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item if it is \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} matter --- bread's wheat, the
        potato, the apple --- it is \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producer}'s manufacture
        directly: name the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} and its stored organ;
  \item if it is \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} matter --- cheese, \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg}, ham ---
        step down the chain: what did the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} eat? and
        that food's maker? continue to the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant};
  \item write the chain with arrows
        (\cref{def:g3:food-chains:chain});
  \item conclusion, always the same: the trail ends in a
        \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf}, in the \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\section{Problem: The Market Gardener's Year}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- light, leaves and the
ledger of a vegetable garden}]\label{pb:g6:plants-are-producers:1}
A market gardener grows potatoes, carrots, beans and
lettuces, and keeps a sharp ledger of what goes in and what
comes out.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The ledger's riddle.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The garden yields two tonnes of vegetables a year;
        the gardener spreads only sacks of compost. Weigh
        the riddle: where do the tonnes come from, if not
        from the sacks? (Answer with the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}' trade.)
  \item Classify the yield: the potato, the carrot, the
        bean \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed}, the lettuce \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf} --- name each one's
        role for the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} (storage organ, working organ,
        \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed} store).
  \item The gardener says: ``my real supplier delivers by
        daylight.'' Translate into
        \cref{prop:g6:plants-are-producers:produce}'s
        terms.
  \item Why does the gardener still need the compost at
        all, if not for the tonnes? (Recall the mint in
        sand.)
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Reading the seasons.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item June's lettuces, shaded under a trial cloth for a
        week, come out pale and stretched. Name the
        experiment this repeats, and the input it removed.
  \item The potato rows are earthed up and their lush tops
        praised: ``good tops, good \omterm{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}{tubers}.'' Explain the
        saying --- what is the connection between working
        \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} above and swelling storage below?
  \item August's drought halts growth though the sun
        blazes. Which mineral input has failed, and why can
        \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light} not substitute for it?
  \item In September the gardener cuts and removes the
        bean \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} but digs their \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{roots} in. A neighbor
        calls this waste. Defend the practice with the
        matter definitions --- what is being returned to
        the soil, for whom?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- Beyond the garden.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The gardener's pig eats the small potatoes; the
        family eats the pig. Write the chain, and state
        where its \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} was originally
        manufactured.
  \item A customer claims her salad is ``sun, mostly''.
        Audit the claim: list the salad \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf}'s actual
        ingredients and the sun's exact role.
  \item Frost kills the last lettuces overnight; they rot
        where they lie by November. Their \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter}
        does not vanish. Anticipate
        \cref{ch:g6:decomposers-and-soil}: who takes it,
        and toward what state?
  \item Summarize the garden in one sentence containing:
        \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}, \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{mineral matter}, \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter}, \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
