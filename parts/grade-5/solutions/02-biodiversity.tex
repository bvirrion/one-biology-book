\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g5:biodiversity} --- Biodiversity}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:1}
One particular kind of \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living thing} --- individuals built on
the same model, reproducing among themselves. Examples: the
house sparrow, the common daisy, the red fox, the garden
snail.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:2}
The variety of life a place holds --- above all its number of
\omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} and their health. The flowering meadow holds dozens of
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} and \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} in one square metre: high
\omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:biodiversity}{biodiversity}. The paved yard holds almost none.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:3}
About two million. Because every serious expedition still
finds unnamed \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} --- beetles especially --- so millions
more must exist beyond the catalogue.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:4}
Many \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} in one \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} (the meadow's square metre); many
\omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitats} side by side (pond, hedge, meadow); variety within a
\omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} (no two garden snails' \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{shells} quite alike).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:5}
In a rich web, a hunter whose prey fails can switch to other
prey, and every role has understudies; the shock is absorbed.
In a poor web each \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} has few options, so one loss drags
others after it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:6}
Bees and hoverflies: pollinating the blossom. Ladybugs:
eating the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} lice. \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{Birds}: patrolling for \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillars}.
Worms: keeping the soil open.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:7}
\omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{Habitat} destroyed (the drained pond); \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{environment} poisoned
(litter and chemicals); too many taken (overfishing). \omterm{prop:g5:biodiversity:loss}{Extinct}
means gone entirely --- everywhere, forever.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:8}
Open answer. A grassy or planted square typically yields ten
or more \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species}; the paved square, close to zero --- the
difference is the measurement.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:9}
Each \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} is a thread: its loss \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} every neighbor ---
whatever ate it, whatever it pollinated or cleaned up ---
with fewer options, and weakens the web's power to absorb
the next shock. And since no one knows all the threads a
\omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} holds, no one can certify a loss harmless in
advance; extinction being forever, the mistake could never
be unmade.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:10}
Protecting storks one by one would mean guarding \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds};
restoring wet meadows rebuilt what the storks \emph{depend
on} --- and a \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} feeds all its inhabitants at once,
which is why frogs, dragonflies and meadow \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flowers} returned
with the storks. No \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}, no inhabitants; \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}
restored, inhabitants return.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:biodiversity:11}
What was removed was not decoration but a workforce: the
hedge and \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flower} \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} housed the pollinators and pest
eaters, whose services appeared in nobody's accounts while
free. \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{Nature}'s work is invisible bookkeeping --- constant,
unpaid, unnoticed --- so its price is discovered exactly
when the workers are evicted and the jobs come back as
bills.
\end{solution}
