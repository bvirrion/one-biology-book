\chapter{The Immune Defenses}\label{ch:g9:immune-defenses}

The doors are held (\cref{prop:g9:microbes-and-infection:doors})
--- and still, sometimes, an invader lands: the splinter's
\omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria} multiplying in the warm tissue, the \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus} already
printing in the airway's \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}. What happens next is the
body's third great system after \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nerves} and \omterm{def:g8:hormonal-communication:glands}{hormones}: the
\omterm{def:g9:immune-defenses:memory}{immune} defenses --- a standing army, a specialist corps
with memory, and the vaccination trick that trains it in
advance.

\section{The rapid response: inflammation}

\begin{definition}[The standing army]\label{def:g9:immune-defenses:standing}
Patrolling the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} and tissues stand the white \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}
\omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}\index{white blood cell} --- the \omterm{def:g9:immune-defenses:memory}{immune} system's
troops, made in the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone}'s marrow, riding
\cref{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:carries}'s network. The
first responders are the \emph{engulfers}: \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} that
flow around an intruder and digest it
(\cref{ex:g6:cell-unit-of-life:drop}'s flowing blob ran
the same maneuver as a free-living hunter --- the body
employs the ancient trick as a defense).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Inflammation, read correctly]\label{prop:g9:immune-defenses:inflammation}
A splinter's aftermath --- redness, warmth, swelling,
throb --- is not the \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:stages}{infection} winning but the defense
arriving. Alarm signals from the damaged tissue widen the
local \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:vessels}{vessels} (\cref{prop:g7:organs-at-work:supply}'s
rerouting, requisitioned): more warm \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}, walls made
leaky, engulfers squeezing out of the \omterm{def:g7:heart-and-circulation:vessels}{capillaries} and
crawling to the site, eating \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria} as they come. Pus
is the battlefield's tally --- spent engulfers, dead
invaders, debris. Most landings end here, in days,
unremarked.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Reading a healing cut]\label{ex:g9:immune-defenses:cut}
Day one: red, warm, tender halo --- \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:vessels}{vessels} wide, troops
arriving. Day two: a little pus --- the tally. Day four:
halo fading, scab dry --- multiplication lost the race to
engulfment, and the \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}'s rebuild
(\cref{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:growth}) closes the
door behind the war. The whole campaign, fought and won
below the scale of worry.
\end{example}

\section{The specialists: recognition and memory}

\begin{proposition}[The specific response]\label{prop:g9:immune-defenses:specific}
When the rapid response is outpaced, the specialist corps
takes the field --- slower, and precise:
\begin{itemize}
  \item every invader carries surface marks --- its
        \emph{antigens}\index{antigen} --- molecular
        shapes the specialists read as foreign (the
        self-markers of
        \cref{ex:g9:unique-individuals:medicine} are the
        same reading, aimed inward);
  \item among the specialist white \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}, a few carry
        receptors fitting \emph{this} antigen; contact
        selects them, and they multiply into a clone army
        (selection's logic, run inside the body in days);
  \item their weapons: \emph{antibodies}\index{antibody}
        --- fitted molecules poured into the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} that
        pin and clump the invader for the engulfers ---
        and killer specialists that destroy the body's own
        hijacked \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}, the only way to stop a \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus}'s
        printers (\cref{ex:g9:microbes-and-infection:contrast});
  \item the battle's cost is part of the illness: the
        fever (a body running hot on purpose --- many
        \omterm{prop:g9:microbes-and-infection:mostly}{pathogens} multiply worse warm), the aches, the
        swollen \emph{lymph nodes} --- the specialists'
        barracks, crowded in wartime.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{definition}[Immune memory]\label{def:g9:immune-defenses:memory}
After victory, part of the clone army stands down into
\emph{memory cells}\index{memory cell}: long-lived
specialists holding the antigen's description. A second
landing of the \emph{same} invader meets a prepared corps
--- response in hours, invader cleared before symptoms:
you are \emph{immune}\index{immunity}. One measles,
never two; last winter's strain unable to relight this
year's outbreak
(\cref{pb:g9:microbes-and-infection:1}). Memory is why
the same cold seems never to come twice --- and why
colds keep coming: their \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{viruses} mutate fast
(\cref{prop:g9:genes-and-dna:explains}) into
descriptions the memory does not hold.
\end{definition}

\section{Training the defense: vaccination}

\begin{proposition}[Vaccination]\label{prop:g9:immune-defenses:vaccination}
\emph{Vaccination}\index{vaccination} is \omterm{def:g9:immune-defenses:memory}{immune} memory
created without the disease: present the specialists with
the invader's \emph{description} --- killed or weakened
\omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:fermentation}{microbes}, harmless fragments bearing the antigen ---
and the whole selection-and-memory program runs, minus
the danger. The trained corps then meets the real
pathogen, if ever, as a second landing. Two consequences
carry the public-health weight:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the vaccinated are protected before the first
        battle --- decisive for diseases whose first
        battle can kill or maim;
  \item enough vaccinated neighbors starve an outbreak
        of catchable hosts
        (\cref{pb:g9:microbes-and-infection:1}'s
        fade-out): protection pools --- the shield
        covers those too young or too ill to be trained
        themselves.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[From the cowpox gamble to the eradication]\label{ex:g9:immune-defenses:history}
The history runs from Jenner's 1796 gamble --- milkmaids
caught mild cowpox and never smallpox; a cowpox
inoculation trained against the killer --- through
Pasteur's deliberate weakened-microbe vaccines
(\cref{ex:g9:microbes-and-infection:history}'s author,
completing his theory), to the twentieth century's
roll-calls: smallpox, humanity's old scourge,
\emph{eradicated} --- the \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus} \omterm{prop:g5:biodiversity:loss}{extinct} in the wild since
the late 1970s --- and polio driven to the map's last
corners. Trained memory, pooled, is the only weapon that
has ever exterminated a human disease.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Reading any immunity event]\label{met:g9:immune-defenses:read}
For any \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:stages}{infection}, \omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery}, vaccine or test result:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item stage the encounter: first landing or second?
        (memory changes everything);
  \item name the responders: engulfers' rapid response,
        specialists' clone army, or standing memory;
  \item read the signs correctly: inflammation and fever
        as defense at work, swollen nodes as barracks;
  \item for a vaccine: identify the presented
        description, and whose protection it pools.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\section{Problem: The Vaccination Ledger}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- a health center's
week, read by the immune method}]\label{pb:g9:immune-defenses:1}
A health center's (invented) week: Monday, infant
vaccinations; Tuesday, a farmer's rusty-wire gash and
booster; Wednesday, a measles case in an unvaccinated
child, and the contacts checked; Thursday, \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} tests
reading antibody levels; Friday, the annual staff
influenza vaccines.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The week's cases.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Monday's infants receive weakened-microbe drops
        and fragment injections. Name what is being
        presented, and which program runs in the
        following weeks.
  \item Tuesday's farmer: the gash is cleaned (which
        defense line?), and the booster given. What
        does a \emph{booster} do to a trained memory
        --- and why does tetanus, of all diseases, allow
        no first-battle gamble?
  \item Wednesday's measles child: first landing, full
        program. Time-line the illness: rapid response
        outpaced, clone army raised, \omterm{def:g7:organs-at-work:recovery}{recovery} --- and
        what stands down after.
  \item The checked contacts divide: vaccinated
        (untroubled), recovered-long-ago (untroubled),
        and one unvaccinated infant, watched anxiously.
        Read each with
        \cref{met:g9:immune-defenses:read}'s first
        question.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The measurements.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Thursday's tests read antibody levels against
        past diseases and vaccines. What does a high
        level certify, and what \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} stock does it
        imply?
  \item One patient's levels have faded years after
        vaccination, and a booster is prescribed.
        Explain fading and boosting in \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{memory-cell}
        terms.
  \item Friday's influenza vaccine is \emph{annual},
        unlike Monday's once-or-twice programs. Which
        property of the influenza \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus}
        (\cref{def:g9:immune-defenses:memory}) forces
        the yearly retraining?
  \item Why does the center vaccinate its \emph{staff}
        with special insistence? (Whose protection
        pools where, in a building full of the ill and
        the fragile?)
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The ledger's
meaning.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The unvaccinated measles child's parents had
        feared vaccines and trusted ``natural''
        first battles. Draft the doctor's gentle
        arithmetic: compare the training's cost with
        the first battle's, for measles.
  \item The watched infant was too young for the
        vaccine. State who was supposed to be
        protecting it, and what that duty is called in
        \cref{prop:g9:immune-defenses:vaccination}.
  \item Connect Tuesday and Wednesday to the two
        historic names of
        \cref{ex:g9:immune-defenses:history}: whose
        idea ran in each consultation?
  \item Close the ledger with the week's one-sentence
        summary: what the center actually administers,
        in this chapter's vocabulary.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
