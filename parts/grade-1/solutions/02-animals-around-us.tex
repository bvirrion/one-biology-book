\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g1:animals-around-us} --- Animals Around Us}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:animals-around-us:1}
Head, body, legs (\omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:parts}{limbs}), tail. The head carries the eyes and the
mouth.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:animals-around-us:2}
(a) $2$; (b) $4$; (c) $6$; (d) $8$; (e) none --- the earthworm has
no legs at all.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:animals-around-us:3}
The sheep: wool, which is thick \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur}. The duck: \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}. The trout:
scales. The snail: a \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{shell} (over a soft, moist body).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:animals-around-us:4}
The blackbird, the bee and the butterfly. They all have wings.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:animals-around-us:5}
The snail slides slowly on its one soft foot, leaving a trail of
slime. The earthworm wriggles: it stretches long and thin, then
pulls itself short again, and moves forward bit by bit.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:animals-around-us:6}
It walks on its legs, it swims with its webbed feet (its legs
again, with toes joined by \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{skin}), and it flies with its wings.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:animals-around-us:7}
The ant feeds by eating other \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} (crumbs of food, seeds,
tiny \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals}), and it moves about on its own six legs. Feeding and
moving: both parts of the definition fit.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:animals-around-us:8}
Open answer. A good description names the parts seen, gives a real
leg count, the covering, the way of moving, and the place --- for
example: ``a snail on the wall: head with two tentacles, soft body,
no legs, a brown \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{shell}, it slides; found after the rain.''
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:animals-around-us:9}
Walking is only one way of moving. Fish have fins and swim --- that
is their way. An \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} must feed and move, not walk: the fish does
both, so it is an \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:animals-around-us:10}
Yes, it is a bird: \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers} and wings are the give-away, even
without flight. The ostrich cannot fly but runs faster than any
person --- being a bird is about the body, not about flying.
\end{solution}
