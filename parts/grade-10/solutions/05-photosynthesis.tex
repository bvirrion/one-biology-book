\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g10:photosynthesis} --- Photosynthesis}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:1}
In words: carbon dioxide + water $\xrightarrow{\text{light}}$ glucose +
oxygen. In symbols:
$6\,\mathrm{CO_2}+6\,\mathrm{H_2O}\to\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}+6\,\mathrm{O_2}$
(light required). Glucose stands for \omterm{def:g10:molecules-of-life:carbs}{carbohydrate} product; \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{cells} actually make
intermediates that feed sucrose, starch and other pathways.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:2}
(a)~\omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:light}{Light-dependent reactions} occur on \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{thylakoid} membranes (stacked as \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{grana}).
(b)~The \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:calvin}{Calvin cycle} occurs in the \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{stroma}, the fluid compartment of the
\omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{chloroplast}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:3}
\omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:chlorophyll}{Chlorophyll} absorbs blue and red wavelengths strongly and reflects or
transmits green light. Under white illumination the leaf therefore appears
green to the eye.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:4}
The \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:calvin}{Calvin cycle} consumes ATP and NADPH produced by the light reactions.
When water is split (\omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:light}{photolysis}), oxygen gas is released.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:5}
Light intensity (brighter lamps or longer day length); $\mathrm{CO_2}$
concentration (enrichment); temperature (heating toward \omterm{def:g10:membranes-enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme} optima, without
overheating). In practice water and mineral supply also affect long-term rates
in a greenhouse.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:6}
``Light-independent'' means light is not a direct reactant of the \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:calvin}{Calvin cycle}
\omterm{def:g10:membranes-enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme} steps. The name misleads because the cycle needs ATP and NADPH from the
light reactions, so it slows or stops in prolonged darkness when those carriers
are not regenerated.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:7}
A plateau means light is no longer limiting: another factor or the machinery's
maximum limits the rate. To test $\mathrm{CO_2}$ limitation, raise
$\mathrm{CO_2}$ at the same high light; if the rate increases, carbon dioxide
was limiting.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:8}
Label in water appears in the $\mathrm{O_2}$ released by \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:light}{photolysis}. Label in
$\mathrm{CO_2}$ appears in \omterm{def:g10:molecules-of-life:carbs}{carbohydrate} (organic product), not in the
$\mathrm{O_2}$ gas that comes from water splitting. That is the classic
isotope evidence for the oxygen source.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:9}
Closed stomata reduce $\mathrm{CO_2}$ entry so the \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:calvin}{Calvin cycle} slows; they
also reduce transpiration and save water. Short-term sugar production falls.
The trade-off is water conservation versus carbon gain on hot dry days.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:10}
\omterm{def:g10:scientific-biology:variables}{Independent variable}: light intensity. \omterm{def:g10:scientific-biology:variables}{Dependent variable}: e.g.\ time for leaf
disks to float or bubbles counted per minute from \emph{Elodea}. \omterm{def:g10:scientific-biology:variables}{Controlled
variables}: temperature, $\mathrm{CO_2}$ source (bicarbonate), leaf disk size
or species, \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:sa-v}{volume} of medium, and timing protocol.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:11}
Large thin leaves maximise light-capture area per unit mass. A low light
\omterm{prop:g10:photosynthesis:compensation}{compensation point} means net carbon gain even in dim light, which suits the
shady understory where intensity is chronically low.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:12}
\omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:chlorophyll}{Chlorophyll} absorption is high in blue and red and low in green, so leaves
reflect green and look green. An \omterm{prop:g10:photosynthesis:absorption}{action spectrum} measures photosynthetic rate
versus wavelength and is similar but not identical because accessory pigments
also harvest light and because quantum efficiency and other factors vary with
wavelength.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g10:photosynthesis:13}
C$_3$: Rubisco fixes $\mathrm{CO_2}$ directly in mesophyll --- efficient in
cooler, moister climates but prone to photorespiration when stomata close.
C$_4$: a four-carbon shuttle concentrates $\mathrm{CO_2}$ near Rubisco in
bundle-sheath \omterm{def:g10:the-cell:cell}{cells}, reducing photorespiration in hot bright habitats at
extra ATP cost. \omterm{rem:g10:photosynthesis:C3C4CAM}{CAM}: stomata open at night to store $\mathrm{CO_2}$, closed by
day to save water in arid habitats. All three still run the \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:calvin}{Calvin cycle} to
make \omterm{def:g10:molecules-of-life:carbs}{carbohydrate}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g10:photosynthesis:1}
\textbf{Part I.} Net: $\mathrm{CO_2}+\mathrm{H_2O}\to$ sugar $+
\mathrm{O_2}$ (balanced form as in the chapter). On a bright day
$\mathrm{O_2}$ rises and $\mathrm{CO_2}$ falls if \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:def}{photosynthesis} exceeds
community respiration. $\mathrm{O_2}$ is produced by water splitting in
\omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{thylakoids}.

\textbf{Part II.} At night $\mathrm{O_2}$ falls and $\mathrm{CO_2}$ rises as
respiration continues without photosynthetic recharge. In continuous darkness
photosynthetic $\mathrm{O_2}$ production stops; animals (snails) with higher
continuous oxygen demand may asphyxiate first as oxygen is depleted.

\textbf{Part III.} An algal film shades plants, lowering light at leaves and
thus plant \omterm{def:g10:photosynthesis:def}{photosynthesis}. Opening the bottle daily makes the system open:
gases exchange with room air and weaken closed-system lessons. Measurements:
rising dissolved $\mathrm{O_2}$ in light; starch tests on leaves; pH rise
(less $\mathrm{CO_2}$/carbonic acid) in light; bubble production.
\end{solution}
