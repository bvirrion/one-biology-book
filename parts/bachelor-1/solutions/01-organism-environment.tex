\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b1:organism-environment} --- The Organism: A System in Interaction with Its Environment}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:1}
Matter (a mammal eats food and exhales carbon dioxide; a plant takes in
carbon dioxide and releases oxygen), energy (a mammal takes the chemical
energy of food and releases heat; a plant takes light and releases heat),
information (a mammal sees a predator; a plant bends toward light).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:2}
$S/V = 3/r$ is halved. Uptake through the surface grows as $r^2$ and
consumption as $r^3$; beyond some radius the surface can no longer feed
the volume, so cells stay in the micrometre range or fold their surface.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:3}
Maintenance of a variable of the \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:milieu}{internal environment} near a \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:homeostasis}{set point}
by \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:homeostasis}{negative feedback}. Sensor: thermoreceptors of the skin and of the
brain; integrating centre: the hypothalamus, holding the \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:homeostasis}{set point} of
\qty{37}{\celsius}; effectors: shivering muscles, skin vessels, sweat
glands.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:4}
Lizard: from $1.0$ to $0.25$, a factor $4$ (two doublings for
\qty{20}{\celsius}). Mouse: from $1.0$ to $3.4$, a factor of about
$3.4$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:5}
Side \qty{1}{mm}: need $\qty{1}{nmol/s}$ ($V = \qty{1}{mm^3}$); supply
$2\times 6 = \qty{12}{nmol/s}$: yes. Side \qty{10}{mm}: need
\qty{1000}{nmol/s}, supply $2\times 600 = \qty{1200}{nmol/s}$: barely.
Limit: $2\times 6L^2 = L^3$ gives $L = \qty{12}{mm}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:6}
Horse: $3.4\times 500^{0.75} = \qty{360}{W}$, \qty{0.72}{W/kg}. Shrew:
$3.4\times 0.005^{0.75} = \qty{0.064}{W}$, \qty{13}{W/kg}. Daily:
$3\times 0.064\times 86400 = \qty{16.6}{kJ}$, i.e.\ \qty{3.3}{g} of food,
two thirds of its own mass.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:7}
$\qty{80}{W} = \qty{4.8}{kJ/min}$, i.e.\ \qty{0.24}{L} of $\mathrm{O_2}$
per minute; per day $80\times 86400 = \qty{6.9}{MJ}$. \qty{2000}{kcal}
= \qty{8.36}{MJ} per day = \qty{97}{W}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:8}
Mouse: $100\times(0.02/70)^{0.75} = \qty{0.22}{m^2}$; elephant:
$100\times(4000/70)^{0.75} = \qty{2080}{m^2}$. Spheres: mouse
$r = \qty{1.7}{cm}$, $S = \qty{0.0036}{m^2}$ (lung sixty times the
body surface); elephant $r = \qty{0.98}{m}$, $S = \qty{12}{m^2}$ (lung
one hundred and seventy times). The larger the animal, the more its lung
must be folded relative to its outside.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:9}
The effectors act only once the sensor has registered a deviation, so a
deviation must exist before it is corrected; the correction then
overshoots slightly before the sensor reports it. The band width is set
by the sensitivity of the sensor (the smallest detectable deviation),
the delay between detection and effect, and the strength of the
effectors.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:10}
$V = \qty{2e-5}{m^3}$, $r = \qty{1.68}{cm}$, $S = 4\pi r^2 =
\qty{3.6e-3}{m^2}$. Loss: $5\times 3.6\times 10^{-3}\times 37 =
\qty{0.66}{W}$. Basal production: \qty{0.18}{W}, four times less. The
mouse must raise its production (shivering, feeding: three to four times
basal), reduce $h$ (nest, huddling) or let its temperature fall (torpor).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:11}
Loss $\propto M^{2/3}$, production $\propto M^{3/4}$: the ratio
production/loss $\propto M^{1/12}$ grows with mass, so small animals have
the least production per unit of surface and large ones the most. Sphere
of mass $M$: $S = 4\pi(3M/4000\pi)^{2/3} = \num{0.0483}\,M^{2/3}$ (SI);
loss $= 5\times 20\times 0.0483\,M^{2/3} = 4.83\,M^{2/3}$; equality with
$3.4\,M^{3/4}$ gives $M^{1/12} = 1.42$, $M \approx \qty{67}{kg}$. Below,
the resting animal is cold without insulation; above, it warms up and
must shed heat.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:organism-environment:12}
Not simpler, differently organised. The plant's exchanges are external
(leaf and root surfaces spread into the medium) and its cells are
exposed to a medium it does not regulate; its ``regulation'' is growth and
form, adjusting the surfaces themselves. The mammal internalises its
exchanges behind a regulated fluid so that its cells never see the
outside. \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:homeostasis}{Homeostasis} is a property of the animal's \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:milieu}{internal environment},
not a measure of complexity: the plant's cells regulate their own
contents, its stomata regulate its water loss, and its body is a
continuously enlarging \omterm{prop:b1:organism-environment:surfaces}{exchange surface} --- a strategy, not an absence.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b1:organism-environment:1}
\textbf{1.} $V = M/\rho$: mouse \qty{2e-5}{m^3} (\qty{20}{cm^3}),
elephant \qty{4}{m^3}.
\textbf{2.} $r = (3V/4\pi)^{1/3}$: mouse \qty{1.68}{cm}, elephant
\qty{0.985}{m}.
\textbf{3.} $S = 4\pi r^2$: mouse \qty{3.55e-3}{m^2} (\qty{35.5}{cm^2}),
elephant \qty{12.2}{m^2}.
\textbf{4.} $S/V = 3/r$: mouse \qty{178}{m^{-1}}, elephant
\qty{3.05}{m^{-1}}; ratio $58$.
\textbf{5.} $S/V \propto 1/r \propto M^{-1/3}$, so the ratio is
$(M_e/M_m)^{1/3} = (2\times 10^5)^{1/3} = 58.5$.
\textbf{6.} Larger for both (a sphere has the smallest surface for its
volume); the mouse, with its tail, ears and slender limbs, departs
more.
\textbf{7.} $P = 10\times S\times 20$: mouse \qty{0.71}{W}, elephant
\qty{2440}{W}.
\textbf{8.} Mouse $3.4\times 0.02^{0.75} = \qty{0.18}{W}$; elephant
$3.4\times 4000^{0.75} = \qty{1710}{W}$.
\textbf{9.} Loss \qty{2440}{W} against production \qty{1710}{W} at
rest: bare skin is roughly adequate, and the elephant is in danger of
overheating rather than cooling when active or in warmer air.
\textbf{10.} Loss \qty{0.71}{W} against \qty{0.18}{W}: four times too
much. Fur must bring $h$ down to about \qty{2.5}{W.m^{-2}.K^{-1}}.
\textbf{11.} Its $S/V$ is the smallest of any land mammal; the ears
add a third to its surface and, being thin and perfused, are radiators
that dump heat when the body is producing more than \qty{12}{m^2} can
lose.
\textbf{12.} $S/V \propto M^{-1/3}$ grows without bound as $M$ falls
while $B/M \propto M^{-1/4}$ grows more slowly; below a few grams no fur
and no feeding rate can cover the loss. Letting the temperature fall at
night (torpor) removes the $T_b - T_a$ term for the hours without food.
\textbf{13.} $2B\times\qty{86400}{s}$: mouse \qty{31}{kJ}, elephant
\qty{295}{MJ}.
\textbf{14.} Mouse $31/18 = \qty{1.7}{g}$; elephant
$295\,000/5 = \qty{59}{kg}$.
\textbf{15.} Mouse $1.7/20 = 8.6\%$ of its mass; elephant $59/4000 =
1.5\%$: the mouse eats six times more per gram.
\textbf{16.} Gut lengths ratio $35/0.4 = 88$; radii ratio $0.985/0.0168
= 59$. The gut grows faster than the body's linear size (as roughly
$M^{0.36}$ here), which increases the elephant's absorptive surface
relative to its volume.
\textbf{17.} $f_0 = 600\times 0.02^{1/4} = \qty{226}{min^{-1}}$;
$\tau_0 = 2/0.02^{1/4} = \qty{5.3}{yr}$.
\textbf{18.} $f = 226\times 4000^{-1/4} = \qty{28}{min^{-1}}$; $\tau =
5.3\times 4000^{1/4} = \qty{42}{yr}$.
\textbf{19.} Mouse $600\times 60\times 24\times 365\times 2 =
\num{6.3e8}$; elephant $28\times 60\times 24\times 365\times 42 =
\num{6.2e8}$.
\textbf{20.} Beats per life $= f\tau \propto M^{-1/4}M^{1/4} = M^0$:
independent of mass, close to a billion.
\textbf{21.} $f = 226\times 70^{-1/4} = \qty{78}{min^{-1}}$ (close to
70); $\tau = 5.3\times 70^{1/4} = \qty{15}{yr}$, far below 80: the
human lifespan is the outlier, three to five times the mammalian trend,
for reasons (brain, sociality, medicine) outside the scaling law.
\textbf{22.} $160\times 4000^{-1/4}/0.02^{-1/4} = 160\times 0.0473 =
\qty{7.6}{min^{-1}}$; heartbeats per breath $600/160 = 28/7.6 = 3.75$
for both, since the two rates share the exponent.
\textbf{23.} $B/M = 3.4\,M^{3/4}/M = 3.4\,M^{-1/4}$; exponent $-1/4$.
\textbf{24.} Mouse $0.18/0.02 = \qty{9.0}{W/kg}$; elephant $1710/4000 =
\qty{0.43}{W/kg}$.
\textbf{25.} Ratio $9.0/0.43 = 21$, equal to $(M_e/M_m)^{1/4} =
(2\times 10^5)^{1/4} = 21.1$: each gram of the mouse burns twenty-one
times faster than each gram of the elephant --- the mass-specific
metabolic ratio is the fourth root of the mass ratio.
\end{solution}
