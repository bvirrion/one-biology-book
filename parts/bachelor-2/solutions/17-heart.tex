\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b2:heart} --- The Heart and the Cardiac Cycle}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:1}
Vena cava $\to$ right \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{atrium} $\to$ tricuspid valve $\to$ right
\omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricle} $\to$ pulmonary valve $\to$ pulmonary \omterm{def:b2:blood-circulation:vessels}{artery} $\to$ lungs
$\to$ pulmonary \omterm{def:b2:blood-circulation:vessels}{veins} $\to$ left \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{atrium} $\to$ mitral valve $\to$ left
\omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricle} $\to$ aortic valve $\to$ aorta.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:2}
Filling (diastole): AV valves open, semilunar closed. Isovolumetric
contraction: all four closed. Ejection: semilunar open, AV closed.
Isovolumetric relaxation: all closed. First sound: the AV valves
closing at the start of systole; second: the semilunar valves
closing at its end.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:3}
\omterm{prop:b2:heart:conduction}{Sinoatrial node} ($t = 0$; the P wave as the atria depolarise);
\omterm{prop:b2:heart:conduction}{atrioventricular node} (reached at about \qty{80}{ms}, holding the
impulse for \qty{100}{ms}: the PR segment); bundle of His and
\omterm{prop:b2:heart:conduction}{Purkinje fibres} (\qtyrange{180}{220}{ms}; the QRS as the \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles}
depolarise); the T wave later as they repolarise.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:4}
Within its working range the \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricle} ejects more when it has been
filled more. If the right \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricle} pumps more, the left receives
more a few beats later and, by the same law, pumps more: any
imbalance corrects itself without a signal.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:5}
\omterm{prop:b2:heart:cycle}{Stroke volume} \qty{80}{mL}; \omterm{prop:b2:heart:cycle}{ejection fraction} $80/130 = \qty{62}{\%}$;
output $80\times 70 = \qty{5.6}{L/min}$. Failing: \qty{60}{mL},
$60/180 = \qty{33}{\%}$, \qty{4.2}{L/min} --- the dilated \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricle}
keeps the output nearly up by working at a large volume, but ejects
only a third of what it holds.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:6}
$W = 100\times 133\times 7\times 10^{-5} = \qty{0.93}{J}$; power
$0.93\times 1.2 = \qty{1.1}{W}$. At \qty{150}{mmHg}: \qty{1.4}{J},
\qty{1.7}{W}. Extra \qty{0.56}{W} mechanical is \qty{3.7}{W}
metabolic, \qty{0.19}{mL} of oxygen a second: \qty{11}{mL/min} more.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:7}
$60/0.5 = 120$ beats a minute; $60/1.5 = 40$. Third: complete \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{heart}
block --- the atria beat at 100 under the node, the \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles} at 33
under a pacemaker of their own, and the AV node conducts nothing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:8}
Drift $20/25 = \qty{0.8}{s}$, plus \qty{0.15}{s}: \qty{0.95}{s}, 63
beats a minute. Acetylcholine: $25/18 = \qty{1.39}{s}$, plus $0.15$:
\qty{1.54}{s}, 39 a minute. Noradrenaline: $20/40 = \qty{0.5}{s}$,
plus $0.15$: \qty{0.65}{s}, 92 a minute.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:9}
The delay lets the atria finish emptying into the \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles} before
the \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles} contract; without it atria and \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles} would
contract together, the AV valves would shut on half-filled
\omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles} and the \omterm{prop:b2:heart:cycle}{stroke volume} would fall by the atrial
contribution. A long PR interval merely postpones ventricular
systole and costs nothing until the delay grows so long that beats
are dropped or conduction fails altogether.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:10}
Rest: $5000/45 = \qty{111}{mL}$; exercise: $\num{30000}/180 =
\qty{167}{mL}$. The Starling law converts the larger venous return
of exercise into a larger \omterm{prop:b2:heart:cycle}{stroke volume}; the sympathetic nerves add
rate and contractility (a lower end-systolic volume). Above about
200 the diastole is too short to fill the \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricle} and the \omterm{prop:b2:heart:cycle}{stroke
volume} falls faster than the rate rises.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:11}
$v = Q/A$: $250/3 = \qty{83}{cm/s}$ and $250/1 = \qty{250}{cm/s}$.
$\Delta P = \tfrac12\times 1060\times v^{2}$: \qty{365}{Pa}
(\qty{2.7}{mmHg}) and \qty{3300}{Pa} (\qty{25}{mmHg}). The \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricle}
generates the extra pressure by thickening its wall; the thick,
stiff \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricle} eventually outgrows its coronary supply and fills
poorly, and fails.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:heart:12}
Automaticity gives a beat without any input; the Starling law
matches output to return and balances the two \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles} without
any input; the nerves and adrenaline only set the rate and
contractility around that self-regulated core. A transplanted
\omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{heart} beats (at about 100, without vagal tone), adjusts its \omterm{prop:b2:heart:cycle}{stroke
volume} to filling, and can raise its output in exercise by the
Starling mechanism and by circulating adrenaline --- but slowly,
and less than an innervated \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{heart}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:heart:1}
\textbf{1.} \qty{70}{mL}; $70/130 = \qty{54}{\%}$; $70\times 70 =
\qty{4.9}{L/min}$.
\textbf{2.} $60/70 = \qty{0.86}{s}$; diastole $0.86 - 0.3 =
\qty{0.56}{s}$.
\textbf{3.} $70/0.3 = \qty{233}{mL/s}$; $233/3 = \qty{78}{cm/s}$.
\textbf{4.} $70/1500 = \qty{0.047}{s}$, about \qty{50}{ms}.
\textbf{5.} $100\times 133\times 7\times 10^{-5} = \qty{0.93}{J}$;
$0.93\times 70/60 = \qty{1.1}{W}$.
\textbf{6.} Both \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles} $1.1\times 1.2 = \qty{1.3}{W}$; at
\qty{15}{\%}, \qty{8.7}{W} metabolic; $8.7/20 = \qty{0.43}{mL}$ of
oxygen a second, \qty{26}{mL/min}.
\textbf{7.} Extracting \qty{0.14}{mL} per millilitre of \omterm{def:b2:blood-circulation:blood}{blood}:
$26/0.14 = \qty{190}{mL/min}$.
\textbf{8.} Interval \qty{0.86}{s}, drift time \qty{0.71}{s}:
$20/0.71 = \qty{28}{mV/s}$.
\textbf{9.} Interval \qty{0.6}{s}, drift \qty{0.45}{s}: \qty{44}{mV/s}.
\textbf{10.} Interval \qty{0.33}{s}, drift \qty{0.18}{s}: \qty{110}{mV/s}.
\textbf{11.} R--R \qty{0.86}{s}; P: atrial depolarisation; QRS:
ventricular depolarisation; T: ventricular repolarisation.
\textbf{12.} The delay lets the atria empty into the \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles}
before they contract; at 180 the whole cycle is \qty{0.33}{s}, so an
unchanged \qty{0.16}{s} delay would consume half of it --- the
sympathetic nerves speed the node's conduction as well.
\textbf{13.} Complete \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{heart} block: the \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles} at 40 a minute,
paced by the bundle or \omterm{prop:b2:heart:conduction}{Purkinje fibres}, the atria at 75 under the
\omterm{prop:b2:heart:conduction}{sinoatrial node}.
\textbf{14.} \qty{120}{mL}; $120/150 = \qty{80}{\%}$; $120\times 180 =
\qty{21.6}{L/min}$.
\textbf{15.} $0.33 - 0.3 = \qty{0.03}{s}$: almost no time to fill;
faster still and the \omterm{prop:b2:heart:cycle}{stroke volume} collapses.
\textbf{16.} $W = 120\times 133\times 1.2\times 10^{-4} = \qty{1.9}{J}$,
$\times 3$ a second: \qty{5.7}{W}; both \omterm{def:b2:heart:anatomy}{ventricles} \qty{6.9}{W};
metabolic \qty{46}{W}; oxygen \qty{2.3}{mL/s}, \qty{140}{mL/min}.
\textbf{17.} $140/0.14 = \qty{1000}{mL/min}$, five times the resting
flow, to be delivered in a diastole shrunk to a tenth of the cycle:
the coronary \omterm{def:b2:blood-circulation:vessels}{arterioles} must dilate to their limit.
\textbf{18.} Rate about 100 without vagal tone, end-systolic volume
unchanged at \qty{60}{mL}: \omterm{prop:b2:heart:cycle}{stroke volume} $150 - 60 = \qty{90}{mL}$,
output \qty{9}{L/min}.
\textbf{19.} Rate alone ($70 \to 180$ at \qty{70}{mL}): $+12.9$;
filling (\qty{20}{mL} more at 180): $+3.6$; contractility
(\qty{30}{mL} less residual at 180): $+5.4$ --- from 4.9 to
\qty{21.6}{L/min} (the three add to $21.9$ because the parts
interact).
\textbf{20.} $233/1 = \qty{233}{cm/s}$, \qty{2.3}{m/s}.
\textbf{21.} $\tfrac12\times 1060\times 2.33^{2} = \qty{2900}{Pa}$,
\qty{22}{mmHg}.
\textbf{22.} $120/0.3 = \qty{400}{mL/s}$, \qty{4}{m/s}; $\tfrac12\times
1060\times 16 = \qty{8500}{Pa}$, \qty{64}{mmHg}.
\textbf{23.} Peak $120 + 22 = \qty{142}{mmHg}$ at rest, about $140 +
64 = \qty{204}{mmHg}$ in exercise; the mean ejection pressure at
rest rises from 100 to 122: work per beat $\times 1.22$.
\textbf{24.} A thicker wall generates more force and lowers the
stress per fibre; but the mass to be perfused grows while the
coronary flow, squeezed by the thick wall in systole and given a
shorter diastole, does not keep pace, and a thick wall relaxes
poorly and fills less: ischaemia and failure follow.
\textbf{25.} \qty{4.9}{L/min} at rest, \qty{21.6}{L/min} in exercise;
\qty{0.93}{J} per beat; gradient \qty{22}{mmHg} at rest and
\qty{64}{mmHg} in exercise.
\end{solution}
