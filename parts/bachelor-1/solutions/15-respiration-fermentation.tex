\begin{solution}{exo:b1:respiration-fermentation:1}
\omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{Glycolysis}, \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:organelle}{cytosol}: 2 pyruvate, 2 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}, 2 NADH. Pyruvate oxidation and
\omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:krebs}{Krebs cycle}, \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:mitochondrion}{mitochondrial matrix}: 6 $\mathrm{CO_2}$, 8 NADH, 2

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:respiration-fermentation:2}
Glucose $+ 2\,\mathrm{NAD^+} + 2\,\mathrm{ADP} + 2\,\mathrm{P_i} \to
2$ pyruvate $+ 2\,\mathrm{NADH} + 2\,\mathrm{H^+} + 2\,\mathrm{ATP} +
2\,\mathrm{H_2O}$. \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{Substrate-level phosphorylation}: a phosphate is
transferred directly from a high-energy intermediate
(1,3-bisphosphoglycerate, phosphoenolpyruvate) to ADP by an \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme},
without a membrane gradient or oxygen.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:respiration-fermentation:3}
NADH and $\mathrm{CO_2}$ (isocitrate to $\alpha$-ketoglutarate), NADH
and $\mathrm{CO_2}$ ($\alpha$-ketoglutarate to succinyl-CoA), GTP
(succinyl-CoA to succinate), $\mathrm{FADH_2}$ (succinate to fumarate),
NADH (malate to oxaloacetate). The two $\mathrm{CO_2}$ come from the
two decarboxylations of the six- and five-carbon acids.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:respiration-fermentation:4}
It regenerates $\mathrm{NAD^+}$ so that \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{glycolysis} can continue and
yield its 2 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} per glucose. It does not oxidise the carbon, extract
the remaining energy (the product keeps nearly all of it) or make more
than a fifteenth of respiration's \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:respiration-fermentation:7}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:respiration-fermentation:8}
Oxygen consumption rises (the chain, freed from the back-pressure of
the gradient, runs at full speed), \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} synthesis stops (no gradient to
drive the synthase), and all the energy of the electrons appears as
heat. Patients burned their \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} fast and died of a body temperature
that nothing could bring down.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:respiration-fermentation:9}
The chain backs up: every carrier becomes reduced, no protons are
pumped, no \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} is made. NADH is not re-oxidised, so the \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:krebs}{Krebs cycle}
and pyruvate dehydrogenase stop for lack of $\mathrm{NAD^+}$.
\omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{Glycolysis}, released from \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} inhibition, runs fast and must regenerate
its $\mathrm{NAD^+}$ by reducing pyruvate to lactate, which floods the
blood.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:respiration-fermentation:10}
$8\times 10 + 7\times 2.5 + 7\times 1.5 - 2 = 80 + 17.5 + 10.5 - 2 =
106$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}; $106/16 = 6.6$ per carbon. Oxygen: palmitate 23
$\mathrm{O_2}$ for 106 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}, \qty{0.22}{O_2} per \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}; glucose 6 for 30,
\qty{0.20}{O_2} per \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}. Per unit of oxygen glucose is \qty{10}{\%}
better: the seal, oxygen-limited underwater, prefers \omterm{def:b1:carbohydrates:monosaccharide}{carbohydrate}; per
unit of mass \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} is twice as good: the hummingbird, mass-limited in
flight, migrates on \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:respiration-fermentation:11}
\omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} alone: $5/3 < \qty{2}{s}$. Phosphocreatine: $25/3 = \qty{8}{s}$.
\omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:fermentation}{Fermentation}: $2\times 3 = \qty{6}{mmol/L}$ of \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} per second, enough
for the demand, for as long as \omterm{def:b1:carbohydrates:polysaccharide}{glycogen} and tolerance to lactate last
(tens of seconds). Oxygen delivery takes a minute or more to rise and
supplies at most about \qty{1}{mmol/L} of \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} per second in a fibre: a
ten-second race is over before respiration has begun to help, and is
paid for by phosphocreatine and \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:fermentation}{fermentation}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:respiration-fermentation:12}
The gradient stores energy as a difference of proton concentration and
of electrical potential across an insulating membrane --- exactly a
charged capacitor plus a concentration \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell}; the chain is the charger,
driven by the fall of electrons; the synthase is a motor turned by the
proton current, exactly a turbine, coupling the flow to a mechanical
rotation that makes \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}. The analogy is loose in that the "battery"
holds only milliseconds of the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell}'s consumption and must be charged
continuously, that the same membrane's other transporters also draw on
it, and that the turbine can run backward, hydrolysing \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} to pump
protons when the chain fails.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b1:respiration-fermentation:1}
\textbf{1.} \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{Glycolysis}: 2 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}, 2 NADH (cytosolic). Pyruvate
dehydrogenase: 2 NADH. Krebs: 6 NADH, 2 $\mathrm{FADH_2}$, 2 GTP.
\textbf{2.} Mitochondrial NADH $8\times 10 = 80$; $\mathrm{FADH_2}$
$2\times 6 = 12$; cytosolic NADH as $\mathrm{FADH_2}$ $2\times 6 = 12$:
104 protons.
\textbf{3.} $104/4 = 26$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}; total $26 + 2 + 2 = 30$.
\textbf{4.} 2 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}.
\textbf{5.} 15.
\textbf{6.} With air $30\times 50 = \qty{1500}{kJ}$: \qty{52}{\%} of
2870. Without: \qty{100}{kJ}, \qty{3.5}{\%} of 2870 but \qty{43}{\%} of
the 235 released --- \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:fermentation}{fermentation} is efficient at capturing what it
releases; it just releases little.
\textbf{7.} Aerated $30/30 = \qty{1}{mmol}$ of glucose per hour; sealed
$30/2 = \qty{15}{mmol}$.
\textbf{8.} Sealed: \qty{30}{mmol} of ethanol and \qty{30}{mmol} of
$\mathrm{CO_2}$ per hour. Aerated: \qty{6}{mmol} of $\mathrm{CO_2}$
released and \qty{6}{mmol} of $\mathrm{O_2}$ consumed.
\textbf{9.} $2\times 10.5 = \qty{21}{g}$ per mole, \qty{0.12}{g} per
gram of glucose.
\textbf{10.} $0.5\times 180 = \qty{90}{g}$ per mole, i.e.\ $90/10.5 =
8.6$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}'s worth against 30 available: \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} is in excess; the yield is
limited by carbon (half the glucose is oxidised to $\mathrm{CO_2}$ to
make the \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}, and biomass needs carbon skeletons and reducing power),
and the surplus \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} is spent on maintenance.
\textbf{11.} To get the same \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} the sealed culture consumes fifteen
times more glucose (question 7) and grows a fifth as much per gram
(question 9): fast sugar consumption, little growth, alcohol ---
Pasteur's observation. Phosphofructokinase, inhibited by \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} and
citrate under aeration, is released when respiration stops.
\textbf{12.} $\mathrm{CO_2}$ $44/44 = \qty{1.0}{mmol}$; $\mathrm{O_2}$
$20/32 = \qty{0.625}{mmol}$: RQ $= 1.6$.
\textbf{13.} $\mathrm{O_2}$: $6x = 0.625$, $x = \qty{0.104}{mmol}$.
$\mathrm{CO_2}$: $6x + 2y = 1.0$, $2y = 0.375$, $y = \qty{0.1875}{mmol}$.
\textbf{14.} Fermented $0.1875/0.292 = \qty{64}{\%}$ of the glucose. \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}
from \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:fermentation}{fermentation} $2y = 0.375$ against $30x = 3.12$ from respiration:
\qty{11}{\%}.
\textbf{15.} $16/23 = 0.70$.
\textbf{16.} Fasted, the animal burns \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} (RQ 0.7); fed, it burns the
\omterm{def:b1:carbohydrates:monosaccharide}{carbohydrate} of the meal (1.0).
\textbf{17.} $96\,500\times 0.22 = \qty{21.2}{kJ/mol}$.
\textbf{18.} $4\times 21.2 = \qty{85}{kJ}$ per \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} of \qty{50}{kJ}:
\qty{59}{\%}.
\textbf{19.} $2\times 96\,500\times 1.14 = \qty{220}{kJ}$; ten protons
store $10\times 21.2 = \qty{212}{kJ}$: \qty{96}{\%} --- the chain wastes
little; the losses are at the synthase and the transporters.
\textbf{20.} $0.96\times 0.59 = 0.57$; $2.5\times 50/220 = 0.57$: the
same figure by both routes.
\textbf{21.} Oxygen consumption rises, \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} yield falls toward that of
\omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:fermentation}{fermentation} (2 per glucose from \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{glycolysis}, and PFK is released, so
glucose consumption rises), heat output rises, growth collapses.
\textbf{22.} \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} synthesis stops; the gradient cannot discharge, the
chain stalls against it, oxygen consumption stops, the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} ferments
what it can. Adding an uncoupler then lets protons leak back: the chain
and oxygen consumption resume, still with no \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}.
\textbf{23.} NADH pumps only through complexes III and IV: $6/4 = 1.5$;
per glucose $8\times 1.5 + 2\times 1.5 + 2\times 1.5 + 4 = 22$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}.
\textbf{24.} With no oxygen the chain cannot take electrons, so NADH
is not re-oxidised and the pool stays reduced; the three
NAD-dependent dehydrogenases of the cycle have no $\mathrm{NAD^+}$ and
the cycle stops --- which is why \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:fermentation}{fermentation} must regenerate
$\mathrm{NAD^+}$ in the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:organelle}{cytosol} for \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:glycolysis}{glycolysis} to go on.
\textbf{25.} 30 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} with air, 2 without: a ratio of 15; P/O 2.5 for
NADH (10 protons over 4 per \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}) and 1.5 for $\mathrm{FADH_2}$ (6 over
4).
\end{solution}
