\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:genomics} --- Genomics and Sequencing}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:1}
A \omterm{def:b3:genomics:sanger}{dideoxynucleotide} has no 3$'$ hydroxyl, so no phosphodiester bond
can be made to the next nucleotide and the chain stops. With only
dideoxy forms every chain would stop at the first position; with only
normal forms none would stop. The mixture makes termination a random
event at each position, so that the products form a complete ladder,
one length for every base of the template.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:2}
$4\times 10^{8}\times \qty{300}{bp} = 1.2\times 10^{11}$ bases. Human:
$1.2\times 10^{11}/3.2\times 10^{9} \approx 38\times$. Bacterium:
$1.2\times 10^{11}/5\times 10^{6} = \num{24000}\times$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:3}
A contig is a contiguous sequence assembled from overlapping \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads}; a
\omterm{def:b3:genomics:assembly}{scaffold} is an ordered, oriented set of contigs with gaps of estimated
size between them; the \omterm{def:b3:genomics:assembly}{N50} is the contig length at which the sorted
contigs reach half the assembled bases. Here the ten \qty{8}{Mb}
contigs alone contain \qty{80}{Mb}, past the \qty{50}{Mb} half-way
point (the seventh reaches \qty{56}{Mb}): \omterm{def:b3:genomics:assembly}{N50} $= \qty{8}{Mb}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:4}
\omterm{prop:b3:genomics:sizes}{Genome size} does not track organismal complexity: an onion has five
times the DNA of a human, a lungfish forty times; yeast and the worm
differ tenfold in DNA with similar gene numbers. The excess is
non-coding: transposable elements and their remains, introns,
satellite repeats.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:5}
$e^{-c} = 10^{-6}$ gives $c = 6\ln 10 = 13.8$. For $c = 8$: $N =
cG/L = 8\times 5\times 10^{6}/150 \approx \num{267000}$ \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads}, $\theta
= 30/150 = 0.2$, contigs $= N e^{-6.4} = \num{267000}\times 0.00166
\approx 440$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:6}
$P(X < 8) = P(X \le 7) \approx P\bigl(Z < (7.5 - 15)/2.74\bigr) =
P(Z < -2.74) \approx 0.003$. At $30\times$ both alleles of a
heterozygote are almost always seen many times, coverage is uneven
(GC-rich regions get fewer \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads}, so some sites see half the average),
and enough \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads} remain to separate a real allele from the $10^{-3}$
errors.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:7}
Every \qty{150}{bp} \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{read} from inside the repeat is identical whichever
copy it came from, so the assembler builds one \qty{6}{kb} node with
$500$ paths entering and $500$ leaving; it cannot tell which entry
pairs with which exit, and the \omterm{def:b3:genomics:assembly}{assembly} breaks into $500$ gaps, the
repeat present once with a coverage $500$ times the mean. \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{Reads} longer
than the repeat plus unique flanks on both sides --- \qty{8}{kb} or
more --- span each copy and resolve it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:8}
$4.8\times 10^{7}/1000 = \num{48000}$ coding differences; one third,
about \num{16000}, alter the protein.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:9}
Expected false positives $10^{6}\times 5\times 10^{-8} = 0.05$. A
relative risk of $1.15$ on a \qty{2}{\%} disease changes a few cases
per thousand; a thousand people contain about twenty cases, far too
few to see it (power grows with the number of cases and the square of
the effect). For an individual, $0.3$ percentage points is
meaningless. But the allele marks a gene whose modest change in
activity alters disease risk --- the gene, and its pathway, may be a
drug target whose complete inhibition has a large effect.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:10}
Population genetic: in bacteria, with enormous populations, selection
removes even slightly costly insertions; in mammals, with small
effective populations, drift lets mildly deleterious non-coding DNA
accumulate --- supported by the inverse correlation across lineages
between \omterm{prop:b3:genomics:sizes}{genome size} and population size, undermined by exceptions.
Structural: eukaryotic genomes are invaded by transposons that
bacteria, with a bias toward deletion and no meiotic refuge for
selfish elements, purge --- supported by the correlation of \omterm{prop:b3:genomics:sizes}{genome size}
with transposon content. Regulatory: complex development needs more
regulatory DNA --- supported by the \omterm{def:b3:genomics:comparative}{conserved non-coding elements}
around developmental genes, undermined by the fact that only
\qty{8}{\%} of the human genome shows any selection at all, so most
non-coding DNA is not regulatory.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:11}
Starts of both kinds fall with total density $\rho = (N_{1} +
N_{2})/G$. A \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{read} of length $L_{i}$ is rightmost in its contig if no
\omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{read} of either kind starts in the $L_{i} - T$ bases after it:
probability $e^{-\rho(L_{i} - T)}$. Hence contigs $= N_{1}
e^{-\rho(L_{1} - T)} + N_{2} e^{-\rho(L_{2} - T)}$. A long \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{read} is
rightmost with an exponentially smaller probability than a short one,
and each long \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{read} also removes the chance of a gap over its whole
length; the same number of bases as short \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads} adds many start
points but each protected window is short, so the long \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads} win.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:genomics:12}
Two \omterm{def:b3:genomics:comparative}{whole-genome duplications} predict that the fourfold pattern is
genome-wide: quartets of paralogous chromosome segments (paralogons)
carrying the same gene families in the same order, with the
duplications dated to the same time for all families; a single cluster
in amphioxus and \emph{Ciona}, which diverged before the events; and
in lampreys, which diverged around them, an intermediate or
independently derived state. Independent duplications of the Hox
cluster alone predict paralogons for Hox only, neighbours with
different histories, and duplication dates that differ between
families. The genome-wide paralogons and the shared dating, as
observed, favour \omterm{def:b3:genomics:comparative}{whole-genome duplication}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:genomics:1}
\textbf{1.} $0.002\times 6\times 10^{8} = 1.2\times 10^{6}$ \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads};
$c = 1.2\times 10^{6}\times 150/4.6\times 10^{6} \approx 39$.
\textbf{2.} $e^{-39} \approx 10^{-17}$: effectively no base uncovered.
\textbf{3.} $\theta = 0.2$; contigs $= 1.2\times 10^{6} e^{-31} \approx
0$: one contig in theory, gaps only at repeats.
\textbf{4.} $c = \num{30000}\times 150/4.6\times 10^{6} = 0.98$;
uncovered $e^{-0.98} = 0.38$, about \qty{1.7}{Mb}; contigs $= \num{30000}
\times e^{-0.78} \approx \num{13700}$.
\textbf{5.} The seven operons give identical \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads} and collapse into one
\qty{5}{kb} contig, entered by seven unique left flanks and left by
seven right flanks whose pairing is unknown: $14$ contig ends, seven
gaps.
\textbf{6.} About \num{4600} genes; coding $0.88\times 4.6\times 10^{6}
= 4.0\times 10^{6}$ bp, mean gene \qty{880}{bp}.
\textbf{7.} $0.998\times 6\times 10^{8}\times 150/3.2\times 10^{9}
\approx 28\times$.
\textbf{8.} About $14$ \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads} per allele.
\textbf{9.} $28\times 10^{-3}/3 \approx 0.009$ \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads} showing a given
wrong base --- the chance of three or more is about $10^{-7}$ --- and
\qty{20}{\%} of $28$ is $5.6$ \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads}; an error passes neither test,
while a real heterozygous allele is expected in $14$.
\textbf{10.} $3.2\times 10^{9}/1500 \approx 2.1\times 10^{6}$
heterozygous sites; in coding sequence $4.8\times 10^{7}/1500 \approx
\num{32000}$.
\textbf{11.} A \qty{150}{bp} \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{read} from an Alu matches many of the $1.1$
million copies almost equally well, so it is placed on the wrong copy
or given a low mapping confidence. Differences between copies then
look like heterozygous variants, and true variants are hidden among
them: variant calls inside Alu are usually filtered out, and the
elements are blind spots of \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{short-read} sequencing.
\textbf{12.} $1.2\times 10^{-8}\times 6.4\times 10^{9} \approx 77$ new
mutations. About $14$ \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads} should show the variant in the child; but
if a parent's site is covered by only five \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads}, a heterozygous allele
is missed with probability $2^{-5} = 3\,\%$ and an inherited variant is
miscalled as new --- so the parents must be sequenced as deeply as the
child.
\textbf{13.} $4.8\times 10^{7}\times 100/150 = 3.2\times 10^{7}$ \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads},
twenty times fewer than the genome: the sequencing cost falls
twentyfold, more than the cost of the capture step.
\textbf{14.} $1.1\times 10^{6}\times 300 = 3.3\times 10^{8}$ bp, about
\qty{10}{\%} of the genome; \qty{10}{\%} of the \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads}, some $6\times
10^{7}$.
\textbf{15.} $\num{60000}\times 1500 = 9\times 10^{7}$ bp; $9\times
10^{7}/1.6\times 10^{10} = 0.56\,\%$.
\textbf{16.} $0.012\times 2.9\times 10^{9} = 3.5\times 10^{7}$
substitutions, $1.7\times 10^{7}$ per lineage; rate $1.7\times
10^{7}/(2.9\times 10^{9}\times 6.5\times 10^{6}) = 9\times 10^{-10}$
per base per year, $2.3\times 10^{-8}$ per generation of 25 years ---
about twice the pedigree rate of $1.2\times 10^{-8}$, a known
discrepancy that suggests longer past generation times or an older
split.
\textbf{17.} Four copies of \num{16000} would be \num{64000}; \num{20000}
remain, so of the \num{48000} extra copies only \num{4000} survive:
\qty{92}{\%} of the duplicates were lost.
\textbf{18.} Worm and human have the same number of genes; complexity
lies in how they are used --- alternative splicing multiplies one gene
into thousands of proteins (\num{38016} for \emph{Dscam}), regulation
combines transcription factors and enhancers in cell-type-specific
ways, \omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{non-coding RNAs} add layers, and proteins interact in networks.
\textbf{19.} Regulatory elements (enhancers, promoters, insulators),
\omterm{def:b3:rna-regulation:ncrna}{non-coding RNA} genes, splicing and localisation signals, origins and
structural sequences. Test an enhancer candidate by placing it before
a reporter gene in a transgenic embryo and looking at where the
reporter is expressed, then deleting the element in the genome with
CRISPR and measuring the neighbouring genes.
\textbf{20.} $0.05/10^{6} = 5\times 10^{-8}$; at $p < 10^{-5}$, $10^{6}
\times 10^{-5} = 10$ false positives expected.
\textbf{21.} Non-carrier odds $0.009/0.991 = 0.00908$; one copy odds
$0.00954$, risk $0.945\,\%$; two copies odds $0.01001$, risk
$0.99\,\%$.
\textbf{22.} The score sums, over the $200$ alleles, the number of risk
copies a person carries weighted by each allele's log odds ratio. The
count has mean $200\times 2\times 0.3 = 120$ and standard deviation
$\sqrt{200\times 2\times 0.3\times 0.7} \approx 9$; the top \qty{1}{\%}
carries about $21$ alleles more than average, and $1.05^{21} \approx
2.8$: nearly threefold the odds of an average person, from alleles
that individually change risk by \qty{5}{\%}.
\textbf{23.} Alleles within a block of tens of kilobases are inherited
together (linkage disequilibrium), so any of them shows the same
association; the genotyped SNP is merely the one on the array. To find
the causal variant: sequence the region in cases and controls, narrow
the set statistically, then test each candidate --- reporter assays
for enhancer activity of each allele, editing each allele in cells and
measuring the nearby genes' expression, colocalisation with
expression-quantitative-trait signals.
\textbf{24.} $1 - e^{-0.5} = 39\,\%$ of the genome is \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{read}. It gives a
direct sample of a population at a known time in the past --- allele
frequencies before later migrations and admixture, the length of
Neanderthal segments (long, because few generations of recombination
had broken them) and hence a date for the admixture --- which modern
genomes can only infer through models.
\textbf{25.} About \num{13700} contigs in the pilot; $28\times$ per
haploid genome, about $14$ \omterm{def:b3:genomics:ngs}{reads} per allele; some $77$ new mutations in
a child.
\end{solution}
