\chapter{Sorting Living Things}\label{ch:g3:sorting-living-things}

Tip a box of mixed buttons on the table and your hands start
\omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{sorting} by themselves: big with big, red with red, four holes with
four holes. Naturalists felt the same urge in front of the living
world's jumble of beetles, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds}, ferns and \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} --- and \omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{sorting}
\omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} carefully turned out to be one of \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{biology}'s most
powerful ideas.

\section{Sorting needs a rule}

\begin{definition}[Sorting and criteria]\label{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}
To \emph{sort}\index{sorting} a collection is to put together the
things that go together. A \emph{criterion}\index{criterion} is
the rule used for the sorting --- ``has six \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}'', ``has
\omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}''. A good criterion for \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} is a feature of
the body you can \emph{observe}: something the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} or \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant}
\emph{has}, not the place it lives or what it happens to be doing.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Two sortings of the same animals]\label{ex:g3:sorting-living-things:two}
Take the cat, the duck, the trout and the ladybug. Sorted by
``lives near water'': duck and trout together. Sorted by ``has
\omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}'': the duck stands alone. Both are \omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{sortings} --- but only
the second uses the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}'s own body, and that is the kind
biologists trust: an \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} can move house, but it cannot change
its \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers} for scales.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Why not sort by home or menu?]\label{rem:g3:sorting-living-things:why}
\omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{Habitat} and menu change with the seasons and with luck --- the
blackbird of \cref{ch:g2:what-animals-eat} switches from worms to
berries without becoming a different \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}. Body features stay.
That is why ``what it has'' beats ``what it does'' as a \omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{sorting}
rule.
\end{remark}

\section{The great groups of animals}

\begin{proposition}[Some big groups]\label{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}
\omterm{prop:g2:caring-for-nature:recycle}{Sorting} by body features gathers \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} into big groups,
including:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{insects}\index{insect}: six \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}, a body in three
        parts --- the ant, the ladybug, the bee, the butterfly;
  \item \emph{fish}\index{fish}: fins and scales, life spent in
        water --- the trout, the goldfish;
  \item \emph{birds}\index{bird}: \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}, wings and a beak ---
        the duck, the blackbird, the ostrich;
  \item \emph{mammals}\index{mammal}: \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur} or hair, and mothers
        that feed their young \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk} --- the cat, the cow, the bat,
        the whale --- and humans;
  \item \emph{amphibians}\index{amphibian}: moist \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}, \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} laid
        in water, a tadpole childhood --- the frog, the newt.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Surprises done right]\label{ex:g3:sorting-living-things:surprises}
Criteria save us from lazy guesses. The bat flies like a bird ---
but it has \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur}, no \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}, and feeds its young \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk}: a mammal.
The whale swims like a \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} --- \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur} reduced to almost nothing, but
\omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk} for its calf and no scales: a mammal too. The ostrich cannot
fly at all, yet \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers} and beak say: bird. The \omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{criterion}, not
the impression, decides.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Sorting an unknown animal]\label{met:g3:sorting-living-things:sort}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Observe the body: \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}, \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur}, \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers} or scales? how many
        \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}? wings? a beak?
  \item Ask about the young if you can: \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk}? \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}? a tadpole
        stage?
  \item Compare with the groups of
        \cref{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups} and place the
        \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} where its \emph{features} fit;
  \item if an impression (``it flies!'') disagrees with a feature
        (``it has \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur}''), trust the feature.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\section{Plants get sorted too}

\begin{proposition}[Sorting plants]\label{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:plants}
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{Plants} are sorted by their features just like \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals}. Useful
criteria: does it make \emph{\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{flowers}} and \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seeds} (the daisy, the
chestnut) or never any (the fern, the moss)? Is its \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{stem} soft and
green, or woody like a \emph{tree}'s trunk? Are the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} broad,
or needles like the pine's?
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Three corners of the park]\label{ex:g3:sorting-living-things:park}
In one park: the rose bush --- \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{flowers}, woody \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{stems}; the moss on
the north wall --- no \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{flowers} ever, no real \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{stem} at all; the pine
--- a tree with needles and cones. Three \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants}, three different
answers to the same criteria.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Sorting is the first step of a science]\label{rem:g3:sorting-living-things:science}
Well-sorted groups let people ask better questions: whatever is
true of \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects} in general --- six \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}, a body in three parts,
often wings --- is suddenly known for hundreds of thousands of
species at once. Later chapters will turn \omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{sorting} into a family
tree of all \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} --- for now, learn to sort by what you
can see.
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:sorting-living-things:1}
What is a \omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{criterion}? Give one good \omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{criterion} for \omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{sorting} \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals},
and one that biologists avoid.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:sorting-living-things:2}
Give the feature (or features) that defines each group: \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects},
\omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish}, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds}, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{mammals}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:sorting-living-things:3}
Place each \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} in its group: the bee, the goldfish, the
ostrich, the cow, the frog.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:sorting-living-things:4}
The bat flies. Why is it a mammal and not a bird? Which features
decide?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:sorting-living-things:5}
The whale lives in the sea. Why is it not a \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish}? Which features
decide?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:sorting-living-things:6}
A spider has eight \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}. Can it be an insect? What does the
\omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{criterion} answer?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:sorting-living-things:7}
Sort these \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} with the criteria of
\cref{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:plants}: the daisy, the moss,
the pine, the rose bush.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:sorting-living-things:8}
Try it: sort the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} of your street or garden --- every one
you can spot in a walk --- into the groups of this chapter. Which
group wins by number?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g3:sorting-living-things:9}
Lea sorts \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} into ``pets'' and ``wild \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals}''. Her friend
sorts the same \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} into ``\omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{mammals}'' and ``\omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds}''. Whose
\omterm{def:g3:sorting-living-things:criterion}{sorting} is the biologist's kind, and why? Use
\cref{rem:g3:sorting-living-things:why}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g3:sorting-living-things:10}
A penguin swims superbly, cannot fly, and is covered in what look
like shiny tiles. A naturalist looks closer: the ``tiles'' are
tightly packed \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}, and there is a beak. Run
\cref{met:g3:sorting-living-things:sort} on the penguin, step by
step.
\end{exercise}
