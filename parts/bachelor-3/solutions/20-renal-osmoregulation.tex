\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:renal-osmoregulation} --- Renal Physiology and Osmoregulation}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:1}
\omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:nephron}{Glomerulus}: filters plasma minus proteins. \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:nephron}{Proximal tubule}: reabsorbs
two thirds of the salt and water, all glucose and amino acids,
bicarbonate. Descending limb of Henle: loses water to the medulla.
Ascending limb: pumps out NaCl, impermeable to water. Distal tubule:
adjusts sodium (\omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:controls}{aldosterone}), secretes potassium. \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:nephron}{Collecting duct}:
water reabsorption under vasopressin, proton and urea handling.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:2}
Clearance: the volume of plasma cleared of the substance per unit
time, $UV/P$. Here $100\times 1.5/2 = \qty{75}{mL/min}$, below the GFR
of \qty{120}{mL/min}: the substance is filtered and partly reabsorbed
(about \qty{38}{\%} of the filtered amount).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:3}
A freshwater fish is saltier than its medium: water enters through
the gills by osmosis, so drinking would only add to the flood; it
excretes copious dilute urine and its gill \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:comparative}{chloride cells} take up
Na$^{+}$ and Cl$^{-}$ actively from the water. A marine fish is less
salty than the sea: it loses water through the gills, drinks sea water
to replace it, and the \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:comparative}{chloride cells} pump the swallowed salt out.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:4}
The \omterm{prop:b3:renal-osmoregulation:countercurrent}{single effect} is the \qty{200}{mOsm/L} difference the thick
ascending limb maintains between the interstitium and its own fluid
by pumping NaCl out while letting no water follow. The ascending limb
must be water-impermeable, or water would follow the salt and the
difference would vanish; the descending limb must be water-permeable so
that the fluid reaching the bend has already been concentrated by the
interstitium and each stage works on saltier fluid than the last. Two
limbs with the same permeability could not multiply.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:5}
Afferent end: $60 - 18 - 28 = \qty{14}{mmHg}$; efferent end: $60 - 18 -
35 = \qty{7}{mmHg}$. As water is filtered the proteins left behind
concentrate and the oncotic pressure rises along the capillary; if it
reaches \qty{42}{mmHg} the net pressure is zero and filtration stops
before the end (filtration equilibrium). The GFR then depends on the
plasma flow: a faster flow concentrates the proteins more slowly, so
filtration continues over a longer stretch and the GFR rises with the
flow.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:6}
Filtered load: $\qty{40}{mL/min}\times\qty{0.14}{mmol/mL} =
\qty{5.6}{mmol/min}$. Excretion: $70\times 1 = \qty{0.07}{mmol/min}$.
Reabsorbed: $5.53/5.6 = \qty{98.75}{\%}$. Sodium clearance: $70\times
1/140 = \qty{0.5}{mL/min}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:7}
$900/1200 = \qty{0.75}{L}$ a day. At \qty{400}{mOsm/L}: $900/400 =
\qty{2.25}{L}$ of urine, so about $2.25 + 0.9 = \qty{3.15}{L}$ must be
drunk (less the water in food): the patient with a failing
concentrating mechanism must drink, and is in danger if they cannot.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:8}
(a) Diabetes insipidus: \numrange{10}{15}{L} of urine at
\qtyrange{50}{100}{mOsm/L}, plasma sodium high whenever drinking lags,
relentless thirst. (b) Inappropriate vasopressin: scant concentrated
urine, water retained, plasma sodium low by dilution, thirst not
increased though drinking continues --- confusion and seizures when
sodium falls far. (c) \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:controls}{Aldosterone} excess: sodium retained with water,
volume expanded, blood pressure high, potassium lost (low plasma
K$^{+}$), plasma sodium near normal since water follows salt and the
kidney escapes partly; urine volume normal. (d) Salt-free diet: renin
and \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:controls}{aldosterone} rise within a day, urine sodium falls to a few
millimoles a day, plasma sodium held, volume and pressure slightly
lower.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:9}
Dilute urine: osmolar clearance $100\times 6/300 = \qty{2}{mL/min}$;
\omterm{prop:b3:renal-osmoregulation:water}{free-water clearance} $6 - 2 = +\qty{4}{mL/min}$: the kidney is shedding
solute-free water, as after a water load with vasopressin switched off.
Concentrated urine: osmolar clearance $1000\times 0.6/300 =
\qty{2}{mL/min}$; \omterm{prop:b3:renal-osmoregulation:water}{free-water clearance} $0.6 - 2 = -\qty{1.4}{mL/min}$:
\qty{1.4}{mL/min} of free water is being returned to the body ---
dehydration, vasopressin high.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:10}
Number the stages from the cortex; let the interstitium at stage $k$
be $I_{k}$. The descending fluid at $k$ equals $I_{k}$; the ascending
fluid at $k$ is $I_{k} - 200$; and the ascending fluid at $k$ is what
left the bend and passed stages $n, n-1, \dots, k+1$. In steady state
the fluid entering at \qty{300}{} leaves the top of the ascending limb
at $I_{1} - 200$, and each stage's interstitium exceeds the one above
by up to the \omterm{prop:b3:renal-osmoregulation:countercurrent}{single effect}, so $I_{k} \le 300 + 200k$ with equality when
every stage works at full effect: the bend reaches $300 + 200n$. The
gradient is therefore bounded by the loop's length (the number of
stages) times the \omterm{prop:b3:renal-osmoregulation:countercurrent}{single effect}, not by any cell's pumping power. $n$
is the loop length divided by the distance over which fluid
equilibrates with the interstitium: long juxtamedullary loops, and the
very long loops of desert rodents, give large $n$; the pump's capacity
and the washout of solute by the vasa recta cap the effect.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:11}
\omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:comparative}{Metabolic water}: $5\times 0.8 = \qty{4}{g}$ of starch gives $4\times 0.6
= \qty{2.4}{g}$. Losses: urine $3/5500\ \unit{L} = \qty{0.55}{mL}$;
evaporation \qty{1.5}{g} (already net of the nose's recovery). Total
loss \qty{2.05}{g} against a gain of \qty{2.4}{g}: a surplus of
\qty{0.35}{g}, which covers the small faecal loss and the seeds'
hygroscopic water is a bonus. It survives without drinking. Without
the nasal recovery the breathing loss would be $1.5/0.3 = \qty{5}{g}$,
and it would die in a few days.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:renal-osmoregulation:12}
In parallel flow the blood and dialysate equilibrate part-way along
the membrane and the concentration difference driving diffusion falls
to zero: at best the blood leaves at half its urea. In countercurrent
flow the freshest dialysate meets the most-cleaned blood and the
gradient is maintained along the whole membrane, so the blood can
leave with nearly the dialysate's inlet concentration --- the same
principle as the \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:nephron}{loop of Henle}. Clearance: $300\times 0.7 =
\qty{210}{mL/min}$ during $3\times 4 = \qty{12}{h}$ a week, i.e.\
$210\times 720 = \qty{151}{L}$ a week; two kidneys at
\qty{125}{mL/min} clear $125\times\num{10080} = \qty{1260}{L}$. The
machine does about an eighth of the kidneys' work, a time-averaged
\qty{15}{mL/min} --- enough to live, not enough to be well.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:renal-osmoregulation:1}
\textbf{1.} Net pressure $55 - 15 - 30 = \qty{10}{mmHg}$; GFR $= 12.5
\times 10 = \qty{125}{mL/min}$.
\textbf{2.} $125\times 1440 = \qty{180}{L/day}$; $180/3 = 60$ times.
\textbf{3.} Inulin: $62.5\times 1/0.5 = \qty{125}{mL/min}$, equal to the
GFR. Creatinine: $1.3\times 1/0.01 = \qty{130}{mL/min}$, a little
larger because the \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:nephron}{proximal tubule} secretes a small amount of
creatinine in addition to what is filtered.
\textbf{4.} PAH clearance $13\times 1/0.02 = \qty{650}{mL/min}$, the
renal plasma flow; blood flow $650/(1 - 0.45) = \qty{1180}{mL/min}$,
about \qty{1.2}{L/min}; filtration fraction $125/650 = 0.19$.
\textbf{5.} Net pressure \qty{15}{mmHg}, GFR \qty{188}{mL/min} (less in
reality, since the oncotic pressure rises along the capillary).
Angiotensin II constricts the efferent arteriole and raises the
glomerular pressure; blocking it lowers the pressure and the GFR a
little. In diabetes the glomeruli filter under high pressure and the
filter wears; lowering the pressure slows the damage by years.
\textbf{6.} The podocytes and their slit diaphragms (or the charge of
the basement membrane): the layer that holds back albumin. Losing
\qty{3}{g/day} lowers the plasma oncotic pressure; fluid leaves the
capillaries everywhere and the tissues swell --- the oedema of the
nephrotic syndrome.
\textbf{7.} Filtered $125\times 5 = \qty{0.625}{mmol/min}$; excreted
$250\times 1 = \qty{0.25}{mmol/min}$; reabsorbed $0.375$, i.e.\
\qty{60}{\%}; clearance $250/5 = \qty{50}{mL/min}$.
\textbf{8.} Filtered $180\times 0.14 = \qty{25.2}{mol/day}$ of Na$^{+}$,
\qty{580}{g} of sodium, \qty{1.47}{kg} as NaCl. Excreted $100\times 1.44
= \qty{144}{mmol/day}$; reabsorbed $1 - 144/\num{25200} = \qty{99.4}{\%}$.
Nearly a kilogram and a half of salt would be lost a day.
\textbf{9.} Filtered $125\times 5 = \qty{0.625}{mmol/min}$, i.e.\
\qty{112}{mg/min}, \qty{162}{g/day}; none excreted, since this is below
$T_{m}$. The filtered load reaches $T_{m}$ at $2.1/0.125 =
\qty{16.8}{mmol/L}$ (\qty{300}{mg/dL}) --- higher than the observed
threshold of about \qty{10}{mmol/L}, because \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:nephron}{nephrons} differ and the
weakest spill first (the splay of the titration curve).
\textbf{10.} Filtered $125\times 20 = \qty{2.5}{mmol/min}$; excreted
$2.5 - 2.1 = \qty{0.4}{mmol/min}$, \qty{72}{mg/min}, $576$ mmol or
\qty{104}{g} a day. Extra urine $576/300 = \qty{1.9}{L}$. The glucose
drags water into the urine (osmotic diuresis), the patient dehydrates
and is thirsty; \qty{104}{g} of glucose is \qty{1.8}{MJ} lost a day, and
the cells, unable to take glucose up, burn fat and protein: weight
falls.
\textbf{11.} Reabsorbed $\approx \qty{25}{mol/day}$; ATP $25/3 =
\qty{8.4}{mol/day}$; $8.4\times 50 = \qty{420}{kJ}$, about \qty{6}{\%}
of \qty{7}{MJ}.
\textbf{12.} To secrete each waste the tubule would need a transporter
that recognises it --- thousands of them, and none for a molecule never
met before. Filtering everything small and reabsorbing the few hundred
species the body wants needs transporters only for what is known to be
valuable; anything unrecognised leaves by default. The price is the
energy of question~11.
\textbf{13.} Osmolar clearance $600\times 1/290 = \qty{2.07}{mL/min}$;
\omterm{prop:b3:renal-osmoregulation:water}{free-water clearance} $1 - 2.07 = -\qty{1.07}{mL/min}$: the kidney is
conserving water.
\textbf{14.} Minimum $600/1200 = \qty{0.5}{L}$; maximum $600/50 =
\qty{12}{L}$; daily need at the minimum $0.5 + 0.9 = \qty{1.4}{L}$.
\textbf{15.} \qty{1000}{mOsm} at \qty{1200}{mOsm/L} needs
\qty{0.83}{L} of urine: net gain \qty{0.17}{L} before the day's solute.
After: the day's total solute is \qty{1600}{mOsm}, \qty{1.33}{L} of
urine, plus \qty{0.9}{L} insensible, \qty{2.23}{L} out for \qty{1}{L}
in --- a deficit of \qty{1.23}{L}, against \qty{1.4}{L} without drinking.
The litre of sea water improves the balance by only \qty{0.17}{L}.
\textbf{16.} Urine at \qty{50}{mOsm/L} for the day: $600/50 =
\qty{12}{L}$; \qty{12.9}{L} must be drunk each day, about \qty{13}{L}.
\textbf{17.} $B$ litres of beer bring $20B$ mOsm; the maximal urine is
$(600 + 20B)/50 = 12 + 0.4B$ litres, and it must carry $B - 0.9$
litres: $B - 0.9 \le 12 + 0.4B$, $B \le \qty{21.5}{L}$. About twenty
litres --- but if little else is eaten the day's solute falls to
\qty{200}{mOsm} and the limit drops to about \qty{8}{L}: the
hyponatraemia of heavy drinkers who do not eat.
\textbf{18.} $298/290 - 1 = \qty{2.8}{\%}$: well past the \qty{1}{\%} that
triggers vasopressin and thirst; both are maximal. Constant solute:
$290\times 42 = 298\times V$, $V = \qty{40.9}{L}$, a deficit of about
\qty{1.1}{L}.
\textbf{19.} Six litres of water dilute the plasma while sweat removes
salt; and exercise, pain and stress release vasopressin regardless of
\omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:problem}{osmolarity}, so the kidney cannot dilute the urine enough to excrete the
excess. Plasma sodium falls, brain cells swell --- seizures and death
have followed. The kidney would need vasopressin switched off and
\qty{6}{L} of urine at $U_{\min}$; the runner should drink to thirst and
replace the salt.
\textbf{20.} $3/5500\ \unit{L} = \qty{0.55}{mL}$ a day. A human kidney
would need $3/1200 = \qty{2.5}{mL}$ for the same solute: the kangaroo
rat uses a fifth of the water.
\textbf{21.} In: \qty{2.4}{g}. Out: $1.6 + 0.3 + 0.55 = \qty{2.45}{g}$.
Balanced to within \qty{0.05}{g} a day, which the hygroscopic water of
the seeds (a few per cent of \qty{5}{g}) supplies.
\textbf{22.} Water $500\times 0.65 = \qty{325}{L}$; a quarter is
\qty{81}{L}; at \qty{5}{L/day}, sixteen days.
\textbf{23.} $150/800 = \qty{0.19}{L}$, about \qty{190}{mL} of brine
--- less than the \qty{100}{mL} of sea water and the fish's water
together, so the bird gains water. Its kidney, at \qty{600}{mOsm/L}
(\qty{300}{mmol/L} of NaCl), would need \qty{0.5}{L} of urine to carry
the same salt, more water than it took in: a kidney that cannot beat
sea water cannot let a bird drink it, and the gland, at twice the
sea's concentration, can.
\textbf{24.} \qty{30}{g} of water in, so about \qty{30}{mL} of urine a
day, at a small fraction of the blood's \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:problem}{osmolarity} (a few tens of
mOsm/L against \num{300}). Salt leaves in that urine and by diffusion
across the gills; without active uptake by the \omterm{def:b3:renal-osmoregulation:comparative}{chloride cells} from
water holding a millimole per litre, the fish would be depleted in
days.
\textbf{25.} GFR \qty{125}{mL/min}, \qty{180}{L} a day; obligatory
urine \qty{0.5}{L} a day; a litre of sea water yields a net
\qty{0.17}{L} once its salt is excreted --- nearly nothing.
\end{solution}
