\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g5:food-webs} --- Food Webs}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:1}
The network of all a \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}'s \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{food chains} drawn at once ---
every \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} once, an arrow for every meal relationship. The
chains cross because most \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} eat several \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} and are
eaten by several.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:2}
\omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{Producers}: the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} (grass, clover, oak \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}). \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{Consumers}:
the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} --- \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{herbivores} like the rabbit, \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{carnivores} like
the buzzard. \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{Decomposers}: the rotters feeding on dead
remains (none drawn in the figure --- they work in the
shadows under all of it).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:3}
The blue tit eats \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillars} and grasshoppers; it \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{is eaten
by} the sparrowhawk.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:4}
Grass $\rightarrow$ grasshopper $\rightarrow$ lizard
$\rightarrow$ buzzard, and grass $\rightarrow$ grasshopper
$\rightarrow$ blue tit $\rightarrow$ sparrowhawk.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:5}
Many \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}, fewer \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{herbivores}, few \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{carnivores}. Because
each floor feeds on the one below: eaters can never outnumber
what there is to eat.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:6}
Because a pair of buzzards needs a meadow's worth of
grasshoppers, several lizards deep, to live on --- the top of
the pyramid stands on the wide floors below.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:7}
Their hunters --- fox, buzzard --- eat better and multiply
after them; the extra hunting melts the rabbit surplus, and
the numbers settle back toward balance.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:8}
Open answer. A defensible garden web: salad and grass
(\omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}) $\rightarrow$ snail, aphid, sparrow;
snail $\rightarrow$ hedgehog; aphid $\rightarrow$ ladybug;
sparrow and ladybug $\rightarrow$ neighborhood cat or
sparrowhawk.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:9}
First step: grasshoppers, less eaten, increase; the buzzard,
poorer by one prey, leans harder on rabbits (its spare
arrow). Second step: more grasshoppers press the grass
harder; more rabbit-hunting nudges rabbits down --- easing
the grass in turn. The web bends and partly absorbs the
loss; the \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} with no spare arrows would have suffered
most, and here everyone had spares.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:10}
Hawks removed; then mice and voles, freed of their main
hunters, multiply; then the multiplied rodents eat the
harvest --- more grain than the hawks ever cost. The
campaigners predicted step one and stopped; the web ran two
steps.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:food-webs:11}
The oak's loss hits its dependents upward: the \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar}
(oak \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} were its only arrow) starves, then the blue tit
leans wholly on grasshoppers, then the sparrowhawk feels
the thinning --- a bottom-up drought like the dry summer.
The sparrowhawk's loss travels downward: blue tits, less
hunted, increase, pressing \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillars} and grasshoppers
--- the hunter-removal story. Here the hawk's loss is
absorbed more easily: its prey has other hunters and the
disturbance fades floor by floor, while the oak's
dependents had no spare arrows at all --- the \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar}'s
whole floor stood on one \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producer}.
\end{solution}
