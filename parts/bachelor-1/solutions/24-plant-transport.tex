\begin{solution}{exo:b1:plant-transport:2}
Evaporation from the wet \omterm{prop:b1:carbohydrates:wall}{cell walls} of the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaf} into the air spaces
creates the force, as surface tension in the walls' fine pores puts
the water under tension. The cohesion of water --- its \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:water}{hydrogen bonds}
--- transmits that tension down the unbroken columns in the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:tissues}{xylem}.
At the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{root} the tension lowers the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:tissues}{xylem}'s \omterm{def:b1:membranes-transport:osmosis}{water potential} below the
soil's and draws water in.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:plant-transport:3}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:plant-transport:4}
Source: an \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:organ}{organ} exporting sugar (a \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaf}, a sprouting tuber), where
sucrose is loaded into the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:tissues}{sieve tubes}, water follows and the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:plantcell}{turgor}
rises. Sink: an \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:organ}{organ} importing it (\omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{root}, fruit, growing tip, filling
tuber), where sucrose is unloaded, water \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves} and the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:plantcell}{turgor} falls.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:plant-transport:6}
Same cross-section: 100 \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:tissues}{tracheids}. Flow per tube $\propto r^4$: one
vessel $10^4$ units, each \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:tissues}{tracheid} 1 unit, a hundred of them 100:
the vessel carries a hundred times more. \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:tissues}{Tracheids} cavitate less
readily and a bubble in one puts only a tiny conduit out of use;
conifers, all \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:tissues}{tracheids}, survive freezing winters that empty a
vessel-bearing tree's wood.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:plant-transport:7}
$\Psi_p = \Psi_{\text{xylem}} - \Psi_s = -0.5 + 1.5 = \qty{1.0}{MPa}$
at the source; at the sink $-0.5 + 0.4 = \qty{-0.1}{MPa}$ in principle
(the tube would need a slight tension, i.e.\ in practice about zero
\omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:plantcell}{turgor}); the difference of about \qty{1}{MPa} drives the flow.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:plant-transport:9}
The ring removes the \omterm{def:b1:plant-transport:phloem}{phloem}, so sugar can no longer reach the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{roots},
which starve and die over months, after which water uptake fails and
the crown dies. The \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:tissues}{xylem} in the wood is untouched, so water still
reaches the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves}, which go on photosynthesising for weeks --- and
sugar accumulates above the ring, swelling the bark there.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:plant-transport:10}

0.45 = \qty{0.27}{g/m^3}$; ratio of fluxes $1.6\times 14/0.27 = 83$ by

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:plant-transport:11}
The column snaps: a bubble expands to fill the vessel, the water
above it is no longer pulled and the vessel stops conducting; the
flow shifts to neighbouring vessels, raising their tension and their
risk; the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaf} it supplied wilts partly. Pits between conduits are
too fine for the bubble's meniscus to pass, so the bubble stays in one
conduit. At night, with tension gone, the bubble may dissolve under
\omterm{prop:b1:plant-water-minerals:rootpressure}{root pressure}; in spring the tree grows a ring of new vessels and
abandons the old.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:plant-transport:12}
The \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:tissues}{xylem} carries water and minerals upward, driven by evaporation at
the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves} --- the sun's energy, none of the plant's; the \omterm{def:b1:plant-transport:phloem}{phloem}
carries sugar (and signals) from sources to sinks, driven by a
pressure difference the plant creates by spending \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} to load and
unload sucrose at the two ends, with the flow in between free. The
plant spends its energy only at the interfaces: the \omterm{prop:b1:plant-water-minerals:uptake}{proton pumps} of
the \omterm{def:b1:plant-transport:stoma}{guard cells} and the \omterm{def:b1:plant-transport:phloem}{phloem} loaders, and the wood it must build to
hold the tension. Everything in between --- the metres of pipe --- runs
on physics.
\end{solution}
