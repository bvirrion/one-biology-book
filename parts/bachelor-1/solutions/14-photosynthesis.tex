\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b1:photosynthesis} --- Photosynthesis and Autotrophy}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:1}
The outer and inner envelope membranes, and the \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{thylakoid} membrane
inside. \omterm{prop:b1:photosynthesis:lightreactions}{Light reactions}: in the \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{thylakoid} membrane (protons pumped into
the lumen). \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:fixation}{Calvin cycle}: in the \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{stroma}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:2}
$2\,\mathrm{H_2O} + 2\,\mathrm{NADP^+} + 3\,\mathrm{ADP} + 3\,\mathrm{P_i}
\to \mathrm{O_2} + 2\,\mathrm{NADPH} + 2\,\mathrm{H^+} + 3\,\mathrm{ATP}$
with eight photons. The oxygen comes from water: algae given
$\mathrm{H_2^{18}O}$ release $\mathrm{^{18}O_2}$; given
$\mathrm{C^{18}O_2}$ they do not (Ruben and Kamen).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:3}
Fixation (\omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:fixation}{RuBisCO}), reduction (to G3P), regeneration (of RuBP). Per
$\mathrm{CO_2}$: 3 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} and 2 NADPH.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:4}
Near \qty{430}{nm} (blue) and \qty{660}{nm} (red). Around \qty{500}{nm}
the \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:pigments}{carotenoids} absorb and pass the energy to \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:pigments}{chlorophyll}, so oxygen is
released although \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:pigments}{chlorophyll} itself absorbs little there.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:5}
Far-red light (\qty{700}{nm}) alone drives little photosynthesis;
added to \qty{680}{nm} light it gives more than the two rates summed.
Two pigment systems with different absorption maxima must cooperate in
series, each needing its own photons: \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:zscheme}{photosystem} I (P700) and
\omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:zscheme}{photosystem} II (P680).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:6}
$0.18\times 96\,500 = \qty{17.4}{kJ/mol}$ of protons; $50/17.4 = 2.9$
protons at least; the synthase uses four, i.e.\ \qty{72}{\%}
efficiency.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:7}
It proves that a proton gradient across the \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{thylakoid} membrane is
sufficient to drive \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} synthesis, with no light and no electron
transport: the coupling is through the gradient. It does not by itself
prove that the \omterm{prop:b1:photosynthesis:lightreactions}{light reactions} make \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} this way in the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaf} (that
needs the uncoupler and pH measurements). With an uncoupler the
gradient collapses before the synthase can use it: no \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:8}
$3\times 5 + 3\times 1 = 18$ carbons; six 3-PGA $= 18$; six G3P $= 18$;
one G3P out (3) and five G3P (15) regenerate three RuBP (15): $3 +
15 = 18$. Balanced.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:9}
Without $\mathrm{CO_2}$ \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:fixation}{RuBisCO} stops: RuBP is no longer consumed
(rises) and 3-PGA no longer made (falls) while the light keeps
reducing it away. Without light there is no \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} or NADPH: 3-PGA is no
longer reduced (rises) and RuBP no longer regenerated (falls) while
fixation continues briefly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:10}
$E = hc/\lambda = 6.63\times 10^{-34}\times 3\times 10^8/6.8\times
10^{-7} = \qty{2.92e-19}{J}$; per mole \qty{176}{kJ}. 48 photons:
\qty{8450}{kJ}; efficiency $2870/8450 = \qty{34}{\%}$. A crop loses the
light not absorbed (half the spectrum, reflection, transmission, gaps
between plants), the photorespiration, the respiration of \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves}, stems
and \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{roots}, the light saturation of the \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzymes} at full sun, and the
seasons when \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves} are absent.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:11}
Per 100 carboxylations, 33 oxygenations releasing $16.7$ carbons: net
$83.3$, a loss of \qty{17}{\%}. Cost of avoiding it: 2 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} $\approx
0.2$ carbon per $\mathrm{CO_2}$, i.e.\ \qty{20}{\%}; the C4 design pays
only when the loss exceeds about a fifth --- in hot, dry conditions,
not in cool ones.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:photosynthesis:12}
Both use membranes that pump protons with the energy of electron
transport and an \omterm{thm:b1:photosynthesis:chemiosmosis}{ATP synthase} that spends the gradient: the mechanism
is the same, and the synthases are homologous. But the electrons run
opposite ways --- from water to NADPH in the \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{chloroplast}, lifted by
light; from NADH to oxygen in the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:mitochondrion}{mitochondrion}, falling
(\cref{ch:b1:respiration-fermentation}) --- and the carbon cycles are
not each other's reverse: the \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:fixation}{Calvin cycle} and the Krebs cycle share
no \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme}, and the reduction of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ uses NADPH while its
release uses $\mathrm{NAD^+}$. The overall equations are reverses; the
machinery is a common inheritance used in two directions, with
different chemistry at the carbon end.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b1:photosynthesis:1}
\textbf{1.} $hc/\lambda = \qty{2.92e-19}{J}$; $\times N_A = \qty{176}{kJ/mol}$.
\textbf{2.} \qty{700}{nm}: \qty{171}{kJ/mol}; \qty{450}{nm}:
\qty{266}{kJ/mol}. A blue photon excites \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:pigments}{chlorophyll} to a higher state
that decays within picoseconds to the same lowest excited state a red
photon reaches; the excess is lost as heat, and the chemistry sees one
photon either way.
\textbf{3.} $1/2.92\times 10^{-19} = \num{3.4e18}$ photons per joule.
\textbf{4.} $\Delta E = 0.82 - (-0.32) = \qty{1.14}{V}$: $\Delta G =
96\,500\times 1.14 = \qty{110}{kJ}$ per mole of electrons.
\textbf{5.} Two photons: about $176 + 171 = \qty{347}{kJ}$; stored
\qty{110}{kJ}: \qty{32}{\%}.
\textbf{6.} Four electrons per $\mathrm{O_2}$ (two water molecules),
giving two NADPH: eight photons.
\textbf{7.} 12 NADPH: 24 electrons, 48 photons.
\textbf{8.} $2.303\times 8.314\times 298\times 3/96\,500 = \qty{0.177}{V}$;
$\qty{17.1}{kJ}$ per mole of protons.
\textbf{9.} $4 + 8 = 12$ protons per $\mathrm{O_2}$: 3 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}.
\textbf{10.} $6\times 3 = 18$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}: exactly the cycle's need on this
accounting; in practice the yield is nearer 2.6 per $\mathrm{O_2}$
(\qty{4.7}{H^+} per \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} in the \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:chloroplast}{chloroplast}) and cyclic flow around
\omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:zscheme}{photosystem} I supplies the rest.
\textbf{11.} $4\times 17.1 = \qty{68}{kJ}$ for one \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} of \qty{50}{kJ}:
\qty{73}{\%}.
\textbf{12.} $10^{-5}\,\mathrm{mol/L}\times 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{L}\times
6\times 10^{23} = 6$ free protons in the lumen against $10^5$ per second
through the synthases: the flux is carried by protons bound to the
buffering groups of the lumen's \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{proteins} and \omterm{def:b1:lipids:fattyacid}{lipids}, which release them
as fast as they leave.
\textbf{13.} $18\times 50 + 12\times 220 = 900 + 2640 = \qty{3540}{kJ}$
for \qty{2870}{kJ} stored: \qty{670}{kJ} (a fifth) dissipated as heat in
the cycle's reactions, which must be downhill to run.
\textbf{14.} $48\times 176 = \qty{8450}{kJ}$; $2870/8450 = \qty{34}{\%}$.
\textbf{15.} $0.34\times 0.45\times 0.9 = \qty{13.8}{\%}$.
\textbf{16.} $400\times 0.005\times 36\,000 = \qty{72}{kJ}$; glucose
$0.138\times 72\,000/2\,870\,000 = \qty{3.5e-3}{mol} = \qty{0.62}{g}$.
\textbf{17.} Net $0.6\times 0.62 = \qty{0.37}{g}$ of glucose,
$\qty{0.15}{g}$ of carbon ($72/180$).
\textbf{18.} Light falling between plants or on soil; \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves} in the
shade of other \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves} working below capacity, and \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves} in full sun
saturated (the \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzymes} cannot use all the photons); respiration of
\omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{roots}, stems and at night; photorespiration; the weeks before the
canopy closes and after it senesces.
\textbf{19.} 33 oxygenations, 16.7 carbons lost, net 83.3 gained.
\textbf{20.} Salvage $33\times 3.5 = 117$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}; cycle 300 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}; total
417 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} for 83.3 carbons: 5.0 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} per net carbon.
\textbf{21.} $3 + 2 = 5$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} per carbon, with no carbon lost --- equal
in \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}, but the C3 plant has also lost \qty{17}{\%} of its carboxylation
capacity; roughly a draw at \qty{25}{\celsius}.
\textbf{22.} 50 oxygenations, 25 carbons lost, net 75; salvage 175 \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP};
$475/75 = 6.3$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} per net carbon against the \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:c4cam}{C4 plant}'s 5, with a
quarter of the carbon lost: C4 wins clearly.
\textbf{23.} Tripling $\mathrm{CO_2}$ shifts \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:fixation}{RuBisCO}'s competition
toward carboxylation, cutting photorespiration and raising net fixation
in C3 plants; \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:c4cam}{C4 plants} already saturate \omterm{def:b1:photosynthesis:fixation}{RuBisCO} with concentrated
$\mathrm{CO_2}$ and gain nothing but a little saving of water.
\textbf{24.} \qty{1}{g} of carbon is \qty{0.083}{mol}: C3, $250\times 0.083 = \qty{21}{mol}$ of water, \qty{375}{g}; CAM, \qty{37}{g}.
\textbf{25.} About 8 photons per $\mathrm{CO_2}$ (48 per glucose);
\qty{34}{\%} from absorbed photons to glucose; about \qty{14}{\%} from
incident sunlight to gross sugar and \qty{8}{\%} to the \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaf}'s net
sugar --- a figure that the losses of a whole plant over a whole
season reduce to \qty{1}{\%}.
\end{solution}
