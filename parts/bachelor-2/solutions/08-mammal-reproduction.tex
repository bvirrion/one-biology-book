\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b2:mammal-reproduction} --- Sexual Reproduction of Mammals}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:1}
Spermatogonia divide from puberty for life; oogonia only in the
fetus. One \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:meiosis}{meiosis} gives four sperm but one egg (and three polar
bodies). A sperm takes about 64 days plus two weeks of maturation;
an egg is arrested in \omterm{prop:b2:meiosis-heredity:stages}{prophase I} from before birth until the month
of its \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:ovary}{ovulation} --- decades --- and again in metaphase II until
fertilisation. A man makes $10^{8}$ sperm a day, some $10^{12}$ in a
life; a woman ovulates about 400 eggs.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:2}
Binding to the \omterm{prop:b2:mammal-reproduction:fertilisation}{zona pellucida}; \omterm{prop:b2:mammal-reproduction:fertilisation}{acrosome reaction} and digestion of a
path; fusion of the membranes and entry of the sperm nucleus;
calcium wave and \omterm{prop:b2:mammal-reproduction:fertilisation}{cortical reaction} (hardening of the zona, loss of
its receptors: the block to polyspermy); completion of \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:meiosis}{meiosis} II
and extrusion of the second polar body; formation of the two
pronuclei, DNA replication, first mitosis.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:3}
\omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:implantation}{Trophoblast} (outer layer) makes the \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:placenta}{placenta} and membranes; \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:implantation}{inner
cell mass} makes the embryo. \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:implantation}{Implantation} begins on day 6 to 7
after fertilisation, in a uterine lining prepared by progesterone
from the \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:ovary}{corpus luteum}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:4}
Oxygen: diffusion. Glucose: facilitated transport. Maternal red
cells: do not cross (the bloods never mix). IgG: receptor-mediated
transport. Urea: diffusion, from fetus to mother. Alcohol: diffuses
freely. Bacteria: mostly do not cross (some, like syphilis and
listeria, do).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:5}
$m = 3, 1, 0.4, 0.1$: $P = 0.95, 0.63, 0.33, 0.095$. Below one half
when $m < \ln 2 = 0.69$, i.e.\ below $7\times 10^{7}$ sperm. The
curve saturates: above about $10^{8}$ extra sperm add almost
nothing, below it fertility falls nearly in proportion to the count.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:6}
Before puberty: $7\times 10^{5}$ lost in 13 years (\num{4750} days):
about 150 a day. After: \num{299000} lost in 37 years (\num{13500}
days): 22 a day; \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:ovary}{ovulation} $400/\num{13500} = 0.03$ a day. Atresia
outnumbers \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:ovary}{ovulation} seven hundred to one; the stock is not spent by
ovulating but by dying.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:7}
Adult: $(30/27)^{2.7} = 1.33$, $S = 1.33/2.33 = 0.57$. Fetal:
$(30/19)^{2.7} = 3.4$, $S = 3.4/4.4 = 0.77$. At the low pressures of
the \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:placenta}{placenta} the fetal blood loads a fifth more oxygen than adult
blood would, and unloads it in tissues at pressures where adult
haemoglobin would already be half empty.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:8}
$2^{k} = 25$: $k = 4.6$ doublings, \qty{9.3}{d}: day 16 to 17 after
fertilisation; day 30 to 31 after the last period --- about the day
the period is missed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:9}
$3\times 10^{-8}\times 6\times 10^{4}\times 20/10^{-3} =
\qty{36}{mL/min}$ against a demand of \qty{25}{mL/min}: the margin
falls from eight to one and a half; the fetus becomes hypoxic under
any extra load, grows slowly and may need early delivery.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:10}
(a) Testes form and secrete both hormones; AMH removes the Müllerian
ducts, but testosterone cannot act: the Wolffian ducts regress and
the external genitalia are female --- a woman with testes and no
uterus. (b) Testosterone acts, AMH does not: a male with epididymis,
vas deferens and male genitalia, plus a uterus and oviducts. (c)
Ovaries and Müllerian derivatives (no \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:testis}{testis}, no AMH); the adrenal
androgens virilise the external genitalia to varying degrees. (d)
\emph{SRY} makes testes, which impose the male path: a man with two
X chromosomes, sterile because the Y genes of \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:testis}{spermatogenesis} are
absent.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:11}
The commonest (\qty{68}{\%}) is the day 4 to 8 split: after the
\omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:implantation}{trophoblast} has formed (so one \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:placenta}{placenta}) but before the amnion
(so two sacs). Before day 3 each half makes its own \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:implantation}{trophoblast},
hence two \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:placenta}{placentas} (\qty{30}{\%}); after day 8 the amnion is
already laid down and is shared (\qty{2}{\%}). Splitting the \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:implantation}{inner
cell mass} after the \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:implantation}{trophoblast} has committed is evidently the
easiest event.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:mammal-reproduction:12}
A marsupial invests little before birth: a short gestation, a tiny
young, and a lactation that can be cut short by dropping the pouch
young if drought comes, so the mother's risk per attempt is low and
she can try again at once. A placental invests heavily in a long
gestation with a large, well-developed young that survives better
at birth, at the cost of a mother committed for months and
vulnerable meanwhile. In productive, predictable environments the
higher survival of placental young wins; Australia's erratic
climate rewards the marsupial's option to abandon, and its isolation
kept placentals out until recently.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:mammal-reproduction:1}
\textbf{1.} $m = 3\times 10^{8}\times 10^{-8} = 3$; $P = 1 - e^{-3} =
0.95$.
\textbf{2.} $1 - e^{-3}(1 + 3) = 0.80$: without the block, four
fertilisations in five would be polyspermic and the embryo triploid.
\textbf{3.} $0.15/5\times 10^{-5} = \qty{3000}{s}$, fifty minutes;
the contractions of the uterus and oviduct carry them.
\textbf{4.} $300/3\times 10^{8} = 10^{-6}$: lost in the acid vagina,
in the cervical mucus, to phagocytes in the uterus, and in the wrong
oviduct.
\textbf{5.} From three days before \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:ovary}{ovulation} to one day after: about
four days.
\textbf{6.} $m = 0.5$, $P = 0.39$; expected cycles $1/P = 2.5$.
\textbf{7.} The sperm's few mitochondria, in the midpiece, are tagged
and destroyed after entry; the egg carries a hundred thousand.
\textbf{8.} $120/16 = 7.5$: seven divisions, $2^{7} = 128$ cells ---
about a hundred.
\textbf{9.} $\log_2 25 = 4.6$ doublings, \qty{9.3}{d}: day 16 to 17;
day 30 to 31 after the period.
\textbf{10.} The \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:ovary}{corpus luteum} regresses about twelve days after
\omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:ovary}{ovulation} unless hCG rescues it; without it progesterone falls, the
lining sheds and the embryo is lost with the period.
\textbf{11.} $\log_2 10^{5} = 16.6$ doublings; by week 10 the
\omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:placenta}{placenta} makes its own progesterone and the \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:ovary}{corpus luteum} is no
longer needed.
\textbf{12.} Eggs sit in \omterm{prop:b2:meiosis-heredity:stages}{prophase I} for decades and the cohesins
holding the bivalents decay, so nondisjunction at \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:meiosis}{meiosis} I rises
steeply with maternal age; sperm are made fresh in two months.
\textbf{13.} After the \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:implantation}{trophoblast} (day 5) and before the amnion (day
8): one \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:placenta}{placenta}, two sacs.
\textbf{14.} $3\times 10^{-8}\times 1.2\times 10^{5}\times 20/3.5\times
10^{-4} = \qty{206}{mL/min}$.
\textbf{15.} $7\times 3.5 = \qty{24.5}{mL/min}$: a margin of eight.
\textbf{16.} $500\times 0.16 = \qty{80}{mL/min}$; extraction
$24.5/80 = \qty{31}{\%}$.
\textbf{17.} $5\times 3.5\times 1440 = \qty{25}{g/d}$; in \qty{28}{L}
of maternal blood; daily flow \qty{720}{L}: the fetus takes about
\qty{4}{\%} of the glucose that passes.
\textbf{18.} Fetal haemoglobin is \qty{77}{\%} saturated at
\qty{30}{mmHg}, the fetus has more red cells and a high cardiac
output, and its tissues are adapted to work at low pressure --- it
lives, in effect, on the summit of Everest.
\textbf{19.} Gas exchange without a working lung, nutrition and
excretion without a working gut or kidney, and the hormones that
keep the pregnancy; it cannot keep out every toxin --- alcohol,
nicotine and some viruses cross.
\textbf{20.} Cervical stretch $\to$ hypothalamus $\to$ oxytocin $\to$
contraction $\to$ more stretch, until delivery removes the stimulus.
Before term the progesterone-dominated uterus has few oxytocin
receptors and no gap junctions, so contractions do not spread and the
loop cannot close.
\textbf{21.} $750\times 2.9 = \qty{2.2}{MJ}$; cost $2.2/0.8 =
\qty{2.7}{MJ}$.
\textbf{22.} Growth $30\times 6 = \qty{0.18}{MJ}$, maintenance
$300\times 4 = \qty{1.2}{MJ}$: \qty{1.4}{MJ}, about two thirds of the
milk's energy; the rest goes to activity, warmth and losses.
\textbf{23.} $270\times 2.7 = \qty{730}{MJ}$, two and a half times the
pregnancy.
\textbf{24.} Suckling raises prolactin and suppresses the pulsatile
release of the hormone that drives \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:ovary}{ovulation}, so \omterm{def:b2:mammal-reproduction:ovary}{ovulation} returns
only months later; without contraception this spaces births by two
to three years.
\textbf{25.} $P = 0.95$; test positive on day 17 after fertilisation
(day 31 of the cycle); oxygen margin eight; a day's milk costs the
mother \qty{2.7}{MJ}.
\end{solution}
