\chapter{A Balanced Diet}\label{ch:g3:balanced-diet}

A builder's site needs bricks, cement, planks --- and fuel for
the machines. Your body is a building site that never closes:
it grows, repairs itself and runs about all day. Its deliveries
arrive three or four times a day, on a plate. What must the
deliveries contain? That is the question of diet.

\section{Why the body needs food}

\begin{proposition}[Food's two jobs]\label{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}
Food does two different jobs:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item it provides \emph{energy}\index{energy (from food)} ---
        the power to move, to keep warm, to keep heart and brain
        running, even during \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep};
  \item it provides \emph{building material} --- what the body
        uses to grow taller, build \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} and \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone}, repair
        scrapes and replace worn parts.
\end{enumerate}
A diet must deliver both, every day.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Fuel even at rest]\label{ex:g3:balanced-diet:rest}
Even lying perfectly still you spend \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy}: the heart beats,
the chest rises and falls, the body stays warm at about
$\qty{37}{\degreeCelsius}$ whatever the weather. That standing
charge is why missing meals makes you weak and cold, not just
hungry.
\end{example}

\section{The food families}

\begin{definition}[The food families]\label{def:g3:balanced-diet:families}
Foods are sorted into \emph{food families}\index{food family} by
what they bring:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{\omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruit} and vegetables} --- vitamins, fibre and
        water; the freshness family;
  \item \emph{cereals and starchy foods} (bread, pasta, rice,
        potatoes) --- the steady \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} family;
  \item \emph{dairy} (\omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk}, yogurt, cheese) --- the bone-building
        family;
  \item \emph{meat, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} and \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}} --- the body-building family;
  \item \emph{fats} (butter, oil) --- concentrated \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy}, needed
        in small amounts;
  \item \emph{sugary foods} --- quick pleasure, needed not at
        all: the treat family;
  \item and \emph{water}\index{water (drink)} --- the only drink
        the body requires, all day long.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Balancing a lunch]\label{ex:g3:balanced-diet:lunch}
Pasta with tomato sauce, a piece of \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish}, green beans, a yogurt,
an apple, water: every family present, treats absent --- a
balanced lunch. Chips, a soda and a chocolate bar feed exactly
two families (fats and sugar) and skip the ones the building
site was waiting for.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[No forbidden foods, only forgotten ones]\label{rem:g3:balanced-diet:balance}
Balance is measured over days, not single bites. A slice of
birthday cake breaks no rule --- the trouble is never one treat,
it is treats \emph{instead of} the families, day after day. The
practical question at each meal is not ``is this food bad?'' but
``which family have I forgotten today?''
\end{remark}

\section{Matching food to the day}

\begin{proposition}[Needs change with the day and the person]\label{prop:g3:balanced-diet:needs}
How much \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} a body needs depends on who it is and what it
does: a growing child needs proportionally more building
material than a grown-up; a day of sport or winter cold burns
more \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} than an afternoon of reading. Hunger grows with the
body's spending.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Why breakfast matters]\label{ex:g3:balanced-diet:breakfast}
By morning, the body has run all night --- heart, breath,
warmth --- on no deliveries at all. Breakfast restocks it for
the morning's lessons and running about. Skip it, and by
mid-morning the site runs short: rumbling stomach, wandering
attention, no strength at play.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Checking a day's balance]\label{met:g3:balanced-diet:check}
At the end of the day, run through the list:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruit} or vegetables at every meal? (aim: about five
        helpings in the day)
  \item starchy food at each meal for steady \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy}?
  \item builders present --- dairy a few times, and meat, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish}
        or \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} once or twice?
  \item water as the day's drink?
  \item treats: a small amount, not a main part?
\end{enumerate}
Whatever family is missing, invite it tomorrow.
\end{method}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:balanced-diet:1}
What are food's two jobs? Give one \omterm{def:g3:balanced-diet:families}{food family} that mainly
serves each job.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:balanced-diet:2}
Name the \omterm{def:g3:balanced-diet:families}{food families}, and the one drink the body requires.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:balanced-diet:3}
Sort this shopping into families: rice, yogurt, carrots, \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs},
butter, apples, bread.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:balanced-diet:4}
Why does the body spend \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} even during \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep}? Name two of
the night's expenses.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:balanced-diet:5}
Check the lunch ``pasta, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish}, green beans, yogurt, apple,
water'' against the balanced plate of the figure: which part of
the plate is each item?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:balanced-diet:6}
Why is breakfast especially important? What happens mid-morning
without it?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:balanced-diet:7}
Who needs proportionally more building material: a child or a
grown-up? Why?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g3:balanced-diet:8}
Try it: run \cref{met:g3:balanced-diet:check} on your own day,
honestly. Which family did you forget --- and which appeared
more than its share?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g3:balanced-diet:9}
``Sweets are forbidden!'' Is that what this chapter says? Give
the more precise rule of \cref{rem:g3:balanced-diet:balance}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g3:balanced-diet:10}
On a ski holiday, everyone is hungrier than at home. Explain
with \cref{prop:g3:balanced-diet:needs} --- name the two extra
expenses a cold, sporty day adds.
\end{exercise}
