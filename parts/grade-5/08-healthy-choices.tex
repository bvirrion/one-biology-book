\chapter{Healthy Choices}\label{ch:g5:healthy-choices}

By now this book has visited most of your body's great
services: the \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:tract}{digestive tract}, the \omterm{def:g4:breathing:organs}{lungs}, the \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} and its
rivers of \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}, the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} and \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones}, the commanding \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}.
This closing chapter of the year asks a practical question:
knowing all that machinery, how do you run it well --- and
who decides? Because from about your age on, more and more
of the decisions are yours.

\section{What the machinery asks for}

\begin{proposition}[The body's daily contract]\label{prop:g5:healthy-choices:contract}
Put the earlier chapters' requests side by side and a daily
contract appears:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{\omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{varied food} and water} --- fuel and building
        material (\cref{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}),
        delivered by the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} to every \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell};
  \item \emph{movement} --- \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones}, \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} and \omterm{def:g4:breathing:organs}{lungs}
        all grow stronger with use
        (\cref{met:g3:bones-and-muscles:care},
        \cref{rem:g4:heart-and-blood:rest});
  \item \emph{\omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep}} --- the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}'s filing and the body's
        repairs (\cref{prop:g5:brain-in-command:protect}); a
        child of this age needs about ten hours;
  \item \emph{clean air, clean hands, cared-for teeth} ---
        the defenses of \cref{ch:g1:healthy-habits} and
        \cref{met:g4:breathing:care}, still on duty.
\end{itemize}
Nothing new --- that is the point. Health is the old rules,
kept.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[One day, seen from inside]\label{ex:g5:healthy-choices:day}
A good day, read as body management: breakfast restocks the
night's spending (\cref{ex:g3:balanced-diet:breakfast});
the walk to school warms up \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} and \omterm{def:g4:breathing:organs}{lungs}; play at noon
is training disguised as fun; supper's vegetables restock
the freshness family; and the early night hands the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}
its filing time. No item is heroic; together they are
health.
\end{example}

\section{New choices, new pressures}

\begin{proposition}[Screens and sleep]\label{prop:g5:healthy-choices:screens}
Screens --- phones, tablets, games --- are neither poison
nor food; they are \emph{time}. The hours they absorb are
taken from somewhere, and the usual victims are exactly the
contract's items: outdoor movement and \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep}. Worse, bright
screens late in the evening tell the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} ``still
daytime'', delaying \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep} beyond the hours given up. The
choice is not screens-or-no-screens, but which hours they
may have --- and the evening's last hour is the worst one
to give them.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The eleven-o'clock experiment]\label{ex:g5:healthy-choices:latenight}
Try it as a fair test (\cref{met:g4:what-plants-need:fairtest})
on yourself: a week of screens off an hour before bed,
against an ordinary week. Most people fall asleep faster,
wake easier and notice better mornings in the first week ---
the control week is the argument.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Substances that trick the body]\label{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}
Some substances harm precisely the machinery this book has
toured:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{tobacco smoke} dirties the \omterm{def:g4:breathing:organs}{lungs}' delicate
        pockets (\cref{met:g4:breathing:care}) and strains
        the \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart}; it is sold by making the first cigarettes
        seem grown-up --- and it holds its buyers by
        \emph{addiction}\index{addiction}: the body, once
        used to it, protests without it;
  \item \emph{alcohol} reaches the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} within minutes
        --- the commander itself
        (\cref{ch:g5:brain-in-command}) --- slowing the loop,
        blurring decisions; a young \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}, still building
        its paths, is more easily harmed than a grown one;
  \item both were already flagged as dangers to the \omterm{def:g5:human-reproduction:pregnancy}{fetus}
        (\cref{prop:g5:human-reproduction:care}) --- the
        same substances, the same reason.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{remark}[Why start-never beats stop-later]\label{rem:g5:healthy-choices:never}
\omterm{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}{Addiction} is a trap with an easy entrance and a hard exit:
starting is one choice, stopping is a fight repeated for
years. That asymmetry is the coldest fact in this chapter,
and the most useful: the cheapest moment to win the fight
is before it begins. People offering a first cigarette are
not offering one --- they are offering all the following
ones.
\end{remark}

\section{Deciding for yourself}

\begin{method}[Running your own contract]\label{met:g5:healthy-choices:run}
From now on, more each year, the keeper of your body's
contract is you:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item know the contract's items ---
        \cref{prop:g5:healthy-choices:contract} is the list;
  \item check yourself honestly now and then, as in
        \cref{met:g3:balanced-diet:check} --- what is
        missing, what is over its share?
  \item change one thing at a time, and give it a fair
        test's week;
  \item on substances, decide your ``no'' \emph{before} the
        moment it is needed
        (\cref{rem:g5:healthy-choices:never}) --- a decision
        made calmly in advance is easier to keep than one
        improvised under friendly pressure;
  \item and use the grown-ups: doctors, nurses, parents and
        teachers are on your side of this contract.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Prepared answers]\label{ex:g5:healthy-choices:answers}
The advance decision of step 4, in practice, is a sentence
ready in the pocket: ``No thanks --- not my thing.'' Said
lightly, once, it usually ends the matter; and friends worth
the name take it the first time. The sentence costs nothing
to prepare and is hardest to compose exactly when it is
needed --- so it is composed now.
\end{example}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:1}
List the four items of the body's daily contract, each with
the organ or service it mainly serves.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:2}
About how much \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep} does a child of your age need, and
what two jobs does the body do with it?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:3}
In what sense are screen hours ``taken from somewhere''?
Name the two usual victims.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:4}
Why is the evening's last hour the worst one to give to a
bright screen?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:5}
Which organs does tobacco smoke harm first, by
\cref{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}? And alcohol?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:6}
What is \omterm{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}{addiction}, and why does it make ``never starting''
so much cheaper than ``stopping later''?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:7}
What is the point of deciding a ``no'' in advance, by step
4 of \cref{met:g5:healthy-choices:run}?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:8}
Try it: run the eleven-o'clock experiment of
\cref{ex:g5:healthy-choices:latenight} for one week and
report --- honestly --- what changed.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:9}
Design the screen experiment as a proper fair test: what
stays the same between the two weeks, what one thing
changes, and what do you measure?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:10}
``One cigarette can't hurt.'' Answer with
\cref{rem:g5:healthy-choices:never}: what is really being
offered with the first one?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:healthy-choices:11}
