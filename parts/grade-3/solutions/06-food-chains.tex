\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g3:food-chains} --- Food Chains}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:food-chains:1}
``\omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{Is eaten by}''. It points from the eaten to the eater ---
following the food.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:food-chains:2}
A \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} (or another \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living thing} that makes its own food).
Because \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} alone feed without eating anyone --- from light,
water and air; every \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} link lives off that first green
link, directly or through others.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:food-chains:3}
Grass $\rightarrow$ grasshopper $\rightarrow$ lizard
$\rightarrow$ buzzard.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:food-chains:4}
The arrows are backward: grass $\rightarrow$ rabbit
$\rightarrow$ fox --- grass \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{is eaten by} the rabbit, which \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{is
eaten by} the fox.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:food-chains:5}
Oak \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} $\rightarrow$ \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar} $\rightarrow$ blue tit
$\rightarrow$ sparrowhawk.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:food-chains:6}
The salad \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{is eaten by} the snail; the hedgehog eats the snail.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:food-chains:7}
For example: water \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} $\rightarrow$ tadpole $\rightarrow$
stickleback $\rightarrow$ heron.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:food-chains:8}
Because its food's food's food is grass: fewer grasshoppers on
the browned grass means fewer lizards, and fewer lizards means
lean hunting for the buzzard. A change in the first link
travels up the whole chain.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:food-chains:9}
Poisoning the snails starves their eater: the hedgehog \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}
or dies. Later, with the hedgehog --- the snails' main enemy
--- gone, the surviving slugs and snails multiply harder than
before: removing links travels both up the chain (hungry
hedgehog) and back down it (unhunted snails).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g3:food-chains:10}
True --- through the middle of the chain. The lizard eats
grasshoppers, and grasshoppers eat grass: if the grass fails,
the failure travels up, link by link, from grass to grasshopper
to lizard. The lizard never touches grass, and depends on it
all the same.
\end{solution}
