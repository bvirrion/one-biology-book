\chapter{Reproduction and the Environment}\label{ch:g8:reproduction-and-environment}

Why does the frog chorus start in May and not November? Why
does a good beech year fill the woods with \omterm{prop:g2:from-seed-to-plant:cycle}{seedlings}, and a
cold wet June empty next spring's pond of froglets? The last
chapter built the mechanism of the next generation; this one
plugs it into the world: reproduction is timed, provisioned
--- and limited --- by the \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{environment}.

\section{Timed by the seasons}

\begin{proposition}[Breeding follows the calendar of plenty]\label{prop:g8:reproduction-and-environment:timing}
\omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{Species} time their reproduction so the \emph{young} arrive
when \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} and food will serve them
(\cref{prop:g6:life-through-seasons:conditions}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item the blue tit lays so its chicks hatch at the spring
        \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar} peak (\cref{ex:g3:food-chains:three}'s
        oak chain, timed to the day);
  \item the frog spawns in the warming pond, giving
        \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}{tadpoles} a summer of growth before winter;
  \item \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flower} to their pollinators' season and
        ripen \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed} to the \omterm{def:g6:how-plants-spread:dispersal}{dispersal} calendar
        (\cref{pb:g6:life-through-seasons:1});
  \item the stag's autumn bellow sets \omterm{def:g2:born-grow-die:cycle}{births} for the next
        \emph{spring} --- the gestation counted in.
\end{itemize}
The cues that start the machinery are the \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{environment}'s
own signals: lengthening days above all, warmth, rain.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The indoor lambs]\label{ex:g8:reproduction-and-environment:lambs}
Sheep breeders exploit the cue directly: ewes come into
breeding condition as days \emph{shorten}; kept under
artificial long-then-shortened \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}, a flock can be
brought to lamb for the profitable early market. The
machinery reads \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}, not calendars --- change the \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}
and the calendar follows. A cue known is a lever.
\end{example}

\section{Provisioned by the habitat}

\begin{proposition}[Reproduction is paid for in matter]\label{prop:g8:reproduction-and-environment:cost}
\omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{Eggs}, \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk}, nectar, \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} and antlers are all
\emph{production} in
\cref{def:g6:matter-from-food:production}'s sense ---
\omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} the parents must first acquire. So the
\omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}'s richness sets the budget:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a well-fed hen lays daily; a starved one stops ---
        the ledger of \cref{pb:g6:matter-from-food:1}
        balancing on the \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg} line;
  \item the beech \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruits} massively only in ``mast years''
        following good growing seasons --- \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed} \omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:farming}{crops} are
        banked summers
        (\cref{ex:g6:plants-are-producers:pantry});
  \item hard years thin the clutch: many \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds} lay fewer
        \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}, or none, when prey is short.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Counting the tits' clutch]\label{ex:g8:reproduction-and-environment:clutch}
Nest-box studies show blue tit clutches tracking the
\omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar} supply: rich oak woods, ten or more \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs};
poor gardens, six or seven. The bird does not decide this
with a ledger --- her body's condition, built from the
\omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}'s food, sets what she can provision. Clutch size
is \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} quality, written in \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}.
\end{example}

\section{Limited by the world: populations}

\begin{definition}[Population]\label{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}
A \emph{population}\index{population} is all the
individuals of one \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} living in one place --- the
pond's frogs, the wood's beeches. Reproduction adds to it;
deaths and departures subtract; the \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{environment} sets the
ceiling.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Why populations do not explode]\label{prop:g8:reproduction-and-environment:limits}
Every \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} over-produces --- thousands of \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} per frog
pair, thousands of \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} per beech
(\cref{prop:g4:animal-reproduction:strategies}) --- yet
ponds are not solid with frogs. The surplus is spent
against the \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{environment}'s checks:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{predation} --- the herons and dragonfly
        larvae of \cref{ex:g4:animal-reproduction:pondcount};
  \item \emph{food and space} --- only so many
        \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillars}, nest holes, patches of \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light} on the
        forest floor;
  \item \emph{\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}} --- late frost on the blossom
        (\cref{exo:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:9}), drought on
        the \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}{tadpoles}' shrinking pond.
\end{itemize}
On average, in a steady \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}, each pair replaces
itself --- the balance of
\cref{prop:g5:food-webs:balance}, seen from the parents'
side.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Two springs at the pond]\label{ex:g8:reproduction-and-environment:twosprings}
Spring 1, mild and wet: high water, early \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects} ---
thousands of \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}, hundreds of froglets, the \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population}
climbs. Spring 2, a drought: the pond shrinks to a
puddle, \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}{tadpoles} crowd, \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} falls
(\cref{prop:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:drains}), herons
feast on the concentrated shoal --- a handful of
froglets. Same frogs, same mechanism, two verdicts: the
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{environment} countersigns every generation.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Humans move the checks]\label{rem:g8:reproduction-and-environment:humans}
Most human effects on wildlife run through these checks,
not through the core mechanism: drained ponds and
grubbed hedges remove breeding \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}
(\cref{prop:g2:where-animals-live:loss}); pesticides
empty the \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar} peak the tits timed for; garden
nest boxes and the restored wet meadows of
\cref{ex:g5:biodiversity:storks} add ceiling back.
Protecting a \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} in practice means protecting the
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{environment} its reproduction is plugged into --- cue,
budget and checks together.
\end{remark}

\begin{method}[Auditing a population change]\label{met:g8:reproduction-and-environment:audit}
When a \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population} rises or falls, audit in order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{timing}: did the cue and the resource peak
        still coincide? (a too-early spring can strand
        the chicks after the \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillars});
  \item \emph{budget}: what did the season's \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} let
        parents provision?
  \item \emph{checks}: which changed --- predators, food,
        space, \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}?
  \item \emph{\omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}}: was breeding ground itself gained
        or lost?
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\section{Problem: The Nest-Box Ledger}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- ten years of blue
tits, audited box by box}]\label{pb:g8:reproduction-and-environment:1}
A school monitors twenty nest boxes for ten years:
laying dates, clutch sizes, fledglings per box --- beside
the weather log and the groundskeeper's diary.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Reading the normal
years.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Years 1--4: laying in late April, clutches of
        eight to ten, most \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} fledging. Name the cue
        that sets late April, and the peak the timing
        aims at.
  \item Year 3's clutches ran a \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg} or two larger after a
        warm caterpillar-rich May in year 2 --- the
        parents wintered well. Which proposition does the
        correlation illustrate?
  \item Even in good years, about a fifth of \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} never
        fledge. List the checks likely responsible, from
        \cref{prop:g8:reproduction-and-environment:limits}.
  \item Why does the school log weather and grounds work
        beside the \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}? (Answer with the audit's four
        questions.)
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The anomalies.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Year 5: a freak warm March brought \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillars}
        out two weeks early; laying dates barely moved.
        Predict year 5's fledging, and name the failure
        line of the audit.
  \item Year 6: the groundskeeper's diary records the old
        oaks' dead \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{limbs} removed and the meadow strip
        mowed monthly. Fledging drops although the
        weather was kind. Which audit lines does the
        diary implicate?
  \item Year 7: a sparrowhawk pair settles. Clutches are
        unchanged, but fledgling counts dip. Which check
        moved, and why did the \emph{clutch} line not
        move with it?
  \item Year 8: boxes doubled to forty; total fledglings
        rise, but \emph{per-box} success holds steady.
        What ceiling did the new boxes raise, and which
        did they leave untouched?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The report.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Write the ten-year summary sentence linking
        clutch size to \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} quality, citing
        \cref{ex:g8:reproduction-and-environment:clutch}.
  \item The town proposes felling the oak row for a car
        park, ``compensating'' with fifty more nest
        boxes. Audit the offer: which of cue, budget,
        checks and \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} do boxes replace, and which
        only the oaks provide?
  \item Recommend two grounds measures for year 11, each
        tied to its audit line.
  \item Close with the chapter's moral in one sentence:
        what, besides two parents, does every fledgling
        need?
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
