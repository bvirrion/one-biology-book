\chapter{Matter From Food}\label{ch:g6:matter-from-food}

A calf of \qty{40}{kg} becomes, in two years, a cow of
\qty{600}{kg}. Half a tonne of new cow --- flesh, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone}, hide,
horn --- appeared from somewhere. The cow ate grass; the new
matter \emph{is not grass}; yet without the grass none of it
would exist. What \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} do with their food is this
chapter's business --- the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumers}' side of
\cref{ch:g6:plants-are-producers}'s ledger.

\section{Animals build their matter from food}

\begin{proposition}[Consumers transform organic matter]\label{prop:g6:matter-from-food:transform}
An \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} builds its own \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} --- \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle}, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone},
fat, \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feather}, \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{shell} --- out of the \emph{\omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} it
eats}. \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion}{Digestion} (\cref{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion})
dismantles the food into \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion}{nutrients}; the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} delivers them
(\cref{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:carries}); and the body's
\omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} (\cref{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:growth}) reassemble
them into the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}'s own substance. \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{Animals} are
\emph{transformers} of matter, never makers-from-mineral:
that trade belongs to the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers} alone.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Grass into cow]\label{ex:g6:matter-from-food:cow}
Follow the half-tonne: the cow grazes hour after hour
(\cref{prop:g2:what-animals-eat:daily} explained the long
hours); her heavy \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion}{digestion}
(\cref{exo:g4:journey-of-food:11}) dismantles the grass into
\omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion}{nutrients}; her \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} carries them to udder, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} and
growing calf-inside-her; her \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} build cow-matter from
grass-matter. The pasture is, quite literally, walking about
in a new form.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Matter changes hands, not nature]\label{rem:g6:matter-from-food:hands}
At every arrow of a \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{food chain}
(\cref{def:g3:food-chains:chain}), \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} changes
owners and is rebuilt --- grass-matter into cow-matter,
cow-matter (the \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk}, the meat) into child-matter. The
\emph{organic} never comes from nowhere: \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumers} pass it
along and reshape it. Only at the chain's green start was it
made new (\cref{prop:g6:plants-are-producers:firstlink});
only at the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{decomposers}' end, next chapter, does it return
to mineral.
\end{remark}

\section{Growth is matter production}

\begin{definition}[An animal's matter production]\label{def:g6:matter-from-food:production}
An \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}'s \emph{production of matter}\index{production of
matter} is the new \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} it builds: growth of the
young (the calf's added kilograms), yearly renewals (\omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur}
molted and regrown, antlers, \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}), repairs
(\cref{rem:g3:bones-and-muscles:alive}'s mended \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone}), and
products (\omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk}, \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}, wool). All of it is rebuilt food.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Weighing production]\label{ex:g6:matter-from-food:weighing}
Farmers weigh production daily: a dairy cow gives some
\qty{25}{kg} of \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk} a day --- \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} she must
rebuild from feed, day after day; a hen's \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg} is some
\qty{60}{g} of packed provisions
(\cref{rem:g2:animals-grow-up:egg}), assembled overnight
from her grain. No feed, no production: the ledger balances
mercilessly.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Not all the food becomes body]\label{prop:g6:matter-from-food:losses}
A \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumer} never converts all its food into new matter:
\begin{itemize}
  \item part is never absorbed, and \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} as droppings
        (\cref{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb});
  \item a large part is \emph{burned} --- combined with the
        breath's oxygen
        (\cref{prop:g4:breathing:exchange}) --- to power
        movement and warmth: matter spent as fuel, leaving
        as exhaled gas;
  \item only the remainder becomes growth and products.
\end{itemize}
Of the grass a cow eats, only a modest fraction ends as new
cow --- which is why each floor of
\cref{prop:g5:food-webs:pyramid}'s pyramid is so much
smaller than the one below it.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The pyramid, explained at last]\label{ex:g6:matter-from-food:pyramid}
A meadow's tonnes of grass feed a herd's worth of rabbits;
the rabbits' kilograms feed a fox or two. At each arrow,
most matter is burned for living or lost, and only a
fraction is passed upward as body. Great hunters are rare
(\cref{prop:g5:food-webs:pyramid}) because they live off
the \emph{remainders of remainders} --- arithmetic, not
bad luck.
\end{example}

\section{Menus, teeth and the one same trade}

\begin{proposition}[Different menus, same transformation]\label{prop:g6:matter-from-food:menus}
\omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{Herbivore}, \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{carnivore}, \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{omnivore}
(\cref{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}) name different
\emph{sources} of \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter}, not different trades. The
cow rebuilding grass and the fox rebuilding rabbit both run
\cref{prop:g6:matter-from-food:transform}'s transformation
--- with equipment matched to the menu
(\cref{prop:g2:what-animals-eat:match},
\cref{exo:g4:journey-of-food:11}): grass demands grinders
and vast \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion}{digestion}, meat rewards seizers and a short gut.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{method}[Auditing an animal's matter]\label{met:g6:matter-from-food:audit}
For any \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}, draw up its matter ledger:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{in}: what \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} does it eat, and from
        whom? (its food-web arrows in,
        \cref{def:g5:food-webs:web});
  \item \emph{built}: what growth, renewals and products
        does it make?
  \item \emph{out}: droppings, and matter burned for
        movement and warmth;
  \item check the balance: production must be the modest
        remainder --- if a claim makes it more (``this pond
        yields more fish-matter than the pond grows food''),
        the claim is wrong.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{example}[The audit in use]\label{ex:g6:matter-from-food:audituse}
Audit the blackbird (\cref{ex:g2:what-animals-eat:winter}):
in --- worms, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects}, berries; built --- its own growth,
each year's new \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}, the clutch of \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}; out ---
droppings on the wall, and the large burned share that
powered a year of flying and singing. A songbird's daily
food weight is huge for its size precisely because flight
burns so much of the ledger.
\end{example}

\section{Problem: The Farm Ledger}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- a small farm audited,
field to table}]\label{pb:g6:matter-from-food:1}
Gran's smallholding: a pasture with one cow, a henhouse of
ten hens fed on grain, a vegetable patch, and the family
kitchen. This weekend, you keep its matter ledger.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}' floor.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item List the holding's \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}. What are their
        mineral inputs, and their \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} source?
  \item The pasture grows about \qty{10000}{kg} of grass
        matter in a season. In whose \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} was every gram
        of it manufactured?
  \item Gran spreads the henhouse manure on the vegetable
        patch. Which rule of
        \cref{prop:g5:people-and-nature:lasting} is she
        keeping, and what exactly does the manure restock?
  \item Why can the cow not simply be fed \omterm{prop:g4:what-plants-need:needs}{mineral salts}
        and water directly, sparing the pasture?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumers}' floor.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The cow eats roughly \qty{70}{kg} of grass a day
        and gives about \qty{20}{kg} of \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk}. Name the
        three destinations of the missing \qty{50}{kg}.
  \item The hens turn grain into \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}. Write the hen's
        ledger --- in, built, out --- and say why a molting
        hen (renewing all her \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}) lays fewer \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} for
        a while.
  \item A buzzard takes a chick. Extend the holding's \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{food
        chain} to it, and state at which links matter was
        \emph{made} and at which it was only transformed.
  \item The family eats \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}, \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk} and vegetables. Trace
        the \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter} of one breakfast --- bread,
        boiled \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg}, \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk} --- each to the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf} where it was
        first manufactured.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The arithmetic of the
floors.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Gran says: ``the pasture feeds one cow; it would
        feed no more than a small den of foxes if foxes ate
        grass-fed rabbits on it.'' Justify her estimate
        with the losses of each floor.
  \item The vegetable patch feeds the family directly.
        Using \cref{exo:g6:matter-from-food:12}'s logic,
        explain why the patch feeds more people per square
        metre than the pasture-cow-milk route.
  \item In drought, the grass stops growing. Predict, in
        order, what happens to the cow's \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk}, her weight,
        and the ledger's ``built'' line --- and why no
        extra sunshine can help while the rain fails
        (\cref{pb:g6:plants-are-producers:1}, question 7,
        holds the key).
  \item Close the ledger with one sentence per floor:
        \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}, \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumers} --- and name who is still
        missing from the accounting (the subject of the
        next chapter).
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
