\chapter{Our Responsibility}\label{ch:g9:our-responsibility}

Nine years of this book end here, and its threads meet in a
plain fact: the \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} that can read this page is the one
\omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} that understands the machinery it belongs to --- and
understanding, as \cref{ch:g8:contraception} first said,
brings choice. This closing chapter gathers the
responsibilities the understanding creates: toward one's own
health, toward other people's, and toward the web of \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living
things} that carries us all.

\section{Responsibility for one's own machinery}

\begin{proposition}[Health as informed self-management]\label{prop:g9:our-responsibility:self}
The book's health threads, tied:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the daily contract
        (\cref{prop:g5:healthy-choices:contract}) ---
        food, movement, \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep}, cleanliness --- is organ
        maintenance you can now justify system by system
        (\cref{prop:g7:heart-and-circulation:keep},
        \cref{met:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:keep},
        \cref{met:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:manual});
  \item the substances' mechanisms are known
        (\cref{prop:g8:protecting-the-nervous-system:substances}),
        the trap's asymmetry priced
        (\cref{rem:g5:healthy-choices:never});
  \item the reproductive choices have their map and their
        addresses (\cref{ch:g8:contraception});
  \item and the body's history is partly dealt
        (\cref{ch:g9:heredity-and-traits}): knowing a
        family's tendencies --- the early gray, the
        furring \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{hearts} --- is not fate but a maintenance
        schedule (\cref{met:g9:unique-individuals:claims}:
        ranges, lived in).
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The check-up, read as biology]\label{ex:g9:our-responsibility:checkup}
A routine check-up is this book in an appointment: \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse}
and pressure (the network read at the roadside,
\cref{ex:g7:heart-and-circulation:measures}), the
reflex hammer (\cref{pb:g8:nervous-communication:1}),
vaccination ledger (\cref{pb:g9:immune-defenses:1}),
family history (the dealt tendencies), and the standing
advice --- the contract, again. Medicine is largely
applied maintenance of mechanisms you now own the
manuals for.
\end{example}

\section{Responsibility toward others}

\begin{proposition}[Health is shared machinery]\label{prop:g9:our-responsibility:others}
Three mechanisms make health a shared account:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{transmission}
        (\cref{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:stages}):
        your \omterm{def:g1:healthy-habits:hygiene}{hygiene}, your cough's cover and your sick-day
        at home are other people's protection;
  \item \emph{pooled \omterm{def:g9:immune-defenses:memory}{immunity}}
        (\cref{prop:g9:immune-defenses:vaccination}):
        vaccination guards, through you, those who cannot
        be trained;
  \item \emph{the commons of medicine}: antibiotics kept
        working (\cref{exo:g9:how-species-change:10}) and
        \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} given (\cref{ex:g9:unique-individuals:medicine}
        --- the register runs on donors) are shared
        resources spent or stocked by individual choices.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[One teenager's shared account]\label{ex:g9:our-responsibility:account}
The week's ordinary entries: stayed home feverish
(transmission cut); boosters current (the pool held); a
finished antibiotic course from spring (the commons
unspent); a first \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} donation planned at eighteen
(the register stocked). None heroic ---
\cref{ex:g5:healthy-choices:day}'s lesson at the scale
of a town: public health is ordinary days, kept.
\end{example}

\section{Responsibility toward the living world}

\begin{proposition}[The stewardship, restated with all its reasons]\label{prop:g9:our-responsibility:stewardship}
The nature threads, tied with their mechanisms:
\begin{itemize}
  \item every \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{food chain} starts green
        (\cref{prop:g6:plants-are-producers:firstlink}),
        every table is an ecosystem's output
        (\cref{prop:g6:producing-our-food:costs});
  \item the webs absorb shocks by variety
        (\cref{prop:g5:biodiversity:web}), and variety is
        the reserve selection runs on
        (\cref{prop:g9:unique-individuals:reserve},
        \cref{exo:g9:how-species-change:12}) ---
        \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:biodiversity}{biodiversity} is not scenery but working capital;
  \item extinction forecloses forever
        (\cref{prop:g5:biodiversity:loss}) --- the one
        deliberate exception, smallpox, proves how
        absolute the word is
        (\cref{exo:g9:immune-defenses:12});
  \item the rules of lasting use are known
        (\cref{prop:g5:people-and-nature:lasting}), their
        violations diagnosable
        (\cref{ex:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:waste}), their
        repairs demonstrated
        (\cref{ex:g5:people-and-nature:river},
        \cref{ex:g5:biodiversity:storks}).
\end{itemize}
Stewardship is the same maintenance mindset as health,
applied to the larger body we live inside.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[What a citizen's biology is for]\label{ex:g9:our-responsibility:citizen}
The votes, purchases and habits of adult life keep
arriving dressed as questions this book has equipped:
Is this fishery taking faster than renewal? Does this
policy guard the pollinators' \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} or pave it? Is
this claimed miracle diet, cure or ``\omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{gene} for X''
coherent (\cref{met:g9:unique-individuals:claims},
\cref{met:g8:contraception:evaluate})? Should this
antibiotic be in this trough? A citizen who can run
these audits is what nine years of chapters were for.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[The graduate's method]\label{met:g9:our-responsibility:method}
Leaving Book 1, carry the method more than the facts:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item observe before concluding
        (\cref{met:g1:animals-around-us:observe} started
        it; every census and notebook since);
  \item test fairly --- one difference, a control
        (\cref{met:g4:what-plants-need:fairtest}, used in
        every grade after);
  \item name the mechanism --- no claim audited until its
        link is named
        (\cref{met:g8:contraception:evaluate});
  \item weigh independent witnesses
        (\cref{met:g9:evidence-of-evolution:weigh});
  \item and respect the living machinery, in your body
        and outside it: it is old, subtle, repairable
        within limits --- and yours to keep.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{remark}[What comes next]\label{rem:g9:our-responsibility:next}
The high-school volume takes these threads up at the next

\end{remark}

\section{Problem: The Graduation Audit}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- one town year,
audited with the whole book}]\label{pb:g9:our-responsibility:1}
Your town's (invented) year in headlines: a winter flu
wave; a spring debate over spraying the plane trees; a
summer \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} kill in the park pond; an autumn campaign
for the new marsh reserve; and, year-round, the
clinic's vaccination and donation drives.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The health file.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The flu wave: name the transmission routes the
        town's advice targeted, and why the clinic
        vaccinated care-home staff first.
  \item The drives: state what the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} register and
        the vaccination ledger each stock, and who
        draws on them.
  \item A columnist mocks ``fuss over hand-washing''.
        Answer with the ward of
        \cref{ex:g9:microbes-and-infection:history} ---
        one paragraph.
  \item The pharmacy reports unfinished antibiotic
        courses returning. Which commons is being
        spent, by which mechanism?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The nature file.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The spraying debate: run the orchard audit
        (\cref{exo:g9:our-responsibility:9}) on
        ``kill every insect'' --- who else dies, and
        what free services stop?
  \item The \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} kill: a hot still week and a
        pea-green pond. Diagnose with
        \cref{ch:g7:oxygen-in-the-water}, and
        prescribe.
  \item The marsh campaign: defend the reserve with
        three mechanisms --- \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}, web, reserve of
        variety --- one sentence each.
  \item A landowner asks ``what has the marsh ever
        done for the town?'' Answer with the free work
        ledger (\cref{exo:g5:biodiversity:11}, the
        river's flood storage of
        \cref{ex:g5:people-and-nature:river}).
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The graduate's
close.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Pick the year's best and worst decision by the
        rules of lasting use, and justify each verdict.
  \item Show the method's five steps at work in how
        the \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} kill was resolved --- from first
        observation to accepted mechanism.
  \item Draft the town paper's year-end editorial in
        four sentences: health shared, nature banked,
        claims audited, method kept.
  \item The last line of the last page of Book 1 is
        yours to write: one sentence, addressed to the
        reader who started at
        \cref{ch:g1:living-or-not}'s snail.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
