\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g1:living-or-not} --- Living or Not Living?}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-or-not:1}
Born, feeds, grows, can have babies, dies. Today a kitten can be
seen feeding (drinking milk, eating) and growing --- it gets bigger
week after week.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-or-not:2}
(a) Living --- it hatched, feeds on nectar, and there can be baby
caterpillars. (b) Not living --- built in a factory, never feeds or
grows. (c) Living --- it grew from a seed. (d) Not living --- a
cloud grows and shrinks, but it was never born, does not feed and
has no babies.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-or-not:3}
It is not living now. It was living: it grew on the tree, fed by the
tree. It comes from a \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living thing} --- the tree.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-or-not:4}
Ask the questions of life together: was the river born? Does it
feed? Does it grow? Can it have baby rivers? No --- moving and
making noise are not signs of life on their own. The river is not
living, even though many \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living things} live \emph{in} it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-or-not:5}
Living: the spider, the apple on the tree. Was living (or comes
from a \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living thing}): the woolly sock (wool from a sheep), the
fallen pine cone (it grew on a pine). Never living: the raindrop.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-or-not:6}
No. The flame fails the questions of life: it was lit, not born,
and above all there are no baby flames that grow up --- a flame can
be copied to another candle, but it does not have young like a
snail or a daisy. (It is a fair question: the flame does trick two
of the signs!)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-or-not:7}
For example: a wooden chair (from a tree), a cotton T-shirt (from a
cotton plant), a woolly blanket (from a sheep), a leather shoe
(from a cow), honey on the shelf (from bees).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-or-not:8}
The bean changes: it swells, then a little white root comes out,
then a green shoot. The pebble stays exactly the same. This shows
the bean was alive --- a quiet, waiting \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living thing} --- and the
pebble is not.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-or-not:9}
Was it born? The snail hatched from an egg; the robot was built.
Can it have babies? There can be baby snails; there are no baby
robots. (Feeding works too: salad makes the snail grow, the battery
does not make the robot grow.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g1:living-or-not:10}
A mushroom is born (it appears from tiny beginnings in the soil),
it feeds (it takes its food from the soil and from dead leaves), it
grows --- sometimes in a single night --- it makes new mushrooms,
and it dies. All the signs of life are there: it is a \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living thing},
even without legs and without green leaves.
\end{solution}
