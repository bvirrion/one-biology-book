\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:cytoskeleton-motility} --- Cytoskeleton Dynamics and Cell Motility}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:1}
Actin: \qty{7}{nm}, globular actin, polar (barbed/pointed), ATP;
cortex, protrusion, contraction with \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:motors}{myosin}. \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:filaments}{Microtubule}: \qty{25}{nm},
$\alpha\beta$-tubulin dimer, polar (plus/minus), GTP; long-range
transport, spindle, cilia. \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:filaments}{Intermediate filament}: \qty{10}{nm},
coiled-coil proteins (keratin, vimentin, lamin), apolar, no nucleotide;
mechanical strength of cell and nucleus.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:2}
The monomer concentration at which an end neither grows nor shrinks,
$k_{\text{off}}/k_{\text{on}}$. The two ends differ because the subunit
hydrolyses its ATP after incorporation, so the ends present different
chemical species (ATP-actin arriving at the barbed end, ADP-actin
leaving the pointed end) with different affinities. With equal \omterm{thm:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:treadmilling}{critical
concentrations} there would be a single equilibrium: no treadmilling,
no flux, and no way to pay for directional turnover.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:3}
Periphery on \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:filaments}{microtubules}: \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:motors}{kinesin}, toward the plus end. Centre:
cytoplasmic \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:motors}{dynein}, toward the minus end. Stress fibre: \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:motors}{myosin} II,
toward the barbed end of actin. \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:cilia}{Cilium}: \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:cilia}{axonemal dynein}, toward the
minus end of the neighbouring doublet (the base).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:4}
Protrusion (Rac for lamellipodia, Cdc42 for filopodia), adhesion
(\omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:migration}{integrins}; downstream of Rac and Rho), contraction (Rho, through
\omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:motors}{myosin} II), retraction of the rear (release of adhesions, driven by the
contraction).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:5}
$C_{c}^{+} = 1/5 = \qty{0.2}{\micro M}$, $C_{c}^{-} = 0.8/1 =
\qty{0.8}{\micro M}$; $C_{\text{ss}} = (1 + 0.8)/(5 + 1) = \qty{0.3}{\micro
M}$; $J = 5\times 0.3 - 1 = 0.5$ subunits per second, \qty{1.35}{nm/s}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:6}
$800/8 = 100$ ATP per second. The metre takes $1/(8\times 10^{-7}) =
1.25\times 10^{6}$ s, about two weeks, and $1.25\times 10^{8}$ steps and
ATP. Diffusion would take \num{16000} years: a thousand-million-fold
difference.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:7}
$F_{\max} = 8.3\times 10^{-20}/3.6\times 10^{-8} = \qty{2.3}{pN}$. The same
energy spread over a longer step gives less force --- a longer lever.
\omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:motors}{Kinesin} (short step, \qty{6}{pN}) suits heavy loads; \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:motors}{myosin} V (long
step) covers more distance per ATP and suits fast, light transport.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:8}
(a) Taxol freezes \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:filaments}{microtubules}: the spindle cannot search, capture and
correct, mitosis arrests at the checkpoint; the crawling cell keeps
protruding but loses long-term polarity. (b) Colchicine removes
\omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:filaments}{microtubules}: no spindle, mitotic arrest; crawling continues on actin
but without persistent direction. (c) Cytochalasin caps barbed ends: no
protrusion, no crawling; mitosis proceeds but cytokinesis fails, giving
binucleate cells. (d) Rac inhibition: no lamellipodia, so no crawling;
division largely unaffected.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:9}
Residence time of the order of the half-time, \qty{20}{s} (time constant
$20/\ln 2 \approx \qty{29}{s}$); immobile fraction \qty{10}{\%}.
Polymerisation at the edge must supply both the advance and the
retrograde flow: $1 + 1.5 = \qty{2.5}{\micro m/min}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:10}
A growing end keeps a cap of GTP-tubulin; behind it hydrolysis
produces strained GDP-tubulin. Loss of the cap --- a random event whose
probability rises as growth slows --- releases the strain and the end
shrinks fast until a new cap forms. Just above the \omterm{thm:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:treadmilling}{critical
concentration}, each \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:filaments}{microtubule} is at any moment either capped and
growing or uncapped and collapsing, so lengths diverge into a long
population and a vanishing one instead of clustering at a mean. The
cell gains rapid exploration of its volume and the ability to
stabilise selectively --- a plus end that reaches a kinetochore or a
cortical site is captured and kept, the others are recycled within
minutes: search and capture.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:11}
At this scale inertia is negligible and the fluid equations are
time-reversible: a motion that retraces itself (an oar going out and
back) returns the body to where it started, whatever the speed of each
stroke. A rotating helix never retraces itself --- its motion is
chiral and continuous --- so it produces net thrust. At \qty{100}{Hz}
with a thousand protons per turn, $10^{5}$ protons per second per
motor.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:12}
Airway cilia cannot beat, mucus and bacteria are not cleared, and
infections recur. Sperm flagella, built on the same \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:cilia}{axoneme}, are
immotile: infertility. In the embryo, motile cilia of the node drive a
leftward flow that sets the left--right axis; without flow the side is
chosen at random, so half the patients have their organs reversed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:1}
\textbf{1.} $\qty{300}{\micro m^3} = 3\times 10^{-13}$ L; $\times
2\times 10^{-4}$ mol/L $= 6\times 10^{-17}$ mol $= 3.6\times 10^{7}$
molecules; $1.8\times 10^{7}$ in filaments.
\textbf{2.} $1.8\times 10^{7}\times \qty{2.7}{nm} = \qty{4.9}{cm}$ of
filament; at \qty{0.25}{\micro m} each, about $2\times 10^{5}$
filaments.
\textbf{3.} Capping protein blocks most barbed ends, and profilin and
thymosin-$\beta$4 bind the monomers so that the truly free ATP-actin is
near the \omterm{thm:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:treadmilling}{critical concentration}; growth is confined to the ends the
cell uncaps.
\textbf{4.} $C_{\text{ss}} = 2.2/12.9 = \qty{0.17}{\micro M}$; $J = 11.6
\times 0.17 - 1.4 = 0.6$ subunits per second; \qty{1.6}{nm/s} $=
\qty{0.1}{\micro m/min}$.
\textbf{5.} $1/0.1 = 10$-fold.
\textbf{6.} \qty{1}{\micro m/min} $= \qty{16.7}{nm/s} = 6.2$ subunits per
second per filament; $\times 2\times 10^{5} = 1.2\times 10^{6}$ ATP per
second, about $0.1\,\%$ of the cell's turnover.
\textbf{7.} $1/(8\times 10^{-7}) = 1.25\times 10^{6}$ s $\approx 14.5$
days; $1.25\times 10^{8}$ steps and ATP.
\textbf{8.} $L^{2}/2D$: $(1)^{2}/(2\times 10^{-12}) = 5\times 10^{11}$ s
$\approx \num{16000}$ years for a metre; $(10^{-5})^{2}/(2\times
10^{-12}) = 50$ s for \qty{10}{\micro m}. Diffusion serves within a
cell body; anything longer needs motors.
\textbf{9.} $5\times 10^{4}/6.02\times 10^{23} = 8.3\times 10^{-20}$ J
$= 20\,k_{B}T$.
\textbf{10.} $8.3\times 10^{-20}/8\times 10^{-9} = \qty{10}{pN}$;
efficiency $6/10 \approx 60\,\%$.
\textbf{11.} $F = 6\pi\times 0.01\times 10^{-7}\times 8\times 10^{-7} =
1.5\times 10^{-14}$ N $= \qty{0.015}{pN}$, four hundred times below
the \omterm{prop:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:physics}{stall force}: one motor is ample against viscous drag; the real
loads are obstacles and tethers.
\textbf{12.} $10^{4}\times 100 = 10^{6}$ ATP per second, $0.1\,\%$ of the
neuron's budget: transport is cheap. But motors stop the moment ATP
falls, so a failure of mitochondria starves the synapse of everything
made in the cell body --- the axonal transport failure seen in
neurodegenerative disease.
\textbf{13.} \qty{10}{\micro m/min} $= \qty{167}{nm/s}$; $167/2.7 = 62$
subunits per second per filament.
\textbf{14.} $200\times 20 = 4000$ filaments; $4000\times 62 = 2.5\times
10^{5}$ subunits per second.
\textbf{15.} $2.5\times 10^{5}$ ATP per second, $0.025\,\%$ of the
budget.
\textbf{16.} $\qty{3}{\micro m}/(\qty{10}{\micro m/min}) = \qty{0.3}{min}
= \qty{18}{s}$; $2.5\times 10^{5}\times 18 = 4.5\times 10^{6}$ subunits
in the network.
\textbf{17.} $1\,\text{pN}\times 2.7\,\text{nm} = \qty{2.7}{pN.nm} =
0.66\,k_{B}T$: a fluctuation of that energy occurs with probability
about $e^{-0.66} \approx 0.5$ --- gaps of one subunit open constantly,
and the ratchet is entirely plausible.
\textbf{18.} Polymerisation itself pushes, the bacterium being the
``membrane'' at the front of its own network; with the cell's Arp2/3
machinery it reaches the lamellipodial speed and more --- up to
\qty{0.5}{\micro m/s}, \qty{30}{\micro m/min}.
\textbf{19.} At $C = K_{d}$, $\theta = 1/2$ and $\mathrm{d}\theta/
\mathrm{d}C = 1/(4C)$: a \qty{2}{\%} difference in $C$ gives $\Delta\theta
= 0.005$; with \num{25000} receptors per side, $125$ more occupied at
the front.
\textbf{20.} Each side has about \num{12500} occupied, fluctuating by
$\sqrt{\num{12500}} \approx 110$, so the difference of two sides
fluctuates by about $160$: the $125$ is lost in the noise at any
instant. Receptors rebind about once a second; averaging over $N$
seconds shrinks the noise by $\sqrt{N}$ --- ten seconds bring it to
$50$ and the gradient is read.
\textbf{21.} Rac (with Cdc42) at the front, Rho at the rear. With active
Rac everywhere the cell protrudes all round, spreads, and goes nowhere.
\textbf{22.} $30/3 = \qty{10}{\micro m/min}$: as given.
\textbf{23.} Protrusion is replaced by pressure-driven blebbing; adhesion
and contraction still depend on actin and \omterm{def:b3:cytoskeleton-motility:motors}{myosin}, so actin remains
essential for the rest of the cycle.
\textbf{24.} The front is rebuilt every \qty{18}{s}: a shift in Rac
activity redirects polymerisation within one network lifetime, the new
front leads, and the slow steps at the rear follow.
\textbf{25.} Treadmilling in vitro \qty{1.6}{nm/s}; about $14.5$ days
to travel the axon; some $2.5\times 10^{5}$ ATP per second for
protrusion.
\end{solution}
