\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b1:body-plans-tissues} --- Animal Body Plans and Tissues}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:1}
Radial: organised around an axis, no left and right (hydra,
jellyfish). Bilateral: one mirror plane, front and rear, back and belly
(earthworm, frog). \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:bodyplan}{Cephalisation} goes with \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:symmetry}{bilateral symmetry}: an animal
that moves head first meets the world at its front, where sense \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:organ}{organs}
and nervous centres concentrate.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:2}
\omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:layers}{Ectoderm}: skin epidermis, \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:nervous}{nervous tissue}. \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:layers}{Mesoderm}: muscle, bone,
blood, \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:connective}{connective tissue}. \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:layers}{Endoderm}: the lining of the gut.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:3}
Epithelial: packed polarised cells in a sheet on a \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:epithelium}{basal lamina};
covering, absorption, secretion. Connective: scattered cells in an
abundant matrix; binding, support, transport. Muscle: cells packed with
contractile filaments; movement. Nervous: \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:nervous}{neurons} and glia; sensing,
integration, command.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:4}
Tight junction (seals the intercellular gap), adherens junction
(fastens, linked to an actin belt), desmosome (mechanical rivet linked
to intermediate filaments), gap junction (channel between the two
cytoplasms).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:5}
Triploblastic, coelomate, with a through gut, \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:segments}{segmented}, soft-bodied
with a \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:segments}{hydrostatic skeleton}: an annelid (earthworm).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:6}
The coelomic fluid of each segment is incompressible. Contracting the
circular muscles makes the segment thin and long (it pushes forward);
contracting the longitudinal muscles makes it short and fat (it anchors
against the burrow wall with its bristles). Waves of the two
contractions passing backward along the body pull the worm forward.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:7}
\omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:segments}{Exoskeleton}: rigid, protective, muscles attached inside; cannot grow,
so the animal must moult and is soft and vulnerable meanwhile; on land
its mass grows as the cube of size while its strength as the square,
which limits arthropods to small sizes. \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:segments}{Endoskeleton}: grows with the
animal, no moulting, less surface protection, muscles attached outside;
permits large sizes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:8}
Tight junctions force every substance to pass through the cells, whose
membranes carry selective transporters; the \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:epithelium}{epithelium} can then choose
what crosses and in which direction. Without them the lumen contents
would leak between the cells into the \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:milieu}{internal environment} (bacteria,
toxins, undigested molecules), and absorbed nutrients would leak back.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:9}
Small intestine. Mucosa (\omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:epithelium}{epithelium} with villi, its loose \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:connective}{connective
tissue}, the thin muscularis mucosae), submucosa (dense \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:connective}{connective
tissue}), muscularis (inner circular, outer longitudinal smooth muscle),
serosa.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:10}
Cartilage cells are fed by diffusion through the gel from surrounding
vessels, so the \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:tissue}{tissue} is thin or slowly metabolising, heals badly and
is used where a smooth, compressible, avascular surface is wanted:
joint surfaces, the ear, the nose, the growing ends of bones. Bone's
cells sit within a mineral that nothing diffuses through and must be
fed by vessels in canals; bone heals well and remodels throughout life,
and forms the load-bearing skeleton.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:11}
Absorption becomes unselective: nutrients cross between cells down
their gradients and the transporters' directional work is lost, while
absorbed molecules leak back. The \omterm{def:b1:organism-environment:milieu}{internal environment} receives whatever
is in the lumen --- ions, water, undigested peptides, bacterial
products --- and loses water and ions into the gut: diarrhoea, oedema
or dehydration, disturbed plasma composition. Bacteria and toxins
crossing the barrier cause inflammation and infection.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:body-plans-tissues:12}
A plan is inherited, not chosen for the animal's needs, and it fixes
what later evolution can do: an \omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:segments}{exoskeleton} demands moulting and keeps
insects small; a vertebrate must build its body from segments even when
it has no use for them; a radial animal cannot easily evolve a head.
The table lists the alternatives that lineages were handed, each
complete and successful within its constraints --- not a ladder, since
the frog's plan is no improvement on the crayfish's, only a different
set of possibilities.
\end{solution}
