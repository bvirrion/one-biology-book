\begin{solution}{exo:g4:heart-and-blood:7}

so the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} makes its delivery rounds more often.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:heart-and-blood:9}

In the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} it handed the oxygen over and took on carbon

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:heart-and-blood:10}
The \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:tract}{small intestine} puts \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion}{nutrients} into the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}; the \omterm{def:g4:breathing:organs}{lungs}
put in oxygen and take out carbon dioxide; the \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} is the
pump that keeps the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} moving between them and out to
every consumer --- \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}, brain, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones}, \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:heart-and-blood:11}
The trained \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} is a stronger \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle}: each \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{beat} moves more
\omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}. Delivering the same supply then needs fewer \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{beats} per
minute --- so the cyclist's calm $50$ does the work of the
untrained $75$, with spare power held in reserve for effort.
\end{solution}
