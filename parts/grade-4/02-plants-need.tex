\chapter{What Plants Need}\label{ch:g4:plants-need}

A healthy \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} is not magic: it \omterm{def:g1:caring:needs}{needs} certain things from its
surroundings. This chapter names those \omterm{def:g1:caring:needs}{needs}, links them to \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} parts,
and shows how a fair test can reveal what a \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} cannot do without.

\section{Plants are living things}

\begin{definition}[Plant needs]\label{def:g4:plants-need:needs}
Most green \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plants} need \emph{light}\index{light (plants)},
\emph{water}\index{water (plants)}, \emph{air}\index{air (plants)}
(including carbon dioxide), and \emph{nutrients}\index{nutrients (plants)}
from soil or another growing medium. Temperature must also stay in a
range the \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} can tolerate.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[No free lunch]\label{prop:g4:plants-need:need}
If a \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} lacks \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{light}, \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{water}, suitable \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{air}, or essential \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{nutrients} for
long enough, it cannot \omterm{def:g2:what-alive:signs}{grow} well and may die.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

work together so the \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} can take in what it \omterm{def:g1:caring:needs}{needs} and \omterm{def:g2:what-alive:signs}{grow}.}

\section{How plant parts help}

\begin{definition}[Roots, stem and leaves]\label{def:g4:plants-need:parts}
\emph{Roots}\index{root} anchor the \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} and take up \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{water} and minerals.
The \emph{stem}\index{stem} supports \omterm{def:g2:plant-parts:main}{leaves} and \omterm{def:g2:plant-parts:main}{flowers} and carries
materials up and down. \emph{Leaves}\index{leaf} are the main \omterm{def:g1:caring:needs}{place}
where green \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plants} capture \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{light} and make \omterm{def:g2:food-energy:food}{food}.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Wilted pot plant]\label{ex:g4:plants-need:wilt}
When soil dries out, \omterm{def:g2:plant-parts:main}{leaves} often droop. Watering can restore firmness
because \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{water} helps \omterm{def:g4:cells:cell}{cells} stay full and stiff. Without \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{water} for too
long, the \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} cannot recover.
\end{example}

\section{Light and making food}

\begin{definition}[Making food (preview)]\label{def:g4:plants-need:food}
Green \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plants} use \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{light} \omterm{def:g2:food-energy:energy}{energy} to make \omterm{def:g2:food-energy:food}{food} (sugars) from simple
materials. This process is called
\emph{photosynthesis}\index{photosynthesis} and is studied more fully
in later grades. \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:parts}{Leaves} need \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{light} and \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{air} for this work.
\end{definition}

\section{Fair tests with plants}

\begin{method}[Testing one need at a time]\label{met:g4:plants-need:test}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Ask a clear question (for example: ``Does this seedling need
        \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{light} to stay green?'').
  \item Change only one factor (for example \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{light} vs darkness).
  \item Keep \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{water}, temperature and pot size the same.
  \item Use several \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plants} in each group if possible.
  \item Observe and record over several days.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Light vs dark]\label{ex:g4:plants-need:dark}
Two trays of bean seedlings receive the same \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{water}. One tray sits on a
sunny sill; the other stays in a dark cupboard. After a week the dark
seedlings are pale and spindly --- evidence that \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{light} matters for
healthy \omterm{def:g2:growing:growth}{growth}.
\end{example}

\section{Too little, too much}

\begin{proposition}[Balance]\label{prop:g4:plants-need:balance}
\omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{Plants} need enough of each resource, not infinite amounts. Overwatering
can drown \omterm{def:g2:plant-parts:main}{roots} by driving out \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{air} from soil spaces; scorching heat or
frost can damage tissues even when \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{water} is present.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Desert and rainforest]\label{ex:g4:plants-need:habitats}
Cacti store \omterm{def:g4:plants-need:needs}{water} and often have spines instead of broad \omterm{def:g2:plant-parts:main}{leaves} ---
suited to dry \omterm{def:g1:caring:needs}{places}. Rainforest \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plants} may have large \omterm{def:g2:plant-parts:main}{leaves} that shed
heavy rain. \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{Plant} \omterm{def:g1:caring:needs}{needs} are universal, but \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} features match local
conditions.
\end{example}
