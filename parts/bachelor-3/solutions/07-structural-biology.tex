\begin{solution}{exo:b3:structural-biology:3}
That the native structure, including the correct pairing of four
disulfides out of $105$ possibilities, is recovered spontaneously from
the unfolded chain: the sequence contains all the information needed,
and the native state is the thermodynamic minimum. Doing it in a test
tube with pure protein excluded any template, enzyme or cellular
machinery as the source of the information.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:structural-biology:4}
NMR: small proteins, below about \qty{40}{kDa}, in solution.
Crystallography: any size that crystallises, from peptides to
ribosomes. \omterm{def:b3:structural-biology:methods}{Cryo-electron microscopy}: large complexes, above about
\qty{50}{kDa}, without crystals. NMR reports motion directly (and
cryo-EM can sort molecules into conformational classes); a crystal
structure is a static average.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:structural-biology:5}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:structural-biology:6}
$Y = [\alpha(1+\alpha) + Lc\alpha(1+c\alpha)]/[(1+\alpha)^{2} +
L(1+c\alpha)^{2}]$. $\alpha = 1$: $3.01/106 = 0.028$ (single site
$0.50$). $\alpha = 10$: $121/242 = 0.50$ ($0.91$). $\alpha = 100$:
$\num{10300}/\num{10601} = 0.97$ ($0.99$). The MWC curve lags far
behind the single site at low ligand, then rises steeply --- from $0.03$
to $0.97$ over two decades where the single site goes from $0.5$ to
$0.99$: that steepness is the \omterm{def:b3:structural-biology:allostery}{cooperativity}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:structural-biology:7}
Bisphosphoglycerate binds only the \omterm{def:b3:structural-biology:allostery}{T state} (in the central cavity), so
it stabilises T: in the model it raises $L$, the $T/R$ ratio of the
unliganded protein, and so shifts the curve to the right --- lower
affinity, more oxygen released at tissue pressures. Stored red cells
lose the compound, $L$ falls, the curve shifts left, and transfused
blood holds its oxygen instead of giving it up in the tissues, until
the cells resynthesise it over a day.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:structural-biology:8}
$d = \lambda/(2\sin\theta) = 0.1/(2\times 0.259) = \qty{0.19}{nm}$: the
backbone and every side chain are placed, individual atoms resolved.
For \qty{0.12}{nm}: $\sin\theta = 0.1/0.24 = 0.417$, $\theta =

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:structural-biology:9}
A charged lysine buried among non-polar side chains costs tens of
kilojoules per mole (its charge unsolvated, the leucine's hydrophobic
burial lost), more than the whole $\Delta G$ of folding: the protein
unfolds or the core rearranges. On the surface the lysine is
hydrated as it would be in the unfolded state and nothing is lost.
$\qty{-30}{kJ/mol} \approx -12RT$ means $K = e^{12} \approx 1.6\times
10^{5}$: one molecule in \num{160000} is unfolded at any moment, and
the whole margin equals two or three hydrogen bonds out of hundreds
--- one mutation's worth.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:structural-biology:10}
The $R$ species total $[R_{0}](1+\alpha)^{n}$ and the $T$ species
$[R_{0}]L(1+c\alpha)^{n}$, so $\bar R = (1+\alpha)^{n}/[(1+\alpha)^{n}
+ L(1+c\alpha)^{n}]$. For $c \to 0$ the $T$ term is $L$, and $\bar R =
1/2$ when $(1+\alpha)^{n} = L$, i.e. $\alpha = L^{1/n} - 1$. For
haemoglobin $(3\times 10^{5})^{1/4} = 23.4$, so $\alpha \approx 22$:
\qty{22}{mmHg}, close to the $P_{50}$ of \qty{26}{mmHg} --- the
conformational switch and half-saturation nearly coincide.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:structural-biology:11}
It was resisted because every known infectious agent replicated
through nucleic acid, and a protein cannot copy its sequence. A
nucleic acid found to be required, or purified protein shown unable to
transmit, would refute it. Support: infectivity survives nucleases,
ultraviolet and ionising radiation at doses that destroy any genome,
but is destroyed by protein denaturants and proteases; recombinant
PrP folded in vitro into fibrils transmits disease to animals, with
the same strain properties on passage. A seed is needed because the
conversion of a normal monomer is prohibitively slow alone --- the
nucleus of the cross-$\beta$ structure must first form, which is the
rate-limiting event --- whereas a pre-formed fibril end converts
monomers rapidly: the shape is copied by templated growth.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:structural-biology:12}
Many sequences \omterm{def:b3:structural-biology:levels}{fold} to the same structure: what a \omterm{def:b3:structural-biology:levels}{fold} needs is a
pattern of hydrophobic and polar positions, a few glycines and prolines
at the turns, and the residues that do the job; everything else on the
surface can change without penalty, so mutations accumulate there while
selection preserves the \omterm{def:b3:structural-biology:levels}{fold} and function. The conserved residues are
therefore the core (as a pattern more than as identities), the
catalytic and binding residues, and structural glycines, prolines and
cysteines. Profile methods weight exactly those columns and so
recognise family members at \qty{15}{\%} identity; protein design runs
the logic backwards, choosing a hydrophobic pattern for a target \omterm{def:b3:structural-biology:levels}{fold}
and letting the surface be anything.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:structural-biology:1}
\textbf{1.} $0.75\times 150 \approx 112$ helical residues; $112\times
\qty{0.15}{nm} = \qty{17}{nm}$; $112/3.6 \approx 31$ turns.
\textbf{2.} $112 - 8\times 4 = 80$ hydrogen bonds.
\textbf{3.} Mass $150\times 110 = \qty{16500}{Da} = 2.7\times 10^{-20}$
g; volume $2.7\times 10^{-20}\times 0.73 = 2.0\times 10^{-20}$ cm$^{3}$
$= \qty{20}{nm^3}$; radius $(3V/4\pi)^{1/3} = \qty{1.7}{nm}$.
\textbf{4.} Extended $150\times 0.36 = \qty{54}{nm}$ against a diameter
of \qty{3.4}{nm}: a factor of $16$.
\textbf{5.} The helical residues are \qty{75}{\%} of the extended
length, $112\times 0.36 = \qty{40}{nm}$; the helix compresses them to
\qty{17}{nm}, a factor $0.36/0.15 = 2.4$.
\textbf{6.} $40\times 4 = \qty{160}{kJ/mol}$ gained, $150\times 0.9 =
\qty{135}{kJ/mol}$ lost: net $\Delta G \approx \qty{-25}{kJ/mol}$, in
the marginal range.
\textbf{7.} $3^{150} \approx 4\times 10^{71}$; $4\times 10^{59}$ s
$\approx 10^{52}$ years.
\textbf{8.} $10^{-2}\times 10^{12} = 10^{10}$ conformations, a fraction
$3\times 10^{-62}$ of the count.
\textbf{9.} Helices in \qty{1}{\micro s} (in parallel); $8! = \num{40320}$
packings at $10^{6}$ per second take \qty{40}{ms}: total about
\qty{40}{ms}, the observed order of magnitude. Yes: solving the parts
independently and then the whole is a funnel --- the search space is
factorised, not exhausted.
\textbf{10.} Expected cycles $1/0.3 = 3.3$, about \qty{33}{s}; after
six cycles $0.7^{6} = 0.12$ remains unfolded.
\textbf{11.} $\Delta G = -RT\ln K$ with $K = 10^{4} \to 10^{2}$: from
$-23.7$ to \qty{-11.9}{kJ/mol}, a loss of \qty{12}{kJ/mol} of stability
($RT = \qty{2.58}{kJ/mol}$, $\ln 100 = 4.6$).
\textbf{12.} Aggregation begins with a nucleus, whose formation rate
rises as a high power of the concentration of the aggregation-prone
unfolded species; a hundredfold more of that species raises the chance
of a seed forming within a lifetime by orders of magnitude, and once a
seed exists growth is fast and self-templating.
\textbf{13.} $\alpha = 100$: $Y = 1.054\times 10^{8}/1.089\times 10^{8} =
0.97$. $\alpha = 20$: numerator $185\,220 + 103\,680 = 288\,900$,
denominator $194\,481 + 622\,080 = 816\,561$: $Y = 0.35$.
\textbf{14.} $\bar R = 1.041\times 10^{8}/1.089\times 10^{8} = 0.96$ in
the lung; $194\,481/816\,561 = 0.24$ in the muscle.
\textbf{15.} $0.97 - 0.35 = 0.62$ of capacity unloaded (two thirds of
the load). Single site: $100/126 - 20/46 = 0.79 - 0.43 = 0.36$.
\textbf{16.} Capacity $150\times 1.34 = \qty{200}{mL/L}$; to the muscle
$0.62\times 200 \approx \qty{125}{mL/L}$. At rest $(0.97 - 0.78)\times
200 = \qty{38}{mL/L}$; $250/38 \approx \qty{6.5}{L/min}$ of blood
flow --- the order of a resting cardiac output.
\textbf{17.} $L = 3\times 10^{6}$, $\alpha = 40$: numerator $2.76\times
10^{6} + 3.29\times 10^{6} = 6.05\times 10^{6}$, denominator $2.83\times
10^{6} + 11.5\times 10^{6} = 14.4\times 10^{6}$: $Y = 0.42$ instead of
$0.78$ --- far more oxygen unloaded at rest, the adaptation of blood to
altitude and of muscle to work.
\textbf{18.} Fetal, $L = 3\times 10^{4}$, $\alpha = 30$: numerator
$893\,730 + 19\,770 = 913\,500$, denominator $923\,520 + 85\,680 =
1\,009\,200$: $Y = 0.91$. Maternal at the same pressure: numerator
$893\,730 + 197\,730 = 1\,091\,460$, denominator $923\,520 + 856\,830
= 1\,780\,350$: $Y = 0.61$. At equal pressure the fetal blood holds more
oxygen, so oxygen flows from mother to fetus across the placenta.
\textbf{19.} $(10^{5}\,\text{nm}/6\,\text{nm})^{3} = 4.6\times 10^{12}$
cells; $1.9\times 10^{13}$ molecules.
\textbf{20.} $d = 0.1/(2\sin 14.5^{\circ}) = \qty{0.20}{nm}$: yes,
every side chain and atom placed.
\textbf{21.} $\sin\theta = 0.1/0.3$, $\theta = \qty{19.5}{\degree}$. In
a disordered crystal the molecules are not in exact register, the waves
scattered at high angle --- which encode fine detail --- no longer add
in phase, and the spots fade at high angle; only the \omterm{def:b3:structural-biology:methods}{low-resolution}
spots survive.
\textbf{22.} Halving $d$ needs twice the signal, hence four times the
particles: about \num{40000} (more in practice, as other errors enter).
\textbf{23.} Membrane proteins, which rarely crystallise from detergent
solutions and can be imaged in a lipid nanodisc; large flexible
complexes --- ribosomes, spliceosomes, ion channels with their
regulators --- whose heterogeneity prevents crystallisation but whose
particles can be sorted into conformational classes.
\textbf{24.} The bound haem and oxygen and their exact geometry, the
ordered water and ions, the second conformation (T against R) and the
proof that the \omterm{def:b3:structural-biology:levels}{fold} is right --- a prediction is a single unliganded
model with no experimental check.
\textbf{25.} Unloading $0.62$ of capacity (single site $0.36$); the
Levinthal time about $10^{52}$ years; a map at \qty{0.20}{nm}.
\end{solution}
