\chapter{Food Webs}\label{ch:g5:food-webs}

\Cref{ch:g3:food-chains} warned us that its neat chains were
single threads pulled from a net: the buzzard also takes
mice, the lizard also eats flies, the grass feeds rabbits
too. This year we stop pulling threads and look at the whole
net --- the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:web}{food web} --- and at what holds it in balance.

\section{From chains to webs}

\begin{definition}[Food web]\label{def:g5:food-webs:web}
The \emph{food web}\index{food web} of a \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} is the
network of \emph{all} its \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{food chains} at once: every \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species}
drawn once, with an arrow (still meaning \emph{\omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{is eaten by}},
\cref{rem:g3:food-chains:arrow}) for every meal
relationship. Most \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} eat several \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} and are eaten
by several --- so the chains cross, and the drawing becomes
a web.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Reading the web]\label{ex:g5:food-webs:reading}
Two readings of the figure. Follow the rabbit's arrows: it
eats grass \emph{and} clover, and \omterm{def:g3:food-chains:chain}{is eaten by} fox \emph{and}
buzzard --- four relationships, where a chain showed two. And
count the buzzard's food arrows: lizard and rabbit --- lose
one, the other remains. The web records exactly the spare
options that \cref{prop:g5:biodiversity:web} praised.
\end{example}

\section{Roles in the web}

\begin{definition}[Producers and consumers]\label{def:g5:food-webs:roles}
The web's \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} sort into roles:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{producers}\index{producer}: the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants},
        which make their own matter
        (\cref{prop:g4:what-plants-need:make}) --- every
        arrow-path starts on one
        (\cref{prop:g3:food-chains:start});
  \item the \emph{consumers}\index{consumer}: the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals},
        living on others --- \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{herbivores} eating producers
        directly, \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{carnivores} eating other consumers,
        \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{omnivores} both (\cref{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds});
  \item and, working in the shadows, the
        \emph{decomposers}\index{decomposer} --- the rotters
        of \cref{ex:g2:caring-for-nature:compost}, who feed
        on dead remains and return their substance to the
        soil. A later year gives them their own chapter.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Sorting the figure]\label{ex:g5:food-webs:sorting}
In the pocket web: \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers} --- grass, clover, oak;
herbivorous \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumers} --- grasshopper, rabbit, \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar};
carnivorous \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumers} --- lizard, blue tit (mostly), fox
(an \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{omnivore} in truth), buzzard, sparrowhawk. Three floors:
green, plant-eating, meat-eating --- with every upper floor
standing on the ones below.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[The shape of the numbers]\label{prop:g5:food-webs:pyramid}
Counting the web's inhabitants floor by floor gives a
constant shape: \emph{many} \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}, \emph{fewer}
\omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{herbivores}, \emph{few} \omterm{def:g2:what-animals-eat:kinds}{carnivores} --- a meadow of millions
of grass \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} feeds thousands of grasshoppers, feeding a
handful of lizards, feeding one pair of buzzards. Each floor
lives on the one below and cannot outnumber its food. That
is why great hunters are always rare.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\section{Balance, and what disturbs it}

\begin{proposition}[The web's balance]\label{prop:g5:food-webs:balance}
Left to itself, a web holds its numbers in rough
\emph{balance}\index{balance (of a food web)}: if rabbits
multiply, their hunters eat better and multiply after them,
and the surplus melts; if rabbits dwindle, hungry hunters
turn to other prey (the spare arrows) and rabbit numbers
recover. The corrections of
\cref{prop:g3:food-chains:depend}, running along many
arrows at once, steady the whole.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The classic mistake]\label{ex:g5:food-webs:mistake}
Farmers once campaigned to destroy \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds} of prey ---
``chicken thieves''. Where they succeeded, mice and voles,
freed of their main hunters, multiplied into plagues that
ate far more grain than the hawks' occasional chicken was
worth. The gardener's snail story
(\cref{exo:g3:food-chains:9}), at the scale of a whole
countryside: remove a web's top floor and the floor below
erupts.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Predicting a disturbance]\label{met:g5:food-webs:predict}
To predict what removing (or adding) a \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} does:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item find the \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} in the web and list \emph{all} its
        arrows, in and out;
  \item its foods, less eaten, tend to increase; its
        eaters, poorer, tend to decrease or switch prey;
  \item follow each change one more step along the web ---
        the second-step effects often surprise;
  \item check which neighbors have spare arrows (they
        absorb the shock) and which depended on this
        \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} alone (they are in danger).
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:1}
What is a \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:web}{food web}, and why do the chains cross?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:2}
Name the three roles of \cref{def:g5:food-webs:roles}, with
one example of each from the figure (\omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{decomposers} excepted).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:3}
From the figure: list everything the blue tit eats, and
everything that eats it.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:4}
Extract from the figure two complete chains that share the
grasshopper.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:5}
State the shape of the numbers, floor by floor. Why can no
floor outnumber the one below?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:6}
Why are buzzards rare and grasshoppers countless, in one
sentence?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:7}
Rabbits have a good year and multiply. By
\cref{prop:g5:food-webs:balance}, what happens next --- and
what melts the surplus?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:8}
Try it: draw the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:web}{food web} of a \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} you know --- garden,
park, pond --- with at least six \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} and every arrow
you can defend. Mark \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers} green.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:9}
Run \cref{met:g5:food-webs:predict} on removing the lizard
from the figure: first-step effects, then one more step.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:10}
Retell \cref{ex:g5:food-webs:mistake} as a chain of
predictions: hawks removed, then what, then what? Which
step did the campaigners fail to predict?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star\star$]\label{exo:g5:food-webs:11}
In the figure's web, compare removing the oak (a \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producer})
with removing the sparrowhawk (a top hunter). Which
neighbors feel each loss first, in which direction does
each disturbance travel, and which loss does the web absorb
more easily here? Use
\cref{ex:g3:food-chains:dry,ex:g3:food-chains:hunter}.
\end{exercise}
