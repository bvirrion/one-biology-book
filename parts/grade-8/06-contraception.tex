\chapter{Contraception}\label{ch:g8:contraception}

The last chapters mapped a mechanism of remarkable power:
two \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} meet, and forty weeks later there is a person.
Human beings are the one \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} that understands its own
mechanism --- and understanding brings a choice the frog
never faces: whether and when to set it running.
\emph{\omterm{prop:g8:contraception:principle}{Contraception}} is the applied science of that choice,
and it works, every method of it, by interrupting a step you
can now name.

\section{The principle: name the step, block the step}

\begin{proposition}[Contraception maps onto the mechanism]\label{prop:g8:contraception:principle}
\omterm{def:g5:human-reproduction:pregnancy}{Pregnancy} requires an unbroken chain
(\cref{prop:g8:sexual-reproduction:core},
\cref{prop:g8:from-egg-to-birth:firstweek}): \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes}
produced --- sperm deposited --- the swim to the \omterm{def:g8:reproductive-systems:female}{oviduct}
--- an \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg cell} released and met --- \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization} ---
implantation. \emph{Contraception}\index{contraception} is
any deliberate interruption of this chain. Every method on
the pharmacist's shelf is an attack on one named link, and
sorting them by their link is the whole theory:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{barrier} methods stop the \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes} meeting;
  \item \emph{hormonal} methods stop the \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg cell} being
        released at all;
  \item other methods make the meeting or the implantation
        fail.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\section{The main methods, by their link}

\begin{definition}[Barrier methods: the condom]\label{def:g8:contraception:condom}
The \emph{condom}\index{condom} --- a thin sheath worn on
the \omterm{def:g8:reproductive-systems:male}{penis} (a \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{female} version lines the vagina) --- blocks
the chain at the deposit step: the semen never enters the
partner's body. Widely available, needed only when needed
--- and unique on the shelf for a second service: it is
the only method that also blocks the passage of the
\emph{infections} transmitted by sex, the subject of
\cref{ch:g9:microbes-and-infection}'s world. That double
duty is why the condom stays in the picture even when
another method is in use.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Hormonal methods: the pill]\label{def:g8:contraception:pill}
The \emph{contraceptive pill}\index{contraceptive pill}
uses \cref{def:g8:puberty:hormones}'s own machinery: taken
daily, its \omterm{def:g8:puberty:hormones}{hormones} hold the \omterm{def:g8:reproductive-systems:female}{ovaries}' cycle in the
suspended state
(\cref{prop:g8:reproductive-systems:cycle}'s event 4
without any \omterm{def:g5:human-reproduction:pregnancy}{pregnancy}) --- \emph{no \omterm{prop:g8:reproductive-systems:cycle}{ovulation}, no \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg
cell}, nothing to fertilize}. Prescribed by a doctor, who
matches method to person; highly reliable when taken
regularly --- and offering nothing at all against
infections, hence the \omterm{def:g8:contraception:condom}{condom}'s standing partnership.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[The shelf, sorted by link]\label{ex:g8:contraception:shelf}
The other common methods, filed by their attacked link:
hormonal implants and patches --- the pill's principle
with better memory (months or years, no daily taking);
the small devices a doctor places in the uterus --- the
meeting and implantation made to fail for years at a
time; and the \emph{emergency pill}, a within-days
after-the-fact \omterm{def:g8:puberty:hormones}{hormone} dose that works chiefly by
delaying an \omterm{prop:g8:reproductive-systems:cycle}{ovulation} not yet happened --- a
last-resort brake, pharmacist-available, and no
substitute for a method used \emph{before}. What none of
these do: anything against infection.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[What does not work]\label{rem:g8:contraception:notwork}
The playground's methods, measured against the chain:
``counting safe days'' founders on
\cref{rem:g8:reproductive-systems:living}'s variability
--- \omterm{prop:g8:reproductive-systems:cycle}{ovulation}'s date is not knowable in advance, and
sperm survive days waiting for it; ``withdrawal in time''
founders on the fact that sperm travel before the finale.
Both fail exactly where the named-link theory predicts:
they block no link reliably. Legend is not a method.
\end{remark}

\section{Choice, responsibility, and the map of help}

\begin{proposition}[The choice is real and shared]\label{prop:g8:contraception:choice}
Facts the mechanism imposes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \omterm{def:g5:human-reproduction:pregnancy}{pregnancy} needs one act, not a relationship's
        length: the chain does not count occasions;
  \item it is a \emph{shared} chain: both partners' \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells},
        both partners' responsibility --- the \omterm{def:g8:contraception:condom}{condom} and
        the pill are not one sex's business each, but a
        conversation;
  \item and no method except abstention is perfect, though
        the good ones come close when used exactly as
        designed --- ``used properly'' is where most
        real-world failures live.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The map of help]\label{ex:g8:contraception:help}
\Cref{ex:g8:puberty:questions}'s addresses, extended:
school nurses and family doctors answer contraception
questions confidentially --- including from minors;
family-planning centers exist in every region precisely
for this, free and without judgment; pharmacists handle
the emergency pill's clock. The system is built so that
nobody has to navigate the chain on legend --- the help
map is part of the method.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Evaluating any method you hear of]\label{met:g8:contraception:evaluate}
For any claimed method, ask in order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{which link} of the chain does it block ---
        can you name it on the map?
  \item \emph{how reliably}, used as real people use it?
  \item does it protect against \emph{infections} too, or
        must the \omterm{def:g8:contraception:condom}{condom} partner it?
  \item what does it ask --- daily memory, a
        prescription, a fitting --- and does that fit the
        user?
\end{enumerate}
No nameable link, no method
(\cref{rem:g8:contraception:notwork} fails at question
1).
\end{method}

\section{Problem: The Family-Planning Consultation}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- an afternoon of
questions at the planning center}]\label{pb:g8:contraception:1}
A family-planning counselor's (invented) afternoon:
four visitors, four situations, one chain map on the
wall.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The map on the
wall.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Draw the wall map: the chain from \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes} to
        implantation, with the three attack points of
        \cref{prop:g8:contraception:principle} marked.
  \item Visitor 1 asks ``how can one pill a day stop
        anything?'' Answer with the machinery: whose
        language does the pill speak, and to whom?
  \item Visitor 1 follows up: ``and if I forget
        some?'' Locate the danger on the map --- what
        resumes when the \omterm{def:g8:puberty:hormones}{hormone} signal lapses?
  \item Why does the counselor's \omterm{def:g8:contraception:condom}{condom}
        recommendation survive every other method
        choice? (The double duty.)
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The situations.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Visitor 2, seventeen and anxious, believed a
        safe-days app made other methods unnecessary.
        Correct the belief kindly:
        which two biological facts defeat the
        calendar?
  \item Visitor 3 needs something with ``no daily
        memory''. Offer the shelf's two candidates and
        their links.
  \item Visitor 4 arrives within a day of a burst
        \omterm{def:g8:contraception:condom}{condom}. Name the option, its clock, its chief
        mechanism --- and the standing method the
        counselor will discuss for afterwards.
  \item All four leave with the same closing line
        about infections. Write it.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The debrief.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item The trainee asks why the center teaches the
        chain rather than just handing out methods.
        Answer with
        \cref{met:g8:contraception:evaluate}'s first
        question.
  \item Sort the afternoon's methods by what each asks
        of its user (question 4 of the method).
  \item The trainee wonders why minors' visits are
        confidential by design. Answer with
        \cref{ex:g8:contraception:help}'s logic.
  \item Close the afternoon in one sentence: what the
        center actually dispenses, beyond boxes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
