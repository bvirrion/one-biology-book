\chapter{Life Through the Seasons}\label{ch:g6:life-through-seasons}

Photograph the same chestnut tree on the first of every month
and pin the twelve pictures in a row: bare \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:tree}{branches}, \omterm{def:g6:life-through-seasons:buds}{buds},
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}, candles of \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flowers}, conkers, gold, bare \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:tree}{branches}
again. Nothing moved --- the tree stood still all year ---
and yet everything changed. \Cref{ch:g3:animals-through-seasons}
followed the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} through the year; now, with a surveyor's
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eyes}, we follow the whole \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{environment}, \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} first.

\section{The environment changes with the seasons}

\begin{proposition}[Seasonal change of conditions]\label{prop:g6:life-through-seasons:conditions}
Through the year, an \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{environment}'s \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}
(\cref{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}) swing
widely: days shorten and lengthen, \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{temperatures} fall and
rise, soil dampness follows rain and drought. A site
surveyed in January and in June gives two different rows in
the table --- so, by
\cref{prop:g6:exploring-our-environment:decide}, expect two
different casts of inhabitants.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[One hedge, two surveys]\label{ex:g6:life-through-seasons:hedge}
The lane's hedge, surveyed twice. June: \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} dense, ten
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} flowering at its foot, bees loud, blackbirds
nesting, snails after rain. January: bare twigs, green
reduced to ivy and moss, no insect flying, blackbirds
foraging in the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf} litter, snails sealed in their \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{shells}
deep in the bank. Same place, same components list ---
living, mineral, traces --- but the living column has
transformed.
\end{example}

\section{How plants pass the bad season}

\begin{definition}[Deciduous and evergreen]\label{def:g6:life-through-seasons:trees}
Trees divide by their winter answer:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{deciduous}\index{deciduous} trees --- oak,
        chestnut, beech --- drop all their \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} in autumn
        and stand bare;
  \item \emph{evergreen}\index{evergreen} trees --- pine,
        holly, ivy on the wall --- keep working \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} all
        winter, usually tough, waxy or needle-shaped ones
        (\cref{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:plants} met the
        needles already).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Buds]\label{def:g6:life-through-seasons:buds}
A \omterm{def:g6:life-through-seasons:trees}{deciduous} tree in winter is not dead: next spring is
already packed on its twigs as \emph{buds}\index{bud} ---
tiny complete shoots, \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} folded small, wrapped in
weatherproof scales. Spring's ``new'' \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} were built the
summer before; the bud is winter storage for them.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Opening a horse-chestnut bud]\label{ex:g6:life-through-seasons:bud}
The horse chestnut's big sticky \omterm{def:g6:life-through-seasons:buds}{buds} open well under a
blade (a grown-up's work): under the varnished scales,
packed in cottony down, lie miniature folded \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} ---
perfect, pale, waiting. A twig cut in February and stood in
water indoors ``\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leafs} out'' weeks early: the shoots were
finished; only warmth was missing.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[The herb layer's winter answers]\label{prop:g6:life-through-seasons:herbs}
Smaller \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} have their own strategies:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{annuals}\index{annual plant} --- the poppy, the
        bean --- die entirely in autumn and pass winter as
        \emph{\omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds}} (\cref{prop:g2:from-seed-to-plant:needs}:
        a \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed} waits out cold safely);
  \item \emph{perennials}\index{perennial plant} --- the
        daffodil, the dandelion --- let their tops die back
        and wait underground as \omterm{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}{bulbs}, \omterm{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}{tubers} or tough
        rootstocks (\cref{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}'s
        storerooms, doing winter duty);
  \item \omterm{def:g6:life-through-seasons:trees}{evergreens} of the ground --- moss, ivy --- simply
        endure.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Where did the daffodils go?]\label{ex:g6:life-through-seasons:daffodil}
The daffodil patch \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flowers} in March, yellows in May, and by
July has vanished without trace --- yet next March it
returns, denser. All summer, autumn and winter it existed as
\omterm{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}{bulbs} below the lawn: the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} never left; it withdrew to
storage.
\end{example}

\section{The animals' calendar, revisited}

\begin{proposition}[Animal answers, in survey terms]\label{prop:g6:life-through-seasons:animals}
\Cref{ch:g3:animals-through-seasons}'s three strategies ---
\omterm{def:g3:animals-through-seasons:migration}{migrate}, \omterm{def:g3:animals-through-seasons:hibernation}{hibernate}, \omterm{def:g3:animals-through-seasons:active}{stay active} --- now read as answers to
the surveyed changes: each \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} tracks its own needed
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} and food.
\begin{itemize}
  \item the swallow follows its food supply to where
        \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} keep \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects} flying;
  \item the hedgehog and the sealed snail wait out the
        months their bodies cannot work through;
  \item \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects} add a fourth answer: most pass winter as
        \emph{\omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}, larvae or chrysalises}
        (\cref{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}) tucked in
        soil, bark or \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf} litter --- the adult dies, the
        \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} winters as its young stages.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Where the June insects are in January]\label{ex:g6:life-through-seasons:insects}
January's hedge shows no insect --- yet June's are all
present: the butterflies as chrysalises under the coping
stones, the grasshoppers as \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} in the soil, the ladybugs
as huddled sleeping adults deep in the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaf} litter, the
bees as a tight warm cluster in the hive. The June survey
walks about; the January survey must dig, lift and peer ---
the inhabitants have changed \emph{form}, not address.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Surveying through the year]\label{met:g6:life-through-seasons:yearsurvey}
To study a site's seasonal life:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item survey the same marked site
        (\cref{met:g6:exploring-our-environment:survey})
        once each season --- same square, same instruments,
        same search time;
  \item record \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} \emph{and} inhabitants each time;
        photograph the site from the same spot;
  \item search winter's hidden forms deliberately: \omterm{def:g6:life-through-seasons:buds}{buds},
        \omterm{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}{bulbs}, \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}, chrysalises, sealed snails --- and put
        every cover back;
  \item lay the four surveys side by side: pair each change
        of inhabitants with a change of \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}, and
        name each absentee's winter form or destination.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{remark}[Nothing simply vanishes]\label{rem:g6:life-through-seasons:vanish}
The year-round table teaches one deep rule: no \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} of
the June survey has simply ceased. Each one is elsewhere
(migrated), asleep (hibernating), in storage (\omterm{ex:g4:plants-without-seeds:bulbtuber}{bulb}, \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed}),
or in another life stage (\omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg}, \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{chrysalis}). ``Gone'' is
never a full answer in \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{biology} --- the follow-up question
is always \emph{gone where, as what?}
\end{remark}

\section{Problem: The Twelve Photographs}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- a year of the chestnut
tree, read frame by frame}]\label{pb:g6:life-through-seasons:1}
Lena photographs the chestnut tree by the gate on the first
of every month for a year, and pins the twelve prints in a
row. Under each print she notes day length and the morning's
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{temperature}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Reading the row.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item January and December show bare \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:tree}{branches}. What, on
        the twigs, proves the tree is alive and spring-ready
        in both frames?
  \item Between which two frames do the \omterm{def:g6:life-through-seasons:buds}{buds} burst open?
        Lena's notes there read: days
        $11$--$13$ hours, mornings
        $8$--\qty{12}{\degreeCelsius}. Which changing
        \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} is the tree answering?
  \item The May frame shows upright ``candles'' of white
        \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flowers}. Using \cref{ch:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds} (or
        \cref{ch:g5:flower-to-fruit}), what must visit
        between the May and September frames for October's
        conkers to exist --- and what is a conker, exactly?
  \item The October frame shows gold \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}; November's,
        bare \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:tree}{branches} and a brown carpet below. Name the
        tree's type from
        \cref{def:g6:life-through-seasons:trees}, and
        where its next spring is stored through the frames
        that follow.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The tree's neighbors.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Under the tree, the March frame catches a patch of
        daffodils absent from the July frame. Where is the
        patch in July, and in what form?
  \item Poppies edge the path in June; in the January frame
        their spot is bare earth. In what form is the poppy
        present in January, and by which strategy of
        \cref{prop:g6:life-through-seasons:herbs}?
  \item The June frame catches a swallow above the tree and
        bees on the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flowers} below; neither appears from
        November to February. For each, say where it is
        then, and as what.
  \item A magpie appears in every single frame. Which
        \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} strategy is that, and what must the magpie
        manage all winter that the swallow escapes by
        leaving?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The rule of the row.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Lena summarizes: ``the tree's year follows the
        \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}' year.'' Support her with two pairings
        of a frame-to-frame change in the tree against a
        change in her day-length and \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{temperature} notes.
  \item Her little brother says the July daffodil patch
        ``died''. Correct him with
        \cref{rem:g6:life-through-seasons:vanish} --- and
        state the follow-up question his word ``gone''
        always deserves.
  \item Predict frame 13 --- next January's photograph ---
        in detail: \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:tree}{branches}, twigs, ground below. Which
        parts of your prediction are certain enough to bet
        on, and why?
  \item A chestnut twig, cut in February and stood in warm
        water indoors, \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leafs} out in three weeks. What does
        this show about \emph{which} condition the \omterm{def:g6:life-through-seasons:buds}{buds}
        were waiting for --- and design the twin-twig fair
        test (\cref{met:g4:what-plants-need:fairtest}) that
        would nail it down.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
