\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g2:what-alive} --- What Is Alive?}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:what-alive:1}
An \omterm{def:g2:what-alive:organism}{organism} is a \omterm{def:g1:living-nonliving:living}{living thing}. Examples: a dog, a tree, a person, a
fish, a daisy.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:what-alive:2}
Any four of: \omterm{def:g2:what-alive:signs}{grow}; need \omterm{def:g2:what-alive:signs}{energy}/\omterm{def:g1:caring:needs}{food} or light; respond to the world;
get rid of waste; \omterm{def:g2:what-alive:signs}{reproduce} (make young).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:what-alive:3}
No. A clock does not \omterm{def:g2:what-alive:signs}{grow}, does not need \omterm{def:g1:caring:needs}{food}, and cannot make baby
clocks. People build and wind it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:what-alive:4}
False. Cars, clocks and toys can move without being alive.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g2:what-alive:5}
A healthy seed can \omterm{def:g2:what-alive:signs}{grow} into a \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} when conditions are right. It
came from a living \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant} and can make a new \omterm{def:g1:plants-animals:plant}{plant}. It is resting,
not dead.
\end{solution}
