\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b1:eukaryotic-cell} --- Functional Organization of the Eukaryotic Cell}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:1}
\omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:nucleus}{Nucleus} (chromosomes, transcription); rough ER (secretory and membrane
proteins); smooth ER (lipids, calcium, detoxification); \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi} (sorting,
modification, packing); \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{lysosomes} (digestion); \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:peroxisome}{peroxisomes} (oxidations,
catalase); \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:mitochondrion}{mitochondria} (respiration, ATP).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:2}
Synthesised by ribosomes bound to the rough ER and threaded into its
lumen (\omterm{prop:b1:eukaryotic-cell:secretory}{signal sequence}); vesicles bud from the ER and fuse with the cis
\omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi}; the protein crosses the stack, is modified and sorted; it \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves}
the trans face in a condensing \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:plantcell}{vacuole} that matures into a secretory
granule; the granule fuses with the apical membrane on a signal and
empties into the duct.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:3}
$7 + 32 = \qty{39}{\%}$. The inner membrane is folded into \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:mitochondrion}{cristae}
because it carries the respiratory chain and ATP synthase, whose
throughput is proportional to its area; the outer is a simple envelope.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:4}
Plant: cellulose wall, large \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:plantcell}{vacuole}, \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:plastid}{plastids} (\omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:plantcell}{plasmodesmata} too).
Animal: \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{lysosomes}, centrioles (and the ability to crawl and
phagocytose).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:5}
ER at \qty{3}{min} (already declining), \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi} at about \qty{15}{min},
granules at about \qty{60}{min}. Transit ER to granule about
\qty{45}{min}; half secreted at about \qty{110}{min}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:6}
The \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi}, held near the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:cytoskeleton}{centrosome} by dynein on \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:cytoskeleton}{microtubules},
fragments and disperses; vesicle traffic slows to diffusion and
transport to the periphery stops; the spindle cannot form and \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells}
arrest in mitosis; cilia, whose core is \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:cytoskeleton}{microtubules}, stop (existing
cilia are stable, but they are not renewed).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:7}
Two membranes with a bacterial-type inner one; circular DNA without
histones; bacterial-size ribosomes sensitive to antibiotics; division
by fission; gene sequences grouping with alphaproteobacteria. It cannot
live alone because most of its genes have moved to the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:nucleus}{nucleus}: it
imports most of its proteins from the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:organelle}{cytosol}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:8}
The sugars are added on the luminal side in the ER and \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi}. Each
vesicle fusion preserves sidedness: the luminal face of a vesicle
becomes the luminal face of the next compartment, and when the vesicle
fuses with the plasma membrane its lumen opens to the outside, so the
sugars face the exterior. What is luminal is, topologically, outside.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:9}
Each granule $\pi d^2 = \qty{3.14}{\micro m^2}$; per day
\qty{6280}{\micro m^2}, i.e.\ \qty{157}{} times the apical face. The
\omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} must retrieve membrane by \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endocytosis}{endocytosis} at the same rate, so the
apical membrane turns over every nine minutes and the retrieved
membrane is returned to the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi} for re-use.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:10}
The \qty{320}{}-residue chain is the precursor with its \qty{20}{}
residue \omterm{prop:b1:eukaryotic-cell:secretory}{signal sequence}, which is normally cleaved on entry into the ER.
With vesicles, the chain enters the lumen during synthesis, the signal
is cleaved, and the membrane protects the product from the protease.
The mutant lacking the signal is not recognised by the ER machinery: it
is made on free ribosomes and stays in the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:organelle}{cytosol}, full length, never
secreted.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:11}
Membranes internalised by \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endocytosis}{endocytosis} and worn \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{organelles} are delivered
to \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{lysosomes} for digestion; without the enzyme, the lipid they contain
cannot be broken down and fills the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{lysosomes}, which swell until the
\omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} fails. \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{Cells} with the fastest membrane turnover (\omterm{def:b1:body-plans-tissues:nervous}{neurons}, which
recycle synaptic vesicles continuously; macrophages) accumulate it
fastest. An injected enzyme carrying the sugar signal that lysosomal
enzymes bear can bind receptors at the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} surface and be taken up by
receptor-mediated \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endocytosis}{endocytosis} into the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{lysosomes} --- the principle of
enzyme replacement therapy.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:eukaryotic-cell:12}
Endosymbiosis accounts well for \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:mitochondrion}{mitochondria} and \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:plastid}{plastids} (two
membranes, own genome and ribosomes, fission, bacterial relatives), and
thus for the eukaryote's energy metabolism. It does not explain the
\omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:nucleus}{nucleus}, the ER and \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi}, or the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:cytoskeleton}{cytoskeleton}, which have no bacterial
counterparts and appear to be inventions of the host lineage ---
infoldings of its membrane and new proteins. ``Federation'' is apt for
the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:prokeuk}{organelles}; ``held together by a membrane system'' points to the
larger, unexplained part: a host \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} that was already eukaryotic in
its architecture before it took the bacteria in.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b1:eukaryotic-cell:1}
\textbf{1.} A short pulse labels a narrow cohort of molecules so their
position marks one time; the excess cold amino acid stops further
incorporation so the cohort is not smeared by later synthesis.
\textbf{2.} ER: \qty{3}{min} (earliest point); \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi} and condensing
\omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:plantcell}{vacuoles}: about \qty{15}{min}; granules: about \qty{60}{min}.
\textbf{3.} ER to \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi} about \qty{10}{min}; \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi} to granule about
\qty{45}{min}.
\textbf{4.} Mostly in granules (\qty{70}{\%}), a tenth in the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi},
a tenth already secreted: the constitutive path and the first granules
formed release some, while most wait for a stimulus.
\textbf{5.} Vesicle budding and transport require energy and fluid
membranes; in the cold the ER-to-Golgi step stops and the protein
accumulates upstream of the block.
\textbf{6.} $10\,\mathrm{g}/\num{8e10} = \qty{1.25e-10}{g} =
\qty{125}{pg}$ per \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} per day.
\textbf{7.} $V = \frac{\pi}{6}(1)^3 = \qty{0.52}{\micro m^3} =
\qty{5.2e-13}{mL}$; protein $\qty{1.05e-13}{g} = \qty{0.1}{pg}$.
\textbf{8.} $125/0.1 = 1250$ granules per day, $0.87$ per minute.
\textbf{9.} $V = 12^3 = \qty{1728}{\micro m^3} = \qty{1.73e-9}{cm^3}$;
mass \qty{1.8e-9}{g} = \qty{1800}{pg}. Secretes $125/1800 = 7\%$ of
its mass daily (the whole \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} is only \qty{20}{\%} protein, so a third
of its protein content).
\textbf{10.} Molar mass $250\times 110 = \qty{27500}{g/mol}$;
$\qty{1.05e-13}{g}/27500\times\num{6e23} = \num{2.3e6}$ molecules.
\textbf{11.} $250/5 = \qty{50}{s}$.
\textbf{12.} Molecules per day $1250\times\num{2.3e6} = \num{2.9e9}$;
one ribosome makes $86\,400/50 = 1728$ per day; needed:
$\num{1.7e6}$ ribosomes, about \qty{40}{\%} of the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell}'s.
\textbf{13.} $\pi d^2 = \qty{3.14}{\micro m^2}$.
\textbf{14.} $1250\times 3.14 = \qty{3900}{\micro m^2}$.
\textbf{15.} $3900/144 = 27$ times: the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} retrieves the same area by
\omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endocytosis}{endocytosis} and returns it to the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi}.
\textbf{16.} $20\,000/3900 = 5$ days: the ER could supply only five
days of granule membrane if none returned; the membrane must recycle
continuously.
\textbf{17.} Each microvillus $\pi\times 0.1\times 1 =
\qty{0.31}{\micro m^2}$; \qty{630}{\micro m^2} for 2000; factor $5.4$.
\textbf{18.} At the beginning of the chain: the \omterm{prop:b1:eukaryotic-cell:secretory}{signal sequence}, twenty
mostly hydrophobic residues.
\textbf{19.} The signal directed the growing chain into the vesicles,
where a signal peptidase removed it; the membrane shields the protein
from the protease.
\textbf{20.} Only proteins with a signal enter the ER; a cytosolic
protein lacks it and stays outside.
\textbf{21.} The enzyme is made on free ribosomes and stays in the
\omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:organelle}{cytosol}; being a digestive enzyme (as an inactive precursor it does
little harm, but if activated) it would digest the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} from inside.
\textbf{22.} In the trans \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi}: the lysosomal enzyme carries a
distinguishing mark (a phosphorylated sugar) recognised by a receptor
that packs it into vesicles bound for the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{lysosomes} rather than into
secretory granules.
\textbf{23.} In the ER (and the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:endomembrane}{Golgi}): vesicle formation, motor
transport along \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:cytoskeleton}{microtubules} and membrane fusion all consume ATP; and
protein synthesis itself stops, so the pulse is not even completed.
\textbf{24.} \qty{45}{min} transit at $0.87$ granules per minute:
about \qty{40}{} granules' worth of protein in transit.
\textbf{25.} Transit from synthesis to granule about \qty{45}{min}
(\qty{55}{min} counting the ER), secretion after about two hours in
the experiment (four hours or more in the living gland, since granules
wait for a meal); \qty{0.9}{} granules released per minute; \qty{7}{\%}
of the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell}'s mass per day.
\end{solution}
