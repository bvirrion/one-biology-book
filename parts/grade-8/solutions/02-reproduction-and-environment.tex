\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g8:reproduction-and-environment} --- Reproduction and the Environment}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:1}
The rule: young must arrive when food and \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} serve
them --- for tit chicks, the spring \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar} peak on the
oaks. The cue that starts her machinery: the lengthening
days.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:2}
\omterm{def:g2:born-grow-die:cycle}{Births} in spring, when grass and mild weather receive the
calves. The months of gestation must be counted backward
--- autumn \omterm{prop:g4:animal-reproduction:where}{mating} is spring arithmetic.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:3}
Because every \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg}, \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed} and drop of \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk} is \omterm{def:g6:plants-are-producers:matter}{organic matter}
the parents must first acquire and rebuild
(\cref{def:g6:matter-from-food:production}). Payments: the
hen's daily \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg}, the beech's mast-year \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed} \omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:farming}{crop}, the
doe's \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk} (or the stag's regrown antlers).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:4}
All the individuals of one \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} in one place.
Reproduction (and arrivals) add; deaths and departures
subtract; the \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{environment}'s food, space, predators and
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} set the ceiling.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:5}
Predation (herons, dragonfly larvae); food and space
(only so much to eat, so many places); \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} (frost,
drought, the shrinking pond).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:6}
\omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{Habitat} quality, written in \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}: a rich \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar}
\omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} builds a hen that can provision ten; a poor one,
six or seven.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:7}
Ewes' breeding machinery is started by shortening days;
artificial lighting replays that signal on the breeder's
schedule, and the flock lambs early. It proves the timing
is read from the cue itself --- change the \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light}, and the
``calendar'' obeys.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:8}
Timing: cue and spawning were normal --- no mismatch.
Budget: parents provisioned normally --- thousands of
\omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}. Checks: all three tightened --- the drought shrank
space and \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen}, crowding starved, herons concentrated
their hunting. \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{Habitat}: the breeding pond itself nearly
vanished --- the decisive line.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:9}
The chicks hatch to a feast already over: \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillars}
peaked while the day-length cue still said ``wait''.
Fledging falls though weather, budget and predators are
kind --- the audit's timing line fails: cue and resource
peak no longer coincide.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:10}
The millions are the strategy: each \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg} is a stake played
against predation, hunger and \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}, and the checks
collect almost all of them --- that is the plan, not its
failure. On average the survivors replace the parents;
the spending \emph{is} how an uncaring strategy buys its
few winners (\cref{exo:g4:animal-reproduction:7} said it
first).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:11}
Draining a marsh: removes breeding \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} --- the
\omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} line. Nest boxes: add space --- easing that
check. May spraying: empties the food peak --- the food
check, at the worst moment. Restoring a wet meadow:
returns \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} and food together
(\cref{ex:g5:biodiversity:storks}).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g8:reproduction-and-environment:12}
The frog sits at the many-and-uncared end of the strategy
line: thousands of cheap \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}, no guarding. The pyramid's
arithmetic guarantees most tadpole-matter must feed the
pond's \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{consumers} rather than become frog --- each floor
passes on only a fraction. And the checks are where that
arithmetic is enforced: predation, food, space and
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} collect the surplus each season, leaving on
average a pair to replace a pair. The pond is not solid
with frogs because the strategy plans for losses, the
pyramid prices them, and the checks collect them.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g8:reproduction-and-environment:1}
\textbf{1.} The lengthening days of spring cue the laying;
the timing aims the hungry chicks at the oaks' \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar}
peak.

\textbf{2.} \Cref{prop:g8:reproduction-and-environment:cost}:
reproduction is provisioned from the \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} --- parents
built by a rich season provision larger clutches after it.

\textbf{3.} Predation on \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} and chicks; food shortfalls
in cold snaps; \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} --- a wet chilling week in the
nest; (space is what the boxes themselves supply).

\textbf{4.} Because \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} alone explain nothing: the audit
needs the cue-and-peak timing (weather log), the budget
(what the season offered), the checks and the \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}
(the grounds work). The ledger is only readable beside
its context columns.

\textbf{5.} Fledging falls: chicks hatch after the early
peak has passed --- the timing line: cue unchanged,
resource moved, coincidence broken.

\textbf{6.} Budget and checks through \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}: dead \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{limbs}
held the \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects} and natural holes, the monthly-mown
strip fed no \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillars} or spiders --- food check
tightened and \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} impoverished, kind weather
notwithstanding.

\textbf{7.} Predation. The clutch was provisioned before
the hawk's toll falls --- the hen's budget set it; the
hawk collects later, on fledglings. Different lines of
the ledger, different collectors.

\textbf{8.} The nest-space check --- more breeding places,
so more total pairs. Untouched: the food ceiling per
territory, which is why each box's own success held
rather than rose.

\textbf{9.} ``Ten years of clutches tracked the grounds:
rich \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar} years and untidy oaks filled the boxes
with eight to ten \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}, poor and tidied years with six
--- clutch size is \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} quality, written in \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}.''

\textbf{10.} Boxes replace only nest space. The oaks are
cue-target and budget at once --- the \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar} peak
the timing aims at and the food that provisions clutch
and chicks --- and a check-easer besides (their \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{limbs}'
natural larder). Fifty boxes on a car park are shelves
in an emptied pantry.

\textbf{11.} Leave the meadow strip unmown through
spring --- restocking the food budget; keep (or replace)
dead \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{limbs} and old oaks --- preserving the \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat} and
the peak the whole calendar aims at.

\textbf{12.} A \omterm{def:g2:where-animals-live:habitat}{habitat}: the cue at the right time, the
food to pay for it, and checks mild enough to leave, on
average, a pair to replace a pair.
\end{solution}
