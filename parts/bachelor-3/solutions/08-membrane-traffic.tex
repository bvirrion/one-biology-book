\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:membrane-traffic} --- Membrane Traffic and Protein Sorting}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:1}
Hydrophobic amino-terminal stretch: \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:signals}{signal peptide}, into the
endoplasmic reticulum (and by default on to the surface). PKKKRKV:
\omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:signals}{nuclear localisation signal}, into the nucleus through the pore.
Amphipathic amino-terminal helix: mitochondrial \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:signals}{presequence}, into the
mitochondrial matrix. KDEL: retrieval from the Golgi back to the
reticulum. Mannose-6-phosphate: from the trans-Golgi to the \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:endocytosis}{endosome}
and \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:endocytosis}{lysosome}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:2}
(1) Without membranes the translated chain was longer than the secreted
protein by about twenty residues: a segment is removed during
secretion. (2) With membranes present during translation the product
had the mature size and was protected from protease: it had entered
the vesicles and lost the segment inside them. (3) Membranes added
after translation did neither: entry is coupled to synthesis
--- cotranslational --- and the segment, the \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:signals}{signal peptide}, works only
on a nascent chain.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:3}
Reticulum to Golgi: \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:coats}{COPII}, forward. Golgi to reticulum: \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:coats}{COPI},
retrograde. Trans-Golgi to \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:endocytosis}{endosome}: \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:coats}{clathrin} with its adaptors,
forward toward the \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:endocytosis}{lysosome}. Plasma membrane to \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:endocytosis}{endosome}: \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:coats}{clathrin},
inward (endocytosis).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:4}
Sugars are added only inside the lumen of the reticulum and Golgi. The
lumenal face of every vesicle stays lumenal through budding and fusion,
and when a vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane its lumenal face
becomes the outside of the cell; the orientation is never inverted, so
what was glycosylated inside ends up outside.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:5}
$t^{*} = \ln(0.05/0.2)/(0.05 - 0.2) = \ln 4/0.15 = \qty{9.2}{min}$;
$B(t^{*}) = (0.2/(-0.15))(e^{-1.85} - e^{-0.46}) = 0.63$. At \qty{60}{min}
$A \approx 0$, $B = 1.33\,(e^{-3} - e^{-12}) = 0.066$, so $C = 0.93$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:6}
The \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:signals}{signal peptide} puts the amino terminus into the lumen; each
hydrophobic stretch is a stop-transfer or start-transfer alternately,
so there are four transmembrane helices (about residues 30--52,
90--112, 150--172, 210--232). Segments: amino terminus lumenal;
52--90 cytosolic; 112--150 lumenal; 172--210 cytosolic; carboxy
terminus lumenal. Glycosylation only on the lumenal segments --- the
amino terminus, 112--150 and the carboxy terminus --- which become
extracellular at the surface.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:7}
$J_{\max} = \num{30000}\times 0.25\times 0.05/0.30 = 1250$ per minute;
surface fraction $k_{r}/(k_{i} + k_{r}) = 0.05/0.30 = 0.17$. Doubling $R$
doubles $J$ exactly; the return rate is the bottleneck (most receptors
are inside), and doubling $k_{r}$ would give $\num{30000}\times 0.25
\times 0.1/0.35 \approx 2140$, a factor $1.7$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:8}
(a) No receptor: the tagged hydrolases follow the default route and
are secreted; \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:endocytosis}{lysosomes} lack enzymes and fill with undigested
material --- a storage disease. (b) No phosphotransferase: the same
phenotype by a different lesion (I-cell disease), the enzymes
untagged and secreted. (c) Neutralised \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:endocytosis}{endosomes}: the receptor cannot
release its cargo, so it is not recycled and the hydrolases are
misdelivered or secreted; LDL and other receptors also stop cycling.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:9}
The tyrosine belongs to the tail \omterm{def:b3:bioinformatics:motif}{motif} that the \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:coats}{clathrin} adaptor
recognises; without it the receptor is not gathered into coated pits.
The receptors bind LDL normally but are spread uniformly over the
surface, excluded from the pits, and the ligand stays outside --- a
disease of sorting, not of binding.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:10}
With $k_{1} = k_{2} = k$, try $B = kt\,e^{-kt}$: $\mathrm{d}B/\mathrm{d}t
= k e^{-kt} - k^{2}t e^{-kt} = kA - kB$, as required, with $B(0) = 0$.
Peak where $1 - kt = 0$: $t^{*} = 1/k$, $B = e^{-1} = 0.37$. In general
$C(t) = 1 - (k_{2}e^{-k_{1}t} - k_{1}e^{-k_{2}t})/(k_{2} - k_{1})$,
which is symmetric under exchange of $k_{1}$ and $k_{2}$: the granule
curve alone cannot say which of the two compartments is the fast one.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:11}
Each vesicle has area $4\pi(50\,\text{nm})^{2} = \qty{0.031}{\micro
m^2}$; a thousand a minute is \qty{31}{\micro m^2/min}, so the whole
surface in $3000/31 \approx \qty{95}{min}$. The cell does not shrink
because membrane returns by exocytosis (recycling vesicles) at the same
rate --- a balance of flows. Each vesicle holds $5\times 10^{-19}$ L; a
thousand a minute for an hour is $3\times 10^{-14}$ L, about
\qty{1.5}{\%} of a \qty{2}{pL} cell's volume per hour.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:membrane-traffic:12}
Botulinum toxin acts in the motor terminal: no acetylcholine is
released, the muscle receives no command and lies limp --- flaccid
paralysis. Tetanus toxin is carried up the motor axon and across to
the inhibitory interneurons that normally restrain motor neurons; with
their \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:fusion}{SNARE} cleaved no glycine or GABA is released, the motor neurons
fire unopposed and every muscle contracts --- spastic paralysis. A
few nanograms of botulinum toxin injected into an overactive muscle
silence it locally for months (until new \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:fusion}{SNARE} is made and the terminal
recovers): a treatment for dystonias, spasticity, squint and wrinkles.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:membrane-traffic:1}
\textbf{1.} $10^{7}\times \qty{25000}{Da}\times 1.66\times 10^{-24}$ g
$= \qty{0.42}{pg}$ per minute, \qty{600}{pg} a day --- twice its own
protein content.
\textbf{2.} Vesicle volume $\tfrac{4}{3}\pi\,35^{3} = 1.8\times 10^{5}$
nm$^{3}$, \qty{30}{\%} of it $5.4\times 10^{4}$; one enzyme molecule
$\tfrac{4}{3}\pi\,2^{3} = 34$ nm$^{3}$: about \num{1600} molecules per
vesicle; $10^{7}/1600 \approx 6200$ vesicles per minute.
\textbf{3.} Area $4\pi\,35^{2} = \qty{0.0154}{\micro m^2}$ each, \qty{95}{\micro
m^2} per minute: the reticulum's \qty{6000}{\micro m^2} in about an
hour if nothing came back.
\textbf{4.} $\qty{95}{\micro m^2} = 9.5\times 10^{7}$ nm$^{2}$, two leaflets
at \qty{0.7}{nm^2}: $2.7\times 10^{8}$ lipids per minute. The cell
returns membrane by \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:coats}{COPI} vesicles and recycling, and synthesises lipid
to replace what leaves for good in granules.
\textbf{5.} Granule volume $5.2\times 10^{8}$ nm$^{3}$, \qty{30}{\%} of it
$1.6\times 10^{8}$: $4.7\times 10^{6}$ molecules per granule; $6\times
10^{8}$ molecules an hour fill about $130$ granules.
\textbf{6.} Each granule adds $\pi d^{2} = \qty{3.1}{\micro m^2}$; $300$
add \qty{940}{\micro m^2} to \qty{50}{\micro m^2}: twentyfold. The
membrane must be retrieved by compensatory endocytosis within minutes.
\textbf{7.} $t^{*} = \ln 2/0.05 = \qty{13.9}{min}$, $B = 0.50$.
\textbf{8.} $A = e^{-3} = 0.05$; $B = 2(e^{-1.5} - e^{-3}) = 0.35$;
$C = 0.60$.
\textbf{9.} \qty{10}{min} and \qty{20}{min}; mean total \qty{30}{min}.
\textbf{10.} $t^{*} = \ln(0.1)/(-0.09) = \qty{25.6}{min}$; $B(t^{*}) =
(0.1/(-0.09))(e^{-2.56} - e^{-0.256}) = 0.77$: the Golgi swells,
engorged with three quarters of the cargo.
\textbf{11.} At the peak $k_{1}e^{-k_{1}t^{*}} = k_{2}e^{-k_{2}t^{*}}$;
substituting into $B$ gives $B_{\max} = (k_{1}/k_{2})^{\,k_{2}/(k_{2} -
k_{1})}$: the power is $k_{2}/(k_{2} - k_{1})$. For $k_{1} > k_{2}$ the
exponent is negative and the base above $1$; for $k_{1} < k_{2}$ the
base is below $1$ and the exponent positive --- in both cases the value
is below $1$ (check: $2^{-1} = 0.5$ here).
\textbf{12.} For $k_{1} \gg k_{2}$, $B(t) \to e^{-k_{2}t}$: the label
is in the Golgi at once and leaves at $k_{2}$ --- the reticulum step is
invisible and the Golgi curve is a simple decay.
\textbf{13.} $\theta = 2/(2+2) = 0.5$; $J = \num{50000}\times 0.2\times
0.1\times 0.5/(0.1 + 0.1) = 2500$ particles per minute, \num{150000} an
hour.
\textbf{14.} $1.5\times 10^{5}\times 1500 = 2.3\times 10^{8}$ cholesterol
molecules an hour; $5\times 10^{9}/2.3\times 10^{8} \approx 22$ hours.
\textbf{15.} Surface fraction $k_{r}/(k_{r} + k_{i}\theta) = 0.5$; one
round trip takes $1/(k_{i}\theta) + 1/k_{r} = 10 + 10 = \qty{20}{min}$:
about $60$ trips in twenty hours.
\textbf{16.} $J = 1250$ per minute. Below saturation $J \propto R\,[L]$,
so with half the receptors the plasma LDL must double for the same
clearance.
\textbf{17.} $R$ back to \num{50000}: clearance restored, plasma LDL
falls back toward normal --- the statin effect.
\textbf{18.} With no receptors the LDL is cleared only by slow
non-specific routes, so it circulates for days instead of hours, is
oxidised, and is taken up by the scavenger receptors of macrophages,
which become the foam cells of atherosclerotic plaques; and statins,
which work by inducing receptors, have nothing to induce.
\textbf{19.} $V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi(0.25)^{3} = \qty{0.065}{\micro m^3} =
6.5\times 10^{-17}$ L. At pH $7.2$: $6.3\times 10^{-8}\times 6.5\times
10^{-17} = 4\times 10^{-24}$ mol --- about $2$ free protons. At pH $4.7$:
$2\times 10^{-5}\times 6.5\times 10^{-17} = 1.3\times 10^{-21}$ mol,
about $780$.
\textbf{20.} $50\times 780 \approx \num{39000}$ protons; $50$ pumps at
$200$ per second move \num{10000} a second: about \qty{4}{s}.
\textbf{21.} Pumping positive charge in makes the lumen positive, and a
few thousand charges would stop the pump; chloride enters through a
channel (or cations leave) to neutralise the charge.
\textbf{22.} Enzymes inactive at neutral pH do no harm if a \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:endocytosis}{lysosome}
leaks or if they are missorted to the cytosol or the surface: the acid
requirement confines digestion to the compartment built for it.
\textbf{23.} Both must carry a pH sensor --- histidines, whose
protonation near pH~6 changes the binding surface: the LDL receptor
folds a domain over its own binding site when protonated, the
mannose-6-phosphate receptor loses affinity likewise.
\textbf{24.} $10^{7.2 - 4.7} = 10^{2.5} \approx 300$-fold concentration;
the trapped base consumes protons, raises the lysosomal pH, and
inactivates the hydrolases --- which is how chloroquine kills malaria
parasites in their food vacuole and how it blocks \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:endocytosis}{autophagy} in
experiments.
\textbf{25.} Golgi peak at \qty{14}{min}; some \num{6200} vesicles leave
the reticulum per minute; \num{150000} LDL particles taken up per
hour.
\end{solution}
