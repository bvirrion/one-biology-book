\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:cancer-biology} --- Cancer Biology}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:1}
An \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:genes}{oncogene} is a gain-of-function version of a gene that promotes
proliferation or survival; one mutant allele suffices (dominant):
\emph{RAS} (point mutation), \emph{MYC} (translocation, amplification),
\emph{HER2}, \emph{BCR--ABL}. A \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour} suppressor restrains
proliferation or enforces arrest, death or repair; both alleles must be
lost (recessive at the cell level): \emph{RB}, \emph{TP53}, \emph{APC},
\emph{BRCA1}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:2}
Sustained proliferation: \emph{RAS}, \emph{MYC}. Insensitivity to
growth suppressors: \emph{RB}, \emph{CDKN2A} (p16). Resisting death:
\emph{BCL2}, \emph{TP53}. Replicative immortality: the \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:telomerase}{telomerase}
promoter. Angiogenesis: \emph{VEGF} induced through HIF (\emph{VHL}
loss). \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:metastasis}{Invasion} and \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{metastasis}: E-cadherin loss, the
epithelial--mesenchymal factors. Genome instability: mismatch-repair
genes, \emph{\omterm{def:b3:dna-repair:dsb}{BRCA}}. Deregulated metabolism: \emph{MYC}, \omterm{prop:b3:cancer-biology:pathways}{PI3K pathway}.
\omterm{prop:b3:cancer-biology:immune}{Immune evasion}: \emph{PD-L1}, MHC loss. Tumour-promoting inflammation:
\emph{NF-$\kappa$B} signalling.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:3}
Children with a family history developed several \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumours} in both eyes,
early; sporadic cases developed a single \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour}, later. He inferred that
two events were needed in a cell: the familial patients had inherited
one and needed only a second, somatic event (frequent enough to occur
in several retinal cells), while sporadic patients needed both to occur
somatically in one cell --- rare, late, single. Hence a gene whose two
copies must both be lost: the first \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour} suppressor.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:4}
A histidine-requiring \emph{Salmonella} mutant is plated without
histidine with the test compound; colonies arise only from cells in
which a new mutation has reverted the defect, so their number measures
mutagenicity. Liver extract is added because many carcinogens are
harmless until liver enzymes convert them into reactive metabolites,
as happens in the body; the bacteria lack those enzymes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:5}
Incidence rises $100$-fold while age doubles: $2^{k-1} = 100$, $k - 1 =
\log_{2}100 = 6.6$, so about seven or eight rate-limiting events on the
naive reading --- fewer if clonal expansion between hits is allowed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:6}
$L = \sqrt{2Dc_{0}/q}$: for $q = 0.01$, $\sqrt{2\times 2\times 10^{-9}
\times 0.05/0.01} = \qty{141}{\micro m}$; for $q = 0.03$,
\qty{82}{\micro m}. A fast-growing \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour} respires faster, exhausts the
oxygen within a shorter distance, and its centre dies at a smaller size.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:7}
$10^{11}\times 10^{-3n} < 1$ needs $n > 3.7$: four courses. After two
courses $10^{5}$ cells remain --- a tenth of a cubic millimetre, far
below the $10^{9}$ that can be detected --- so the \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour} is
``gone'' by every test while a hundred thousand cells remain; treatment
must continue for the planned number of courses, not until the \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour}
disappears.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:8}
Both lesions activate the same pathway (receptor $\to$ \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:genes}{Ras} $\to$ ERK);
once one is present the other confers no further advantage and is not
selected. An EGFR inhibitor blocks the receptor; a cell that acquires a
KRAS mutation downstream restores the signal with the receptor still
blocked, and is selected during treatment --- resistance by bypass.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:9}
E7 frees E2F from Rb, driving the infected epithelial cell through the
\omterm{def:b3:cell-cycle-apoptosis:checkpoints}{restriction point} without growth factors; E6 destroys \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:genes}{p53}, so the
inappropriate proliferation and the damage it causes trigger neither
arrest nor \omterm{def:b3:cell-cycle-apoptosis:apoptosis}{apoptosis}. Two hallmarks are supplied at once, but the
remaining ones --- immortality, \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:metastasis}{invasion}, instability --- need further
somatic mutations, which take decades to accumulate in the persistently
infected cells. The vaccine raises neutralising antibodies against the
capsid and prevents the infection; given before exposure, there is
never an infected cell to transform.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:10}
After the $i$th hit the clone grows by $g$, so $g^{i}$ times as many
cells are at risk of the next hit; the probability of the full sequence
in the lineage descended from one original cell is $g\cdot g^{2}\cdots$
--- for constant $g$ per hit the cumulative risk becomes $N(ut)^{k}
g^{k-1}$ up to order-dependent factors, and the incidence keeps the
power $t^{k-1}$ with a prefactor $g^{k-1}$ larger. If the expansions
themselves grow with time (exponentially growing clones), the curve
steepens further, so a slope of $6$ is produced by fewer than seven
drivers: the Armitage--Doll $k$ is an upper bound on the number of
rate-limiting drivers, and sequenced \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumours} show two to eight.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:11}
(a) Drug A alone faces $\mu_{A}N = 100$ pre-existing resistant cells:
$P_{0} = e^{-100} \approx 0$; the A-resistant clone survives and
regrows during the eighteen weeks of A, and by the time B starts it is
again a large population, so B faces $\mu_{B}N' \gg 1$ as well --- the
sequence fails twice. (b) Together: a doubly resistant cell arises at
$10^{-14}$ per division, $\mu_{A}\mu_{B}N = 10^{-5}$, $P_{0} =
0.99999$. Combination from the start, when $N$ is smallest, is the
rule because the product of two small rates is what makes resistance
unlikely.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:cancer-biology:12}
With $100$ times more cells and the same $u$ and $k$, cumulative risk
$N(ut)^{k}$ would be $100$ times higher --- every elephant should die of
\omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{cancer} young. It does not, so $u$ or $k$ must differ: twenty copies of
\emph{TP53} make the apoptotic response to damage stronger and remove
mutant cells before they expand (lowering the effective $u$ for that
step); large animals also have lower per-cell mutation rates (better
repair, fewer divisions per cell per year because their cells turn over
more slowly), and may require more hits (an extra suppressor layer).
\omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{Cancer} suppression evolved with body size.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:cancer-biology:1}
\textbf{1.} $10^{12}/10^{3} = 10^{9}$ cells; $\log_{2}10^{9} \approx 30$
doublings.
\textbf{2.} $30\times 100 = 3000$ days, about $8$ years.
\textbf{3.} \qty{1}{kg} $\approx 10^{12}$ cells: ten more doublings,
$1000$ days, under three years --- a third of the time to detection.
\textbf{4.} The early doubling time must have been about $60$ days, or
the \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour} grew faster when small and slowed as it grew (the usual
pattern, as supply and space fail).
\textbf{5.} A cycle of $2$ days would double the population every $2$
days; a doubling time of $100$ means the net growth is $2\,\%$ of the
birth rate: nearly every cell born is matched by one dying, or few are
cycling --- birth and death almost balanced, growth the small
difference.
\textbf{6.} The drugs kill cycling cells, so a \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour} with a small
fraction in cycle loses a smaller fraction per course; but the fast
\omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour}, having lost more, also regrows between courses. On balance the
fast \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumours} (leukaemias, lymphomas, testicular \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{cancer}) are the ones
chemotherapy cures.
\textbf{7.} Ratio $300$ over an age ratio $80/30 = 2.67$: $k - 1 =
\ln 300/\ln 2.67 = 5.8$, $k \approx 7$.
\textbf{8.} $N(ut)^{k} = 10^{8}\times (7\times 10^{-5})^{7} \approx
10^{-21}$: absurd against a lifetime risk near $1$ in $3$. Missing:
clonal expansion multiplying the cells at risk after each hit, mutation
rates raised by instability, many more divisions (stem cells cycling
for decades), and fewer true drivers.
\textbf{9.} Multiplying by $g^{k-1} = 100^{6} = 10^{12}$ gives $10^{-9}$
--- still tiny with seven events; with four events and expansions,
$10^{8}\times (7\times 10^{-5})^{4}\times 10^{6} \approx 2\times
10^{-3}$, the right order. The true picture is few drivers and large
expansions.
\textbf{10.} $(ut)^{k-1}/(ut)^{k} = 1/(ut) = 1/(10^{-6}\times 40) =
2.5\times 10^{4}$: a carrier's incidence at forty is tens of thousands
of times the sporadic one --- hereditary \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{cancers} strike young.
\textbf{11.} One event tenfold faster: incidence $\times 10$; two:
$\times 100$. The observed fifteen- to twenty-five-fold risk suggests
smoke acts on more than one step.
\textbf{12.} On stopping, $u$ falls back for the events still to come,
so the incidence stops climbing as steeply; but the hits already
accumulated in the cells remain, so the ex-smoker's cells are nearer to
\omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{cancer} than a never-smoker's and the excess risk persists, frozen
rather than erased.
\textbf{13.} $L = \sqrt{2\times 2\times 10^{-9}\times 0.05/0.03} =
\qty{82}{\micro m}$.
\textbf{14.} Fully oxygenated up to a diameter $2L \approx
\qty{160}{\micro m}$. A \qty{2}{mm} spheroid has a living rim of
\qty{82}{\micro m}: alive fraction $1 - (918/1000)^{3} = 0.23$.
\textbf{15.} Midway cells are \qty{75}{\micro m} from a vessel, just
within $L$ --- oxygenated in principle, but \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour} vessels carry less
oxygen and flow intermittently, so the midway cells are hypoxic.
Hypoxic cells are two to three times more resistant to radiation
(oxygen is needed to make the damage permanent), and hypoxia triggers
p53-dependent \omterm{def:b3:cell-cycle-apoptosis:apoptosis}{apoptosis}, so it selects the cells that have lost \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:genes}{p53}.
\textbf{16.} Halving the density spreads vessels by $\sqrt{2}$:
\qty{212}{\micro m} apart, midway cells \qty{106}{\micro m} away, beyond
$L$: they die or, surviving hypoxic, become more aggressive and
angiogenic.
\textbf{17.} $30/2 = 15$ times more glucose. \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{Tumours} therefore
concentrate glucose analogues, and a positron-emitting
fluorodeoxyglucose scan lights them up.
\textbf{18.} Starved \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumours} shrink to the diffusion-limited size and
persist, dormant and hypoxic, or co-opt existing vessels; hypoxia
selects aggressive clones. But pruning the chaotic \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour} vasculature
lowers the interstitial pressure and evens the flow, so chemotherapy
penetrates better --- the combination works where either alone does
not.
\textbf{19.} $10^{9}\times 10^{-2n} < 1$: $n > 4.5$, five courses.
\textbf{20.} $21$ days $= 0.21$ doublings, regrowth $\times 1.16$; net
factor per cycle $0.0116$; $\ln 10^{-9}/\ln 0.0116 = 4.65$: still five
cycles.
\textbf{21.} $10^{-7}\times 10^{9} = 100$ resistant cells expected;
$P_{0} = e^{-100} \approx 0$: resistance is certain.
\textbf{22.} $\mu_{1}\mu_{2}N = 10^{-14}\times 10^{9} = 10^{-5}$;
$P_{0} = 0.99999$. At $10^{12}$ cells, $10^{-2}$, $P_{0} = 0.99$.
\textbf{23.} $10^{6}$ cells: one drug $\mu N = 0.1$, $P_{0} = 0.90$; two
drugs $10^{-8}$, $P_{0} \approx 1$. Treat when the population is small,
and treat with combinations.
\textbf{24.} The T~cells attack many neoantigens at once, so no single
mutation escapes them; escape requires losing antigen presentation
itself (MHC, $\beta_{2}$-microglobulin) or suppressing the T~cells, a
different and rarer class of event. The same high mutation load that
makes a \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour} certain to resist a kinase inhibitor gives it many
neoantigens: mismatch-repair-deficient, ultraviolet- and smoke-induced
\omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumours} respond best.
\textbf{25.} About $8$ years to detection; living \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour} \qty{82}{\micro m}
deep around a vessel; $P_{0} = 0.99999$ that a two-drug combination
faces no resistant cell in a \qty{1}{cm^3} \omterm{def:b3:cancer-biology:hallmarks}{tumour}.
\end{solution}
