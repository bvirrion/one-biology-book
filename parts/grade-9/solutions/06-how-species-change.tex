\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g9:how-species-change} --- How Species Change}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:1}
Variety of inherited \omterm{def:g9:heredity-and-traits:traits}{traits}; over-production met by the
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{environment}'s checks; unequal collection --- the suited
escape a little oftener and leave more heirs. Consequence:
the suited \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{alleles}' share grows generation by generation
--- \omterm{prop:g9:how-species-change:selection}{natural selection}, and with time, \omterm{prop:g9:how-species-change:selection}{evolution}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:2}
Variety: inherited pale and dark forms. Check: sighted
bird predation against bark. Unequal collection: on sooty
trunks the pale are seen and eaten --- dark's share
climbs; on clean pale bark the dark are seen --- pale's
share recovers. One engine, either direction.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:3}
The \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population}: no individual moth darkens. Selection
shifts the \emph{frequencies} of inherited forms across
generations --- individuals are dealt and spent; the
deck's composition is what drifts.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:4}
Effort is not inherited --- a stretched neck is the
blacksmith's \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arm}. The mechanism: necks varied heritably;
longer-necked variants fed higher, survived the lean
checks oftener and left more heirs carrying the
longer-neck \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{alleles} --- the \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population}'s necks lengthened,
no giraffe's did.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:5}
The engine ticks in generations, and microbe generations
turn in hours: days hold thousands of ticks. Add a savage
check (the antibiotic) and rare resistant mutants inherit
the ward within the week.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:6}
Both were the unequal check on an inherited variety ---
deciding who breeds. The difference: the breeder chose on
purpose toward a goal; the \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds} chose nothing --- their
hunting's consequences did the selecting.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:7}
Fact 1: it can only compare the variants \emph{present}.
Selection is a sieve over the current deck --- it cannot
summon the unmutated, unshuffled better design, only
promote the best on hand; hence re-jobbed \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{limb} plans, not
fresh engineering.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:8}
Variety: beak depth, heritable (ringers' pedigrees show
it). Check: the drought's hard \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} --- small beaks
cannot crack the remaining food. Bookkeeping: deep-beaked
\omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds} feed, survive, breed; next season's hatchlings
average deeper --- the deep \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{alleles} gained share. No
split: one \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population}, one tick, measured.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:9}
Separation: the sea --- castaways breed alone. Divergence:
each island's \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} run the engine its own way,
accumulating its own \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{alleles}, generation on generation.
The test: reunited \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds} failing to interbreed --- by the
\omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} definition, the fork is complete.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:10}
Both apply the check raggedly. The unfinished course
kills the susceptible and releases the survivors ---
enriched for resistance --- mid-selection; farm-scattered
doses run the same partial sieve across vast microbe
\omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{populations} daily. A check that culls without finishing
is a breeding program for what it spares.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:11}
The checks chapter established that most of every
generation's surplus is collected --- predation, hunger,
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}; the uniqueness chapter established that the
collected differ heritably, deck by deck. Selection is
nothing added: it is the observation that those checks,
falling on that variety, cannot fall equally --- the two
chapters multiplied together.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:how-species-change:12}
Selection spends variety; the shuffle and \omterm{prop:g9:genes-and-dna:explains}{mutation}
restock it. A clone \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population} offers the sieve one deck:
when \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} turn, selection has nothing to promote
--- the vineyard stands or falls as one \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant}. Without
the trickle of new spellings and the deal of new
combinations, the engine seizes: variation is not a
by-product of the mechanism, it is the fuel.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g9:how-species-change:1}
\textbf{1.} The \omterm{def:g6:how-plants-spread:dispersal}{dispersal} chapter: only the long-range
services --- wings, drift, the storm-blown --- reach an
oceanic island; the passenger list is the services'
manifest.

\textbf{2.} That the archipelago's finches descend from
mainland castaways --- shared \omterm{def:g6:classification-kinship:attribute}{attributes} in detail mean
shared ancestry, and their nearest kin names the source
\omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{population}.

\textbf{3.} The \omterm{def:g4:adapted-to-their-world:adaptation}{adaptation} chapter reads the fit ---
each beak answers its island's food problem. This
chapter adds the \emph{coming-about}: castaway variety,
island checks, unequal collection, generations of
bookkeeping --- the fit's manufacture, where before we
could only see the fit
(\cref{rem:g4:adapted-to-their-world:how}'s promised
answer).

\textbf{4.} Separation: sea-bound \omterm{def:g8:reproduction-and-environment:population}{populations} breed
alone from the start, each island a sealed engine room
--- speciation's first ingredient supplied by geography
wholesale.

\textbf{5.} Check arrives (hard \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} only); the
inherited variety in beak depth is collected unequally
(small beaks starve); the survivors' breeding writes
the shift into the next generation's average --- one
tick of the engine, caliper-documented.

\textbf{6.} That its ``direction'' is only ever the
current \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}: selection tracks the check, and a
reversed check reverses the drift --- no destination,
just consequences, season by season.

\textbf{7.} \Cref{prop:g9:how-species-change:speciation}
mid-run: divergence far enough advanced that
interbreeding falters --- a fork closing before the
notebooks' \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eyes}.

\textbf{8.} Speciation's ticks are generations by the
thousands --- watched fully only in fast-turning life
(the ward's \omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:fermentation}{microbes}). For finches, the method stands
witness instead: pattern, \omterm{prop:g6:classification-kinship:kinship}{kinship}, live ticks and
half-closed forks converge on the process no single
lifetime spans.

\textbf{9.} (1) Variety: castaway founders, shuffled and
mutating. (2) Checks: each island's own foods and
dangers. (3) Bookkeeping: each island's suited \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{alleles}
gain, island by island. (4) Separation: the sea between,
holding each ledger apart. (5) Timescale: thousands of
generations --- and the forks stand complete.

\textbf{10.} Only what its founders carried plus its own
\omterm{prop:g9:genes-and-dna:explains}{mutations} since: each island's deck is a castaway's
sample, and selection could promote only the best
\emph{available} beak from it --- islands with richer
decks or luckier \omterm{prop:g9:genes-and-dna:explains}{mutations} got nearer answers.

\textbf{11.} The generation time: hours in the ward,
years on the islands --- same three facts, same
bookkeeping, ticks apart by a factor of thousands.

\textbf{12.} Both are checks deciding who breeds: the
fancier's deliberate hand and the drought's mindless
hard \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} do identical bookkeeping on inherited
variety --- which is why the changes wrought in pigeon
lofts in centuries certify what nature's checks can
write in millions of years.
\end{solution}
