\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b1:digestion-absorption} --- Digestion and Absorption}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:1}
Mouth: chewing, salivary amylase. Oesophagus: transport. Stomach:
acid, pepsin, storage and mixing. Small intestine: bile and pancreatic
\omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzymes} complete \omterm{def:b1:digestion-absorption:nutrition}{digestion}; absorption. Large intestine: water and
salt recovered, \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:fermentation}{fermentation} by microbes. Rectum: storage and
egestion.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:2}
Pepsin in the stomach at pH 2; trypsin, chymotrypsin and
carboxypeptidases from the pancreas in the small intestine at pH 8;
brush-border peptidases at pH 7. As zymogens, so that they do not
digest the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells} that make them; they are activated only in the lumen.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:3}
Glucose: sodium symport in, carrier out, to the portal vein and the
liver. \omterm{def:b1:proteins:aminoacid}{Amino acid}: sodium symport in, carrier out, portal vein. \omterm{def:b1:lipids:fattyacid}{Fatty
acid}: diffusion in from a micelle, re-esterified, packed into a
chylomicron, exocytosed into the lacteal and the \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:compartments}{lymph}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:4}
Bile salts emulsify \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} into micrometre droplets and carry the
products of lipase in micelles to the \omterm{def:b1:digestion-absorption:surface}{enterocytes}. Without them the
\omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} stays as large drops with almost no surface for lipase, and the
\omterm{def:b1:lipids:fattyacid}{fatty acids} released cannot be delivered to the membrane: \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} passes
through undigested.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:5}
$100/162 = \qty{0.62}{mol}$ of glucose units, hence about
\qty{0.62}{mol} of bonds and \qty{0.62}{mol} of water (\qty{11}{g});
$620/180 = \qty{3.4}{mmol/min}$ of glucose absorbed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:6}
Acid releases secretin, which makes the pancreas secrete bicarbonate
that neutralises the chyme; \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} and peptides release cholecystokinin,
which contracts the gall bladder (bile enters), stimulates the
pancreatic \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzymes}, and inhibits gastric emptying, so that the
duodenum receives chyme only as fast as it can neutralise and digest
it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:7}
Glucose was concentrated eightfold against its gradient: \omterm{def:b1:membranes-transport:transporters}{active
transport}. Without sodium the transport stops at equilibrium: the
uptake is coupled to the sodium gradient (the sodium--glucose
\omterm{prop:b1:membranes-transport:secondary}{symporter}), not to \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} directly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:8}
Chylomicrons, a micrometre across, cannot cross the tight capillary
wall but can enter the open-ended lacteals; the \omterm{def:b1:mammal-organization:compartments}{lymph} joins the blood
at the neck, so \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} reaches the whole body before the liver, which
would otherwise take it all up first. Entering the portal blood
directly, the chylomicrons would clog the liver's sinusoids and
saturate its capacity to process \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} after every meal.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:9}
It moistens and floats the fibre for rumination; its bicarbonate and
phosphate \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:buffer}{buffer} the acids the microbes produce, holding the rumen
near pH 6.5; and it carries urea from the blood, which the microbes
turn into ammonia and then into their own \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein}, recycling nitrogen
that would otherwise be lost in urine.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:10}
Without pancreatic amylase, proteases, lipase and bicarbonate: \omterm{def:b1:carbohydrates:polysaccharide}{starch}
is partly digested by saliva and the brush border only; \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{proteins} are
cut by pepsin but not to absorbable size; \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} is not digested at all
and appears in bulky, pale, greasy faeces. Sugars and \omterm{def:b1:carbohydrates:glycosidic}{disaccharides}
(brush-border \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzymes} remain), some \omterm{def:b1:carbohydrates:polysaccharide}{starch}, and small peptides can
still be handled; \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat} and most \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} cannot.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:11}
The caecal droppings carry the microbial \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} and the B vitamins
made in the caecum, which lies beyond the small intestine and so
cannot be digested on the first passage; eaten, they pass the stomach
and small intestine and are absorbed. The cow ferments before the
stomach, so its microbes are digested downstream without any second
passage. The horse's caecum is also downstream, and the horse neither
eats its droppings nor has any other route: its microbial \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} is
lost.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:digestion-absorption:12}
The microbes give the cow the energy of \omterm{def:b1:carbohydrates:polysaccharide}{cellulose} as \omterm{def:b1:lipids:fattyacid}{fatty acids},
\omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} made from poor grass and even from urea, and every B vitamin;
the cow gives them a warm, buffered, anaerobic vat kept full and
stirred, and a hundred litres of saliva a day. The design's costs: a
tenth of the energy \omterm{def:b1:flowering-plant-organization:organs}{leaves} as methane, no glucose is absorbed so the
liver must make it all from propionate, \omterm{def:b1:digestion-absorption:nutrition}{digestion} takes three days,
and the vat and its contents weigh a fifth of the animal --- a cow
cannot sprint, and a lion can.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b1:digestion-absorption:1}
\textbf{1.} Fibre \qty{5}{kg}, \omterm{def:b1:carbohydrates:polysaccharide}{starch} and sugars \qty{2}{kg}, \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein}
\qty{1.5}{kg}.
\textbf{2.} $0.7\times 5 + 2 = \qty{5.5}{kg}$.
\textbf{3.} $5500\times 17.5 = \qty{96}{MJ}$; VFA $0.75\times 96 =
\qty{72}{MJ}$.
\textbf{4.} $0.08\times 96 = \qty{7.7}{MJ}$; $7700/55 = \qty{140}{g}$
of methane, about \qty{200}{L}.
\textbf{5.} $0.15\times 5500 = \qty{825}{g}$.
\textbf{6.} Most dietary \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} is degraded by the microbes in the
rumen and rebuilt as microbial \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein}; the microbes flow on to the
abomasum and small intestine, where they are digested like any meat.
\textbf{7.} \qty{30}{\%} of the fibre, \qty{1.5}{kg}: partly fermented
in the colon, mostly excreted.
\textbf{8.} Escaped dietary \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} $0.4\times 1500 = \qty{600}{g}$,
digested \qty{360}{g}; microbial \qty{825}{g}; total \qty{1185}{g}
$\times 17.5 = \qty{20.7}{MJ}$.
\textbf{9.} $72 + 20.7 = \qty{93}{MJ}$.
\textbf{10.} $72/93 = \qty{77}{\%}$ (about \qty{60}{\%} once the heat
of \omterm{def:b1:respiration-fermentation:fermentation}{fermentation} and the energy of the colon's contribution are
accounted in fuller budgets; on this reckoning, three quarters).
\textbf{11.} \qty{93}{MJ} is short of \qty{110}{}: the cow must eat
more (\qty{12}{kg}), or better grass, or draw on her \omterm{def:b1:lipids:triglyceride}{fat}.
\textbf{12.} Propionate; \omterm{def:b1:biosyntheses-integration:gluconeogenesis}{gluconeogenesis} in the liver.
\textbf{13.} $0.9\times 2000\times 17.5 = \qty{31.5}{MJ}$.
\textbf{14.} $0.7\times 1500\times 17.5 = \qty{18.4}{MJ}$.
\textbf{15.} $0.45\times 5 = \qty{2.25}{kg}$; VFA $0.75\times 2250\times
17.5 = \qty{29.5}{MJ}$.
\textbf{16.} $0.15\times 2250 = \qty{340}{g}$, lost in the faeces (the
hindgut is beyond the small intestine).
\textbf{17.} $31.5 + 18.4 + 29.5 = \qty{79}{MJ}$; VFA share $29.5/79 =
\qty{37}{\%}$.
\textbf{18.} No: it needs $110/79\times 10 = \qty{14}{kg}$ of grass, and
manages by eating for sixteen hours a day and passing food through
twice as fast.
\textbf{19.} Cow $93/10 = \qty{9.3}{MJ/kg}$; horse $79/10 =
\qty{7.9}{MJ/kg}$.
\textbf{20.} Gross intake $10\,000\times 0.85\times 17.5 =
\qty{149}{MJ}$ (lignin and ash excluded); methane \qty{7.7}{MJ} is
\qty{5}{\%} of it; a hundred cows: $100\times 140\times 365 =
\qty{5.1}{t}$ of methane a year.
\textbf{21.} Cow $10\times 70/24 = \qty{29}{kg}$ of dry matter in the
gut (plus ten times that of water); horse $10\times 36/24 =
\qty{15}{kg}$.
\textbf{22.} In principle all \qty{340}{g}, worth \qty{6}{MJ} and its
essential \omterm{def:b1:proteins:aminoacid}{amino acids}; a horse's caecal contents are not separated
into a soft nutrient-rich fraction as a rabbit's are, and an animal of
half a tonne cannot recover its own droppings from the ground in a
useful state.
\textbf{23.} The rumen microbes make \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} from urea nitrogen and
straw carbon, and the cow digests the microbes: she needs almost no
dietary \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein}. The horse's microbes are beyond its small intestine,
so their \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein} is lost, and the horse must find its \omterm{def:b1:proteins:aminoacid}{amino acids} in
the food itself.
\textbf{24.} The horse absorbs glucose and stores \omterm{def:b1:carbohydrates:polysaccharide}{glycogen} in its
muscles for anaerobic sprinting; the cow absorbs none, makes glucose
slowly from propionate, and carries a hundred-litre vat --- built to
graze, not to run.
\textbf{25.} About three quarters of the cow's metabolisable energy on
this budget (\qtyrange{60}{70}{\%} in a full accounting) as volatile
\omterm{def:b1:lipids:fattyacid}{fatty acids}, against a third for the horse; \qty{9.3}{MJ} against
\qty{7.9}{MJ} per kilogram of grass.
\end{solution}
