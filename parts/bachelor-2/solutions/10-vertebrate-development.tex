\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b2:vertebrate-development} --- Embryonic Development of Vertebrates}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:1}
\omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:egg}{Cleavage}: rapid mitoses without growth, producing the \omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:blastula}{blastula}.
\omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:blastula}{Blastula}: a hollow ball or disc of small cells around the
\omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:blastula}{blastocoel}. Gastrulation: the cell movements that bring mesoderm and
endoderm inside, producing the three-layered gastrula with a gut
cavity and notochord. Neurulation: the folding of the dorsal
ectoderm into the \omterm{prop:b2:vertebrate-development:neurulation}{neural tube}, with the segmentation of the somites,
producing the neurula with the vertebrate body plan.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:2}
Ectoderm: epidermis, brain and spinal cord, peripheral nerves
(\omterm{prop:b2:vertebrate-development:neurulation}{neural crest}), lens and pigment cells. Mesoderm: notochord, muscle,
bone and cartilage, kidney, heart and blood, dermis. Endoderm: the
gut lining, liver, pancreas, lungs, thyroid.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:3}
Little \omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:egg}{yolk}: complete, nearly equal \omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:egg}{cleavage} and gastrulation by
involution through a round blastopore in a hollow ball (frog:
complete but unequal, the yolky vegetal cells large and slow). Much
\omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:egg}{yolk} (chick): \omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:egg}{cleavage} confined to a disc on top of the \omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:egg}{yolk}, and
gastrulation through a slit, the primitive streak, in the flat
disc, the layers spreading over the \omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:egg}{yolk} instead of enclosing a
cavity.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:4}
\omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:induction}{Induction} is the change of a competent tissue's fate by a signal
from a neighbouring tissue. Vegetal cells induce mesoderm in the
overlying marginal zone; the \omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:induction}{organiser} (dorsal lip mesoderm) induces
the neural plate in the dorsal ectoderm; the optic vesicle induces
the lens in the head ectoderm.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:5}
$2^{12} = 4096$ cells of radius $1/2^{4} = \qty{62.5}{\micro m}$;
surface multiplied by $2^{4} = 16$. The extra surface is the
membrane of the epithelia that gastrulation will fold into skin and
gut, and the area through which the \omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:blastula}{blastula} exchanges with its
surroundings.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:6}
$10^{-4}\times 2^{k} = 0.4$: $2^{k} = 4000$, $k = 12$. With four
times the DNA the ratio starts at $4\times 10^{-4}$ and reaches $0.4$
at $2^{k} = 1000$: the tenth division, two earlier.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:7}
$k = \ln 2/600 = \qty{1.16e-3}{s^{-1}}$; $\lambda = \sqrt{2\times
10^{-12}/1.16\times 10^{-3}} = \qty{42}{\micro m}$. Thresholds at
$x = \lambda\ln 2 = \qty{29}{\micro m}$ and $\lambda\ln 10 =
\qty{96}{\micro m}$: bands of 29, 67 and the remainder.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:8}
Doubling the source shifts both boundaries outward by $\lambda\ln
2 = \qty{29}{\micro m}$: the first band doubles, the second keeps
its width. Doubling $k$ divides $\lambda$ by $\sqrt 2$
(\qty{29}{\micro m}) and, since $c_0 = j/\sqrt{Dk}$, divides $c_0$ by
$\sqrt 2$ as well: the boundaries move to $\lambda'\ln(c_0'/T) =
29\times(0.69 - 0.35) = \qty{10}{\micro m}$ and $29\times(2.30 -
0.35) = \qty{57}{\micro m}$ --- both bands shrink.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:9}
A dorsal lip from an unpigmented newt gastrula was grafted to the
ventral side of a pigmented one; it rolled in and the host formed a
second embryo --- \omterm{prop:b2:vertebrate-development:neurulation}{neural tube}, somites, gut --- joined to the first.
The pigment difference showed that the second axis was made of host
cells, so the graft had induced them rather than built the axis
itself. It excluded self-differentiation of the graft as the
explanation, and showed that a small region carries the signal that
organises the whole axis.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:10}
(a) Belly skin: the early ectoderm is competent but not yet induced,
so it follows its new surroundings. (b) Neural tissue: by the late
gastrula it has been induced and is determined. (c) Neural plate:
late-gastrula belly ectoderm is still competent, and the notochord
induces it. (d) No dorsal structures: a ball of belly tissue, since
nothing induces the mesoderm to dorsal fates or the ectoderm to
neural ones.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:11}
At \qty{18}{\celsius}: $90 + 11\times 30 = \qty{420}{min}$, 7 hours.
At \qty{10}{\celsius}: \qty{17.5}{h}. The transition falls at the
twelfth division in both cases because what triggers it is the
ratio of DNA to cytoplasm, which depends on the number of divisions
and not on how long they took: the clock counts doublings, not
hours.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:vertebrate-development:12}
In regulative development cells acquire their fates by induction
from neighbours, so the embryo can rebuild after loss (twinning from
a split egg, regeneration of a grafted region); in mosaic
development fates are inherited with localised cytoplasmic
determinants, so each blastomere makes only its part and a split
embryo makes two half-larvae. The difference is one of degree: the
frog's grey crescent is a determinant, and mosaic embryos use
inductions later; every embryo mixes inheritance and conversation,
vertebrates leaning on conversation for longer.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:vertebrate-development:1}
\textbf{1.} Egg $\tfrac43\pi(0.7)^{3} = \qty{1.44}{mm^3}$; nucleus
$\tfrac43\pi(0.03)^{3} = \qty{1.1e-4}{mm^3}$; ratio $8\times
10^{-5}$.
\textbf{2.} $2^{k} = 4000$: $k = 12$, 4096 cells.
\textbf{3.} $75 + 11\times 30 = \qty{405}{min}$, about 6.75 hours.
\textbf{4.} Radius $0.7/2^{4} = \qty{44}{\micro m}$; ratio $8\times
10^{-5}\times 4096 = 0.32$ --- that of an ordinary cell: the nuclei
have caught up with the cytoplasm.
\textbf{5.} $2^{4} = 16$.
\textbf{6.} About $4095\,c$ of DNA; the egg holds enough stored
histones, polymerases and nucleotides for twelve divisions, so no
transcription is needed.
\textbf{7.} Zygotic genes switch on, divisions slow and lose
synchrony, cells become motile. Injecting extra DNA brings the
transition forward by as many divisions as the DNA is in excess,
which a counter of divisions or of time could not explain.
\textbf{8.} Opposite the sperm entry point, at the grey crescent
fixed by the rotation of the egg cortex after fertilisation.
\textbf{9.} Dorsal mesoderm first (prechordal plate, then notochord)
onto the roof of the archenteron in the midline; lateral mesoderm
(somites, lateral plate) beside it; the endoderm lines the
archenteron; the ectoderm spreads over the outside by epiboly.
\textbf{10.} $\qty{2000}{\micro m}/\qty{600}{min} = \qty{3.3}{\micro
m/min}$: three times a fibroblast --- a coordinated sheet moves
faster than a lone cell.
\textbf{11.} The cell movements need zygotic gene products (new
adhesion molecules, motility) and slowed cycles; before the
transition the cells can only divide.
\textbf{12.} Every chordate has a notochord at some stage, and
vertebrates build the vertebral column around it; the \omterm{prop:b2:vertebrate-development:neurulation}{neural tube}
is induced by it and is the consequence, not the origin, of the
plan.
\textbf{13.} Without bottle-cell contraction no blastopore forms,
nothing rolls in, and the embryo stays a ball with its layers in
place: no gut, no notochord, no axis.
\textbf{14.} $k = \ln 2/1200 = \qty{5.8e-4}{s^{-1}}$; $\lambda =
\sqrt{10^{-12}/5.8\times 10^{-4}} = \qty{42}{\micro m}$.
\textbf{15.} $x_1 = 42\ln 2.5 = \qty{38}{\micro m}$; $x_2 = 42\ln 12.5
= \qty{105}{\micro m}$.
\textbf{16.} Bands of \qty{38}{\micro m} (2.5 cells), \qty{67}{\micro
m} (4.5 cells), and the remainder.
\textbf{17.} $x_1 = 42\ln 1.25 = \qty{9}{\micro m}$; $x_2 = 42\ln 6.25
= \qty{77}{\micro m}$: both boundaries move inward by $\lambda\ln 2 =
\qty{29}{\micro m}$; the first band shrinks from 38 to 9, the middle
band keeps its width, the outer band grows.
\textbf{18.} Moved before reading, it becomes fate A --- it reads the
concentration where it now is; moved after, it keeps fate B --- it
was determined.
\textbf{19.} $x_T = \lambda\ln(c_0/T)$: a change of source by a factor
$f$ moves every boundary by $\lambda\ln f$, so a twofold error
shifts the pattern by only $0.7\lambda$, a fraction of a field that
spans several $\lambda$ --- the pattern is nearly insensitive to the
exact amount of morphogen.
\textbf{20.} Dorsal lip to ventral: a second axis. Ventral marginal
zone to dorsal: it is re-specified by its surroundings and makes
dorsal tissue; nothing extra.
\textbf{21.} In a neurula the ectoderm is no longer competent: the
graft makes notochord but induces no second \omterm{prop:b2:vertebrate-development:neurulation}{neural tube}.
\textbf{22.} Split along the crescent's plane: two complete embryos;
perpendicular: one embryo and a ball of belly tissue.
\textbf{23.} Alone: an epidermal ball. With vegetal cells: mesoderm
--- muscle, notochord --- induced in the cap.
\textbf{24.} To prove that the second axis was induced in host cells
and not built by the graft; had the graft been indistinguishable,
self-differentiation could not have been excluded. A graft from a
different species works (they used two newt species), which is what
made the cells distinguishable.
\textbf{25.} Twelfth \omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:egg}{cleavage}, 4096 cells, at about 6.75 hours; bands
of 38 and \qty{67}{\micro m}, and everything beyond \qty{105}{\micro
m}.
\end{solution}
