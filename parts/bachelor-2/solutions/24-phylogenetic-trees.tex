\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b2:phylogenetic-trees} --- Phylogenetic Trees and the Molecular Clock}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:1}
\omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{Homology}: a character inherited from a common ancestor --- the
humerus, radius and ulna of a bat's wing and a whale's flipper.
\omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{Analogy}: a similar character evolved independently --- the wings of
bats and birds as flight surfaces (the bones are homologous as
forelimbs, the wing as such is not), or the camera eye of the
octopus and the vertebrate. \omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{Synapomorphy}: a derived \omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{homology} shared
by a \omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{clade} and defining it --- feathers for birds, the amniotic egg
for amniotes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:2}
Birds: monophyletic. Reptiles without birds: paraphyletic. Fish
without tetrapods: paraphyletic (lungfish are closer to us than to
trout). Flying vertebrates (bats, birds, pterosaurs): polyphyletic.
Mammals: monophyletic. \omterm{def:b2:unicellular-diversity:prokaryote}{Prokaryotes}: paraphyletic (bacteria and
archaea, with the eukaryotes left out --- and archaea closer to
eukaryotes than to bacteria).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:3}
Sister of birds: crocodiles. Sister of mammals: all the other
amniotes (turtles, lizards, crocodiles, birds together). Smallest
\omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{clade} containing lizards and birds: (lizards,(crocodiles, birds)),
the diapsids without turtles on this tree. Swapping crocodiles and
birds at their node changes nothing: a node can be rotated freely.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:4}
Unrooted: $3$; $3\times 5\times 7 = 105$; $3\times 5\times
7\times 9\times 11\times 13\times 15 = \num{2027025}$. Rooted trees
for 4 taxa: each of the 3 unrooted trees has 5 branches, giving 15.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:5}
$((A,B),(C,D))$: sites 1, 2, 3 one step each, site 4 two steps
($\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{G}$ on both sides), site 5 one: 6 steps.
$((A,C),(B,D))$: $2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 8$. $((A,D),(B,C))$: $2 + 2 + 2 +
2 + 1 = 9$. The first tree is most parsimonious; on it site 4 is
homoplastic (the $\mathrm{A}\to\mathrm{G}$ change, or its reverse,
occurs twice).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:6}
Join $A$ and $B$ at height 3. Then $d(AB,C) = 14$, $d(AB,D) = 16$,
$d(C,D) = 10$: join $C$ and $D$ at height 5. Then $d(AB,CD) = (14 + 16
+ 14 + 16)/4 = 15$: root at height $7.5$. Tree $((A,B),(C,D))$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:7}
$d = -0.75\ln(1 - 4p/3)$: $0.052$, $0.38$, $1.21$. At $p = 0.6$ the
argument of the logarithm is $0.2$ and the slope $\mathrm{d}d/\mathrm{d}p
= 1/(1 - 4p/3) = 5$: a sampling error of $0.02$ in $p$ becomes
$0.1$ in $d$, and the assumption of equal rates at every site,
false in every real gene, adds a bias of the same size. The gene
is saturated.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:8}
$t = d/2k = 0.02/(2\times 10^{-9}) = 10$ million years. At 500
million years, $d = 2\times 10^{-9}\times 5\times 10^{8} = 1.0$ and
$p = 0.75(1 - e^{-4/3}) = 0.55$: three quarters of the way to
saturation, where the correction is steep and the date unreliable
--- a slower gene is needed for such splits.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:9}
\qty{52}{\%}: the \omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{clade} is recovered in barely half the resamples;
the data are nearly silent on it, and the alternatives together
are as likely. \qty{99}{\%}: the signal is consistent across the
sites; the \omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{clade} is strongly supported (given the model and the
alignment --- systematic errors such as \omterm{prop:b2:phylogenetic-trees:pitfalls}{long-branch attraction} are
not detected by the bootstrap). For the first, add sequence (more
genes), add taxa that break the branches, and check a different
method.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:10}
Two lineages that have each changed at a large fraction of sites
have, at any site where both changed, a one-in-four chance of
landing on the same base (with four bases at equal rates). If each
has changed at \qty{40}{\%} of sites, they have both changed at
\qty{16}{\%} and agree by chance at \qty{4}{\%} of all sites ---
which can exceed the fraction of sites carrying the true
synapomorphies that link each to its real, slowly-evolving
relatives. Parsimony counts matches without asking why they occur
and joins the two. Adding taxa that branch off along each long
branch divides it into shorter segments, so that the chance
matches are distributed among several shorter branches and the
true synapomorphies of each segment become visible; a likelihood
model that expects many chance matches on long branches corrects
for them directly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:11}
$d = -0.75\ln(1 - 0.12) = 0.096$; $t = d/2k = 0.096/(4\times 10^{-8})
= 2.4$ million years. Substitutions: $0.096\times \num{16000} =
1500$, relative precision $1/\sqrt{1500} = \qty{2.6}{\%}$: a
statistical error of $\pm 0.06$ million years. But the rate is
calibrated on other splits and may be wrong by a factor of two
for this lineage (rate variation between lineages, and the
difference between the pedigree rate measured over generations and
the rate measured over millions of years); and the gene's
divergence predates the species' divergence, since the ancestral
population was polymorphic --- so the true split is more recent
than the gene's, by a time that depends on the ancestral
population size. (The accepted date, from many nuclear genes and
fossils, is 6 to 7 million years: the mitochondrial clock here is
too fast.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:phylogenetic-trees:12}
\omterm{prop:b2:phylogenetic-trees:pitfalls}{Incomplete lineage sorting}: when two speciations are close in
time, an ancestral polymorphism may sort so that a gene's tree
groups species differently from the species tree; horizontal
transfer: a gene received from another lineage carries that
lineage's history; \omterm{prop:b2:genome-diversification:duplication}{gene duplication}: comparing a paralogue in one
species with the orthologue in another gives the duplication's
date, not the speciation's. A species tree is obtained by using
many genes from across the genome and taking the tree the majority
support (or a model that expects a known fraction of discordant
genes), by choosing orthologues carefully, and by preferring, in
\omterm{def:b2:unicellular-diversity:prokaryote}{prokaryotes}, the ribosomal and informational genes that are seldom
transferred.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:phylogenetic-trees:1}
\textbf{1.} Sites 1, 2 and 4 (states shared by $W$ and $H$ alone,
derived relative to the camel): synapomorphies of $\{W,H\}$. Sites 3
and 6: synapomorphies of $\{W,H,C\}$. Site 5 is an autapomorphy of
$W$, a change in one lineage that groups nothing; no site is
homoplastic on the molecular tree.
\textbf{2.} One change per site: 6 steps.
\textbf{3.} Sites 1, 2 and 4 now need two changes each, sites 3, 5
and 6 one: 9 steps.
\textbf{4.} The whale--hippo tree, by three steps. Three sites out
of a thousand is a small margin that a few homoplasies could
overturn; strengthening it means more genes, more taxa (other
ruminants, peccaries, the deer), and a bootstrap.
\textbf{5.} The \omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{clade} recurs in five resamples out of six: moderate
support, well above chance but short of the \qty{95}{\%}
conventionally called strong.
\textbf{6.} The outgroup must lie outside the group but close enough
that its sequence is still alignable and unsaturated; the camel is
an even-toed ungulate outside the cow--pig--hippo--whale group. A
fish would differ at nearly saturated levels, its states would be
uninformative about which state is ancestral within the mammals,
and its long branch would attract the fastest-evolving ingroup
lineage.
\textbf{7.} $d = 0.0625$, $0.107$, $0.131$, $0.155$.
\textbf{8.} Join $W$ and $H$ at height $0.031$. Then $d(WH,C) =
0.107$, $d(WH,P) = 0.131$, $d(C,P) = 0.131$, everything to $M$
$0.155$: join $WH$ and $C$ at $0.054$. Then $d(WHC,P) = 0.131$: join
at $0.065$. Finally join $M$ at $0.078$. Tree $(M,(P,(C,(W,H))))$.
\textbf{9.} Yes: the same tree, the same nesting.
\textbf{10.} A whale that had changed twice as fast would be
farther from everything, hippo included; UPGMA, which places each
tip at height zero and joins the closest pair, would join hippo
with cow first and leave the whale outside --- exactly the
anatomists' tree, for the wrong reason.
\textbf{11.} $0.0625\times 1000 = 62.5$ substitutions; Poisson
standard deviation $\sqrt{62.5} = 7.9$.
\textbf{12.} $7.9/62.5 = \qty{13}{\%}$.
\textbf{13.} $k = d/2t = 0.107/(1.2\times 10^{8}) = 8.9\times 10^{-10}$
per site per year.
\textbf{14.} $t = 0.0625/(2\times 8.9\times 10^{-10}) = 35$ million
years.
\textbf{15.} Pig: $0.131/(1.79\times 10^{-9}) = 73$ million years;
camel: $0.155/(1.79\times 10^{-9}) = 87$ million years.
\textbf{16.} $35 \pm 4.5$ million years (\qty{13}{\%}).
\textbf{17.} The rate scales as $1/t_{\text{cal}}$ and the date as
$t_{\text{cal}}$: $\pm 5/60 = \qty{8}{\%}$, so $\pm 3$ million years
--- combined with the Poisson error, about $\pm 5$ million years.
\textbf{18.} $d = -0.75\ln(1 - 0.6) = 0.69$: the gene has changed at
two thirds of its sites in effect, the correction has multiplied
$p$ by $1.5$ and its slope is $2.5$; it is near saturation for this
split and its date is worth little.
\textbf{19.} Absence of a character is not a shared derived state:
whales lost the hind limb and its ankle entirely, so nothing can be
said about which ankle they would have had. A \omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{synapomorphy} is
evidence of a \omterm{def:b2:phylogenetic-trees:tree}{clade}; its loss in one lineage is not evidence
against membership.
\textbf{20.} The fossil whales have the double-pulley ankle: whales
descend from an ancestor that had it, which places them inside the
even-toed ungulates. The molecular prediction is confirmed by the
rocks.
\textbf{21.} Paraphyletic: an ancestor with all its descendants
except the whale line.
\textbf{22.} \omterm{prop:b2:phylogenetic-trees:pitfalls}{Incomplete lineage sorting} of an ancestral polymorphism
across two rapid speciations (cow, hippo and whale split within a
few million years), or comparison of paralogues. Decide by
sequencing many genes and taking the tree that most of them, and
the concatenation, support; check for duplication in each family.
\textbf{23.} $d = -0.75\ln(1 - 0.333) = 0.30$; $k = d/2t =
0.30/(7\times 10^{7}) = 4.3\times 10^{-9}$ per site per year, five
times the nuclear gene.
\textbf{24.} Mitochondrial DNA is replicated by a polymerase with
weaker proofreading, exposed to the oxygen radicals of the
respiratory chain, and not repaired by the nuclear machinery; it
also lacks recombination, so damage accumulates.
\textbf{25.} Tree $(M,(P,(C,(W,H))))$: whales are the hippos' sister
group; $k = 8.9\times 10^{-10}$ per site per year; whale--hippo
divergence $35 \pm 5$ million years.
\end{solution}
