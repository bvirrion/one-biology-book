\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b3:adaptive-immunity} --- Adaptive Immunity and Vaccination}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:1}
One \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{lymphocyte}, one specificity (Nossal and Lederberg: each cell
secreted \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:antibody}{antibody} to one \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{antigen} only); the receptor is made before
\omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{antigen} is met (Tonegawa: the gene is rearranged in the B~cell, not in
response to \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{antigen}); \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{antigen} selects and expands the matching clones
into effector and \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:response}{memory cells}; self-reactive clones are deleted during
development.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:2}
Two heavy and two light chains, each with a variable domain at the tip
and constant domains behind; two identical binding sites formed by six
hypervariable loops; an Fc stem that other cells and \omterm{def:b3:innate-immunity:complement}{complement} read.
IgM: first \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:antibody}{antibody}, pentamer, \omterm{def:b3:innate-immunity:complement}{complement} activation. IgG: main blood
and tissue \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:antibody}{antibody}, \omterm{def:b3:innate-immunity:complement}{opsonisation}, neutralisation, crosses the placenta.
IgA: dimer secreted onto mucosal surfaces and into milk. IgE: bound to
mast cells, against parasites, the mediator of allergy. IgD: on naive
B~cells, of minor known function.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:3}
Class~I: all nucleated cells; peptides from cytosolic proteins cut by
the proteasome; 8--10 residues; read by CD8 cytotoxic T~cells. Class~II:
\omterm{def:b3:innate-immunity:innate}{dendritic cells}, \omterm{def:b3:innate-immunity:innate}{macrophages}, B~cells; peptides from proteins taken up
and digested in \omterm{def:b3:membrane-traffic:endocytosis}{endosomes}; 13--25 residues; read by CD4 helper T~cells.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:4}
$R_{0}$ is the mean number of cases one case produces in a wholly
susceptible population; the threshold is $p_{c} = 1 - 1/R_{0}$. Mumps
\qty{80}{\%}; pertussis \qty{92}{\%}; 1918 influenza \qty{60}{\%}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:5}
$50\times 20\times 5 = 5000$ heavy chains, $60\times 4 = 240$ light:
$1.2\times 10^{6}$ combinations; with \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:antibody}{junctional diversity} $1.2\times
10^{10}$ --- ten times the number of B~cells, so the animal expresses
at most a tenth of what it could make, and almost every cell carries a
unique receptor.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:6}
$1000\times 10^{-3} = 1$ mutation per cell per division; $20$ in twenty
divisions. Among $10^{5}$ cells, $2\times 10^{6}$ mutation events fall
on $3000$ possible single substitutions: each is sampled hundreds of
times, and the double and triple combinations besides --- the \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:germinal-centre}{germinal
centre} explores the neighbourhood of the receptor exhaustively.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:7}
T~cells are selected in the \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:tolerance}{thymus} to recognise peptides on the host's
own MHC alleles; another person's alleles have differently shaped
grooves that hold different peptides in different orientations, so the
same viral peptide is not seen. A graft's foreign MHC molecules are
themselves recognised by a large fraction of T~cells as ``self MHC with
a strange peptide'', hence rejection. The polymorphism persists because
a population with many alleles presents a broader sample of any
pathogen's peptides and no pathogen can escape everyone; heterozygotes
present twice the range and are favoured.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:8}
(a) No \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:antibody}{V(D)J recombination}: no B or T~cells, severe combined
immunodeficiency. (b) No AIRE: thymic cells fail to display tissue
\omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{antigens}, self-reactive T~cells escape, \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:tolerance}{autoimmunity} against many
organs. (c) No \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:tolerance}{FoxP3}: no regulatory T~cells, fatal early-onset
\omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:tolerance}{autoimmunity} and allergy. (d) No AID: no \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:antibody}{class switching} or
hypermutation --- abundant IgM, almost no IgG or IgA, low-affinity
antibodies, recurrent infections. (e) No CD4 T~cells: no help for
B~cells or \omterm{def:b3:innate-immunity:innate}{macrophages}, poor \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:antibody}{antibody} responses and susceptibility to
opportunistic infections --- the immunodeficiency of AIDS.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:9}
$p_{c} = 1 - 1/6 = 0.83$; coverage $0.83/0.9 = 93\,\%$. At \qty{80}{\%}
coverage the immune fraction is $0.72$, $R_{\text{eff}} = 6\times 0.28
= 1.7$. Among the susceptibles the epidemic spreads as if $R_{0}$ were
$1.7$ (the immune merely dilute the contacts), and the final-size
relation $s_{\infty} - \ln s_{\infty}/1.7 = 1$ gives $s_{\infty}
\approx 0.31$: about \qty{69}{\%} of the susceptibles, \qty{19}{\%} of
the population, are infected.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:10}
From $\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}t = -\beta si$ and $\mathrm{d}i/\mathrm{d}t
= \beta si - \gamma i$, $\mathrm{d}i/\mathrm{d}s = -1 + \gamma/(\beta s)$,
so $i + s - (\ln s)/R_{0}$ is conserved; with $s = 1$, $i = 0$ at the
start and $i = 0$, $s = s_{\infty}$ at the end, $s_{\infty} - \ln
s_{\infty}/R_{0} = 1$. Solutions: $R_{0} = 1.5$, $s_{\infty} = 0.42$
(\qty{58}{\%} infected); $2$, $0.20$ (\qty{80}{\%}); $3$, $0.06$
(\qty{94}{\%}); $5$, $0.007$ (\qty{99.3}{\%}). Not everyone: as the
susceptibles are used up the effective reproduction number falls below
one while some remain, and the epidemic burns out before reaching them.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:11}
$1.5^{n} = 1000$: $n = \ln 1000/\ln 1.5 \approx 17$ successive
improvements. With one mutation per cell per division and one in a
hundred improving, at least a hundred cells must be screened per round
to find one; in practice thousands, since half the mutations destroy
binding and selection is noisy. Seventeen rounds of two divisions each
take about nine days --- the observed two to three weeks, given the
inefficiencies. Cycles separate mutation (dark zone) from testing
(light zone) so that each variant is judged against \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{antigen} before it
is allowed to multiply, exactly as a genetic algorithm alternates
variation and selection.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b3:adaptive-immunity:12}
Women: several immune genes lie on the X chromosome, some (TLR7)
escape inactivation and are expressed from both copies, and oestrogens
enhance B~cell survival --- test: men with an extra X (Klinefelter)
have a female-like risk of lupus, which they do. \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:mhc}{HLA}: the associated
alleles present the relevant self peptides well, or fail to present
them in the \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:tolerance}{thymus} so the clones are not deleted --- test: mice
\omterm{def:b3:genetic-engineering:transgenic}{transgenic} for the human allele develop the disease when given the
\omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{antigen}. The rise: less early microbial exposure and altered
\omterm{def:b3:microbiomes:symbiosis}{microbiomes} (\omterm{def:b3:bacteriology:antibiotics}{antibiotics}, diet, caesarean birth) give fewer regulatory
T~cells and more \omterm{def:b3:innate-immunity:inflammation}{inflammation} --- tests: diabetes-prone mice raised
germ-free or on \omterm{def:b3:bacteriology:antibiotics}{antibiotics} develop diabetes more, and children of
immigrants acquire the incidence of the new country within a
generation.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b3:adaptive-immunity:1}
\textbf{1.} $6000\times 320 = 1.9\times 10^{6}$; $\times 3\times 10^{4}
= 5.8\times 10^{10}$.
\textbf{2.} $10^{11}$ cells against $5.8\times 10^{10}$ possible: a body
could hold every receptor about twice, but clones are unequal and most
sequences never happen to be made, so each body expresses a large but
incomplete sample --- and two people share only a small fraction of
their receptors.
\textbf{3.} $10^{11}/10^{5} = 10^{6}$ cells; in a node of $10^{8}$,
about $1000$.
\textbf{4.} $10^{9}/10^{5} = 10^{4}$ precursors --- a hundred times fewer;
the newborn also has immature follicles and \omterm{def:b3:innate-immunity:innate}{dendritic cells}, no
memory, and limited T~cell help, so responses are slow and small, and
maternal IgG bridges the gap.
\textbf{5.} It spans the V--D and D--J junctions, where nucleotides are
removed and added at random: its sequence is not encoded anywhere in
the genome. Sitting between the two chains at the centre of the
binding site, it is the loop most often in contact with the epitope.
\textbf{6.} Before \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{antigen}, diversity must be broad so that something
binds whatever comes; after \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{antigen}, effort is better spent varying
the few receptors already known to bind. A coarse search then a fine
one is far more efficient than either alone.
\textbf{7.} Seven days is $21$ divisions: $100\times 2^{21} = 2.1\times
10^{8}$ cells; \qty{30}{\%}, $6.3\times 10^{7}$ \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:response}{plasma cells}.
\textbf{8.} $6.3\times 10^{7}\times 2000\times \num{86400} = 1.1\times
10^{16}$ molecules a day; $\times 1.5\times 10^{5}\times 1.66\times
10^{-24}$ g $= \qty{2.7}{mg}$; in \qty{3}{L}, \qty{0.9}{mg/L} per day.
\textbf{9.} \qty{27}{mg} in \qty{3}{L}: \qty{9}{mg/L} $= \qty{0.009}{g/L}$,
a thousandth of the total IgG --- the \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:antibody}{antibody} to any one \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:lymphocytes}{antigen} is a
small fraction of the whole.
\textbf{10.} A hundredfold is $\log_{2}100 = 6.6$ half-lives, about
$140$ days. Years of \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:antibody}{antibody} come from long-lived \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:response}{plasma cells} in the
marrow, which secrete continuously, and from \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:response}{memory cells} re-stimulated
on re-exposure.
\textbf{11.} Nine divisions: $10^{5}\times 512 = 5\times 10^{7}$ cells at
day 3 --- a thousand times the primary's $5\times 10^{4}$ at day 3, and
a quarter of what the primary reached only at day 7.
\textbf{12.} The rearranged VDJ lies next to C$_{\mu}$, so the first
transcript is IgM; switching to C$_{\gamma}$ needs T~cell help and AID,
which take days. \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:response}{Memory cells} have already switched, so their progeny
make IgG at once.
\textbf{13.} $1000\times 10^{-3} = 1$ per division; $20$ over twenty.
\textbf{14.} One in a hundred; $10^{4}\times 0.01 = 100$ improved cells
per division.
\textbf{15.} $1.5^{n} = 1000$: $n \approx 17$; $17\times \qty{12}{h}
\approx 8.5$ days.
\textbf{16.} Half the daughters lose binding: without selection, $0.5^{20}
\approx 10^{-6}$ of the lineage would still bind after twenty
divisions. The light zone must remove the losers at every cycle so that
only functional and improved receptors go on.
\textbf{17.} The first dose leaves memory B~cells of raised affinity and
switched class; the second dose seeds new \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:germinal-centre}{germinal centres} with them,
and hypermutation and selection resume from a higher starting point,
so the antibodies that emerge are better still.
\textbf{18.} Some infected people eventually make antibodies against
conserved sites that the \omterm{def:b3:virology:virus}{virus} cannot change (broadly neutralising
antibodies), after years of maturation. The \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:germinal-centre}{germinal centre} can be
steered: a sequence of immunogens that first engages the right germline
precursors and then rewards binding to the conserved site could guide
maturation along that path in months rather than years.
\textbf{19.} $p_{c} = 1 - 1/15 = 93.3\,\%$. One dose: $0.933/0.93 =
100.4\,\%$ --- unattainable; two doses: $0.933/0.97 = 96.2\,\%$.
\textbf{20.} Immune $0.90\times 0.97 = 0.873$; $R_{\text{eff}} = 15\times
0.127 = 1.9$: measles circulates.
\textbf{21.} $60\,000\times (0.10 + 0.90\times 0.03) = 7600$ susceptibles
per birth cohort; after five years $38\,000$ --- a pool in which an
imported case finds enough susceptibles in its contacts to sustain
chains.
\textbf{22.} Among the susceptibles, $s_{\infty} - \ln s_{\infty}/1.9 =
1$ gives $s_{\infty} \approx 0.23$: about \qty{77}{\%} of them, some
$29\,000$ cases.
\textbf{23.} Immune $0.95\times 0.97 = 0.92$; $R_{\text{eff}} = 15\times
0.08 = 1.2$ --- still above one; the threshold needs \qty{96}{\%} with
two doses, and measles is the disease that punishes the last few per
cent.
\textbf{24.} Infants are protected only by the immunity of those around
them (and by maternal \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:antibody}{antibody} for a few months): if chains of
transmission cannot form, no case reaches them. At \qty{85}{\%} coverage
the immune fraction is $0.82$ and $R_{\text{eff}} = 2.6$: outbreaks
run, and the infants, unvaccinated and most vulnerable, are infected.
\textbf{25.} Repertoire about $6\times 10^{10}$; \qty{2.7}{mg} of IgG a
day from one response's \omterm{def:b3:adaptive-immunity:response}{plasma cells}; \qty{96}{\%} two-dose coverage.
\end{solution}
