\chapter{How Animals Grow Up}\label{ch:g2:animals-grow-up}

A kitten looks like a small cat from its very first day. But the
frog in the pond began as a fish-shaped tadpole with no \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}, and
the butterfly on the buddleia spent weeks as a crawling
\omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar}. Young \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} do not all resemble their parents ---
some must transform completely on the way to adulthood.

\section{From the egg or born alive}

\begin{definition}[Two ways to be born]\label{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}
Young \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} come into the world in two ways:
\begin{itemize}
  \item most hatch from an \emph{egg}\index{egg} laid by the
        mother: birds, fish, frogs, snails, insects;
  \item others are \emph{born alive}\index{born alive}, coming out
        of the mother's body already formed, and drink her
        \emph{milk}\index{milk}: cats, dogs, cows, rabbits --- and
        humans.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[In the henhouse and in the basket]\label{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:henhouse}
The hen lays \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} and sits on them to keep them warm; after three
weeks, chicks break the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{shell} and hatch. In the basket by the
stove, the cat's kittens were \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{born alive} the same night, and they
found her \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk} within the hour.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[An egg is not a stone]\label{rem:g2:animals-grow-up:egg}
Inside a sat-on \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg}, a chick is quietly being built, fed by the
yellow yolk. The \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg} is not waiting --- it is working. That is why
the hen keeps her \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} warm, and why an \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg} from the fridge, which
was never going to hatch anyway, must not be blamed on the cold.
\end{remark}

\section{Growing without transforming}

\begin{definition}[Growing like the parents]\label{def:g2:animals-grow-up:direct}
Many young \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} are \emph{miniatures of their
parents}\index{miniature of the parents}: the kitten is a small
cat, the foal a small horse, the chick a small hen. Growing up,
for them, means getting bigger and stronger --- the body's shape
stays the same.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Kitten to cat]\label{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:kitten}
The newborn kitten drinks \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{milk} with closed \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eyes}. At one month it
plays and pounces; at six months it hunts like a grown cat; at one
year it is an adult, perhaps with kittens of its own. Small cat,
bigger cat, cat: the same shape all along the way.
\end{example}

\section{Growing by transforming}

\begin{definition}[Metamorphosis]\label{def:g2:animals-grow-up:metamorphosis}
Some young \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} look nothing like their parents, and must
change their whole body shape to become adults. That great change
is called \emph{metamorphosis}\index{metamorphosis}. The tadpole
becomes a frog; the \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar} becomes a butterfly.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[The frog's road]\label{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}
From the frog's \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} in the pond hatch \emph{tadpoles}\index{tadpole}:
round-headed, long-tailed, legless, breathing in water like fish.
Then, week by week: back \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} appear, then front \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}; the tail
shrinks away; and one day a small frog climbs out onto the bank.
Same \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} --- rebuilt.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The butterfly's road]\label{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}
From the butterfly's \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg} hatches a \emph{caterpillar}\index{caterpillar},
which does little but eat \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} and grow. One day it wraps itself
in a case, the \emph{chrysalis}\index{chrysalis}, and stops moving.
Inside, the body is rebuilt; after some weeks the case splits, and
out climbs a butterfly, unfolding wet new wings. \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{Egg}, \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar},
\omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{chrysalis}, butterfly: four stages of one and the same \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Reading an animal's life cycle]\label{met:g2:animals-grow-up:read}
For any \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} you meet:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item ask how it is born: from an \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg}, or \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{born alive}?
  \item ask what the young looks like: a miniature of its parents,
        or a different shape?
  \item if it is a different shape, it will transform: look for the
        stages of its \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:metamorphosis}{metamorphosis};
  \item draw the cycle as a wheel, with an arrow from the adult
        back to the \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{remark}[Milk, or straight to work]\label{rem:g2:animals-grow-up:care}
Kittens are fed and washed by their mother for weeks; a newly
hatched tadpole gets no help at all and must feed itself from the
first day. Some young \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} are cared for, others start life
alone --- both roads lead to adulthood.
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:animals-grow-up:1}
Give the two ways young \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} come into the world, with one
example of each.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:animals-grow-up:2}
From an \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg}, or \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{born alive}: (a) a chick; (b) a kitten; (c) a
frog; (d) a rabbit; (e) a snail?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:animals-grow-up:3}
What does a kitten drink first? Which \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals} of this chapter
drink the same at \omterm{def:g2:born-grow-die:cycle}{birth}?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:animals-grow-up:4}
What is \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:metamorphosis}{metamorphosis}? Name the two famous examples of this
chapter.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:animals-grow-up:5}
Put the frog's stages in order: frog --- \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} --- tadpole with
\omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} --- tadpole.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:animals-grow-up:6}
Put the butterfly's stages in order: butterfly --- \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar} ---
\omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{egg} --- \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{chrysalis}. At which stage does the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} eat \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} and
grow the most?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:animals-grow-up:7}
Which young \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} is a miniature of its parents: the tadpole, or
the foal? What does growing up mean for that one?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g2:animals-grow-up:8}
Why does the hen sit on her \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs}? What is happening inside them,
by \cref{rem:g2:animals-grow-up:egg}?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g2:animals-grow-up:9}
In the pond, Zoe finds a small \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} with a round head, a long
tail, two back \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} and no front \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs}. Using the frog cycle
figure, say exactly where it is on its road, what it was before,
and what comes next.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star\star$]\label{exo:g2:animals-grow-up:10}
``The \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{caterpillar} died in its case, and a butterfly moved in.''
What really happened inside the \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}{chrysalis}? Answer with
\cref{def:g2:animals-grow-up:metamorphosis} and
\cref{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:butterfly}.
\end{exercise}
