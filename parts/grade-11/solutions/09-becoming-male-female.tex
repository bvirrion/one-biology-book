\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g11:becoming-male-female} --- Becoming Male or Female}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:1}
The father's: every egg carries an X, while half the sperm carry an X
and half a Y, so XX and XY are equally likely.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:2}
Two undifferentiated gonads, each with two ducts beside it: a
Wolffian duct (future male tract) and a Müllerian duct (future female
tract). Identical in XX and XY embryos.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:3}
On the Y \omterm{def:g11:cell-cycle-mitosis:chromosome}{chromosome}; expressed in the undifferentiated gonad around
week 7, its \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} switches on the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes} that make the gonad a
testis.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:4}
\omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:hormones}{Testosterone} maintains and develops the Wolffian ducts into the male
tract; anti-Müllerian hormone makes the Müllerian ducts regress.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:5}
A brain centre releases pulses of a hormone that makes the pituitary
secrete LH and FSH, which activate the gonads. Secondary sexual
characteristics: the changes produced by the sex hormones --- body
hair, voice, breasts, muscle and fat distribution, the growth spurt,
growth of the reproductive organs.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:6}
It developed a female tract, despite its Y \omterm{def:g11:cell-cycle-mitosis:chromosome}{chromosome}: the male tract
requires the testis's hormones, and the female tract forms without any
gonad.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:7}
\omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:hormones}{Testosterone} builds the male ducts but does not remove the female
ones; the testis must secrete a second substance for that --- AMH.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:8}
Testes (Sry acts); Wolffian ducts kept, Müllerian regressed; male
external anatomy. Sterile: sperm production requires \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes} of the Y
that the transgene does not supply.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:9}
Oestrogens: 50\% at about 11, plateau at about 14--15. \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:hormones}{Testosterone}:
50\% at about 13, plateau at about 16--17.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:10}
The bones stop growing when the sex hormone reaches its adult level;
oestrogens reach it about two years before testosterone does, so
girls' growth plates close earlier.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:11}
No testis: the gonad becomes an ovary-like gonad; no testosterone or
AMH: Müllerian ducts develop, Wolffian regress, female external
anatomy at birth. At \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:puberty}{puberty} the gonads, lacking normal eggs, secrete
little; without hormone treatment secondary characteristics develop
poorly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:12}
Testes (\omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:sry}{SRY} works). Wolffian ducts regress (no response to
testosterone); Müllerian ducts regress (AMH acts): no internal tract
of either kind. External anatomy female (its male form needs
testosterone action). At \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:puberty}{puberty}, testosterone converted into
oestrogens produces breasts and a female build, without body hair.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:13}
Once a gonad has become a testis or an ovary, its hormones could shape
the ducts and the body exactly as in mammals; what differs is the
first step --- a temperature-sensitive process replaces \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:sry}{SRY} as the
switch that decides the gonad.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:14}
The X carries hundreds of \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes} needed by every \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell}, so no embryo
survives without one; the Y carries almost nothing except the
testis-determining and sperm-related \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes}, so XX individuals lack
nothing they need. A Y passes only to sons because daughters receive
the father's X.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:becoming-male-female:15}
It means that the female tract develops when no testicular hormone
intervenes --- a fact about which signals are needed, not a ranking.
It reflects a common embryonic plan on which one lineage of \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells},
the testis, imposes an alternative by two hormones: the two sexes are
two outcomes of one programme, differing by a switch.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g11:becoming-male-female:1}
\textbf{1.} The ovary is not needed for the female tract, which forms
without any gonad; the testis is needed for the male tract and for the
removal of the female ducts.

\textbf{2.} After day 22 the ducts have already started to
differentiate under the gonad's influence; the experiment must act
before the decision.

\textbf{3.} The testis's hormones, carried by the blood to the ducts:
the graft, far from the ducts, still masculinises them.

\textbf{4.} \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:hormones}{Testosterone} builds the male ducts; it does not remove the
female ones.

\textbf{5.} A second testicular substance must make the Müllerian
ducts regress; it could be confirmed by extracting it from testes and
implanting it alone, expecting female ducts to disappear and male ones
not to form --- which is what AMH does.

\textbf{6.} On the operated side, no testosterone or AMH: female duct,
no male duct. On the intact side: male duct, female duct regressed.

\textbf{7.} On the graft's side, male duct and no female duct; on the
other side, female duct only.

\textbf{8.} On the crystal's side, both ducts present; on the other
side, the female duct only.

\textbf{9.} Each foetus serves as its own control: the difference
between the two sides can only be due to the local presence of the
testis, the testosterone or their absence.

\textbf{10.} Its ovaries are removed or overridden and the grafted
testis, from another animal, makes no sperm in a foreign host; the
tract is male but no gametes are produced.

\textbf{11.} \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:sry}{SRY} present: testis; testosterone and AMH: male tract,
male anatomy; at \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:puberty}{puberty}, testosterone: male characteristics. Sterile
(no Y \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes} for sperm).

\textbf{12.} No working \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:sry}{SRY}: no testis; no hormones: female tract and
anatomy; poorly developed gonads.

\textbf{13.} Testes; Wolffian ducts developed (testosterone acts);
Müllerian ducts persist too (no response to AMH): a male with, in
addition, a uterus and oviducts; male anatomy and \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:puberty}{puberty}.

\textbf{14.} Only the third: functional testes and male ducts,
although the persisting uterus may hinder the descent of the testes.
The first lacks sperm \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes}; the second has no functional gonads.

\textbf{15.} Chromosomal sex predicts anatomical sex only through the
steps between: a working \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:sry}{SRY}, working hormones, working receptors.
Change any step and the two levels separate.

\textbf{16.} Girls: 20\% at 10, spurt from about 11, stop at about 15.
Boys: 20\% at about 12.5, spurt from about 13.5, stop at about 17.

\textbf{17.} Girls about 4 years, boys about 3.5 --- but boys start
from a taller base, having grown at the childhood rate two years
longer.

\textbf{18.} Boys about $3.5 \times 8 = \qty{28}{cm}$, girls $4 \times
7 = \qty{28}{cm}$: the spurts are similar; the adult difference comes
mostly from the two extra years of childhood growth before the boys'
spurt.

\textbf{19.} An early rise brings the growth plates to closure years
early: the child grows fast now but stops at a short adult height.
Delaying the hormones prolongs childhood growth.

\textbf{20.} \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:hormones}{Testosterone}, which builds the male ducts, and
anti-Müllerian hormone, which removes the female ones; both made by
the testis, an organ switched on by the single \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} \omterm{prop:g11:becoming-male-female:sry}{SRY}.
\end{solution}
