\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g7:oxygen-in-the-water} --- Oxygen in the Water}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:1}
About \qty{10}{mg} per litre --- roughly thirty times less
than a litre of air carries.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:2}
The air above, dissolving in at the surface --- fastest
where the water is stirred, splashed and foamed; and the
\omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers} below, releasing \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} by day --- fastest in
bright \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{light} with abundant green growth.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:3}
The \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals}' \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{respiration}, the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants}' own \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{respiration} at
night, and the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers}' --- which grows huge when dead
matter is plentiful.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:4}
Warm water holds less \omterm{def:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:dissolved}{dissolved oxygen} (the ceiling drops)
while every respirer's engine runs faster (the demand
rises): less supply, more need, at once.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:5}
By day the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}' manufacture adds \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} faster than
\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{respiration} drains it --- the curve climbs to a late
afternoon peak. All night every respirer draws on the stock
with no manufacture at all --- the curve slides to its
lowest just before sunrise.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:6}
Rich: trout (or trout fry) and mayfly larvae under the
stones. Poor: only tolerant \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} --- carp, pond snails,
and snorkel-bearing larvae that fetch air from the surface.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:7}
The night's \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{respiration} --- \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish}, \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants} and mud
\omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers} together --- drains the pond to its minimum
just before sunrise, so overloaded ponds suffocate their
\omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} at dawn. Remedies: from supplier 1, stir the surface
--- run a fountain or cascade through the night; from
supplier 2, thin the overgrowth and mud so night
\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{respiration} shrinks (and daylight production reaches the
water, not just the surface mat).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:8}
Waste in --- the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers} feast and multiply --- their
massed \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{respiration} drains the \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} --- the demanding
\omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} vanish and the tolerant take over. Downstream the
waste is finally consumed, the riffles re-aerate the water,
and the demanding \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} return: the drain closed, the
suppliers caught up.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:9}
A probe records one instant; the inhabitants record
history. A demanding \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} lives at a station only if the
water has stayed good through its whole residence ---
including the dawns and the bad weeks no single visit
catches. The census is a season's minimum, written in
\omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:10}
The pea-soup green is a huge population of green single
\omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}: by day they produce, but every warm night the whole
soup respires --- and the hot still week lowers the ceiling
and stops the surface refilling. The dawn minimum heads for
zero: \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} kill weather. Run the fountain hardest through
the night and small hours --- daylight can look after
itself.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:11}
Trout are demanding \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species}: cold rivers hold the high
ceiling they need; weirs and splashing raceways keep the
refilling constant against the crowded \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fishes}' drain. A
warm still lake fails on ceiling and refilling at once ---
carp country, not trout country.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:12}
Currency: every underwater respirer draws on the one
\omterm{def:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:dissolved}{dissolved} stock --- who thrives where is priced in \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen}
(\cref{prop:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:map}). Money supply:
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{temperature} sets how much the water can hold --- cold
raises the ceiling, warmth lowers it
(\cref{prop:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:drains}). Biggest
spenders: given dead matter, the \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers}' massed
\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{respiration} outdrains everyone --- the creamery pipe's
lesson (\cref{ex:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:waste}).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g7:oxygen-in-the-water:1}
\textbf{1.} K1 is cold --- high ceiling --- and churned by
the weir --- surface refilling at full speed: near-ceiling
readings. K2 is warm --- lowered ceiling --- and unstirred:
a modest reading is all its suppliers can hold.

\textbf{2.} K1's demanding \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} certify rich, steady
\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen}; K2's tolerant cast --- carp, snails, snorkelers ---
marks a water that runs poor, at least in its bad hours.

\textbf{3.} The \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers}' \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{respiration}: the creamery's
organic-rich outflow feeds a massed population of them, and
their feasting drains the stock far below what \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{temperature}
alone would explain.

\textbf{4.} Two kilometres of consumption have used up the
waste --- the feast is over and the spenders have thinned
--- while the stony riffle has stirred \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} back in: both
suppliers restored, the drain closed, and the census
certifies the recovery.

\textbf{5.} A daily wave: dawn trough at \qty{4}{mg/L},
afternoon crest at \qty{9}{mg/L}. The traders: daylight
manufacture (adding by day) and round-the-clock \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{respiration}
(draining alone all night).

\textbf{6.} Winter cold raises the ceiling, so the water
holds more; and cold slows every respirer --- \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish}, \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants},
mud \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers} --- so the drains shrink. Steadier, too,
because the \omterm{def:g5:food-webs:roles}{producers}' daily wave is smaller.

\textbf{7.} K3 first (already drained, and heat speeds its
\omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers}); K2 second (low ceiling, still water, dawn
troughs); K4 third (recovering but downstream of trouble);
K1 last (cold and churned --- the weir keeps refilling
whatever the weather).

\textbf{8.} Before sunrise is the daily minimum: readings
then record the worst the river's life must survive. An
afternoon schedule would file the year under its best
moments.

\textbf{9.} The probe recovers within weeks --- the drain
closes as the food for \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{decomposers} stops. The census
recovers more slowly and in order: first the tolerant cast
thins, then drifting and flying colonists
(\cref{ch:g6:how-plants-spread}'s logic, \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal} version)
\omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{re-seed} the demanding \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} --- mayfly larvae first from
upstream, trout last.

\textbf{10.} The shade also keeps the reach cool --- and
cold is the ceiling. Felling would add daylight production
but lower the ceiling and speed every drain in the warmed
water; on a slow reach the loss likely outweighs the gain.
Answer: keep the trees, stir the water instead.

\textbf{11.} The census: mayfly larvae cannot fake a good
season --- they lived every dawn of it. The mediocre
afternoon reading is one instant, perhaps an off day; the
larvae are the season's sworn record.

\textbf{12.} ``Keep the river cold, stirred and unloaded,
and the \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} --- and everything that breathes it ---
looks after itself.''
\end{solution}
