\chapter{Sexual Reproduction}\label{ch:g8:sexual-reproduction}

Frog song in May, cherry blossom in April, the stag's autumn
bellow, a sunflower turning its crowded face to the bees:
across the living world, enormous \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} is spent on one
project --- the next generation. Earlier years collected the
pieces (\cref{ch:g4:animal-reproduction},
\cref{ch:g5:flower-to-fruit}); this year assembles them into
one theory, and asks the sharper question: why \emph{two}
parents at all?

\section{One mechanism across the living world}

\begin{definition}[Gametes]\label{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}
The reproductive \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} of
\cref{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual} bear the general
name \emph{gametes}\index{gamete}: the \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{male} gamete (\omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{sperm
cell} in \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals}, the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen} grain's \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} in flowering
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plants}) and the \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{female} gamete (the \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg cell}, in ovary or
ovule). Gametes are single \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}
(\cref{ch:g5:first-look-at-cells}), and each is half a
beginning: alone, a gamete dies; joined, two make a life.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The invariant core]\label{prop:g8:sexual-reproduction:core}
Wherever \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{sexual reproduction} runs --- \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} or fir tree,
snail or human --- the core is invariant:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item two parents produce \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes}, \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{male} and \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{female};
  \item \emph{\omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}}: one \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{male} \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gamete} joins one
        \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{female} \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gamete}, forming a single new \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} --- the
        \emph{\omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{egg cell} fertilized}, or
        \emph{zygote}\index{zygote};
  \item the \omterm{prop:g8:sexual-reproduction:core}{zygote} divides and divides
        (\cref{prop:g5:first-look-at-cells:growth}),
        building a new individual that carries something
        from \emph{both} parents
        (\cref{rem:g4:animal-reproduction:mix}).
\end{enumerate}
Around this core, everything else is variation: where the
\omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes} meet, how many, who cares for the young.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The variations, sorted]\label{ex:g8:sexual-reproduction:variations}
The old collection files neatly under the core. External
against internal \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}
(\cref{prop:g4:animal-reproduction:where}): the meeting
place. \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:ovivivi}{Oviparous} against \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:ovivivi}{viviparous}
(\cref{def:g4:animal-reproduction:ovivivi}): the \omterm{prop:g8:sexual-reproduction:core}{zygote}'s
lodging. Codfish millions against elephant singletons
(\cref{prop:g4:animal-reproduction:strategies}): the
numbers ledger. \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{Pollination} by bee or wind
(\cref{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}): the \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{male} \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gamete}'s
travel service. One mechanism, a catalogue of settings.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Plants run the same core]\label{rem:g8:sexual-reproduction:plants}
Set \cref{ch:g5:flower-to-fruit} beside
\cref{ch:g4:animal-reproduction} and the match is exact:
\omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{stamens} and \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistil} produce the \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes}, the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen} tube is
internal \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}'s corridor, the \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed} lodges the
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant}'s \omterm{def:g5:human-reproduction:pregnancy}{embryo} with its packed lunch
(\cref{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}). Only \omterm{def:g4:plants-without-seeds:vegetative}{vegetative
reproduction} (\cref{def:g4:plants-without-seeds:vegetative})
stands apart --- one parent, no \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes}, exact copies: the
contrast that makes the question of the next section
sharp.
\end{remark}

\section{Why two parents?}

\begin{proposition}[Sexual reproduction shuffles]\label{prop:g8:sexual-reproduction:shuffle}
The runner's strawberry copies
(\cref{rem:g4:plants-without-seeds:copy}) show what one
parent yields: sameness. Two parents yield the opposite.
Because every \omterm{prop:g8:sexual-reproduction:core}{zygote} combines one \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gamete} from each parent
--- and, as a later chapter will show, no two \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes} even
of one parent carry quite the same inheritance --- \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{sexual
reproduction} deals every offspring a \emph{new
combination}. Siblings differ; no two \omterm{prop:g2:from-seed-to-plant:cycle}{seedlings} of one pod
match; the variety within every \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species}
(\cref{rem:g5:biodiversity:scales}) is largely this
shuffle's work.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The orchard's two bets]\label{ex:g8:sexual-reproduction:bets}
The orchard of \cref{rem:g4:plants-without-seeds:copy}
plays both games knowingly. Grafts and \omterm{met:g4:plants-without-seeds:cutting}{cuttings}: one
parent, exact copies of the excellent tree --- and one
shared weakness, so a blight that fells one can fell all.
Pips: two parents, every \omterm{prop:g2:from-seed-to-plant:cycle}{seedling} a fresh combination ---
most unremarkable, some worse, a rare one better than
either parent, and no two alike for a blight to sweep.
Copying banks the present; shuffling explores the future.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Variety is the species' reserve]\label{rem:g8:sexual-reproduction:reserve}
Why the shuffle matters beyond the orchard:
\cref{prop:g5:biodiversity:web} showed webs absorbing
shocks through variety of \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species}; within one \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species},
variety of individuals plays the same part. When
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} turn --- a new disease, a harsher winter --- a
varied \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} is likelier to hold someone suited to the
new terms. What that hints at for the history of life,
\cref{ch:g9:how-species-change} will make explicit; note
here only that \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{sexual reproduction} is what keeps the
reserve stocked.
\end{remark}

\section{Reading any species' reproduction}

\begin{method}[The reproduction dossier]\label{met:g8:sexual-reproduction:dossier}
For any \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species}, compile:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes}: who produces which, where;
  \item meeting: external or internal \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}, and
        the travel service (water, \omterm{prop:g4:animal-reproduction:where}{mating}, pollinator,
        wind);
  \item lodging: \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} laid or young carried; with what
        provisions;
  \item numbers and care: the strategy line's position
        (\cref{prop:g4:animal-reproduction:strategies});
  \item season: when, and timed by what --- next
        chapter's question.
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Two dossiers side by side]\label{ex:g8:sexual-reproduction:dossiers}
The stickleback (\cref{exo:g4:animal-reproduction:10}):
\omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes} shed in water, external \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}, \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:born}{eggs} in a
built nest, dozens, guarded by the father, spring.
The cherry: \omterm{def:g8:sexual-reproduction:gametes}{gametes} in blossom, internal \omterm{def:g5:flower-to-fruit:steps}{fertilization}
down the \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen} tube, \omterm{def:g5:human-reproduction:pregnancy}{embryos} lodged in \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{seeds} inside
\omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruit}, thousands, provisioned then abandoned to the
\omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds}' delivery service
(\cref{exo:g6:how-plants-spread:12}), timed to April.
Same form, any \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species}: the dossier is the theory in
use.
\end{example}

\section{Problem: The Breeding Program}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- a plant breeder's
year, run on the theory}]\label{pb:g8:sexual-reproduction:1}
A breeder wants a new apple: crisp like variety A,
blight-resistant like variety B. Her tools are exactly
this chapter's.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The cross.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item She takes \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pollen} from A's \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{stamens} with a brush
        and touches it to B's \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{pistils}, then bags the
        \omterm{def:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:parts}{flowers} against bees. Name each step's role in
        the core --- and why the bags matter
        (\cref{met:g5:flower-to-fruit:diagnose} used the
        same brush).
  \item What grows in B's \omterm{prop:g3:flowers-fruits-seeds:transform}{fruit} after her visit, and
        what two inheritances does each pip combine?
  \item She sows three hundred pips. Why does she expect
        three hundred \emph{different} trees, by
        \cref{prop:g8:sexual-reproduction:shuffle}?
  \item Most \omterm{prop:g2:from-seed-to-plant:cycle}{seedlings} will be unremarkable, a few
        worse than either parent, perhaps one just
        right. Which example of the chapter predicted
        exactly this spread?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Fixing the winner.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Year eight: one tree bears crisp,
        blight-resistant apples. If she sows its pips,
        what happens to the prized combination --- and
        why?
  \item Instead she multiplies the winner by grafting
        --- which reproduction is that, and what does
        it guarantee?
  \item Her whole future orchard will be that one
        tree's copies. Name the risk she has accepted,
        and the chapter's phrase for it.
  \item Propose the standard insurance: what should
        her orchard also keep, and why does the
        strategy echo
        \cref{rem:g8:sexual-reproduction:reserve}?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The theory,
audited.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Her program used both of the orchard's bets in
        sequence. Say which phase used which, and what
        each bought her.
  \item A colleague breeds sticklebacks the same way
        --- choosing parents, raising the shuffled
        young, keeping the best. List the dossier
        entries (\cref{met:g8:sexual-reproduction:dossier})
        where \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} practice differs from apple
        practice, and the core steps where it cannot
        differ.
  \item The breeder says: ``sex proposes, selection
        disposes --- I only choose among what the
        shuffle deals.'' Connect her slogan to the
        variety-reserve of
        \cref{rem:g8:sexual-reproduction:reserve}.
  \item One sentence to close: what did the program
        need \omterm{def:g4:animal-reproduction:sexual}{sexual reproduction} \emph{for}, and what
        did it need \omterm{def:g4:plants-without-seeds:vegetative}{vegetative reproduction} for?
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
