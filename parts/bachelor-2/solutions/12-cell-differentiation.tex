\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b2:cell-differentiation} --- Cell Differentiation: The Skeletal Muscle Cell}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:1}
\omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{Differentiation}: the stable expression of one cell type's genes and
functions. \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{Determination}: commitment to a fate before it is
expressed. \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{Potency}: the range of fates still open. Zygote:
totipotent; inner-cell-mass cell: pluripotent; satellite cell:
unipotent (muscle only) --- a \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{stem cell} nonetheless; \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{muscle fibre}:
differentiated, post-mitotic, no \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{potency}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:2}
Dermomyotome cell (Pax3) $\to$ induced \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{myoblast} (\omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD}, Myf5),
dividing while FGF lasts $\to$ cycle exit, myogenin $\to$ alignment
and fusion into a \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{myotube} $\to$ muscle genes on, \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:fibre}{myofibrils}
assembled (MRF4) $\to$ mature fibre with peripheral nuclei, plus
quiescent satellite cells (Pax7).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:3}
That the nucleus of a differentiated cell keeps every gene needed
to make a whole animal, and that egg cytoplasm can reset its
programme: \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{differentiation} is reversible and not a loss of DNA. It
did not show that every differentiated nucleus can be reset (most
transfers failed), nor how the reset works, nor that the
differentiated cell itself could change fate in place.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:4}
Z discs bounding the \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:fibre}{sarcomere}; thin actin filaments anchored to
them; thick myosin filaments centred on the M line; the A band
(thick filaments) and I band (thin only). On shortening the thin
filaments slide toward the M line and the Z discs approach each
other: the I band and the H zone narrow, the A band --- the thick
filament length --- does not change, and neither filament shortens.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:5}
$0.2/2.5\times 10^{-6} = \num{80000}$ \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:fibre}{sarcomeres}; $0.2/25\times 10^{-6}
= \num{8000}$ nuclei; one \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{myoblast} per nucleus, so about \num{8000}
\omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{myoblasts} fused (satellite cells add more later).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:6}
FGF induces Id, which binds the partner protein \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD} needs to form a
DNA-binding dimer; \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD} is present but idle, and the cells keep
dividing. When FGF is withdrawn Id decays, \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD} dimerises, myogenin
is switched on, and the cells exit the cycle and fuse. \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{Myoblasts}
making Id constitutively never fuse: they divide indefinitely and
form no muscle.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:7}
Condition $0.4m = m^{2}/(1 + m^{2}) + 0.02$. At $m = 2.1$: left
$0.84$, right $4.41/5.41 + 0.02 = 0.835$: a steady state. Low
state: for small $m$, $0.4m \approx m^{2} + 0.02$, so $m \approx
0.055$ (left $0.022$, right $0.023$). Raised to 1, which is above
the threshold near $0.41$, the cell climbs to $2.1$ and stays: it is
committed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:8}
With $k = 0.6$: at $m = 1$, degradation $0.6$ exceeds synthesis
$0.52$; at $m = 0.5$, $0.30 > 0.22$; at $m = 2$, $1.2 > 0.82$: the
line lies above the curve everywhere beyond the low state, which is
the only steady state. A cell whose \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD} is degraded too fast
cannot hold the on state: it relapses into the undifferentiated
state whatever the induction --- no muscle.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:9}
(a) No \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{myoblasts}, no skeletal muscle: the two factors are redundant
for \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{determination}, so both must go. (b) \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{Myoblasts} form and align
but never fuse or make muscle proteins: myogenin is required for
\omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{differentiation}. (c) Nearly normal, because Myf5 substitutes for
\omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD} in \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{determination}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:10}
In a fibroblast the muscle genes lie in chromatin that \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD} can
open; in a liver cell they may be packed in heterochromatin, or the
liver's own \omterm{prop:b2:cell-differentiation:transcriptional}{master regulators} may repress them, so the cell is not
competent. Test: treat liver cells with a drug that loosens
chromatin (a histone deacetylase inhibitor) before adding \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD}, or
add \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD} together with a factor that displaces the liver
programme, and score myosin expression.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:11}
Sustained low-frequency firing raises intracellular calcium for
long periods; calcium-activated pathways switch on the genes of
slow myosin, mitochondrial biogenesis, myoglobin and capillary
growth, and switch off the fast isoforms. The fibre's master
programme (\omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD} state, \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:fibre}{sarcomere} architecture) is untouched:
training changes which of the muscle genes are expressed, not the
identity of the cell, and no signal from a nerve can reopen the
cartilage programme that was closed at \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{determination}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:cell-differentiation:12}
The on state of a self-activating factor is a stable point of a
feedback loop, kept by continuous synthesis, not by any change in
the genes; chromatin marks add a second layer that keeps silenced
genes closed. Resetting therefore requires driving the circuit
below its threshold and reopening the chromatin at once, which egg
cytoplasm or four factors can do only in a small fraction of
cells: possible, because nothing is lost, but inefficient, because
two independent locks must be picked together.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:cell-differentiation:1}
\textbf{1.} $0.2/2.5\times 10^{-6} = \num{80000}$ \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:fibre}{sarcomeres};
$0.2/25\times 10^{-6} = \num{8000}$ nuclei.
\textbf{2.} About \num{8000} per fibre; $4\times 10^{9}$ for the
muscle.
\textbf{3.} $\pi(30\times 10^{-6})^{2}\times 0.2 = \qty{5.7e-10}{m^3}$
per fibre; $2.8\times 10^{-4}$ \unit{m^3}, \qty{0.28}{L}, for the
muscle.
\textbf{4.} $5.7\times 10^{-10}/8000 = \qty{7e-14}{m^3} =
\qty{70000}{\micro m^3}$: 35 ordinary cells' worth per nucleus.
\textbf{5.} The interior is packed with \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:fibre}{myofibrils} that must run
uninterrupted from end to end; nuclei at the edge leave the lattice
continuous and sit near the membrane and its signals.
\textbf{6.} $4\times 10^{9}/2000 = 2\times 10^{6}$: 21 doublings, 10.5
days.
\textbf{7.} $0.4m = m^{2}/(1 + m^{2}) + 0.02$. At $m = 0.055$: left
$0.022$, right $0.003 + 0.02 = 0.023$.
\textbf{8.} At $m = 2.1$: left $0.84$, right $0.815 + 0.02 = 0.835$.
\textbf{9.} At $m = 0.41$: left $0.164$, right $0.144 + 0.02 = 0.164$.
Just below it synthesis is less than degradation, so $m$ falls
toward $0.055$; just above, synthesis exceeds degradation and $m$
rises toward $2.1$: any displacement grows --- unstable.
\textbf{10.} $0.6 > 0.41$: the first cell goes to the on state and
differentiates; $0.3 < 0.41$: the second relaxes to $0.055$ and
does not.
\textbf{11.} With $b = 0.4$ the synthesis curve starts at $0.4$ and
stays above the line $0.4m$ until $m \approx 3.3$: the low crossing
is gone. The cell differentiates spontaneously, without induction.
\textbf{12.} Division halves $m$; the daughters inherit $2.1/2 =
1.05$, above the threshold $0.41$, and climb back to $2.1$: the state
is re-established in each daughter. Commitment survives as long as
the halved level stays above the threshold.
\textbf{13.} $4$ per mm $\times$ \qty{200}{mm} $= 800$ satellite cells
per fibre; \qty{10}{\%} of \num{8000} nuclei, \num{800}, must be
replaced.
\textbf{14.} The injured segment holds $80$ satellite cells; $800/80 =
10$, so 4 doublings ($\times 16$, giving \num{1280}: 800 fuse, 480
remain to restore the pool) --- \qty{72}{h}, three days.
\textbf{15.} Three days of division against two to three weeks
observed: the rest is clearance of debris by macrophages, the
activation lag, fusion, \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:fibre}{myofibril} assembly, reinnervation and
maturation of the new segment.
\textbf{16.} \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD} (and myogenin) must switch the muscle genes back on
in the activated cells; division and \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{differentiation} are exclusive
--- Id must fall and the cycle stop before myogenin can act and
fusion occur.
\textbf{17.} $0.95^{n} = 0.5$: $n = 13.5$, about fourteen injuries.
\textbf{18.} $800\times 0.95^{20} = 800\times 0.36 \approx 290$.
\textbf{19.} Each contraction tears fibres, each tear calls on the
pool, and the pool shrinks by a few percent each time; after years it
is exhausted, the torn fibres are not rebuilt, and fibroblasts fill
the gaps with collagen.
\textbf{20.} $(80/60)^{2} = 1.78$; nuclei $8000\times 1.78 = \num{14200}$:
about \num{6200} extra from satellite cells.
\textbf{21.} $0.28\times 1.78 = \qty{0.5}{L}$.
\textbf{22.} Changed: the myosin isoform genes, mitochondrial and
capillary density, myoglobin. Unchanged: the muscle identity (\omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:myogenesis}{MyoD}
state), the \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:fibre}{sarcomere} architecture, the nuclei and their genome.
\textbf{23.} Fibres are post-mitotic and cannot multiply; the only
routes are thicker fibres and more nuclei per fibre from satellite
cells.
\textbf{24.} Training: limited thickening, since the fibre cannot add
nuclei; injury: no repair --- the destroyed segments are replaced by
fibrous tissue, as in dystrophy.
\textbf{25.} \num{8000} nuclei per fibre; threshold $m \approx 0.41$;
about three days of satellite-cell division for a repair.
\end{solution}
