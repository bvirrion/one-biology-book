\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g5:healthy-choices} --- Healthy Choices}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:1}
\omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{Varied food} and water: fuel and building material for every
\omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}, delivered by the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}. Movement: \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones}, \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart}
and \omterm{def:g4:breathing:organs}{lungs}, which strengthen with use. \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{Sleep}: the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}'s
filing and the body's repairs. Cleanliness --- air, hands,
teeth: the body's defenses against \omterm{def:g1:healthy-habits:germs}{germs} and dirt.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:2}
About ten hours. The \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} files the day (and dreams); the
body repairs and grows.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:3}
A day has fixed hours, so hours on screens come out of other
activities. The usual victims: outdoor movement and \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:4}
Because a bright screen tells the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} ``still daytime'',
delaying \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep} beyond the hour itself --- the evening screen
takes its own hour and part of the night after it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:5}
Tobacco smoke: the \omterm{def:g4:breathing:organs}{lungs}' delicate air pockets first, and
the \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} it strains. Alcohol: the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} --- the commander
--- within minutes, and a young, still-building \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} most
of all.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:6}
The body, grown used to a substance, protests without it ---
so the user is held. Starting is a single easy choice;
stopping is a fight repeated for years: the trap's entrance
is wide and its exit narrow, which prices ``never'' far
below ``later''.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:7}
A calm decision made in advance is easier to keep than one
improvised under friendly pressure --- the moment of the
offer is the worst moment to start thinking.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:8}
Open answer. Typical report: falling asleep faster, easier
waking, better mornings in the screens-off week.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:9}
Same bedtime, same meals, same day's activities both weeks
--- the one change: screens off an hour before bed in week
two only. Measure: time to fall asleep (estimated), how
waking feels, perhaps the morning's mood, noted daily.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:10}
All the following ones. \omterm{prop:g5:healthy-choices:substances}{Addiction} means the first cigarette
is the entrance to the trap: what is offered is not one
smoke but the beginning of the body's demand for the next
--- the easy entrance to the hard exit.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:healthy-choices:11}
The gas exchange chapter explains the clean-air line and the
smoke warning: \omterm{def:g4:breathing:movements}{breathing} exists to load oxygen through
delicate surfaces smoke dirties. The \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{racing-heart} example
explains the movement line: \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} and \omterm{def:g4:breathing:organs}{lungs} are trainable ---
effort is their exercise. The brain-protection proposition
explains the \omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep} line and the alcohol warning: the
commander needs its filing hours, and substances reaching it
blur the very loop that runs everything else.
\end{solution}
