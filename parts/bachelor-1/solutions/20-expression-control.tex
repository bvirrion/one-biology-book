\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b1:expression-control} --- Control of Gene Expression}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:1}
\emph{lacI} (\omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{repressor} \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{gene}), promoter, operator, \emph{lacZ},
\emph{lacY}, \emph{lacA}, and the CAP site. No lactose: off (\omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{repressor}
bound). Lactose, glucose present: barely on (\omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{repressor} off, but no
cAMP--CAP). Lactose, no glucose: fully on. Neither sugar: off.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:2}
A product acting in trans is a diffusible molecule that can act on any
copy of its target in the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell}: the \omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{repressor}. A site acting in cis is
a \omterm{def:b1:nucleic-acids:chain}{DNA} sequence that affects only the \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{genes} physically linked to it:
the operator.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:3}
Lag about \qty{36}{min} (from \qty{2}{h} to \qty{2.6}{h}); doubling
\qty{20}{min} on glucose, \qty{30}{min} on lactose.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:4}
\omterm{def:b1:expression-control:enhancer}{Enhancer}: a regulatory sequence, often distant, that raises a \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{gene}'s
transcription when factors bind it. \omterm{def:b1:expression-control:enhancer}{Transcription factor}: a \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein}
with a DNA-binding domain and an activation or repression domain that
regulates transcription. Housekeeping \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{gene}: one expressed in every
\omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} at a steady level.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:5}
$I^-$: constitutive. $O^c$: constitutive. $I^s$: uninducible.
$I^-/I^+$: inducible (the $I^+$ \omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{repressor} acts in trans). $I^s/I^+$:
uninducible ($I^s$ dominant). $I^+\,O^c\,Z^- / I^+\,O^+\,Z^+$: inducible
--- the only working $Z$ is behind a normal operator.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:6}
Lac: the sugar (inducer) binds the \omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{repressor} and \emph{releases} it
from the operator --- the \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzymes} are made when their substrate is
present. Trp: the \omterm{def:b1:proteins:aminoacid}{amino acid} (corepressor) binds the \omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{repressor} and
\emph{enables} it to bind --- the \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzymes} are made when their product
is absent. Catabolic pathways are switched on by their input,
anabolic ones off by their output.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:7}
On glucose: normal growth (glucose needs no CAP). On lactose alone:
almost no growth --- without cAMP, CAP cannot activate the lac
promoter, so the \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme} stays at a few percent of induced levels even
with the \omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{repressor} released. On both: growth on glucose, then a stop;
no second phase. \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{Enzyme} low in every case.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:8}
Attenuation needs the \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:ribosome}{ribosome} to be translating the messenger while
the polymerase is still transcribing it, so that the \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:ribosome}{ribosome}'s
position shapes the \omterm{def:b1:nucleic-acids:chain}{RNA} behind the polymerase. In eukaryotes
transcription is in the nucleus and translation in the \omterm{def:b1:eukaryotic-cell:organelle}{cytosol}, and
the message is not translated until it has been processed and
exported.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:9}
\omterm{def:b1:expression-control:enhancer}{Enhancers} act at a distance, in either orientation and on either
side, so they cannot work by being read or by positioning the
polymerase directly; they work by binding factors that contact the
promoter through a loop of \omterm{def:b1:nucleic-acids:chain}{DNA}, for which distance and orientation
matter little.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:10}
$5000\times 4096\times 4 = \num{8.2e7}$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}: \qty{0.8}{\%} of the
budget. Growth rate lower by \qty{0.8}{\%}: per generation the mutant's
share falls by the factor $2^{-0.008} = 0.9945$. From $1:1$ to
$1:99$: $0.9945^n = 1/99$, $n = \ln 99/0.00555 = 830$ generations ---
a few weeks of continuous culture.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:11}
Each \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} type carries a specific set of \omterm{def:b1:expression-control:enhancer}{transcription factors}, which
bind the \omterm{def:b1:expression-control:enhancer}{enhancers} of its \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{genes} and recruit acetylases that open
their chromatin, while the \omterm{def:b1:genomes:gene}{genes} of other types are methylated and
packed into heterochromatin that no factor can reach. The factors
maintain one another's expression (a network with stable states), and
the chromatin marks are copied at replication, so daughters inherit
both the factors and the open and closed regions. The \omterm{def:b1:genomes:genome}{genome} is the
same; the accessible \omterm{def:b1:genomes:genome}{genome} is not, and the inaccessibility is
inherited.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b1:expression-control:12}
A bacterium's controls are tuned to the medium: a sugar, an \omterm{def:b1:proteins:aminoacid}{amino
acid}, a temperature binds a \omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{repressor} or a \omterm{prop:b1:expression-control:trp}{sigma factor} and the
response is complete in minutes and reversed as soon as the signal
goes --- expression tracks the environment. A body's \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} answers to
signals too, but its main decisions were taken during development and
locked in chromatin: what it can express was set by the factors it
inherited and the marks on its \omterm{def:b1:nucleic-acids:chain}{DNA}, changed only by division and
mostly irreversible. The bacterium is a reader of the present; the
differentiated \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell} has a memory that is its identity.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b1:expression-control:1}
\textbf{1.} $0.5\times 10^{-3}\times 180 = \qty{0.09}{g/L} =
\qty{9e-5}{g/mL}$.
\textbf{2.} $9\times 10^{-5}/1.5\times 10^{-12} = \num{6e7}$ divisions.
\textbf{3.} $10^7 + \num{6e7} = \num{7e7}$ \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cells} per mL.
\textbf{4.} $7 = 2^n$: $n = 2.8$ doublings, \qty{56}{min}.
\textbf{5.} Off: lactose (allolactose) has released the \omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{repressor},
but glucose keeps cAMP low, so CAP is not bound and the promoter is
nearly silent; also the permease is scarce, so little lactose enters.
\textbf{6.} cAMP rises and binds CAP, which binds the promoter.
\textbf{7.} Negative control was lifted but positive control was
absent: a weak promoter without CAP starts rarely (\omterm{def:b1:expression-control:cap}{catabolite
repression}).
\textbf{8.} $5000/1800 = 2.8$ tetramers per second; $2.8\times 4096 =
\num{11400}$ residues per second.
\textbf{9.} Capacity $20\,000\times 20 = \num{4e5}$ residues per second:
\qty{3}{\%} of the \omterm{def:b1:gene-expression:ribosome}{ribosomes}.
\textbf{10.} $5000\times 4096\times 110\times 1.66\times 10^{-24} =
\qty{3.7e-15}{g}$: \qty{2.5}{\%} of the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell}'s \omterm{def:b1:proteins:peptide}{protein}.
\textbf{11.} $5000/5 = 1000$.
\textbf{12.} Lactose cannot cross the membrane without the permease;
a \emph{lacY}$^-$ mutant has the \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme} but no substrate inside and
does not grow on lactose (nor induce well, since the inducer is made
from lactose inside the \omterm{def:b1:cell-unit-of-life:cell}{cell}).
\textbf{13.} $1.0\times 10^{-3}\times 342 = \qty{0.342}{g/L}$, worth
$2\times 180 = \qty{360}{g}$ of glucose per mole, i.e.\ \qty{0.36}{g/L}
$= \qty{3.6e-4}{g/mL}$.
\textbf{14.} $3.6\times 10^{-4}/1.5\times 10^{-12} = \num{2.4e8}$
divisions; final $\num{7e7} + \num{2.4e8} = \num{3.1e8}$ per mL.
\textbf{15.} $3.1/0.7 = 4.4 = 2^n$: $n = 2.1$ doublings, \qty{64}{min}.
\textbf{16.} Glucose \qty{56}{min}, lag \qty{30}{min}, lactose
\qty{64}{min}: \qty{150}{min} in all.
\textbf{17.} Lactose must first be hydrolysed, an extra enzymatic step
whose rate limits the supply of glucose; and the permease uses the
proton gradient to import it, a cost glucose transport does not pay.
\textbf{18.} $I^-$: \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme} at \num{5000} throughout (constitutive, once
glucose is gone; on glucose CAP still limits it, so the level is
intermediate); its lag is shorter or absent, so the curve shows a
smaller pause; the cost slows it slightly on glucose.
\textbf{19.} CAP$^-$: grows on glucose to $\num{7e7}$, then stops: the
\omterm{def:b1:expression-control:operon}{lac operon} cannot be activated, the \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme} stays near 5 molecules, and
the lactose is never used.
\textbf{20.} On lactose only: none in \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme} level once induced (both
fully on) and essentially none in growth; $O^c$ merely lacks the
delay of induction. On glucose only: $O^c$ makes the \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme} uselessly
(as far as CAP allows), wild type does not.
\textbf{21.} $5000\times 4096\times 4 = \num{8.2e7}$ \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP}: \qty{0.8}{\%}
of $10^{10}$.
\textbf{22.} Generation time longer by \qty{0.8}{\%}: growth rate
lower by \qty{0.8}{\%}, $\delta = 0.008$ doublings per generation.
\textbf{23.} $r = 2^{-0.008} = 0.99447$; $r^n = 0.111$: $n =
\ln 0.111/\ln 0.99447 = 2.20/0.00555 = 400$ generations.
\textbf{24.} On lactose the \omterm{def:b1:enzymes:enzyme}{enzyme} is needed anyway, so the mutant
pays nothing and gains the lag: it is not selected against and is
sometimes selected for; and laboratory strains are grown for
convenience, not competition.
\textbf{25.} Induction factor 1000 (5 to \num{5000} molecules);
unnecessary synthesis costs \qty{0.8}{\%} of the \omterm{def:b1:water-small-molecules:atp}{ATP} budget; the
experiment lasts about two and a half hours.
\end{solution}
