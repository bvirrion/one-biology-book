\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange} --- The Lungs and Gas Exchange}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:1}
\omterm{def:g4:breathing:movements}{Breathing} in: the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{rib} \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} lift and widen the cage while
the \omterm{prop:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:ventilation}{diaphragm} flattens downward; the stretched chest pulls
the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} wide and air flows in --- muscular work.
\omterm{def:g4:breathing:movements}{Breathing} out at rest: the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} relax and the stretched
chest springs back, pushing air out --- release, nearly
free.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:2}
The broad \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} floor under the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs}, closing the chest
off below; its flattening is the deep half of every
breath in.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:3}
The \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs}' air pockets: hundreds of millions of thin-walled
bubbles at the airways' finest tips, each wrapped in fine
\omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:vessels}{blood vessels}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:4}
Inhaled: about $21$ percent \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen}, almost no \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{carbon
dioxide}. Exhaled: about $16$ percent \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} and about $4$
percent \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{carbon dioxide}. The differences --- five points of
\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} taken, four of \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{carbon dioxide} added --- are the
exchange itself.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:5}
The large-thin-moist, blood-lined crossing surface. Shared
with the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills}' \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers}
(\cref{prop:g7:how-animals-breathe:spec}) and the

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:6}
Tars coating and irritating the airways (the overloaded
broom and its cough); alveolar walls breaking down ---
surface lost; a smoke gas riding the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}'s \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} seats;
narrowed airways throttling the bellows.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:7}
In must stretch chest and \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} against their spring ---
that stretching is the work. The resting out is the spring
itself returning: the widened chest and stretched \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs}
recoil, and the recoil pushes the air.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:8}
The exchange needs surface as much as fresh air: crossing
happens only where air meets blood-lined wall. Faster
bellows through fewer, baggier pockets ventilates surface
that no longer exists --- the missing factor is the
crossing area itself.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:9}
The smoker climbs with narrowed airways (throttled
intake), a reduced alveolar surface (less loading per
breath), and \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} seats occupied by smoke's gas (less
carried per round). To pay the same muscular bill, the
\omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} must run more rounds --- hence the gasping arrival
at a sprint-level \omterm{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:pulse}{pulse}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:10}
Both are trained control of the \emph{outward} phase:
adding measured \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} to what is normally a passive
spring --- the diver to vent expanding air steadily, the
player to meter one long controlled outflow. (Their deep
intakes train the inward work too.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:11}
Deeper-and-faster \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} multiplies loading at the
alveolar counter; the faster, harder \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart} multiplies
rounds at the transport counter; rerouting widens the
\omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}' local \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:vessels}{vessels} at the delivery counter. Three
adjustments, one multiplied delivery
(\cref{ex:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:accounting}'s
balanced books).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:12}
The border: hundreds of millions of \omterm{def:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:alveoli}{alveoli} sum to about
half a tennis court of exchange surface, folded into a
chest --- across which five percentage points of every
breath's \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} cross inward and four of \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{carbon dioxide}
cross out. The slow fire: smoke's tars and irritation
consume that border year by year --- coating its
approaches, collapsing its pockets, seizing its transport
seats --- cumulative, quiet, and only partly reversible,
like a border burned faster than it can be rebuilt.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:1}
\textbf{1.} Phase in: \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{rib} \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} lift and widen the cage,
\omterm{prop:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:ventilation}{diaphragm} flattens --- costs \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy}. Phase out (rest):
\omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} release, chest and \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} spring back --- nearly
free. Cycle about $15$ times a minute.

\textbf{2.} The \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} cling to the chest's inner wall:
widen the chest and the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} are stretched wide with it,
and the outside air flows in to fill the stretched space.
The pump works by reshaping the room, not squeezing the
organ.

\textbf{3.} About $15$ cycles a minute at rest; in effort
the cycles quicken \emph{and} deepen --- more breaths,
and more air per breath.

\textbf{4.} Panting: quick shallow cycles, nearly all
\omterm{prop:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:ventilation}{diaphragm}. Sighing: one extra-deep stretch and long
release --- a reset of the spring. Candles: the outward
phase with \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle} added to the spring.

\textbf{5.} The \omterm{def:g7:lungs-and-gas-exchange:alveoli}{alveoli}: hundreds of millions of
thin-walled bubbles, each wrapped in fine \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:vessels}{vessels},
summing to about half a tennis court.

\textbf{6.} Large: the summed court. Thin: bubble walls a
\omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}'s breadth. Moist: their lining. Blood-lined: the
wrapping \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:vessels}{vessels}. Specification met on all four counts.

\textbf{7.} Five points kept out of $21$ --- roughly a
quarter of the inhaled \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} is taken per breath.

\textbf{8.} Of the surface's moisture: air leaving has
swept across wet walls (and warm ones), so exhaust
carries away water and warmth --- the misted mirror is
the surface's upkeep cost, paid with every breath.

\textbf{9.} Intake: tars coat and irritate the airways.
Pump: narrowed airways throttle every cycle. Surface:
alveolar walls break down and merge --- area lost.
Transport: smoke's gas occupies the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood}'s \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen}
seats.

\textbf{10.} Coughs and colds inflame and clear --- the
machinery repairs and returns to service. Broken alveolar
walls do not rebuild: each merged pocket is exchange area
struck permanently off the books --- capital, not
running costs.

\textbf{11.} (1) Admit no smoke: capital losses are
forever. (2) Run on clean air: filters and brooms have
finite capacity. (3) Exercise the pump under load: deep
effort maintains \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} and ventilates the whole
surface. (4) Intake through the nose in cold and dust:
pre-conditioning protects the works.

\textbf{12.} Alone among the body's pumps, the bellows
answers both the automatic services and the will ---
because the same outflow that vents \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{carbon dioxide} is
the wind of speech and song, and a machine lent to
language must accept the speaker's hand on the controls.
\end{solution}
