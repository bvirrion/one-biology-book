\chapter{Evidence of Evolution}\label{ch:g9:evidence-of-evolution}

Twice this book has stopped at the edge of a great claim:
the \omterm{prop:g6:classification-kinship:kinship}{kinship} tree's \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:tree}{branches} all meet
(\cref{rem:g6:classification-kinship:implies}), and every
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}{living thing} reads one code
(\cref{rem:g9:genes-and-dna:universal}). The claim they
point to has a name --- \emph{evolution}: \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} change
across generations, and all descend from common ancestors.
Before the next chapter asks \emph{how}, this one does what
\cref{exo:g6:classification-kinship:12} demanded: it lays
out the evidence.

\section{The witness of the rocks}

\begin{definition}[Fossils]\label{def:g9:evidence-of-evolution:fossils}
A \emph{fossil}\index{fossil} is a preserved trace of past
life --- \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{shell}, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone}, leaf-print, footprint --- sealed in
sedimentary rock. Rock layers stack in time order, oldest
lowest; reading upward reads history. Fossils are the
remains half of
\cref{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:components}'s
inventory, kept for millions of years.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[What the layered record shows]\label{prop:g9:evidence-of-evolution:record}
Read in order, the record testifies three times:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{faunas change}: each layer-age holds its own
        cast --- no rabbits among the trilobites, no
        trilobites in the rabbit layers; most \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species} of
        every deep layer are \emph{\omterm{prop:g5:biodiversity:loss}{extinct}}
        (\cref{prop:g5:biodiversity:loss}'s word, with
        deep time behind it);
  \item \emph{order is nested like the tree}: \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} appear
        in deep layers, four-limbed amphibian-kin above
        them, reptile-kin above those, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{mammals} and \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds}
        higher still --- the \omterm{def:g4:classifying-animals:classification}{classification}'s boxes
        (\cref{pb:g6:classification-kinship:1}) stack in
        time exactly as \omterm{prop:g6:classification-kinship:kinship}{kinship} predicts;
  \item \emph{intermediates exist}: the famous slab of
        \emph{Archaeopteryx} --- \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers} and wings on a
        toothed, long-boned-tailed reptile frame ---
        stands where bird and reptile \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:tree}{branches} part; \omterm{def:g9:evidence-of-evolution:fossils}{fossil}
        whales with working hind \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} stand where
        \cref{ex:g6:classification-kinship:whale}'s
        land-mammal ancestry demanded them.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The whale series]\label{ex:g9:evidence-of-evolution:whales}
The whale's dossier, filled by rocks: successive \omterm{def:g9:evidence-of-evolution:fossils}{fossils},
in time order, run from a wolf-sized fourlegged shore
mammal, through forms with shrinking \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} and
strengthening tails, to legless full swimmers --- while
today's whale still builds tiny hidden hip-and-leg \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones}
(\cref{ex:g6:classification-kinship:placing} noted the
vestiges) and still nurses, breathes air and grows fetal
hair. Prediction, \omterm{def:g9:evidence-of-evolution:fossils}{fossils}, vestiges: three keys, one
lock.
\end{example}

\section{The witness of living bodies}

\begin{proposition}[Anatomy remembers]\label{prop:g9:evidence-of-evolution:anatomy}
Living bodies carry their history:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{the shared plan}: bat wing, whale flipper,
        horse \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{leg} and your \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arm} are one \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bone} arrangement
        (\cref{pb:g6:classification-kinship:1}'s
        \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{four-limb} fork) stretched, shortened and re-jobbed
        --- inheritance explains what fresh engineering
        would never repeat;
  \item \emph{vestiges}: the whale's hips, the flightless
        beetle's sealed wings, your own tailbone and the
        appendix's shrunken state --- parts kept because
        ancestry builds on, not from scratch;
  \item \emph{\omterm{def:g5:human-reproduction:pregnancy}{embryos} agree}: \omterm{def:g4:classifying-animals:vertebrate}{vertebrate} \omterm{def:g5:human-reproduction:pregnancy}{embryos} of \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish},
        chick and human run strikingly alike through
        their early weeks
        (\cref{prop:g8:from-egg-to-birth:timetable}'s
        plan-laying) --- shared build instructions,
        diverging late.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{proposition}[The molecules vote]\label{prop:g9:evidence-of-evolution:molecules}
The newest witness is
\cref{rem:g9:genes-and-dna:universal}'s code. Compare one
well-kept \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{gene}'s spelling across \omterm{def:g5:biodiversity:species}{species}: human and chimp
differ in few letters, human and mouse in more, human and
\omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} in more still, human and \omterm{ex:g6:producing-our-food:bread}{yeast} most --- and the
distances, tallied \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{gene} after \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{gene}, redraw the
\emph{same tree} the anatomists drew from \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones} and the
paleontologists from layers. Three independent witnesses,
one family tree: that agreement is why biologists stopped
arguing about \emph{whether} and turned to \emph{how}.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Watching it move]\label{ex:g9:evidence-of-evolution:live}
Change is even watchable where generations turn fast.
The peppered moth's dark form spread through soot-stained
industrial woods and receded when the air cleared;
insecticide-proof \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects} and antibiotic-defying \omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:fermentation}{microbes}
(\cref{ch:g9:microbes-and-infection} will meet the
medical case) arise within years; island finches'
beak-size distributions shift with drought and plenty,
measured live by ring-and-caliper studies. Small moves,
short times --- the mechanism caught running, whatever
its long-run reach.
\end{example}

\begin{method}[Weighing historical evidence]\label{met:g9:evidence-of-evolution:weigh}
Evolution is history, and historical claims are tested
like the cold case of
\cref{pb:g9:unique-individuals:1} --- by convergence of
independent witnesses:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item ask what the claim \emph{predicts}: which
        intermediates, which vestiges, which molecular
        distances;
  \item check each witness separately: rocks, bodies,
        molecules, live watches;
  \item weigh their independence: witnesses that could
        not have colluded, agreeing, multiply
        credibility;
  \item hold the verdict open to the next find --- and
        note when finds keep landing where the claim
        said to dig (the whale \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} were sought, and
        found).
\end{enumerate}
\end{method}

\section{Problem: The Museum's New Wing}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- curating the
evidence hall}]\label{pb:g9:evidence-of-evolution:1}
The natural history museum builds an evolution wing, and
the class curates four rooms: Rocks, Bodies, Molecules,
Live Watches.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- Room by room.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Rocks: order the wall --- trilobite slab, first
        \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish}, amphibian-kin tracks, reptile \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{skeletons},
        \emph{Archaeopteryx} cast, early mammal jaw ---
        and write the room's one-sentence caption from
        \cref{prop:g9:evidence-of-evolution:record}.
  \item Bodies: the centerpiece spreads bat wing, whale
        flipper, horse \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{leg} and human \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arm} from one
        \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{skeleton} stand. Caption the shared plan --- and
        add the vestige cabinet's three exhibits.
  \item Molecules: the wall chart tallies one \omterm{def:g9:genes-and-dna:gene}{gene}'s
        letter-differences: chimp 1, mouse 8, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} 24,
        \omterm{ex:g6:producing-our-food:bread}{yeast} 41 (illustrative counts). What must the
        chart's tree match, for the room to work?
  \item Live Watches: choose two exhibits from
        \cref{ex:g9:evidence-of-evolution:live} and say
        what each shows moving.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The visitors'
questions.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item ``Why are there still monkeys, if we descend
        from ancestors shared with them?'' Answer with
        the tree's branching --- cousins, not ladders
        (\cref{def:g6:classification-kinship:tree}).
  \item ``Where are the missing links?'' Point to the
        found ones --- two from this chapter --- and
        state what each stood between.
  \item ``Is evolution not just a theory?'' Answer with
        the historical-evidence method: what
        ``theory'' means here, and what the four rooms'
        agreement establishes.
  \item ``Could tomorrow's dig overturn it?'' State
        what kind of find would --- rabbits among
        trilobites --- and what its absence, dig after
        dig, has meant.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The wing's
closing panel.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
  \item Draft the panel linking the wing to the
        \omterm{def:g4:classifying-animals:classification}{classification} hall next door: why the old
        nested boxes were the tree all along.
  \item Add the molecule paragraph: what the shared
        code says about the trunk.
  \item Add the honest paragraph: what the wing shows
        (change, \omterm{prop:g6:classification-kinship:kinship}{kinship}, intermediates) and what it
        does not yet explain --- naming the mechanism
        question the next hall will take up.
  \item Close with one sentence a twelve-year-old will
        remember, containing \emph{family} and
        \emph{time}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
