\chapter{Living and Nonliving Things}\label{ch:g1:living-nonliving}

Look around you. A bird flies. A rock sits still. A flower grows. A
pencil does not. Biology begins with a simple question: which things
are \emph{alive}?

\section{What is living?}

\begin{definition}[Living thing]\label{def:g1:living-nonliving:living}
A \emph{living thing}\index{living thing} is something that is
alive. Living things need food, water and air. They can grow. They
can have young. Plants, animals and people are living things.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Nonliving thing]\label{def:g1:living-nonliving:nonliving}
A \emph{nonliving thing}\index{nonliving thing} is not alive. It
does not grow by itself. It does not need food. Rocks, water, toys
and cars are nonliving things.
\end{definition}

\omimg{living-nonliving.jpg}{0.52\linewidth}{\omterm{def:g1:living-nonliving:living}{Living things} (green leaves,
a bird) and \omterm{def:g1:living-nonliving:nonliving}{nonliving things} (a rock, a ball) side by side --- sorting is
the first skill of the biologist.}

\begin{example}[In the classroom]\label{ex:g1:living-nonliving:class}
The teacher is living. The desk is nonliving. A pot plant is living.
The pot itself is nonliving. A picture of a dog is nonliving --- it
cannot run or bark.
\end{example}

\section{How can we tell?}

\begin{method}[Is it living?]\label{met:g1:living-nonliving:test}
Ask these questions. If you answer ``yes'' to most of them, the
thing is living.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Does it need food or water?
  \item Can it grow bigger over time?
  \item Can it make more of its own kind (have young or seeds)?
  \item Does it move by itself, or turn toward light, or react when
        touched?
\end{enumerate}
A toy robot may move, but it does not grow and does not need food.
It is nonliving.
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Sort them]\label{ex:g1:living-nonliving:sort}
Cat, stone, apple tree, spoon, fish, cloud: living are the cat, the
apple tree and the fish. Nonliving are the stone, the spoon and the
cloud.
\end{example}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:living-nonliving:1}
Name three \omterm{def:g1:living-nonliving:living}{living things} and three \omterm{def:g1:living-nonliving:nonliving}{nonliving things} you can see
from your seat.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:living-nonliving:2}
Is a bicycle living or nonliving? Give two reasons.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[$\star$]\label{exo:g1:living-nonliving:3}

\end{exercise}

\end{exercise}

\end{exercise}
