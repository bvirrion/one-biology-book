\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g11:cancer} --- Genome Damage and Cancer}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:1}
\omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{Cancer}: \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} dividing without the normal controls and invading
neighbouring tissue. \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{Tumour}: the mass formed by such a clone.
Metastasis: a new \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{tumour} founded elsewhere by \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} that travelled
through blood or lymph.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:2}
A \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} whose \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} relays growth signals and pushes the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} into
the cycle; mutated so that the \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} is permanently active it
becomes an oncogene. One jammed accelerator drives the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} whatever
the other \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} does.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:3}
It halts the cycle when \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:information}{DNA} is damaged and triggers repair or
self-destruction. One working \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} still makes enough \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} to
brake; only the loss of both removes the brake.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:4}
Ultraviolet light (skin \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{cancers}), tobacco tar (lung \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{cancer}), ionising
radiation, asbestos, alcohol, papillomavirus (cervical \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{cancer}).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:5}
All its \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} descend from one \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} in which the first \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation}
occurred; they share that \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation} and the ones that followed, and
markers such as the same inactive X \omterm{def:g11:cell-cycle-mitosis:chromosome}{chromosome}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:6}
About 200 at 40 and 2700 at 80: a factor of 13 for a factor of 2 in
age --- several events multiplying, not one proportional cause.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:7}
About 10 and 17. A 20-a-day smoker: $17 \times 1\% = 17\%$, one in
six.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:8}
Without p53 the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} no longer pauses to repair damaged \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:information}{DNA} before
replication, nor destroys itself when the damage is severe: lesions
are copied into \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} at every division, and \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} with
scrambled genomes survive.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:9}
Every \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} of her body already lacks one \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele}; a single somatic
\omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation} of the other completes the loss, instead of two independent
ones. With one step taken from birth, the remaining steps are reached
sooner and in more \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}. Her sisters inherit the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} from the
same parent with probability $1/2$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:10}
The drugs kill dividing \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} indiscriminately: hair-root \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}, the
gut lining and the bone marrow divide fast and are hit along with the
\omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{tumour} --- hence hair loss, nausea, and a shortage of white \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:11}
Stopping ends the supply of new \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations}, and the lining's \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} are
gradually replaced, removing many clones that carried early steps.
But some clones with several steps persist, and the \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} already
present are not erased; the risk stays above a non-smoker's.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:12}
$10^{12} \times 10^{-7} = 10^5$ \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} acquire the first \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation}.
Each founds a clone that may divide thousands of times, so the second
\omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation} is sought not in one \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} but in the whole clone's
divisions, and likewise the third: the steps multiply the target, and
the true probability is many orders of magnitude above $10^{-21}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:13}
The viral \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} removes the brake without touching the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}: the
infected \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} behaves as if p53 were lost and accumulates \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations}
that complete the process. The vaccine makes the immune system
destroy the virus before it infects the cervix; once \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} are
infected and transformed, the vaccine no longer helps --- hence
vaccination in early adolescence.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:14}
A polyp is a clone that has taken one or two steps; removing it
removes the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} in which the remaining steps could occur, so the
\omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{cancer} never forms. Since the polyp-to-cancer sequence takes ten to
twenty years, a ten-year interval catches most polyps before they
complete it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:cancer:15}
Incidence rises with age because the steps take decades, but the
steps are taken during life, largely under exposures --- tobacco
alone accounts for a third of \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{cancer} deaths, and infections,
alcohol, sun and diet for much of the rest. Removing an exposure at
any age reduces the \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} still to come; screening removes clones
before the last step. Old age is when the account is presented, not
when it is written.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g11:cancer:1}
\textbf{1.} They are present in the \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{tumour} and absent from the
patient's other \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}, so they arose in a body \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell}. Germline
\omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} would be in every \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell}, healthy tissue included.

\textbf{2.} That most of the \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{tumour}'s \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} were caused by
benzopyrene --- the \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutagen}{mutagen} of tobacco smoke left its signature.

\textbf{3.} A nonsense \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation}: a premature stop \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:code}{codon} truncates
p53, which cannot work.

\textbf{4.} p53 is a brake: one working \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} still brakes, so both
had to go (one mutated, one deleted). The receptor is an accelerator:
one permanently active \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} drives the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} whatever the other
\omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} does.

\textbf{5.} The receptor \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} is the accelerator (proto-oncogene
turned oncogene); p53 is the brake (\omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{tumour} suppressor).

\textbf{6.} $20 \times 365 \times 35 \approx \num{255000}$ cigarettes.

\textbf{7.} About $\num{255000}$ substitutions per \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} --- fifty times
the 5000 found. Most lesions are repaired, and \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} with too many
\omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} die; 5000 is the surviving fraction.

\textbf{8.} They occurred early, in the founding \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} or its first
descendants, so every \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{tumour} \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} inherited them; the others arose
later in sub-clones.

\textbf{9.} $10^{10} \approx 2^{33}$: 33 doublings, at 100 days each
about 9 years.

\textbf{10.} If the \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{tumour} took 9 years or more to grow from its
founding \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell}, the last key \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation} occurred around 25 years into
the 35 years of smoking, or earlier.

\textbf{11.} $17 \times 1\% = 17\%$.

\textbf{12.} 17 \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{cancers}, of which 1 would have occurred anyway: 16 in
100 attributable to smoking.

\textbf{13.} No new \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} are added; the lining renews itself and
many clones carrying early steps are shed and replaced over the
years; the remaining clones have fewer steps than they would have
acquired by continuing.

\textbf{14.} Several rare \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} must accumulate in one lineage,
and the lineage must then grow to a detectable size: each stage takes
years, and their sum is decades.

\textbf{15.} One \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutation} per \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} per cigarette, in some $10^9$
lining \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}, over $\num{255000}$ cigarettes, is $10^{14}$ \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations}
scattered over the lung; a few will hit p53 or a proto-oncogene in a
\omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cell} that already carries another step. It is the number of trials
that makes the improbable certain.

\textbf{16.} Smokers: $\num{27000}$ deaths among $\num{11.25e6}$, about
240 per 100\,000; non-smokers: 3000 among $\num{33.75e6}$, about 9 per
100\,000.

\textbf{17.} About 27 times --- of the same order as the figure's 17
to 25 for heavy smokers; the population figure mixes light and heavy
smokers and ex-smokers.

\textbf{18.} About 3000 a year, the non-smokers' share. Not
immediately: the \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} already present in current and former
smokers will produce \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{cancers} for another twenty or thirty years.

\textbf{19.} Screening every smoker saves about 20\% of
$\num{27000}$, some 5000 deaths a year; stopping smoking would
eventually prevent about $\num{24000}$ --- five times more.

\textbf{20.} The G-to-T signature proved the \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{tumour}'s \omterm{def:g11:mutations:mutation}{mutations} came
from tobacco smoke; the \omterm{def:g11:cancer:cancer}{cancer} was made by an oncogene activated and
both \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{alleles} of p53 lost; not smoking would have prevented it.
\end{solution}
