\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g7:how-animals-breathe} --- How Animals Breathe}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:1}
A large, thin, moist exchange surface where \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} (or body
fluid) meets the surroundings. Large: enough total crossing;
thin: an easy crossing; moist: the gases cross dissolved.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:2}
\omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{Lungs}: \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{mammals}, \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{birds} (also \omterm{prop:g4:classifying-animals:classes}{reptiles}, adult \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{amphibians}).
\omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{Gills}: \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish}, \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}{tadpoles} (also mussels, water larvae).
\omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{Tracheae}: grasshoppers, bees --- \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects} generally. \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{Skin
breathing}: the earthworm; \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{amphibians} as a supplement.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:3}
Water enters at the mouth, is pushed over the feathery \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills}
--- where the exchange happens --- and \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} under the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gill}
covers: a one-way current, pumped by mouth and covers in
alternation.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:4}
Through \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{tracheae}: a network of fine air tubes running from
openings along the body and branching to every organ --- air
delivered door-to-door, so the \omterm{prop:g4:journey-of-food:absorb}{blood} need carry little.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:5}
Out of water its feathery \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gill} surfaces collapse and stick
together: the large surface shrinks to almost none, and
without surface there is no crossing --- plenty of \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen},
no machine to take it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:6}
\omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{Skin breathing} --- and the moist condition fails: the drying
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} can no longer pass gases, and the worm suffocates in
the open air. Large and thin do not save a surface that is
no longer moist.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:7}
Tadpole: \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills}. Adult: simple \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} plus the moist \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} ---
with the \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} alone serving through the torpid pond-bottom
winter.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:8}
Its machine is the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lung} --- inherited from milk-feeding land
ancestors --- so it must surface to breathe air, however
fish-like its life: held under, it drowns. The dress is the
water's; the machine is the family's.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:9}
A carried air store --- a diver's tank under the wing
covers, from which its \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{tracheae} draw. It must return to the
surface regularly to renew the bubble.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:10}
(a) Mussel: resident of water, no visible movements but
\omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills} inside the \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{shell}, washed by a pumped current. (b)
Grasshopper: air resident; rows of tiny openings, pulsing
abdomen --- \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{tracheae}. (c) Grass snake: a reptile commuter
--- \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs}; it swims with nostrils up and surfaces to
breathe.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:11}
\omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{Tracheae} deliver by branching air tubes, superb over
millimetres and too slow over long distances: a whale-sized
body's innards would lie too far from any opening. The
machine sets a size ceiling, and \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{insects} live under it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g7:how-animals-breathe:12}
The specification: a large, thin, moist meeting surface of
body and surroundings. \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{Gills} meet it with feathery surfaces
spread by water; \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} with millions of pockets folded in
moist shelter; \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{tracheae} with the summed inner surface of
their finest tips, moist at the ends; \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} with the body's
whole moist outside kept large relative to a small thin
body. Four holders, one held thing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g7:how-animals-breathe:1}
\textbf{1.} Trout fry and sticklebacks: \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills}. \omterm{ex:g2:animals-grow-up:frog}{Tadpoles}:
\omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills} (the legged ones mid-rebuild). Adult frogs: \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs}
plus \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}. Pond snails: \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs} --- surface commuters. Diving
beetle: \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{tracheae}, fed from its carried bubble. Mayfly
larvae: \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills} (the feathery tufts). Earthworms: \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}.

\textbf{2.} \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{Gills}. The waving renews the water against the
\omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gill} surface --- self-made current where the still pond
provides none, keeping fresh oxygen-bearing water at the
crossing.

\textbf{3.} The pond snails, the diving beetle, the adult
frogs (and any visiting grass snake): all the lung- and
bubble-users --- their machines take \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} from air, so
the surface is their filling station.

\textbf{4.} The \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} rebuild of \omterm{def:g2:animals-grow-up:metamorphosis}{metamorphosis}: \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills}
serving the legless tadpole are being traded for the \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{lungs}
(and \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{breathing} \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin}) the shore-bound froglet will need.

\textbf{5.} The gill-users suffer first: their whole supply
is the water's dwindling dissolved stock. The commuters ---
snail, beetle, frog --- draw on the unlimited air above and
merely commute more often.

\textbf{6.} The top millimetre touches the air: \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen}
dissolves in from above, so the film-layer is the least
poor water in a suffocating pond. The sticklebacks are
queuing at the last well.

\textbf{7.} The \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills} clog: fine mud settles on the
feathery surfaces and blocks the crossing. Expect the
gill-breathers to flee the cloud, and cover-pumping to
labor --- coughing spasms to clear the silt.

\textbf{8.} \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{Skin breathing}, and the moist-but-airless
condition: the waterlogged burrows hold too little \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen},
so the worms surface to breathe --- trading suffocation
below for drying risk above.

\textbf{9.} Table as assigned (machine and medium per
\omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animal}; commuters: snail, beetle, frog). Heading every
column: the large-thin-moist exchange surface --- the one
specification all four machines hold.

\textbf{10.} Frogs: provided for --- torpid in the mud,
\omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{skin-breathing} their small winter need. Snails and beetle:
troubled --- their surface is sealed; they winter dormant
with lowered needs or trapped bubbles. \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{Fish}: provided for
while the water's stock lasts --- cold slows every engine
note (\cref{ex:g7:what-is-respiration:demand}), and \omterm{def:g7:how-animals-breathe:four}{gills}
serve the reduced demand under the ice.

\textbf{11.} The spider's silk bell is a fixed air tank
where the beetle's bubble is a carried one: both store
surface air below water and both keep serving an
air-machine (the spider's book-lung-and-tracheae
equipment) in the wrong medium.

\textbf{12.} Because the pond offers \omterm{def:g7:what-is-respiration:respiration}{oxygen} in two media
--- sparse and dissolved below, rich above --- and each
machine harvests one medium at one size of life: only with
all four at work is every corner, from mud to surface
film, inhabited.
\end{solution}
