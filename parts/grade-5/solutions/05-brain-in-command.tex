\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g5:brain-in-command} --- The Brain in Command}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:1}
The \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}: receives all reports, makes all decisions, issues
all orders. The \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{spinal cord}: the thick cable running from the
\omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} down inside the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{spine}. The \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nerves}: the branching threads
carrying messages between \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} or cord and every organ.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:2}
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{Sense organs} report to the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}; the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} decides and
composes orders; \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nerves} carry the orders to the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}, which
contract. \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{Sense organs} report, the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} decides, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}
obey.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:3}
The \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} in the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{skull}; the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{spinal cord} inside the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{spine}'s
column of stacked small \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{bones}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:4}
The delay between a signal and the body's response --- about a
fifth of a second for a simple task in a practiced person, a
little more in children.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:5}
The \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} of the foot reported (touch and pain), the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nervous
system} decided the retreat, and the \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{leg} \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} obeyed by
jerking the foot away. (So fast a retreat is handled partly by
the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{spinal cord} itself --- the command center's emergency
desk.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:6}
It keeps \omterm{def:g4:breathing:movements}{breathing} going day and night, and adjusts the
\omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart}'s pace to the body's needs (also: storing memories,
dreaming).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:7}
\omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{Sleep} --- its filing-and-repairs time --- and a large steady
share of the body's \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} and oxygen. Deprived of either, it
reacts more slowly, scatters its attention and decides badly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:8}
Open answer. Typical catches run around $10$--$20$ cm;
several tries usually improve and steady the value a little.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:9}
Because every catch must run all three steps --- the \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eyes}'
report must travel, the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} must compute and decide, the
order must travel back and the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} contract --- and each
step takes real time. Skill shortens the loop; nothing
removes it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:10}
Because the best brakes act only after the driver's loop has
run --- and at highway speed the car covers about \qty{7}{m}
before the order ``brake!'' even reaches the foot. The gap
between cars is room for the loop. Attention shortens the
report-and-decide steps; a distracted \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} adds metres.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g5:brain-in-command:11}
The \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}'s part --- the deciding and ordering: practice laid
down better paths, so the same reports now route to the right
orders quickly and without effort. \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{Muscle} training grows the
\omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscle}'s strength; skill training grows the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}'s routing.
One builds the motor, the other the commander.
\end{solution}
