\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b2:asexual-reproduction} --- Asexual Reproduction and Cloning in Plants}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:1}
\omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{Clone}: the genetically identical descendants of one parent by
mitosis. \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{Genet}: the genetic individual, all that grew from one
zygote. \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{Ramet}: one physiologically independent unit of a \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{genet}.
Organs: runners (strawberry), rhizomes (couch grass), tubers
(potato), bulbs (onion), suckers (aspen), leaf plantlets
(\emph{Kalanchoe}).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:2}
The vascular cambia of scion and rootstock must be aligned and in
contact; callus bridges the cut and differentiates xylem and phloem
across it. The rootstock supplies roots (water, minerals, vigour,
soil tolerance, size control), the scion the fruiting shoot (the
variety). Each part keeps its own genome and the cells never fuse:
two \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:vocabulary}{genotypes} side by side, a chimera; a hybrid would carry a
mixture of both genomes in every cell.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:3}
\omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{Vegetative multiplication}: a body part roots into a new plant.
\omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:apomixis}{Apomixis}: a \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:seed}{seed} forms without fertilisation, the embryo a \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{clone} of
the mother. \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:apomixis}{Parthenogenesis}: an animal develops from an unfertilised
egg. Only \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:apomixis}{apomixis} keeps the \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:seed}{seed} --- with its coat, \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:seed}{dormancy} and
dispersal.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:4}
Sterilise the explant; place it on agar with sugar, minerals,
vitamins and balanced auxin and cytokinin (callus); raise the
cytokinin-to-auxin ratio to induce shoots; subculture the shoots
every few weeks; transfer to an auxin-rich medium to root; wean the
plantlets under high humidity and then in soil.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:5}
Each plant is replaced by $1 + 24 = 25$: after 4 years $25^{4} =
\num{390625}$ plants, needing \qty{39000}{m^2}, nearly four hectares.
Space, light and nutrients, the short reach of a runner, and the
death of plants under crowding stop the exponential phase within a
couple of years; the \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{clone} then advances as a front.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:6}
$\num{14000}/130 \approx 110$ generations of trunks. Radius of a disc
of \qty{4.3e5}{m^2}: $\sqrt{4.3\times 10^{5}/\pi} = \qty{370}{m}$;
advance $370/\num{14000} = \qty{0.026}{m}$ a year, under three
centimetres.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:7}
$p_t = 2^{t}p_0/(1 + (2^{t} - 1)p_0)$: $t = 5$: $0.0032$; $t = 10$:
$0.1024/1.1023 = 0.093$; $t = 15$: $3.28/4.28 = 0.77$. It never
spreads if its fitness is below half the sexual lineage's: a
relative fitness $w$ gives $q' = 2wq$, which decays for $w <
\tfrac12$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:8}
$10 : 1$: roots; $1 : 1$: undifferentiated callus; $1 : 10$: shoots.
A cutting already has a shoot and needs roots, which auxin induces
at the cut base.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:9}
1, 10, 100, \num{1000}, \num{10000} kg: after the third season only
\qty{1}{t}, after the fourth \qty{10}{t}: four seasons. True \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:seed}{seed}
gives thousands of \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:seed}{seeds} per plant but potato is a \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:vocabulary}{heterozygous}
tetraploid, so the seedlings segregate and none is the variety.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:10}
$e^{-5} = 0.0067$: 670 mutation-free individuals in the large
population, about 7 in the small one. Seven individuals can all
fail to leave offspring by chance within a few generations (the
probability that none of them reproduces is of order $e^{-7}$ per
generation), after which the best class is gone for ever and the
next-best becomes the best: a click of the ratchet, repeated until
the load is unbearable. With 670 the loss is practically impossible
--- until a bottleneck shrinks $N$ or the \omterm{thm:b2:genome-diversification:rate}{mutation rate} rises; the
ratchet only ever turns one way.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:11}
In the \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{clone} every host is susceptible: the strain spreads
unchecked at the rate the spores travel. In the sexual population
the \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:vocabulary}{genotype} it overcomes is one of $2^{10} = 1024$ combinations,
present in about a thousandth of the plants, scattered among
resistant neighbours, so the epidemic starves. The Lumper was one
\omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{clone} grown over most of a country; the blight found every plant
open, and there was no variation to breed from until other
varieties were imported.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:asexual-reproduction:12}
\omterm{prop:b2:asexual-reproduction:whysex}{Muller's ratchet}, the combining of favourable \omterm{def:b2:genome-diversification:mutation}{mutations}, and the
Red Queen each give sex a benefit that can exceed a factor two in a
large, parasitised, changing world. Bdelloid rotifers are the
awkward case: forty million years without males and no ratchet
meltdown. They appear to escape by extreme desiccation resistance
(which kills their parasites), by taking up foreign DNA and by
unusually efficient \omterm{prop:b2:genome-diversification:repair}{DNA repair} --- exceptions that show what the
rule normally requires.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:asexual-reproduction:1}
\textbf{1.} $4\times 10^{4}$ plants.
\textbf{2.} $1 + 6\times 2 = 13$.
\textbf{3.} \num{1300}; \num{16900}; \num{219700}.
\textbf{4.} $13^{t} = 400$: $t = \ln 400/\ln 13 = 5.99/2.56 = 2.3$
years --- full during the third year.
\textbf{5.} Radius of a hectare disc $\sqrt{10^{4}/\pi} = \qty{56}{m}$;
at \qty{0.5}{m} a year, \num{113} years.
\textbf{6.} A hundred scattered founders fill the field exponentially
from a hundred fronts in two years; one founder would fill its
neighbourhood in two years and then crawl for a century.
\textbf{7.} $5^{n} \ge 10^{6}$: $n \ge 8.6$, nine subcultures
($1.95\times 10^{6}$ shoots), \qty{36}{weeks}.
\textbf{8.} $0.8\times 1.95\times 10^{6} = 1.6\times 10^{6}$ plants.
\textbf{9.} $0.98$; $0.98^{10} = 0.82$.
\textbf{10.} $10\times 0.98 = 9.8$ clean lines expected; from ten
cuttings, one.
\textbf{11.} The virus travels through plasmodesmata and the phloem,
and the apical dome has no vascular connection and divides faster
than the virus spreads, so the outermost tenth of a millimetre is
usually uninfected.
\textbf{12.} Laboratory: uniform, virus-free plants at once, but
costly, and any \omterm{def:b2:genome-diversification:mutation}{mutation} or contamination in the culture is
multiplied a million times. Runners: free and on site, but three
seasons lost and every virus in the mother plants carried along.
\textbf{13.} Apomicts number $pN$ and give $F$ \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:seed}{seeds} each; sexuals
$(1 - p)N$ give $F/2$ \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:seed}{seeds} each (half their effort is \omterm{def:b2:plant-life-cycles:heterospory}{pollen}, which
makes no \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:seed}{seeds}); the apomict share of \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:seed}{seeds} is $Fp/(Fp + F(1 - p)/2)
= 2p/(1 + p)$.
\textbf{14.} $p_5 = 0.032/1.031 = 0.031$; $p_{10} = 1.024/2.023 =
0.51$: it passes one half at generation 10.
\textbf{15.} $p' = 2p(1 - cp)/\bigl(2p(1 - cp) + (1 - p)\bigr)$.
\textbf{16.} $p' = p$ when $2(1 - cp^*) = 1$: $p^* = 1/2c = 0.625$.
Below $p^*$, $2(1 - cp) > 1$ and $p$ rises; above, it falls: stable.
\textbf{17.} $p^* \ge 1$ when $c \le \tfrac12$: the apomict takes over
unless the parasite costs it more than half its output when common.
\textbf{18.} $c = 0.6$: $p^* = 0.83$. To keep sex in the population
the parasite must, at high apomict frequency, cut the \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{clone}'s
fitness by more than the twofold advantage --- the Red Queen must
run fast.
\textbf{19.} Three chromosome sets cannot pair in \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:meiosis}{meiosis}, so its
gametes are unbalanced; it is locked out of recombination, and
\omterm{prop:b2:asexual-reproduction:whysex}{Muller's ratchet} and the parasites work on it unopposed --- which is
why apomictic dandelion lineages are young and keep being remade
from sexual ancestors.
\textbf{20.} $10^{-9}\times 4.8\times 10^{8} = 0.48$ per division.
\textbf{21.} $2\times \num{14000}\times 20 = 5.6\times 10^{5}$
divisions; $0.48\times 5.6\times 10^{5} = 2.7\times 10^{5}$
\omterm{def:b2:genome-diversification:mutation}{mutations}.
\textbf{22.} $2.7\times 10^{5}/4.8\times 10^{8} = 5.6\times 10^{-4}$ of
the genome; about \num{5400} coding differences.
\textbf{23.} Competition among cell lineages in the meristem (a
slower-dividing mutant line is displaced) and among \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{ramets} (a trunk
carrying a bad \omterm{def:b2:genome-diversification:mutation}{mutation} suckers less). Weaker because most somatic
\omterm{def:b2:genome-diversification:mutation}{mutations} are \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:vocabulary}{heterozygous} and masked, and nothing exposes them:
no \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:meiosis}{meiosis} makes them \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:vocabulary}{homozygous} and no single-cell zygote stage
tests the whole genome in one cell.
\textbf{24.} Not uniform: its trunks differ at hundreds of thousands
of sites. But the differences are mostly neutral and masked, so the
\omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{clone} is one \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:vocabulary}{genotype} in effect --- a mosaic of somatic variants of
one \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{genet}.
\textbf{25.} Field full after 2.3 years; one meristem gives
$1.6\times 10^{6}$ plants in nine months; with $c = 0.8$ the apomict
settles at $p^* = 0.625$, the sexuals keeping \qty{37.5}{\%}.
\end{solution}
