\chapter{The Brain in Command}\label{ch:g5:brain-in-command}

A ball flies toward you; you catch it. Half a second, no
thought at all --- yet in that half-second, your \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eyes} reported
a flying object, something computed its path, and dozens of
\omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} were ordered into exactly the right catch. The
something is the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}, and this chapter follows its commands
along the body's wiring.

\section{The command network}

\begin{definition}[The nervous system]\label{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}
The \emph{nervous system}\index{nervous system} is the body's
command network:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{brain}\index{brain}, in the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{skull}'s shelter
        (\cref{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}), is the
        center: it receives all reports and issues all orders;
  \item the \emph{spinal cord}\index{spinal cord}, a thick
        cable of nerve tissue, runs from the brain down inside
        the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{spine}'s protective column;
  \item the \emph{nerves}\index{nerve}, white threads
        branching from brain and spinal cord to every organ,
        carry the messages --- at more than
        \qty{100}{m/s} for the fastest.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Report, decide, order]\label{prop:g5:brain-in-command:loop}
Every action runs the same loop
(\cref{prop:g1:the-five-senses:brain} was its first sketch):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the \emph{\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{sense organs}} report: \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nerves} carry their
        messages \emph{to} the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain};
  \item the \emph{\omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}} puts reports together, decides, and
        composes orders;
  \item \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nerves} carry the orders \emph{from} the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} to the
        \emph{\omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}} (\cref{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}),
        which contract on command.
\end{enumerate}
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{Sense organs} report, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} obey --- neither ever decides.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[The catch, replayed slowly]\label{ex:g5:brain-in-command:catch}
Replay the half-second: \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eyes} track the ball and report,
continuously; the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}, comparing report after report, works
out where the ball will be; orders stream out to \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{legs} (a step
left), trunk (lean), \omterm{def:g1:my-body:parts}{arm} and fingers (open --- now close). No
single organ ``caught the ball'': \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eyes}, \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} and \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles}
each played their line of
\cref{prop:g5:brain-in-command:loop}.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[More than movement]\label{rem:g5:brain-in-command:more}
The same \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} that catches balls also stores your memories,
holds your attention on this page, dreams at night --- and
runs services you never think about: it keeps \omterm{def:g4:breathing:movements}{breathing} going
day and night (\cref{def:g4:breathing:movements}) and adjusts
the \omterm{def:g4:heart-and-blood:heart}{heart}'s pace to the body's needs
(\cref{ex:g4:heart-and-blood:effort}). Some commands are
yours to give; others the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} gives for you.
\end{remark}

\section{Measuring the loop: reaction time}

\begin{definition}[Reaction time]\label{def:g5:brain-in-command:reaction}
The loop is fast but not instant: report, decision and order
each take their share. The delay between a signal and the
body's response is the \emph{reaction time}\index{reaction
time} --- for a simple task (catch, press, grab), about a
fifth of a second in a practiced person, a little more in
children.
\end{definition}

\begin{method}[The ruler test]\label{met:g5:brain-in-command:ruler}
\omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:reaction}{Reaction time} with no instrument but a ruler:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a partner holds a ruler hanging vertically, zero mark
        down, between your open thumb and finger;
  \item without warning, the partner lets go; you catch the
        ruler as fast as you can;
  \item read where you caught it: the fewer centimetres slid
        past, the faster your loop;
  \item repeat several times and keep your typical value ---
        single tries jump around.
\end{enumerate}
The falling ruler turns time into centimetres: about
$\qty{15}{cm}$ corresponds to a sixth of a second.
\end{method}

\begin{example}[Why the passenger seat gasps first]\label{ex:g5:brain-in-command:driver}
A fifth of a second seems nothing --- until speed multiplies
it. A car at highway speed covers about \qty{7}{m} during a
fifth-of-a-second reaction: the driver has not yet touched
the brake, and the car has crossed a classroom. \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:reaction}{Reaction
time} is why distances between cars exist, and why a driver's
attention is a safety organ.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Training the loop]\label{rem:g5:brain-in-command:training}
Practice shortens and sharpens the loop --- not the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{nerves}'
speed, but the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}'s routing. The beginner juggler drops
everything; a month later the same \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} orders the same
hands through three balls without apparent effort. Learning
a skill is the \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} laying down better paths --- one more
thing that grows with exercise, like the \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:muscle}{muscles} of
\cref{met:g3:bones-and-muscles:care}.
\end{remark}

\section{Protecting the commander}

\begin{proposition}[Sleep, food, safety]\label{prop:g5:brain-in-command:protect}
The \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain} is the body's most protected organ --- \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{skull}
helmet, \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{spine} column --- and the most demanding: though a
small part of the body's weight, it consumes a large share
of its \omterm{prop:g3:balanced-diet:jobs}{energy} and oxygen
(\cref{prop:g4:heart-and-blood:carries}), and it does its
filing and repairs during \emph{\omterm{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}{sleep}}
(\cref{prop:g1:healthy-habits:needs}). A tired \omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}
commands badly: slower reactions, scattered attention,
grumpy decisions.
\end{proposition}
\admitted

\begin{example}[Helmets are for the loop]\label{ex:g5:brain-in-command:helmet}
\omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{Bones} mend (\cref{rem:g3:bones-and-muscles:alive}); the
\omterm{def:g5:brain-in-command:system}{brain}'s tissue, once badly hurt, mostly does not. That is
the calculation behind bicycle helmets: \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{skull} plus foam
where the body's own protection might not be enough. Cheap
insurance for the organ running everything else.
\end{example}
