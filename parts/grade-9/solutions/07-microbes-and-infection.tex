\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g9:microbes-and-infection} --- Microbes and Infection}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:1}
\omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{Bacteria} (nucleus-free single \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}), single-celled \omterm{def:g6:decomposers-and-soil:decomposers}{fungi}
like \omterm{ex:g6:producing-our-food:bread}{yeast} and the pond's other single-celled life --- and
\omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{viruses}, apart in kind: no \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}, no life of their own,
only packaged genetic text that borrows a living \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}'s
machinery.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:2}
The trillions of harmless \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria} resident on \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{skin} and in
the gut. Services: aiding \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:digestion}{digestion}, and occupying the
ground --- crowding \omterm{prop:g9:microbes-and-infection:mostly}{pathogens} off the landing sites.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:3}
Transmission (a cough's droplets); entry (a cut, the
airways); multiplication (divisions doubling by the hour,
or hijacked \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} copying); disease (the septic finger's
throb, the cold's raw throat).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:4}
The cut's bacterium multiplies between \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} in the warm
wet tissue, poisoning its neighborhood. The cold \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus}
multiplies inside the airway lining's \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}, each
hijacked \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell} releasing a crowd of copies.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:5}
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{Skin}: the wall at entry. The linings' brooms and baths
(airway cleaning, tears, \omterm{def:g4:journey-of-food:tract}{stomach} acid): entry and early
multiplication. The \omterm{prop:g9:microbes-and-infection:mostly}{resident flora}: entry --- ground
occupied. \omterm{def:g1:healthy-habits:hygiene}{Hygiene}'s soap, cooking and cold: transmission
and multiplication.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:6}
Transmission --- the fever's traveler carried on doctors'
hands from autopsy room to delivery ward. Pasteur showed
the travelers themselves: \omterm{def:g6:producing-our-food:fermentation}{microbes} as the agents of
souring, disease and \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:stages}{infection} --- and heat as their
stopper.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:7}
Antibiotics starve and break \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria} --- independent
\omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} with their own machinery to poison. A cold's \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus}
has no machinery of its own: it runs on \emph{yours}, and
a drug aimed at its workings aims at your \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells}' ---
which is why the bacterium's medicine \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves} the \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus}
untouched.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:8}
Wash before eating: blocks hand-to-mouth transmission.
Wash after the toilet: blocks the gut route's exit-to-
entry loop. Blow-and-cover the cough (and the sneeze's
\omterm{def:g1:my-body:joint}{elbow}): blocks droplet transmission at the source. (Teeth
and food rules block the mouth's multiplication sites.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:9}
Pathogen: food-poisoning \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria}, on the chicken.
Transmission: the dish itself. Entry: the gut. Multiplied
by: three warm car hours --- conditions-rule feeding a
doubling every half hour or so. Cheapest block: the cold
chain --- a cool-box (or eating it fresh); next-cheapest,
thorough cooking beforehand.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:10}
Because hands are couriers between compartments:
Semmelweis's doctors were clean by any daily standard yet
carried the ward's killer from corpse to mother. The wash
that matters is the one \emph{at the boundary} --- raw
chicken to salad, toilet to table --- where the route
would otherwise connect.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:11}
The antibiotic attacks multiplication --- breaking the
dividing \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria}. Used raggedly it becomes a selection
check run half-way: the susceptible die, the resistant
minority inherits the ward --- breeding the pathogen the
next patient meets.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g9:microbes-and-infection:12}
The text: a scrap of genetic letters, the same alphabet as
every \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}'s (\cref{rem:g9:genes-and-dna:universal}). The
printer: a living \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cell}'s reading-and-building machinery,
which the entered text turns to printing copies of
itself. Life's edge: alone, the \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus} does nothing on
\cref{prop:g1:living-or-not:signs}'s list --- no feeding,
no growth, no division; borrowed, it runs a parody of
reproduction. Hard to drug: there is no virus-machinery
to poison --- only the printer, and the printer is you.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g9:microbes-and-infection:1}
\textbf{1.} Droplet transmission --- coughed and spoken
--- with shared-surface help at the table-tennis bats;
entry by the airways.

\textbf{2.} Multiplication's: each case's hijacked lining
\omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} print copies that \omterm{def:g2:from-seed-to-plant:seed}{seed} the next cases --- the
doubling is the \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus}'s borrowed machinery, compounding.

\textbf{3.} Disease. From the damage of hijacked, failing
lining \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} --- and, in part, from the defense's own
battle, as the next chapter details.

\textbf{4.} Because assemblies connect every class's
droplet-range for an hour: transmission scales with
crowding, and the school's two densest mixings were the
route between compartments.

\textbf{5.} Sick students home: transmission removed at
source. Windows: droplet doses diluted --- transmission.
Hand-washing: the surface route cut --- transmission.
Wiping equipment: the shared-object route cut ---
transmission. (All aimed at the same cheapest stage.)

\textbf{6.} ``Antibiotics kill \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria}; this agent is a
\omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{virus} running on the children's own \omterm{def:g5:first-look-at-cells:cell}{cells} --- the drug
cannot touch it, and scattering it anyway would only run
a selection sieve over everyone's resident \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria},
breeding resistance for the day antibiotics are truly
needed.''

\textbf{7.} Agent: food-poisoning \omterm{def:g9:microbes-and-infection:sorted}{bacteria}. Transmission:
the dessert. Entry: the gut. Multiplication: unrefrigerated
hours --- warmth, moisture, food, time, all granted.
Block: the fridge --- \omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions} refused; the two
outbreaks share a school and nothing else.

\textbf{8.} An outbreak map is transmission made visible:
who-sat-where draws the route --- rows, partners,
assemblies --- and the route, once drawn, shows where to
cut. Random treatment fights cases; the map fights the
spread.

\textbf{9.} Transmission: the index case's droplets, row
and partners first, assemblies to the school. Entry:
airways, class by class. Multiplication: two-day
doublings through week one. Disease and fade-out: fevers
crest, measures cut the routes, the susceptible pool
thins --- the curve turns down.

\textbf{10.} The measures' blocks starved the routes ---
and the pool itself was smaller than the roll: students
carrying last winter's cousin-strain immunity could not
be lit, and an outbreak dies when too few catchable
remain: the next chapter's memory, previewed.

\textbf{11.} Semmelweis's: cut the courier routes ---
home isolation, washed hands, wiped bats. Pasteur's:
know the traveler --- name the agent, refuse it
\omterm{def:g6:exploring-our-environment:conditions}{conditions}, aim the remedy at its kind and not at
custom.

\textbf{12.} ``Outbreaks are cheapest to stop at
transmission --- soap, air, distance and a closed
cool-box beat any medicine that waits for
multiplication.''
\end{solution}
