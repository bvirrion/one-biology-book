\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:b2:plant-meristems} --- Plant Vegetative Development: Meristems and Growth}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:1}
A \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{meristem} is a persistent population of undifferentiated dividing
cells from which the plant's tissues derive. \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{Shoot apical meristem}:
leaves, stem, \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:flower}{flowers}; \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{root apical meristem}: the root; \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:wood}{vascular
cambium}: secondary xylem (\omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:wood}{wood}) and phloem; cork \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{cambium}: the
\omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:wood}{periderm} (cork) of the \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:wood}{bark}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:2}
\omterm{prop:b2:plant-meristems:ram}{Root cap} (protection, lubrication, gravity sensing, cells sloughed
off); \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{meristem} with the \omterm{prop:b2:plant-meristems:ram}{quiescent centre} (division); \omterm{prop:b2:plant-meristems:ram}{elongation zone}
(cells lengthen ten- to twentyfold); \omterm{def:b2:cell-differentiation:differentiation}{differentiation} zone (\omterm{prop:b2:plant-meristems:ram}{root
hairs}, mature xylem and phloem). Lateral roots arise from the
pericycle of the mature root, opposite the xylem poles.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:3}
Bean plants were decapitated: the lateral buds grew out. On a
second set the cut stump received an agar block containing auxin:
the buds stayed dormant; on the control set, a plain agar block: the
buds grew. Conclusion: auxin from the apex is what inhibits the
lateral buds --- \omterm{prop:b2:plant-meristems:apical}{apical dominance} is hormonal.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:4}
Sixty years old; the sapwood is the last fifteen rings; alive are
the \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{cambium}, the phloem, the ray and parenchyma cells of the
sapwood, and the cork \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{cambium} --- a thin shell around a dead core.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:5}
$0.1\times(P - 0.3)$: \qty{0.01}{h^{-1}} (doubling in $\ln 2/0.01 =
\qty{69}{h}$), \qty{0.03}{h^{-1}} (\qty{23}{h}), \qty{0.05}{h^{-1}}
(\qty{14}{h}). At \qty{0.25}{MPa} the turgor is below the yield
threshold: growth stops, before any wilting.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:6}
Leaves 1--8 at 0, 137.5, 275, 52.5, 190, 327.5, 105, \qty{242.5}{\degree}.
Sorted: 0, 52.5, 105, 137.5, 190, 242.5, 275, 327.5 --- the smallest
gap is \qty{32.5}{\degree}. Each leaf sits in a gap of the previous
ones, so the eight shade one another as little as possible and the
crown fills the disc of light evenly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:7}
$2\pi r h\Delta r$: at \qty{5}{cm}, $2\pi\times 0.05\times 12\times
0.003 = \qty{0.011}{m^3}$, \qty{5.7}{kg}, \qty{2.8}{kg} of carbon; at

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:8}
\emph{clv3}: no brake --- enormous \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{meristems}, extra leaves and floral
organs, fasciated stems. \emph{wus}: no accelerator --- the \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{meristem}
runs out after a few leaves and is repeatedly re-founded. Double
mutant: the \emph{wus} \omterm{def:b2:meiosis-heredity:vocabulary}{phenotype}, so WUS acts downstream of CLV3:
CLV3's job is to repress WUS, and without WUS there is nothing to
repress.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:9}
Transport: \qty{20}{h}. Diffusion: $(0.2)^{2}/(2\times 10^{-9}) =
\qty{2e7}{s}$, about 230 days. Polar transport makes a hormonal
signal usable over the length of a plant within a day, and gives
it a direction.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:10}
\qty{3}{cm} a day of mature tissue with cells of \qty{200}{\micro m}:
\num{150} cells per file per day. A hundred dividing cells must
therefore each divide 1.5 times a day: a cycle of about \qty{16}{h}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:11}
Gibberellin lengthens internodes; a plant that cannot respond has
short internodes and a short stem. Heavy nitrogen fertiliser makes
a tall wheat grow taller and heavier-headed until it falls over
(lodges) and rots; a short stiff stem stands, and puts the extra
carbon into grain rather than straw, so the harvest index rises. In
the wild a short plant is overtopped and shaded by its neighbours
and loses.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:b2:plant-meristems:12}
\omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{Meristems} let a plant add organs where and when conditions favour
them --- leaves toward light, roots toward water --- and abandon
those that do not pay; every axillary bud is a spare growing
point, so grazing, fire and pruning are survived by regrowth; and
because the \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{meristems} are perpetually embryonic, a \omterm{def:b2:asexual-reproduction:clone}{genet} has no
fixed lifespan. The cost: a plant cannot move or reorganise what it
has built --- an organ once placed stays where it is, and a body
built by accretion must carry its dead past (the heartwood) with it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:b2:plant-meristems:1}
\textbf{1.} $150/3 = 50$ leaves per apex; \num{10000} for the tree.
\textbf{2.} 0, 137.5, 275, 52.5 and \qty{190}{\degree}.
\textbf{3.} $8\times 137.5 = 1100 = 3\times 360 + 20$; $13\times 137.5
= 1787.5 = 5\times 360 - 12.5$; $21\times 137.5 = 2887.5 = 8\times 360
+ 7.5$: the 22nd leaf is the first within \qty{10}{\degree} of the
first.
\textbf{4.} Each new leaf sits in the widest gap, so leaves shade
one another least; it is auxin, whose polar transport (PIN
carriers) drains the surroundings of each primordium, that sets
the spacing.
\textbf{5.} $\num{10000}\times 80\,\text{cm}^{2} = \qty{80}{m^2}$: the
whole crown is this season's leaves.
\textbf{6.} All of it; the first leaves are built from starch stored
in the wood and roots the previous year.
\textbf{7.} A tripled apex makes primordia faster --- two or three per
plastochron --- so leaves and leaf area could triple, but the shoots
would be fasciated, with ribbon-like stems and disordered
phyllotaxis, and much of the extra leaf would shade itself.
\textbf{8.} Day: $0.05\times(0.55 - 0.35) = \qty{0.01}{h^{-1}}$; night:
$0.05\times 0.40 = \qty{0.02}{h^{-1}}$.
\textbf{9.} Day: $2e^{0.12} = \qty{2.25}{cm}$, \qty{0.25}{cm} added;
night: $2.25e^{0.24} = \qty{2.87}{cm}$, \qty{0.61}{cm} added.
\textbf{10.} Mean rate \qty{0.015}{h^{-1}} over \qty{96}{h}: $2e^{1.44}
= \qty{8.4}{cm}$.
\textbf{11.} Day: $0.05\times 0.30 = \qty{0.015}{h^{-1}}$; night:
$0.05\times 0.50 = \qty{0.025}{h^{-1}}$.
\textbf{12.} $0.05\times 0.05 = \qty{0.0025}{h^{-1}}$, a quarter of the
normal day rate. Osmotic adjustment raises the cell's solute content
so that, at the same water potential, turgor rises back above the
threshold.
\textbf{13.} By day transpiration lowers the water potential of the
leaf and turgor with it, so $P - Y$ is small; at night turgor is
restored. Growth is limited by water, not by sugar, hour by hour.
\textbf{14.} $2\pi\times 0.10\times 8\times 0.005 = \qty{0.025}{m^3}$;
\qty{12.6}{kg} dry.
\textbf{15.} \qty{6.3}{kg} of carbon.
\textbf{16.} $80\times 4\times 150 = \qty{48}{kg}$ of carbon.
\textbf{17.} $6.3/48 = \qty{13}{\%}$. Other sinks: the leaves
themselves, roots, branches and twigs, the respiration of every
living cell (about half of what is fixed), reserves, \omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:flower}{flowers} and
\omterm{def:b2:angiosperm-reproduction:seed}{seeds}.
\textbf{18.} Radius \qty{0.20}{m}: $2\pi\times 0.20\times 8\times
0.005 = \qty{0.050}{m^3}$, twice this year's --- the circumference
the \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{cambium} works along has doubled (and the tree will be taller).
\textbf{19.} A dry or cold year; or a defoliation by insects, frost
or fire. A climate cause narrows the same rings in every tree of the
region and correlates with weather records; damage affects one tree
or stand, and frost or fire leaves anatomical scars in the ring.
\textbf{20.} The buds nearest each cut grow out within days into
several shoots; in a month the tree is bushier and denser, with more
but shorter shoots.
\textbf{21.} Auxin on the cut replaces the apex's signal: the buds
stay dormant and no branching follows.
\textbf{22.} Every clipping removes the apices and releases the buds
below; six times a year, each release adds a tier of short shoots,
and the surface fills with them.
\textbf{23.} From dormant axillary buds on the stump and adventitious
buds formed by the \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{cambium}; each bud is a complete \omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:meristem}{meristem} able to
rebuild the shoot system, whereas an animal's head is a single,
non-replaceable \omterm{def:b2:vertebrate-development:induction}{organiser} with no reserve copies.
\textbf{24.} More cytokinin reaching the buds promotes their outgrowth
--- watering a pruned plant makes it branch more.
\textbf{25.} 50 leaves per apex, \num{10000} per tree; internode rates
\qty{0.01}{h^{-1}} by day and \qty{0.02}{h^{-1}} by night; the year's
\omterm{def:b2:plant-meristems:wood}{wood} \qty{0.025}{m^3}, \qty{12.6}{kg} dry, \qty{6.3}{kg} of carbon.
\end{solution}
