\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g4:adapted-to-their-world} --- Adapted to Their World}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:1}
A feature of body or behavior that fits a \omterm{def:g1:living-or-not:living}{living thing} to its
\omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{environment}'s conditions. \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{Animal}: the polar bear's double
insulation of \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur} and fat. \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{Plant}: the cactus's water-storing
\omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{stem}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:2}
No water: drinking a hundred litres at once and lasting days
without, plus the humps' fat store. Soft sand: wide two-toed
feet. Blowing dust: closable nostrils and long double
eyelashes. (Heat and hunger: the humps again --- fat for lean
crossings.)
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:3}
Cold: thick \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur} over a deep fat layer. Icy water and snow:
broad, slightly webbed paws --- snowshoes and paddles. Wary
prey on white ice: the camouflage of a white coat.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:4}
The \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{spines} are shrunken \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}: they lose almost no water and
defend the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:plant}{plant} against thirsty \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:animal}{animals}. The barrel \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{stem}
stores water, and has taken over the \omterm{def:g1:plants-around-us:parts}{leaves}' light-catching
work.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:5}
The cushion shape breaks the freezing wind: inside it the air
stays milder. The shape itself is the shelter.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:6}
Hunted: camouflage colors, armor or \omterm{def:g3:bones-and-muscles:skeleton}{spines}, speed, wide-set
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eyes} watching all round (two suffice). Hunters: forward-facing
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eyes}, sharp senses, claws, speed (two suffice).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:7}
Dense soft \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur} --- a mammal; huge shovel-shaped front claws
--- a digger; tiny weak \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{eyes} --- life in the dark. Together:
an underground digging mammal, the mole.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:8}
Open answer. Example for the duck: webbed feet (paddling),
water-shedding \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{feathers} (staying dry and warm afloat), flat
sifting beak (feeding from water).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:9}
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{Ears} are radiators: big thin ones shed body warmth. In the
desert, shedding warmth is the problem to solve --- enormous
\omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{ears} cool the fox. On the ice, keeping warmth is everything
--- tiny \omterm{def:g1:the-five-senses:sense}{ears} lose the least. One organ, two climates, two
opposite sizes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:10}
Streamlining, webbed feet and breath-holding say: a swimmer
and diver; dense water-shedding \omterm{def:g1:animals-around-us:covering}{fur} says: a mammal living
half in cold water; whiskers say: feeling its way in dark or
murky water. A water-dwelling, dam-building mammal of rivers
and lakes --- the beaver.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g4:adapted-to-their-world:11}
The water set it. Whale and trout inherited nothing
swimming-shaped from a shared family --- one is a mammal, the
other a \omterm{prop:g3:sorting-living-things:groups}{fish} --- yet the same problem, moving fast through
water, favors the same streamlined answer in both. \omterm{def:g4:adapted-to-their-world:adaptation}{Adaptations}
answer the \omterm{def:g2:caring-for-nature:environment}{environment}'s problems; similar problems can draw
similar shapes from very different families.
\end{solution}
