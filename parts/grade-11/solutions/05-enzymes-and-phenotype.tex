\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype} --- Enzymes and the Phenotype}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:1}
\omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{Genotype}: the set of \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{alleles} carried. \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{Phenotype}: the set of observable
characteristics, at the molecular, cellular and organismal scales.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:2}
The pocket of the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} where the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{substrate} binds and is transformed.
Specificity: the site fits one \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{substrate} (or one family of \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{substrates})
and catalyses one reaction.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:3}
Near \qty{37}{\celsius}; at \qty{45}{\celsius} about a third remains.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:4}
Molecular: tyrosinase absent or inactive, no melanin made. Cellular:
pigment \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} present but empty of melanin. Organismal: white hair,
very pale skin and eyes, sensitivity to light and sunburn.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:5}
Pepsin's fold works only in strong acid (pH 2); the intestine is
neutral to slightly alkaline, where the curve shows zero activity.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:6}
One working \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} produces half the normal \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme}, but an \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme}
molecule works thousands of times per second: half the amount still
converts enough tyrosine to make normal pigment. The \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{phenotype} is
saturated; only two failing \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{alleles} show.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:7}
Their albinism comes from blocks in two different \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{genes} (two different
\omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzymes} of the pathway, or of melanin's transport). The child inherits
one working \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} of each \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene} from the parent whose block is
elsewhere, so both \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzymes} work and pigment is made.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:8}
Phenylalanine accumulates; tyrosine (and what derives from it) is
lacking. Since melanin is made from tyrosine, its shortage reduces
pigment: fair hair and skin are common in untreated phenylketonuria.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:9}
Where milk is a staple, digesting it provides energy and calcium
without illness, so the persistent \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} is an advantage. Where
adults drink no milk the \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} changes nothing; it is "healthier"
only in the environment that supplies the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{substrate}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:10}
For tyrosinase: molecular, the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{substrate} no longer fits, no melanin;
cellular, empty pigment \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells}; organismal, albinism. For lactase:
lactose undigested; intestinal \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} starved of the sugar and the
colon fermenting it; intolerance.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:11}
Its extremities stay warmer than \qty{35}{\celsius}, the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} barely
works, and the kitten is pale all over. Outdoors in winter the ears
and paws cool, the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} becomes active, and the fur that regrows
there comes in dark.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:12}
Molecular: half the haemoglobin is the sickle form. Cellular: at
normal oxygen the \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} stay round; at very low oxygen some sickle.
Organismal: healthy in ordinary life, at risk at altitude or when
diving --- and protected against malaria. The environment (oxygen,
malaria) decides whether the carrier's \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{phenotype} is a burden or an
advantage.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:13}
At \qty{41}{\celsius} the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzymes} still hold most of their activity
(the curve is near its top); at \qty{43}{\celsius} activity is falling
steeply as \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{proteins} begin to unfold, and if the unfolding proceeds it
is irreversible: \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzymes} are destroyed rather than slowed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:14}
More \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:organelle}{mitochondria} and capillaries per muscle fibre; a larger heart
with a bigger stroke volume; more haemoglobin per litre of blood;
larger \omterm{def:g10:exercise-and-energy:glycogen}{glycogen} stores. Each is a \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{phenotype} produced by the same
\omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{genotype} under a different environment: "genetic" traits are the
range the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{genotype} allows, not a fixed value.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:15}
The \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{genotype} fixes which \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} is made and how it behaves: no \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme}
1 (phenylketonuria), no tyrosinase (albinism), a heat-sensitive
tyrosinase (Siamese). The environment can change the outcome only
within what the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} allows: a diet removes the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{substrate} and saves
the brain, but cannot make the missing \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme}; cold makes the Siamese
\omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} work, but no temperature makes the albino's; and nothing in the
environment gives the albino its pigment. The \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{genotype} sets the range;
the environment chooses within it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:1}
\textbf{1.} Trunk \qty{38}{\celsius}: about 2\%, pale. Legs
\qty{36}{\celsius}: about 20\%, pale. Paws \qty{33}{\celsius}: 80\%,
dark. Ears and muzzle \qty{31}{\celsius}: over 90\%, dark.

\textbf{2.} Pale body and legs, dark paws, ears, muzzle (and tail):
the Siamese pattern.

\textbf{3.} 100\% everywhere: a uniformly pigmented cat.

\textbf{4.} In the womb every part of the kitten is at
\qty{38}{\celsius}, the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} is inactive throughout, and no pigment
is laid down before birth; the points darken as the extremities cool
after birth.

\textbf{5.} Around \qty{35.5}{\celsius}, where the activity crosses
30\%: the steep descending part of the curve between 33 and
\qty{37}{\celsius} fixes the boundary.

\textbf{6.} Dark: at \qty{30}{\celsius} the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} is fully active in
the growing hairs.

\textbf{7.} Pale: at \qty{38}{\celsius} the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} is inactive.

\textbf{8.} Pigment is deposited in the hair as it grows and cannot be
removed or added afterwards; the colour changes only when the hairs
are shed and replaced by new ones grown at the local temperature.

\textbf{9.} The \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{genotype} of the patch's \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} is the same before and
after, and the same as the rest of the trunk; only the temperature of
the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} changed, and with it the pigment. The action is on the
\omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme}'s activity, a molecular event.

\textbf{10.} All the cat's \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} descend from one egg and carry the
same \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{alleles}; and the shaved-patch experiments change the colour of a
region without changing its \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} --- an \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} cannot be switched by
an ice pack.

\textbf{11.} Molecular: a tyrosinase that works only below about
\qty{35}{\celsius}. Cellular: pigment \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} full of melanin in the
extremities, empty in the trunk. Organismal: a pale cat with dark
points.

\textbf{12.} The \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acid} sits where it stabilises the fold; the
replacement holds the fold together at low temperature but lets it
loosen a few degrees higher, so the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{active site} loses its shape
before the ordinary \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme}'s does.

\textbf{13.} The ordinary \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele}'s \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} is fully active at body
temperature; half the normal amount is still more than enough to make
pigment everywhere. The Siamese \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} is recessive to it.

\textbf{14.} Molecular: no working \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} at any temperature, against
a conditionally working one. Cellular: no melanin anywhere, against
melanin in the cool regions. Organismal: an all-white cat with pink
eyes, against the pointed pattern. Cold changes nothing for the
albino.

\textbf{15.} That they carry a temperature-sensitive \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{allele} of the
same \omterm{def:g10:universal-dna:gene}{gene}; probably a similar substitution destabilising the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme},
arisen independently in each species.

\textbf{16.} Twentyfold; \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} 1, which converts phenylalanine to
tyrosine.

\textbf{17.} Excess phenylalanine interferes with the developing
brain's \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} specifically (their supply of other \omterm{def:g10:chemistry-of-life:families}{amino acids} and
their maturation); skin \omterm{def:g10:cells-common-unit:cell}{cells} tolerate it. The molecular defect is the
same everywhere; the cellular consequence is not.

\textbf{18.} A fifth of \num{1200} is \qty{240}{\micro mol/L}, below
\num{360}: safe. Without the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} the blood level simply follows
the intake, so the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{phenotype} is set by the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{substrate} supply --- the
environment --- rather than by the missing \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} alone.

\textbf{19.} Phenylalanine is needed to build the body's \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{proteins} and
cannot be synthesised; with none, growth stops. The intake must be
enough for \omterm{def:g11:gene-expression:protein}{protein} synthesis and no more, which requires measuring
and adjusting.

\textbf{20.} The \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{genotype} fixed the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:enzyme}{enzyme} (heat-sensitive in the cat,
absent in the child); the environment set its conditions (skin
temperature; dietary phenylalanine); the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{phenotype} is the result of
the \omterm{def:g11:enzymes-and-phenotype:phenotype}{genotype} expressed in a given environment.
\end{solution}
